### 2. On three nodes, two polynomials share the samples and not the limit

The value at mesh zero is different:

\[
H(0)=\Bigl(-\frac1{16}\Bigr)\Bigl(-\frac1{36}\Bigr)\Bigl(-\frac1{64}\Bigr)
=-\frac1{16\cdot 36\cdot 64}.
\]

Since \(16\cdot 64=1024\) and \(1024\cdot 36=36864\), this is \(H(0)=-1/36864\). Adding \(H\) preserves the three samples and moves the limit at zero by \(-1/36864\).

The printed weights are \(w=(4/15,-81/35,64/21)\). On denominator \(105\),

\[
\frac4{15}=\frac{28}{105},\qquad
-\frac{81}{35}=-\frac{243}{105},\qquad
\frac{64}{21}=\frac{320}{105},
\]

and \(28-243+320=105\), so \(\sum w_i=1\).
