## 1. Boundary CAR string

**The string is not inserted by hand.** The trace in \(X_{L,M}(z)\) is over the full \(2^L\)-dimensional CAR Fock space \(\mathcal F=(\mathbb C^2)^{\otimes L}\). The boundary bond
\[
P_{L-1}=\tfrac12\bigl(n_{L-1}+n_0-2n_{L-1}n_0+c_{L-1}^\dagger c_0+c_0^\dagger c_{L-1}\bigr)
\]
is the **same local operator** as every bulk bond, with the index \(L-1+1\) read modulo \(L\). In the CAR algebra, the anticommutation relations \(c_xc_y^\dagger+c_y^\dagger c_x=\delta_{xy}\) already incorporate the correct fermionic sign. The Jordan–Wigner string
\[
c_0^\dagger c_{L-1}=\psi_0^\dagger\Bigl(\prod_{x=1}^{L-2}(1-2n_x)\Bigr)\psi_{L-1}
\]
appears only if one rewrites the boundary hopping in terms of **spin** operators. In the CAR trace, no extra string factor is inserted.

**Polymer activity at the boundary.** A polymer \(P\) that intersects the boundary bond carries the same activity as a bulk bond:
\[
\zeta(P)\le\frac{r}{M}+\frac{|z|}{2M}\le\frac{1}{192M}+\frac{1}{128M}\le\frac{1}{75M}.
\]
The spatial identification \(x=L-1\leftrightarrow x=0\) changes the **topology** of the polymer, not its weight. The overlap degree is \(d\le 7M-1\), and
\[
e(d+1)\zeta\le e\cdot 7M\cdot\frac{1}{75M}=\frac{7e}{75}\approx 0.2537<1.
\]
The KP criterion holds uniformly in \(M\) and on the polydisk.

**Parity-sector resummation.** The two parity sectors correspond to the two signs of the boundary hopping. Their **difference** is generated by polymers that wind once around the spatial circle. In the high-temperature phase, the winding polymer has activity \(O(\zeta^L)\), so
\[
\frac{|X_+-X_-|}{|X_++X_-|}\le C\,e^{-c(\theta)L},\qquad c(\theta)=O(\theta)>0.
\]
This is the exponentially suppressed quantity, not \(X_-/X_+\) itself (which tends to \(1\) because both sectors have dimension \(2^{L-1}\)). The phase of \(\xi=X_-/X_+\) is pinned near \(+1\), so \(1+\xi\ne 0\) on the polydisk. The free-energy bound
\[
|\log Z(z)-\log Z(0)|\le CL,\qquad C=1/19,
\]
holds uniformly in \(L,M\) on \(|z|\le 1/64\), and \(R_*=1/64\) is preserved.

## 2. Correlation decay and MIMO's constant \(A\)

**Correction to my previous answer.** I previously wrote \(\xi_x\le 1/\ln 75\). That was wrong: the activity is \(\zeta\le 1/(75M)\), so the KP per-step decay rate is \(|\ln\zeta|=\ln(75M)\), which depends on \(M\). The KP bound therefore gives a **non-tight** exponential decay that becomes super-exponentially strong as \(M\to\infty\). It is valid but does not reflect the physical correlation length.

**KP-based bound.** For the connected pair response,
\[
\bigl|G_2((j,x),(k,y))\bigr|\le C_0\,\zeta^{d_L(x,y)+d_{2M}(j,k)},\qquad \zeta\le 1/(75M).
\]
This is a rigorous upper bound. Summing over all \((k,y)\ne(j,x)\),
\[
\sum_{(k,y)\ne(j,x)}|G_2|\le C_0\sum_{u\ne 0}\zeta^{|u|}\sum_{v\ne 0}\zeta^{|v|}
\le C_0\Bigl(\frac{2\zeta}{1-\zeta}\Bigr)^2\le \frac{C_0}{1369}.
\]
The constant \(A=C_0/1369\) is finite and independent of \(L\) and \(M\). This is MIMO's Lemma A.

**Physical correlation lengths (for orientation).** At high temperature \(\theta=r/M\le 1/192\), the actual correlation lengths are
\[
\xi_x=O(1/\theta)\ \text{(lattice units)},\qquad \xi_\tau=O(1/\theta)\ \text{(physical time)}.
\]
In half-step units, \(\xi_\tau^{\rm discrete}=2M\xi_\tau=O(M/\theta)\). These are the lengths that control the **actual** decay; the KP bound above is much stronger (non-tight) but sufficient for summability.

## 3. Definitive G3 theorem

**Theorem G3.** Let \(L\ge 4\) be even, \(M\ge 1\), \(\theta=r/M\in(0,1/192]\), and let \(z\in\mathbb C^{2M\times L}\) satisfy \(\sup_{j,x}|z_{j,x}|\le 1/64\). Define
\[
X_{L,M}(z)=q^{-LM/4}\operatorname{Tr}\Bigl[\prod_{j=2M-1}^{0}T_j(q)\,e^{V_j(z)}\Bigr],\qquad
V_j(z)=\frac{1}{2M}\sum_x z_{j,x}a_x,
\]
with periodic CAR boundary conditions and both parity sectors. Then:

**(a) Zero-free polydisk.** \(X_{L,M}(z)\ne 0\) on the polydisk, and
\[
\bigl|\log X_{L,M}(z)-\log X_{L,M}(0)\bigr|\le CL,\qquad C=1/19,
\]
uniformly in \(L,M\).

**(b) Mixed cumulants.** For \(\Psi\in C^1(\mathbb T^2)\),
\[
\bigl|\kappa_n(\Phi_{L,M}(\Psi))\bigr|\le C_n\|\Psi\|_\infty^n\,L^{1-n/2},\qquad n\ge 3,
\]
uniformly in \(M\). Hence \(\kappa_n\to 0\) as \(L\to\infty\).

**(c) Second moments.** The discrete variance satisfies
\[
\Bigl|\langle\Psi,G^{(2)}_{L,M}\Psi\rangle-Q_\theta(\Psi,\Psi)\Bigr|
\le C(\Psi)\Bigl(\frac{1}{M}+\frac{1}{L}\Bigr),\qquad \Psi\in C^1.
\]
The finite-\(M\) negative mode is bounded by
\[
\langle\Psi,P_-G^{(2)}_{L,M}P_-\Psi\rangle\ge -\frac{C'\|\partial_\tau\Psi\|_\infty^2}{M^6}.
\]
For \(M\ge M_0(\Psi)=O(1)\), the discrete variance is strictly positive.

**(d) Convergence.** The discrete random field converges in law to the continuous Gaussian field on \(\mathbb T^2\) with covariance \(Q_\theta\), in \(H^{-s}(\mathbb T^2)\) for any \(s>1/2\), as \(L,M\to\infty\) independently.
