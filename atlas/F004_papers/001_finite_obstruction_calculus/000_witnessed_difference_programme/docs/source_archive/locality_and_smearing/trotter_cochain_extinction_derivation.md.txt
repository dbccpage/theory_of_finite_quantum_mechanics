# The Observable Invisibility and Smearing Convergence Theorem for the Periodic Meron Chain

**AuthorityEffect:** None.

## 0. Declarations, normalization, and scope

Let \(L=4\) unless another value is explicitly stated. Let \(M\geq 1\), let \(N=2M\), and let the sampled profile be

\[
\Psi_{\mathrm{grid}}
=
\left(
\Psi\left(\frac jN,x_k\right)
\right)_{0\leq j<N,\;0\leq k<L}.
\]

Here \(x_k=k/L\). The physical smeared second cumulant is normalized by

\[
q_{L,M}(\Psi)
=
\frac{1}{L N^2}
\Psi_{\mathrm{grid}}^{\top}
G^{(2)}_{L,M}
\Psi_{\mathrm{grid}}.
\]

The exact response matrices and the CAR Hessian convention are taken as the audited repository objects. In particular:

- \(G^{(2)}_{4,1}\) is the \(8\times 8\) one-particle-sector response;
- the surrounding CAR Fock space is \(16\)-dimensional;
- the matrices \(G^{(2)}_{4,M}\) for \(M=2,3\) have dimensions \(16\times16\) and \(24\times24\);
- the two time sectors are staggered, so
  \[
  v_j=(-1)^j,\qquad j=0,\ldots,N-1.
  \]

All spectral claims below concern the declared matrices under this convention. A change of CAR normalization, temporal staggering, or physical normalization rescales the listed eigenvalues.

Two limitations are part of the mathematical statement:

1. The exact \(M=1,2,3\) receipts establish the listed behavior at \(L=4\), but do not by themselves determine the exact characteristic polynomial for general \(M\).
2. Convergence of a second cumulant is not enough to establish convergence of a field to a centered Gaussian law. The Gaussian conclusion in Section 3 therefore requires an additional WDP independence or mixing hypothesis that is stated explicitly.

# SECTION 1. EXACT SPECTRAL ANATOMY AND THE CLOSED-FORM BRANCH

## 1.1 The staggered vector and the sign convention

For an even number \(N=2M\), define the spatial-uniform temporal-staggered response vector by

\[
\bigl(v_{\mathrm{stag}}^{(N)}\bigr)_{(j,k)}
=
(-1)^j,
\qquad
0\leq j<N,\quad 0\leq k<L.
\]

Its squared norm is

\[
\left\|v_{\mathrm{stag}}^{(N)}\right\|^2
=
LN.
\]

At \(L=4\) and \(M=1\), the notation reduces, after removing the fixed spatial factor, to

\[
v_{\mathrm{stag}}
=
(-1,-1,-1,-1,1,1,1,1)^\top.
\]

The alternating sequence satisfies

\[
v_{j+1}-v_j=-2v_j.
\]

Consequently, for the forward-difference convention

\[
(Df)_j=f_{j+1}-f_j,
\]

with periodic boundary conditions,

\[
Dv_{\mathrm{stag}}=-2v_{\mathrm{stag}},
\qquad
D^*Dv_{\mathrm{stag}}=4v_{\mathrm{stag}}.
\]

Thus the staggered mode is a graph-Laplacian eigenmode with eigenvalue \(+4\), not a lattice graph null mode. Any earlier treatment that placed this vector in \(\ker(D^*D)\) uses an inconsistent sign or boundary convention.

## 1.2 Closed-form evaluation of the \(M=1\) branch

Let \(r>0\) denote the declared response parameter. The exact one-particle response matrix \(G^{(2)}_{4,1}(r)\) is symmetric in the audited convention. Acting on the staggered branch gives the exact characteristic relation

\[
G^{(2)}_{4,1}(r)v_{\mathrm{stag}}
=
\lambda_{\mathrm{stag}}(r)v_{\mathrm{stag}},
\]

where direct rational elimination of the four row equations yields

\[
4(r+2)(r^2+4r+8)\lambda+r^4=0.
\]

The relevant root is therefore

\[
\boxed{

=
-\frac{r^4}
{4(r+2)(r^2+4r+8)}

\]

The denominator factorizes as

\[
(r+2)(r^2+4r+8)
=
r^3+6r^2+16r+16,
\]

so an equivalent branch equation is

\[
4(r^3+6r^2+16r+16)\lambda+r^4=0.
\]

This is the exact rational eigenvalue relation in the declared normalization. No irrational eigenvalue is introduced by this branch.

At \(r=1/10\),

=
-\frac{(1/10)^4}
{4(21/10)(281/100)}

\boxed{-\frac1{706440}}.

At \(r=1/192\),

\[

\frac{1+768+294912}{192^2}

\frac{295681}{192^2}.

Substitution gives

\[
\boxed{

\]

The branch expansion follows from

\[
\frac{1}{(1+2r)(1+2r+2r^2)}.
\]

Using

\[
\frac1{1+2r}
=
1-2r+4r^2-8r^3+\cdots

and

\[
\frac1{1+2r+2r^2}

-\frac{r^4}{4}

In particular, the first nonzero term has order \(r^4\), while the order-\(r^5\) coefficient does not vanish.

# SECTION 2. PHYSICAL SMEARED VARIANCE AND OBSERVABLE FILTERING

## 2.1 Definition of the smeared second cumulant

Let \(N=2M\). With the declared physical normalization,

\[
\boxed{
q_{L,M}(\Psi)
=
\frac{1}{L N^2}
\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}
\sum_{k=0}^{L-1}
\sum_{a,b=0}^{L-1}
G_{ab}^{(2)}
\left(
\frac jN,x_k
\right)
\left(
\frac jN,x_b
\right).
}
\]

This normalization is fixed by the declared smeared CAR variance. It prevents the increase of the number of time samples from being counted as an artificial growth of variance.

For the staggered vector, the physical eigenvector has entries

\[
V_{(j,k)}=(-1)^j.
\]

Its squared norm is \(LN\), and therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
q_{L,M}(V_{\mathrm{stag}})
&=
\frac{1}{LN^2}
\lambda_{\min}(M)
\left\|V_{\mathrm{stag}}\right\|^2\\
&=
\frac{1}{LN^2}
\lambda_{\min}(M)\,LN\\
&=
\frac{\lambda_{\min}(M)}{N}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{
q_{L,M}(V_{\mathrm{stag}})
=
\frac{\lambda_{\min}(M)}{2M}.
}
\]

## 2.2 Alternating observable profile

Define the observable mode sampled at time \(\tau_j=j/N\) by

\[
A_M(\tau)
=
\frac1N
\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}
(-1)^j
\Psi(\tau_j,\tau).
\]

Pairing the terms \(j=2k\) and \(j=2k+1\) gives

\[
A_M(\tau)
=
\frac1N
\sum_{k=0}^{M-1}
\left[
\Psi\left(\frac{2k}{N},\tau\right)
-
\Psi\left(\frac{2k+1}{N},\tau\right)
\right].
\]

By the mean-value estimate for each interval of length \(1/N\),

\[
\left|
\Psi\left(\frac{2k}{N},\tau\right)
-
\Psi\left(\frac{2k+1}{N},\tau\right)
\right|
\leq
\frac1N
\|\partial_\tau\Psi\|_\infty.
\]

Summing \(M\) paired intervals,

\[
|A_M(\tau)|
\leq
\frac{M}{N^2}
\|\partial_\tau\Psi\|_\infty.
\]

Since \(N=2M\),

\[
\boxed{
|A_M(\tau)|
\leq
\frac{\|\partial_\tau\Psi\|_\infty}{4M}.
}
\]

This estimate is valid for a continuously differentiable \(\tau\)-profile. The spatial variable in the preceding notation is replaced by the fixed observable variable \(x\); more generally, the same estimate holds pointwise in \(x\) with the corresponding derivative norm.

If \(\Psi\) is independent of time, then

\[
A_M(x)
=
\frac{\Psi(x)}{2M}
\sum_{j=0}^{2M-1}(-1)^j
=
0.
\]

Thus the alternating time filter annihilates every time-independent profile in the declared sample set.

## 2.3 Projection onto the negative sector

Write the exact spectral decomposition

\[
G^{(2)}_{L,M}
=
G_++G_0+G_-,
\]

where \(G_-\) is the negative-semidefinite contribution. Assume the one-negative-mode branch in the relevant range:

\[
G_-=-\mu_M P_-,
\qquad
\mu_M=|\lambda_{\min}(M)|>0,
\qquad
P_-^2=P_-.
\]

For a profile whose overlap with the negative eigenvector is represented by \(A_M\), its negative contribution is

\[
q_{L,M}^{\mathrm{neg}}(\Psi)
=
-\frac{\mu_M}{2M}\,
\left\|A_M^{\mathrm{grid}}\right\|_2^2,
\]

under the physical normalization

\[
q_{L,M}^{\mathrm{neg}}
=
\frac1{LN^2}
\left\langle
P_-\Psi_{\mathrm{grid}},
G_-
P_-\Psi_{\mathrm{grid}}
\right\rangle.
\]

Taking absolute values and applying the pointwise filter bound,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\left|q_{L,M}^{\mathrm{neg}}(\Psi)\right|
&\leq
\frac{\mu_M}{2M}
\sum_{j=0}^{2M-1}|A_M(\tau_j)|^2\\
&\leq
\frac{\mu_M}{2M}(2M)
\left(
\frac{\|\partial_\tau\Psi\|_\infty}{4M}
\right)^2\\
&=
\boxed{
\frac{\mu_M}{32M^2}
\|\partial_\tau\Psi\|_\infty^2
}.
\end{aligned}
\]

Therefore,

\[
\boxed{
\left|q_{L,M}^{\mathrm{neg}}(\Psi)\right|
\leq
\frac{|\lambda_{\min}(M)|}{32M^2}
\|\partial_\tau\Psi\|_\infty^2.
}
\]

This is the Observable Filtering Theorem in the declared normalization.

## 2.4 Exact rate and bounded-evaluator interpretation

The bound has two independent factors:

\[
\left|q_{L,M}^{\mathrm{neg}}(\Psi)\right|
\leq
\underbrace{\frac{|\lambda_{\min}(M)|}{2M}}_{\text{physical staggered variance}}
\underbrace{\frac{\|\partial_\tau\Psi\|_\infty^2}{16M}}_{\text{alternating-observable attenuation}}.
\]

For a fixed profile, the explicit filter factor is exactly of order \(M^{-1}\). The additional physical normalization contributes another factor \(M^{-1}\), so the displayed negative contribution is bounded by

\[
O_{L,\Psi,r}(M^{-2})
\]

whenever \(M\mapsto|\lambda_{\min}(M)|\) remains bounded.

The exact \(M=1,2,3\) eigenvalues satisfy

\[
\frac1{706440}
>
\frac{35281}{90644998440}
>
\frac{12188547841}{55802492242070440}
\]

in absolute value. This finite sequence supports decay of the negative eigenvalue receipt across the first three smear levels. It does not establish a formula for \(|\lambda_{\min}(M)|\) at all later \(M\).

For the time-independent part \(\Psi=\Psi_0(\tau,x)\) of a profile, exact cancellation gives

\[
A_M^{\Psi_0}=0
\]

for every even \(N=2M\). Hence, in that subspace,

\[
\boxed{
q_{L,M}^{\mathrm{neg}}(\Psi_0)=0
}
\]

without taking a limit.

The word “invisible” is therefore an operational statement about the alternating sampled observable: the bounded evaluator has zero overlap with time-independent profiles and an explicit \(M^{-1}\) filter factor for \(C^1\) profiles. It is not a statement that the negative eigenvector ceases to exist in the raw finite matrix.

# SECTION 3. REPAIRED STRICT-POSITIVITY AND CONVERGENCE

## 3.1 The spatial lattice null mode

The discrete spatial quadrature acts on functions sampled at

\[
x_k=\frac{k}{L}.
\]

Consider

\[
\Psi(\tau,x)
=
\sin(2\pi Lx).
\]

At every lattice point,

\[
\Psi(\tau,x_k)
=
\sin(2\pi k)
=
0.
\]

Therefore

\[
\Psi_{\mathrm{grid}}=0
\]

and, for any discrete response matrix acting on that sampled vector,

\[
\boxed{
q_{L,M}\bigl(\sin(2\pi Lx)\bigr)=0.
}
\]

The same profile is also annihilated by the spatial difference operator. The alternating time sequence does not repair this separate spatial null mode.

Consequently, a lower bound of the form

\[
q_{L,M}(\Psi)\geq c>0
\]

for all sampled profiles is incompatible with the declared lattice response. Strict positivity requires a margin condition on a selected profile class or a specified physical quotient.

## 3.2 Continuous covariance and the certified margin

Let the continuous periodic covariance kernel be represented by

\[
K_\theta(\tau-\tau',x-x')
=
\sum_{p\in\mathbb Z}
\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}
\widehat K_{p,k}
e^{2\pi i(p\tau+kx)}.
\]

For the reduced positive-Matsubara family,

\[
\widehat K_{0,k}
=
\frac{\theta^2}{8\pi^2 k^2},
\qquad
k\neq0,
\]

and the response is restricted to the physical low-frequency sector. The same notation can be used for a single positive Matsubara mode,

\[
\boxed{
\widehat K_n
=
\frac{\theta^2}{8\pi^2 n^2}>0,
\qquad n\neq0.
}
\]

Define the continuous quadratic form by

\[
Q_\theta(\Psi,\Psi)
=
\int_{\mathbb T^2}
\Psi(\tau,x)\,
K_\theta*\Psi(\tau,x)
\,d\tau\,dx.
\]

For a function with a finite spatial Fourier expansion,

\[
\Psi(\tau,x)
=
\sum_{k\in F}
a_k(\tau)e^{2\pi ikx},
\]

Parseval’s identity gives

\[
Q_\theta(\Psi,\Psi)
=
\sum_{k\in F}
|k|\,
\widehat K_{0,k}
\int_0^1
|a_k(\tau)|^2\,d\tau.
\]

Under the stated mode selection,

\[
Q_\theta(\Psi,\Psi)
=
\frac{\theta^2}{8\pi^2}
\sum_{k\in F}
\frac1{|k|}
\int_0^1|a_k(\tau)|^2\,d\tau.
\]

All coefficients in the sum are positive. Positivity is therefore strict for profiles having a nonzero coefficient in an admitted positive Matsubara mode.

The repaired convergence statement requires the profile-dependent margin

\[
\boxed{
Q_\theta(\Psi,\Psi)
=
q_{L,\infty}(\Psi)
\geq m_\Psi>0.
}
\]

This is a continuous-sector condition. It excludes sampled lattice artifacts such as \(\sin(2\pi Lx)\).

## 3.3 Discrete-to-continuous approximation error

Suppose the discrete response admits a uniform consistency estimate of the form

\[
\left|
G^{(2)}_{L,M}(\tau_j)
-
K_{\theta,M}(\tau_j-\tau')
\right\|_{L^1}
\leq
\delta_{L,M},
\qquad
\delta_{L,M}\to0,
\]

where \(K_{\theta,M}\) is the exact periodic covariance kernel associated with the \(M\)-point time discretization. Assume also the Riemann-sum consistency

\[
\sup_{\tau}
\left|
\frac1N
\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}
K_{\theta,M}(\tau-\tau_j)
-
K_\theta(\tau)
\right|
\leq
\varepsilon_{L,M},
\qquad
\varepsilon_{L,M}\to0.
\]

For a \(C^1\) profile on the compact torus, let

\[
\|\Psi\|_{C^1}
\]

denote the sum of the relevant first-derivative sup norms and the zeroth-order sup norm. Standard boundedness of the periodic convolution on \(C^1\) then gives

\[
\left|
q_{L,M}(\Psi)
-
q_{L,\infty}(\Psi)
\right|
\leq
E_{L,M}(\Psi),
\]

where one admissible form is

\[
\boxed{
E_{L,M}(\Psi)
=
C_L
\|\Psi\|_{C^1}^2
\left(
\frac{L}{N}
+
\delta_{L,M}
+
\varepsilon_{L,M}
\right).
}
\]

The constant \(C_L\) depends on a declared norm bound for the response kernels. The terms have distinct sources:

- \(L/N\) is the temporal Riemann-sum error;
- \(\delta_{L,M}\) is the response-kernel discretization error;
- \(\varepsilon_{L,M}\) is the exact-kernel Riemann-sum error.

If the response matrices are defined as exact entries of \(K_{\theta,M}\), then \(\delta_{L,M}=0\), and

\[
E_{L,M}(\Psi)
=
C_L
\|\Psi\|_{C^1}^2
\left(
\frac{L}{2M}
+
\varepsilon_{L,M}
\right).
\]

The first inequality is therefore a consequence of the declared consistency assumptions, not of the finite \(M=1,2,3\) receipts alone.

## 3.4 Emergent Positivity Theorem

### Statement

Let \(\Psi\in C^1(\mathbb T^2)\) satisfy

\[
q_{L,\infty}(\Psi)
=
Q_\theta(\Psi,\Psi)
\geq m_\Psi>0.
\]

Assume the consistency estimates above with

\[
E_{L,M}(\Psi)\longrightarrow0.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\left|
q_{L,M}(\Psi)
-
Q_\theta(\Psi,\Psi)
\right|
\leq
E_{L,M}(\Psi)
}
\]

and hence

\[
\boxed{
q_{L,M}(\Psi)
\geq
m_\Psi-E_{L,M}(\Psi).
}
\]

A computable positivity threshold is any \(M_0\) such that

\[
E_{L,M_0}(\Psi)<m_\Psi.
\]

Under the stated monotone kernel error model, this yields

\[
\boxed{
q_{L,M}(\Psi)>0
\qquad
\text{for all }M\geq M_0.
}
\]

For the simplified exact-kernel estimate,

\[
E_{L,M}(\Psi)
=
C_L\|\Psi\|_{C^1}^2
\left(
\frac{L}{2M}+\varepsilon_{L,M}
\right),
\]

a sufficient threshold is

\[
\boxed{
M_0(\Psi,m_\Psi)
=
\left\lceil
\frac{C_LL\|\Psi\|_{C^1}^2}
{2\left(
m_\Psi-
C_L\|\Psi\|_{C^1}^2\varepsilon_{L,\infty}
\right)}
\right\rceil,
}
\]

provided

\[
C_L\|\Psi\|_{C^1}^2\varepsilon_{L,\infty}<m_\Psi
\]

and the kernel error is bounded by \(\varepsilon_{L,\infty}\).

This threshold is conditional on the stated kernel-consistency estimate. A sharper repository-specific threshold requires the full matrix definition of \(G^{(2)}_{L,M}\).

## 3.5 Covariance convergence

The convergence estimate implies convergence of the quadratic form:

\[
\boxed{
q_{L,M}(\Psi)
\longrightarrow
Q_\theta(\Psi,\Psi)
}
\]

for each fixed \(C^1\) test profile in the chosen class.

It also yields convergence of the associated finite-dimensional covariance bilinear forms whenever the reconstruction from sampled values is uniformly bounded. In particular, for finitely many fixed profiles \(\Psi_a\),

\[
\left|
q_{L,M}(\Psi_a,\Psi_b)
-
Q_\theta(\Psi_a,\Psi_b)
\right|
\longrightarrow0.
\]

## 3.6 What is required for a Gaussian limit

A convergence statement for second cumulants does not determine the full law of a field. Gaussian convergence requires an additional hypothesis, for example one of the following:

1. the WDP observables are jointly Gaussian at every \(M\) in the declared CAR construction;
2. a WDP mixing estimate bounds the cumulant of order \(k\geq3\) by a quantity tending to zero;
3. an exact Wick factorization of the CAR state is available.

Under any such hypothesis, the convergence of second-cumulant bilinear forms, together with tightness, supports convergence of the observable vector laws to a centered Gaussian law with covariance \(Q_\theta\).

Without that extra hypothesis, the narrowest conclusion is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Convergence of the sampled second-cumulant forms, not Gaussian law convergence.}
}
\]

The positive Matsubara coefficients

\[
\widehat K_n
=
\frac{\theta^2}{8\pi^2n^2}>0
\]

establish strict positivity on the retained continuous spatial sectors. They do not remove the separately sampled spatial lattice null modes.

# SECTION 4. THE WDP OBSERVABLE COCHAIN ADAPTER

## 4.1 Cohomological structure

Let

\[
d_0:C^0(K)\to C^1(K),
\qquad
d_1:C^1(K)\to C^2(K)
\]

be the temporal and spatial coboundary maps. Define

\[
Q^1
=
C^1/\operatorname{im}d_0.
\]

The finite Hodge decomposition used here is

\[
C^1
=
\operatorname{im}d_0
\oplus
\ker\Delta_1
\oplus
\operatorname{im}d_1^*,
\]

where

\[
\Delta_1=d_0d_0^*+d_1^*d_1.
\]

The summands are mutually orthogonal with respect to the declared \(C^1\) inner product, and the decomposition depends on boundary conditions. On a closed periodic complex, \(\ker\Delta_1\) contains the harmonic representative. On the finite interval, the listed decomposition requires the specified boundary conditions.

For a cochain \(C\), write

\[
[C]
=
[C]_{\operatorname{im}d_0}
+
[C]_{\ker\Delta_1}
+
[C]_{\operatorname{im}d_1^*}.
\]

The \(Q^1\) seminorm is

\[
\|[C]\|_{Q^1}
=
\|[C]_{\ker\Delta_1}\|.
\]

A coboundary component has zero \(Q^1\) norm.

## 4.2 WDP subdivision invariant

Under the declared WDP subdivision rule, an equal-weight edge subdivision leaves the observable variance

\[
\Phi_1
=
\|q\|_{Q^1}
\]

unchanged.

In particular, if

\[
[h]\in H^1,
\]

the WDP degree-two cocycle condition is

\[
\boxed{
\Gamma_2([h])=0.
}
\]

Thus subdivision does not introduce a nonzero degree-two class for an admissible closed observable. The invariant concerns the quotient class and its \(Q^1\) norm; it does not assert that the raw representative is identical before and after subdivision.

## 4.3 Definition of the low-frequency physical sector

A Fourier-observable adapter requires a declared set of retained time frequencies. For a periodic length-\(N\) chain, let

\[
\mathcal F_{\mathrm{low}}
=
\left\{
p\in\mathbb Z:
|p|\leq p_{\max}
\right\},
\]

with the convention that the zero-frequency mode is removed by the physical quotient when it represents a coboundary or a nonphysical constant component.

Let

\[
\Pi_{\mathrm{low}}^N
=
\frac1N
\sum_{p\in\mathcal F_{\mathrm{low}}}
e^{2\pi ipjk/N}
\]

be the exact discrete Fourier projector. Here \(k\) is the temporal index and \(j\) is the dual Fourier index. Extend \(\Pi_{\mathrm{low}}^N\) to spatially constant observables by tensor product with the identity on the retained physical spatial sector.

This operator is an exact finite-dimensional low-pass projection. It is preferable to an unspecified “physical” projection because its frequency support is explicit.

## 4.4 The WDP Observable Adapter

For \(C\in C^1(K)\), define

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal A_{\mathrm{obs}}^{(M)}[C]
=
\Pi_{\mathrm{low}}^{(2M)}
\bigl[
C-C_0
\bigr],
}
\]

where \(C_0\) is the declared nonphysical exact or mean coboundary component. Equivalently, if

\[
C=C_{\mathrm{exact}}+C_{\mathrm{phys}}+C_{\mathrm{coh}},
\]

with

\[
C_{\mathrm{coh}}\in\operatorname{im}d_0,
\]

then set

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal A_{\mathrm{obs}}^{(M)}[C]
=
\Pi_{\mathrm{low}}^{(2M)}
C_{\mathrm{phys}}.
}
\]

The adapter has the following structural properties:

1. **Coboundary removal**
   \[
   \mathcal A_{\mathrm{obs}}^{(M)}[d_0h]=0.
   \]

2. **Quotient descent**
   \[
   \mathcal A_{\mathrm{obs}}^{(M)}[C+d_0h]
   =
   \mathcal A_{\mathrm{obs}}^{(M)}[C].
   \]

3. **Finite-band selection**
   \[
   \widehat{\mathcal A_{\mathrm{obs}}^{(M)}C}(p,\cdot)=0
   \quad
   \text{for }|p|>p_{\max}.
   \]

4. **Subdivision interpretation**
   the same physical frequency window is used before and after an admissible equal-weight subdivision, so \(\Phi_1\) is compared on the same quotient class.

These properties define the adapter at the observable level. They do not identify the raw negative response branch with a cohomological class.

## 4.5 Raw Trotter defect versus observed deficit

Let the raw discrete cochain be decomposed as

\[
C_M
=
C_{\mathrm{phys},M}
+
C_{\mathrm{coh},M}
+
C_{\mathrm{def},M},
\]

where \(C_{\mathrm{def},M}\) is the declared raw Trotter coboundary defect. Under a convention in which that defect is modeled as

\[
C_{\mathrm{def},M}\in\operatorname{im}d_1,
\]

it is not automatically a nonzero element of \(H^1\).

The observed adapter may be defined so that

\[
\mathcal A_{\mathrm{obs}}^{(M)}C_M
=
C_{\mathrm{phys},M}.
\]

Its negative-deficit functional is then

\[
\Phi_{1,M}^{\mathrm{obs}}
=
\left\|
\left[
\mathcal A_{\mathrm{obs}}^{(M)}C_M
\right]
\right\|_{Q^1}.
\]

The operator \(\mathcal A_{\mathrm{obs}}^{(M)}\) is contractive on the declared norm when implemented as an orthogonal Fourier multiplier. Hence

\[
\boxed{
\Phi_{1,M}^{\mathrm{obs}}
\leq
\left\|
C_{\mathrm{phys},M}
\right\|.
}
\]

The raw matrix may still contain a negative eigenvalue. Section 2 shows that its contribution to the alternating smooth observable is bounded by

\[
\frac{|\lambda_{\min}(M)|}{32M^2}
\|\partial_\tau\Psi\|_\infty^2.
\]

Therefore the observed deficit satisfies

\[
\boxed{
\left|
\Phi_{1,M}^{\mathrm{neg}}
\right|
\leq
\frac{|\lambda_{\min}(M)|}{32M^2}
\|\partial_\tau\Psi\|_\infty^2,
}
\]

provided the adapter’s output norm is dominated by the same continuous derivative seminorm.

This is the precise sense in which the raw negative branch can be invisible in the quotient observable while remaining present in the unfiltered finite-dimensional response matrix.

## 4.6 Boundary imposed by the available definitions

The phrase “vanishing negative deficit in \(Q^1\)” has a definite meaning:

\[
\boxed{
\left\|
\left[
\mathcal A_{\mathrm{obs}}^{(M)}C_M
\right]_{-}
\right\|_{Q^1}
\longrightarrow0.
}
\]

The Observable Filtering Theorem supplies a sufficient bound for the sampled negative quadratic component. A stronger assertion that every negative cochain component is exactly zero for all \(M\) is not supported by the finite receipts.

Similarly, WDP subdivision invariance of \(\Phi_1\) does not imply equality of raw finite matrices. It concerns the observable class and the declared norm.

# SECTION 5. CLAIM HYGIENE AND EVIDENCE LEDGER
