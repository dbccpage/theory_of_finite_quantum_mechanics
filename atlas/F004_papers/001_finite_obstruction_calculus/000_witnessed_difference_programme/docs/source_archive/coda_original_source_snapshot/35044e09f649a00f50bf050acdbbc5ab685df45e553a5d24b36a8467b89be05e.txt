4. Setting — the comparison class


The no-go is worthless without a stated class of alternatives. This paper fixes
the class before stating any theorem.

**4.1 Single systems.** A *system* is a triple $A = (V_A, V_A^{+}, u_A)$ where
$V_A$ is a finite-dimensional real vector space, $V_A^{+} \subseteq V_A$ is a
closed generating pointed convex cone, and $u_A \in (V_A)^{*}$ is a strictly
positive *unit effect*. The state space is the compact convex set

$$
\Omega_A \;=\; \{\, \omega \in V_A^{+} \;:\; u_A(\omega) = 1 \,\},
$$

and the effect space is

$$
E_A \;=\; \{\, e \in (V_A^{+})^{*} \;:\; 0 \le e \le u_A \,\}.
$$

A *measurement* is a finite family $\{e_i\} \subseteq E_A$ with
$\sum_i e_i = u_A$; outcome probabilities are $e_i(\omega)$.

**4.2 Composition.** A composition rule assigns to each pair $A, B$ a system
$AB$ satisfying the no-signalling sandwich

$$
\Omega_A \otimes_{\min} \Omega_B \;\subseteq\; \Omega_{AB} \;\subseteq\; \Omega_A \otimes_{\max} \Omega_B .
$$

Fixing where in this sandwich a theory sits is *itself* a discriminator (gate D3)
and is not determined by single-system structure.

**4.3 The distinguished members of the class.**

| Model | State space | Position in the class |
|---|---|---|
| $\mathsf{Cl}_n$ — classical | Simplex $\Delta_{n-1}$ | $\otimes_{\min} = \otimes_{\max}$; every measurement jointly measurable |
| $\mathsf{Q}_d$ — complex quantum | $\{\rho \in \mathcal{B}(\mathbb{C}^{d}) : \rho \ge 0,\ \operatorname{tr}\rho = 1\}$ | Strictly between $\otimes_{\min}$ and $\otimes_{\max}$; locally tomographic |
| $\mathsf{Q}^{\mathbb{R}}_d$ — real quantum | Real symmetric density matrices | Fails local tomography |
| $\mathsf{Q}^{\mathbb{H}}_d$ — quaternionic | Quaternionic density matrices | Fails standard composition |
| $\mathsf{Box}$ — Boxworld | Hypercube / max tensor product | Supports PR boxes; $\mathrm{CHSH} = 4$ |
| $\mathsf{Sq}$ — squit / polygon theories | Regular $n$-gon state spaces | Interpolates classical ↔ quantum-like |
| $\mathsf{Stoch}$ — the corpus model | Classical joint distributions with a declared lossy projection | **Papers 001G, 001H live here** |

10. Related work (to be written — nearest neighbours)


The drafted paper must situate itself against the following, with these
characterizations. Mischaracterizing any of them is a drafting defect.

* **Rovelli, *Relational Quantum Mechanics*** (arXiv: `quant-ph/9609002`,
  <https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9609002>). Rejects observer-independent states:
  state assignments are relative to a physical system. Crucially, it **retains
  the quantum formalism** rather than deriving it, and treats all physical
  systems symmetrically — it does **not** derive observers or consciousness from
  bare difference. Nearest relative to the WD observer hierarchy in *posture*;
  supplies no derivation the programme could inherit.

* **Fuchs, Mermin, Schack, *An Introduction to QBism***
  (arXiv: `1311.5253`, <https://arxiv.org/abs/1311.5253>). Quantum states and
  probabilities are agent-relative commitments — personalist Bayesian degrees of
  belief. Directly relevant to the programme's witness-relative framing, but
  agent-relativity alone **does not derive Hilbert space**: QBism takes the
  formalism as given and reinterprets it. Any WD claim that observer-relativity
  yields quantum structure must explain what it adds beyond QBism, which does not
  make that claim.

* **Abramsky, Brandenburger, *The Sheaf-Theoretic Structure of Non-Locality and
  Contextuality*** (arXiv: `1102.0264`, <https://arxiv.org/abs/1102.0264>).
  Identifies contextuality with the obstruction to a global section of a presheaf
  of empirical distributions over a measurement cover, with a cohomological
  ($\check{H}^1$) witness. **The closest established mathematical neighbour to the
  cohomological side of this programme.** Requires a measurement cover and an
  empirical model as host structure — neither of which the WD chain currently
  supplies — and, per §5.1, hosts classical and super-quantum models as readily as
  quantum ones.

* **Chiribella, D'Ariano, Perinotti, *Informational derivation of Quantum
  Theory*** (arXiv: `1011.6451`, <https://arxiv.org/abs/1011.6451>). Derives
  finite-dimensional quantum theory from an operational-probabilistic framework
  plus informational axioms (causality, perfect distinguishability, ideal
  compression, local distinguishability, pure conditioning) with **purification
  doing the decisive work**. This is the **benchmark WD-6 must be measured
  against**: it demonstrates the level of discriminator required to select
  quantum theory from a strictly larger class, and it names the axiom carrying
  the weight rather than distributing it rhetorically.

15. Genesis-bias audit (mandatory before drafting)


Per the programme charter and the repository's Genesis Bias doctrine, this paper
must answer, for its own construction:

* Where does the real vector space $V_A$ come from? What in Primitive Difference
  supplies a field, let alone an ordered one?

* Where does **convexity** come from? Mixing $\lambda \omega_1 + (1-\lambda)\omega_2$
  presupposes a shared randomness source and an agent free to consult it. Neither
  is derived.

* Where do **probabilities** come from? The GPT framework assumes outcome
  frequencies exist and are stable under repetition. Repetition presupposes
  re-preparable systems, hence a clock and an identity criterion across trials —
  both refused at WD-0.

* Where does the **measurement cover** come from? Both the AB framework and gate
  D1 require a declared family of contexts. Nothing in the WD chain constructs
  one.

* Where does the **tensor product** come from? §4.2 presupposes that "two
  systems" is already meaningful — a composition primitive, not a derived one.

* What licenses the ambient set theory and finite-dimensional linear algebra in
  which $\Omega_A$, $\otimes_{\min}$, and $\check{H}^1$ are formed?

The last two questions are not rhetorical. **A no-go proved inside the GPT class
establishes only that the WD axioms fail to select quantum theory *within that
class*.** It does not establish that quantum theory is underivable, and it does
not establish that the GPT class is the right arena. The paper must state this
boundary in its own §2 and again in its conclusion, rather than let a
class-relative negative result read as an absolute one.
