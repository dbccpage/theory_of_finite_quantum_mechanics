Abstract

This paper combines the finite constructions of constitutive succession and duration without identifying either with physical time. Bare recurrence is symmetric and supplies no secondness. Packet-local immediate succession is obtained only after a recurrent cut registers another through immediate pole incidence, a typed K11 witness algebra is supplied, closed incidence witnesses are refused by M3, and a K6 recurrence signature declares which comparisons preserve type. The resulting immediate succession is irreflexive and asymmetric but generally intransitive; a separate witness-bearing relation supplies strict partial constitutive order. Duration requires further structure: a declared additive valuation on succession-edge occurrences, carried in an ordered abelian group with an edgewise positivity receipt. Duration is additive on explicitly factorized finite intervals. Exactness earns endpoint-only duration; closed nonexact periods, active local closure failures, and insufficient receipts remain distinct. A clock is then a calibrated coupling between a target interval and a reference recurrence, not a detector of a temporal substance. The synthesis exposes three independent adequacy gates — recurrence detector, duration gauge, and clock coupling — and proves by finite countermodels that no earlier layer uniquely selects the next. Continuous and quantum time remain separately priced reconstruction problems.

### 3.2 Constitutive Arrow theorem

**Theorem 3.1 — Constitutive Arrow.** Under M3, K11, and an admissible $\Sigma$, immediate succession $\succ_{\Sigma}$ is irreflexive and asymmetric.

*Proof.* Reflexive succession would give $\alpha\prec\alpha$, contrary to M3. Succession in both directions would supply two grounded edge witnesses; their K11 join is a forbidden nonempty closed witness. $\square$

Immediate succession is generally intransitive. From

$$
\beta\succ_{\Sigma}\alpha,
\qquad
\gamma\succ_{\Sigma}\beta,
$$

K11 yields $\alpha\lhd\gamma$; it need not yield $\gamma\succ_{\Sigma}\alpha$, because immediate registration $\alpha\prec\gamma$ may be absent.

### 3.3 Constitutive order theorem

**Theorem 3.2 — Strict constitutive order.** Under K11 and M3, $\lhd$ is transitive and irreflexive.

*Proof.* Typed join gives transitivity. M3 refuses every nonempty closed witness and therefore gives irreflexivity. $\square$

The theorem supplies a strict partial order, not a total order. Incomparability supplies no simultaneity and no global present.

Henson, ["The causal set approach to quantum gravity"](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0601121) (2006), and S. Surya, ["Directions in Causal Set Quantum Gravity"](https://arxiv.org/abs/1103.6272) (2011). B. F. Dribus, ["On the Axioms of Causal Set Theory"](https://arxiv.org/abs/1311.2148) (2013), proposes a generally nontransitive local causal relation beneath causal order, a bounded structural analogue of $\succ_{\Sigma}$ versus $\lhd$; events, causal meaning, and discrete gravity remain inputs. Energetic causal sets add energy-momentum on causal links, conservation, amplitudes, and probabilities, placing them further downstream (M. Cort├¬s and L. Smolin, ["Energetic Causal Sets"](https://arxiv.org/abs/1308.2206), 2014).

3. **Rovelli and Barbour remove different things and must not be conflated.** C. Rovelli, ["Forget time"](https://arxiv.org/abs/0903.3832) (2009), removes a preferred independent variable and treats mechanics as relations among variables within a classical/quantum formalism. This is a deparametrization and correlation setting; it does not construct the $\delta_1$ relation. J. B. Barbour, ["The Emergence of Time and Its Arrow from Timelessness"](https://cqi.inf.usi.ch/qic/94_Barbour.pdf) (1994), begins from complete relative configurations, a configuration space of structured wholes, comparison and best matching, curves, and extremal action. Its comparator resembles a later gauge problem only after those structures are admitted. Neither framework supplies this paper's duration-gauge or clock-adequacy receipts.

4. **Process Physics is a negative contrast case.** R. T. Cahill, ["Process Physics: From Quantum Foam to General Relativity"](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0203015) (2002), begins with a self-organizing semantic or relational-information neural-network process and describes time as a distinct non-geometric process. Process, self-reference, iteration/growth, stochasticity, and information structure are therefore already present. Removing a geometric time coordinate does not by itself remove succession from the foundations.

5. **Lynds supplies a representational warning, not a GTOC derivation.** P. Lynds, ["Time and Classical and Quantum Mechanics: Indeterminacy vs. Discontinuity"](https://arxiv.org/abs/physics/0310055) (2003), is useful where coordinate values and measurement labels are reified as frozen physical atoms. The stronger moves to universal physical indeterminacy, a complete Zeno solution, claims about imaginary time, or rejection of chronons do not follow here. Exact pointwise representation does not imply stasis, and a finite packet neither proves nor refutes continuum time.

The GTOC contrast is therefore **an admission-first, witness-gated, no-unpriced-import construction of temporal structure**. “Admission” here names a rule of the calculus; it is not semantic authority, certification, or human promotion.

> Existing approaches often remove a preferred time parameter, primitive point-instant, or continuum geometry only after admitting events, intervals, causal order, configurations, dynamics, or correlations. GTOC instead begins from an admitted Difference–Structure root plus named rulings and separately prices succession, constitutive order, tally and scalar resources, duration, residual classification, and clock calibration. This is a claim about assumption accounting—not greater physical completeness, fundamental status, semantic truth, or authority.

The full K6 packet, detector adequacy, interval validation, duration additivity, residual classifier, clock adequacy, continuum lifts, causal interpretation, and physical time remain unmechanized or open.
