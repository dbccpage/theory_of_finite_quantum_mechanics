## Post 3 — what Many Worlds says (317 characters)

Record which slit the electron took. The equations pair left with a detector that says left and right with one that says right. With perfectly distinct records, the screen alone shows no interference. Everett follows both terms through the next step, when someone reads the detector. Each reader remembers one result.

## Post 4 — why it attracts, what it costs (376 characters)

Everett keeps the same rule for how the electron, detector and reader change together. That leaves a puzzle. If one reader sees left and another sees right, what makes one result a 70% bet? Counting readers gives no answer. There is a second question too. Which records persist long enough to count as branches? Copies in the surroundings help explain why some records endure.

## Post 5 — how I look at it (351 characters)

How I look at it. Take two accounts that use the same state, the same evolution and the same rule for predicting measurements. Calling the terms worlds leaves every one of those predictions intact. I want to know which experiment could distinguish any further claim. That question also tells me where the mathematics ends and my interpretation begins.

## Post 6 — what would settle it (422 characters)

Imagine marking the two routes, then reversing every interaction that left a record, before the particle reaches the screen. Ordinary quantum mechanics predicts the interference can return. Some collapse models predict a residual loss under specified conditions. Testing one means looking for its signal while accounting for stray records, ordinary noise and instrument error. A dark band fading on its own settles little.
