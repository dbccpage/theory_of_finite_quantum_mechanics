# Publication description

Manuscript edition 2.4.5 · 2026-10-16. Unpublished correction candidate; no version DOI assigned.

The Witnessed Difference Programme: From Finite Distinction to Witnessed Measurement

This monograph studies when a reported observation preserves an answer to a
specified question. Its report-fiber criterion states that an answer can be
recovered from a report exactly when it is constant on every report fiber.
Linear repair quotients, finite state machines, observation selection and
conditional quantum constructions instantiate this criterion with explicit
carriers, maps and assumptions. The paper also treats unbounded histories and
conditional continuum limits; its scope is not exclusively finite.

This edition identifies six internal papers within the single monograph:
finite distinction and probability; observer and measurement; histories and
admissible operations; conditional quantum interfaces; limits and analytic
extensions; and relative completeness and proof supplements. Each retains
its own premises and evidence boundary. The update also corrects the scope
description of the Mathlib-backed formal modules; it adds no quantum
reconstruction theorem or full-package formalization claim. The publication
clearance pass also carries the nonnegative-weight and free empirical-table
formation premises into the reconstruction summary, and withdraws an
unsupported global MUB6 classification inferred from a declared lookup table.
The finite parity and Tao-matrix witnesses retain their narrower scope.

The package contains the 23-chapter manuscript and appendices, selected Lean
formalizations, exact finite implementations, tests, notebooks and retained
source material. Native functional values are evaluated by `omega_functional`.
The Python distribution has software version 2.4.5. The reasoning increment
connects concrete finite certificates to the abstract Horn calculus through
proved executable Lean checkers and closed kernel replay of Python/Rust exports.
The graph reachability interpretation is now formalized. These scoped results
leave source-language semantics, production deployment and empirical adequacy
as explicit obligations.
The Python adapters transport proposed inputs and checked results. Mathematical
validity, adequacy of a report for a source or physical system, and permission
to act are separate claims.

See the [current evidence guide](current_claim_evidence.md) and
[package guide](../README.md) for verification scope and commands. Stored
coverage and axiom reports are evidence only for their recorded source and
execution. A SHA-256 manifest detects changes relative to that manifest; it is
not an author signature, a proof of a theorem, or a grant of authority.
