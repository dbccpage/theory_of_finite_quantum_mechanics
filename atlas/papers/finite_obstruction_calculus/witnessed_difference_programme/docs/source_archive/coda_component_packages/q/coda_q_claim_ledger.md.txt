## 2. Mathematical Scoping & Epistemic Boundaries

1. **Completion is not Compactness or Computable Selection**:
   The existence of $\widehat{X}$ adds formal limit points of Cauchy sequences. It does not render an arbitrary metric space compact, nor does it provide a mechanical decision procedure for equality of arbitrary Cauchy representations without a modulus of convergence.
2. **Enclosure is an Inequality, not an Identification**:
   Enclosure bounds of the form $|x - m| \le \delta$ assert containment within a ball/interval. A returned midpoint $m = (lo+hi)/2$ is an approximation candidate, not the point $x$ itself. In particular, for $\sqrt{2}$, $(m)^2 \neq 2$ for all rational $m$.
3. **Continuum Queries Require Modulus of Continuity or Fibre Invariance**:
   A finite sample vector $p(x) = (f_1(x), \dots, f_k(x))$ determines the target value on every realized profile if and only if $q$ is constant on every fibre of $p$ ($p(x) = p(y) \implies q(x) = q(y)$). This is a realized-fibre statement; constructing a total factor on an ambient profile type needs a value for unrealized profiles (an inhabited target suffices classically). Off-profile variation permits non-trivial query divergence (demonstrated by $t \mapsto t(t-1)$ colliding on $\{0, 1\}$ while differing at $1/2$).
4. **Concrete mathlib proofs and computational scope**:
   The completion and enclosure declarations in `lean4/Algebra.lean`
   use mathlib's metric completion and real numbers. Their printed axiom
   dependencies must be checked per declaration; no zero-axiom claim is made.
   Python examples check finite rational calculations and do not prove
   completion, density, surjectivity, or uniform extension theorems.
