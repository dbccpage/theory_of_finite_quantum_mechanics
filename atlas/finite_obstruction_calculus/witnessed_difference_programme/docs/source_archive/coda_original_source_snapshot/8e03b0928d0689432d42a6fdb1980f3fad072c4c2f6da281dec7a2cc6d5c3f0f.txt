## Abstract

We isolate three finite constructions that are often conflated. First, an
oriented graph supplies real primitive differences and a positive weighted L1
cochain quotient. Second, a U(1) edge connection is separately declared; its
covariant difference, connection Laplacian, gauge action, and closed-loop
holonomy obey exact finite identities. A real cochain maps to such a connection
only after choosing a phase scale, and the map loses periodic information.
Third, the generalized Gell-Mann matrices form a normalized basis of traceless
Hermitian operators on an independently declared label space `C^N`; no graph
constructs that carrier. The results provide auditable algebraic ingredients,
not a derivation of quantum mechanics, Feynman amplitudes, Born probabilities,
or dynamics.

## Why a researcher should care

Discrete gauge calculations are useful only when their carriers and observer
choices are explicit. Treating normalized real edge labels as phases, or graph
vertices as an automatic SU(N) internal space, silently changes the problem.
The typed separation here makes the valid covariance and positivity statements
reusable without the accompanying category errors.

## Falsifiability

The paper is refuted by a finite instance where the quotient LP has a duality
gap, the declared gauge transformation breaks covariance, closed-loop holonomy
changes under gauge, the connection Laplacian is not Hermitian positive
semidefinite, the cochain exponential fails to respect exact-repair gauge
classes, or the generalized Gell-Mann matrices fail their count, Hermiticity,
trace, or normalization identities. The companion tests exercise each claim.

## 1. Graph cochains and two L1 quantities

Let `G=(V,E)` be a finite directed graph. For `f in R^V`, define

`(d0 f)(u,v)=f(v)-f(u)`.

Fix strictly positive edge weights `w`. For an edge cochain `omega`, distinguish

`I1(omega)=sum_e w_e |omega_e|`

from the quotient value

`Phi1([omega])=min_f I1(omega+d0f)`.

`I1` depends on the representative; `Phi1` does not. Calling the raw norm
`Phi1` is incorrect unless no repair subspace is present.

### Theorem QMC-T1 — finite quotient and dual

`Phi1` is an attained norm on `C1/im d0` and

`Phi1([omega])=max { z^Tomega : d0^Tz=0, |z_e|<=w_e }`.

**Proof.** This is the positive weighted finite quotient-norm and LP-duality
argument: split the residual into positive and negative parts; the free vertex
potential imposes the circulation equation. `□`

On a graph with no cycles the quotient is zero. An edgeless graph therefore
cannot generate a nontrivial connection, Hamiltonian, or SU(N) carrier from
primitive difference alone.

## 2. Declared U(1) connections

A U(1) connection is an assignment `A_e in C` with `|A_e|=1` for each oriented
edge. For a complex vertex field `psi`, define

`(D_A psi)(u,v)=psi(v)-A_(u,v) psi(u)`.

A vertex gauge is `g:V->U(1)`. Set

`psi'(v)=g(v)psi(v)`,

`A'_(u,v)=g(v) A_(u,v) conjugate(g(u))`.

### Theorem QMC-T2 — gauge covariance

`(D_A' psi')(u,v)=g(v)(D_A psi)(u,v)`.

**Proof.** Substitute the definitions and use
`conjugate(g(u))g(u)=1`. `□`

Consequently the edgewise squared magnitude and its positive weighted sum are
gauge invariant.

For a closed oriented walk `gamma`, multiply `A_e` when traversing an edge in
its declared direction and `conjugate(A_e)` in reverse. The result is the
holonomy `Hol_A(gamma)`.

### Theorem QMC-T3 — closed-loop gauge invariance

`Hol_A'(gamma)=Hol_A(gamma)` for every closed walk.

**Proof.** Gauge factors telescope along the walk; the initial and final vertex
coincide. `□`

For an open path the corresponding transport is gauge covariant at its two
endpoints, not invariant.

## 3. Connection Laplacian

Give the edge space any positive diagonal weight matrix `W`. Define

`Delta_A = D_A^* W D_A`.

### Theorem QMC-T4 — Hermitian positivity and gauge equivalence

`Delta_A` is Hermitian positive semidefinite, and gauge-related connections
produce unitarily equivalent Laplacians.

**Proof.** Hermiticity is immediate from the adjoint form, and
`<psi,Delta_A psi>=||W^(1/2)D_Apsi||_2^2>=0`. QMC-T2 says
`D_A' G=G_E D_A` for diagonal unitary vertex and edge gauge matrices, so
`Delta_A'=G Delta_A G^*`. `□`

This is finite connection-Laplacian algebra. It is not a Schrödinger equation;
a Hamiltonian, time parameter, units, and physical interpretation would be
additional declarations.

## 4. From a real cochain to a connection

Choose a real scale `alpha` and define

`Exp_alpha(omega)_e = exp(i alpha omega_e)`.

### Theorem QMC-T5 — descent to gauge classes

If `omega'=omega+d0f`, then `Exp_alpha(omega')` is gauge equivalent to
`Exp_alpha(omega)` via `g(v)=exp(i alpha f(v))`.

**Proof.** On `(u,v)`,
`exp(i alpha(omega_e+f(v)-f(u)))`
equals `g(v)Exp_alpha(omega)_e conjugate(g(u))`. `□`

The scale `alpha` is observer data. The exponential loses values modulo
`2pi/alpha` when `alpha` is nonzero. Neither `I1` nor `Phi1` canonically selects
`alpha`, and normalizing each cochain by its own L1 mass does not define a fixed
linear or functorial phase convention.

## 5. Independent SU(N) label carrier

Now separately declare a complex label space `H=C^N`, `N>=2`. Let `E_jk` be the
matrix units. The generalized Gell-Mann family consists of

- `E_jk+E_kj` for `j<k`;
- `-iE_jk+iE_kj` for `j<k`;
- for `1<=ell<=N-1`,
  `sqrt(2/(ell(ell+1))) diag(1,...,1,-ell,0,...,0)`.

### Theorem QMC-T6 — normalized traceless-Hermitian basis

The family has `N^2-1` elements, is a real basis of the traceless Hermitian
matrices, and satisfies

`Tr(lambda_a lambda_b)=2 delta_ab`.

**Proof.** There are `N(N-1)/2` symmetric, the same number antisymmetric, and
`N-1` diagonal elements. Each is visibly Hermitian and traceless. Distinct
off-diagonal supports are orthogonal; symmetric and antisymmetric partners have
zero trace product; diagonal elements are orthogonal by direct summation. The
displayed scale makes every squared norm two. Their number equals the real
dimension `N^2-1`, hence orthogonality makes them a basis. `□`

With `T_a=lambda_a/2`, commutators define real antisymmetric structure constants
by `[T_a,T_b]=i sum_c f_abc T_c`. The label space and its basis are not generated
by an arbitrary graph. They may be attached to vertices or edges only by a
separate modeling choice.

## 6. L1 masses and complex amplitudes

Given declared nonnegative masses `mu_j`, not all zero, one may define
`p_j=mu_j/sum mu` and, after separately declaring phases `phi_j`, set

`psi_j=sqrt(p_j) exp(i phi_j)`.

Then `sum |psi_j|^2=1`. This elementary embedding is many-to-one: it discards
overall scale, and the phases are not determined by the masses. If the input is
a signed cochain, taking absolute values also discards signs. Therefore the map
does not derive Born's rule or a quantum state from primitive difference.

## 7. Computational companion

One production module implements graph incidence, weighted L1 quotient LPs,
U(1) gauge transformations, covariant differences, graph-validated path
holonomy, connection Laplacians, cochain exponentiation, generalized Gell-Mann
matrices, and the declared mass/phase embedding. Holonomy replay checks edge
indices, orientations, walk continuity, endpoints, and required closedness.
Tests include edgeless and cyclic graphs, open and closed paths, random gauges,
PSD spectra, exact-repair gauge equivalence, all Gell-Mann Gram identities for
several `N`, and rejected implicit-phase inputs. Two notebooks present the
shortest positive replay and a falsification suite.

## 8. Scope and nonclaims

This paper does not derive quantum mechanics, Feynman path amplitudes, Born's
rule, Hilbert space from a graph, SU(N) from graph adjacency, a physical
Hamiltonian, continuum gauge theory, measurement, or authority. It provides
finite typed constructions whose extra choices remain visible.
