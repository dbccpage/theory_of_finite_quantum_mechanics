## Constraint-Level Repair Routing via Multi-Channel Obstruction Diagnostics

**Author:** Jeremy H. Carroll
**Date:** June 2026
**Version:** v2.1.1 Pre-Print

Abstract

**Main result (informal).** A second-order controller routes repair operators — numeric repair, structural lift, SAT repair, rollback, branch, or wait — by reading several observer-relative obstruction channels (geometric quotient residual, closure defect, representative distortion, logical UNSAT, relational drift). The normalized composite score it minimizes is a *routing utility only*: it is subordinate to per-channel diagnostics, the closure-ratio guardrail $R_{cl}\le R_{\max}$, and typed terminals, so the controller cannot "fake repair" **in a declared protected channel, at the resolution of the declared detector**, by lowering a weighted sum while that channel worsens. Both qualifiers are load-bearing and neither is free: a defect in an undeclared channel, or below detector resolution, is invisible to every guard in this paper (§5.2).

This paper defines a second-order repair controller for constraint-system evolution. The system monitors separable obstruction channels, including geometric quotient residual, logical UNSAT residual, closure defect, representative distortion, and relational drift. It selects repair operators according to a cost-sensitive policy over declared constraint generators, while remaining subordinate to finite carriers, observer-relative diagnostics, guardrails, and typed operator contracts.

The controller is an operational repair policy, not a model of cognition, consciousness, or subjective awareness. Its composite obstruction score is a routing utility, not an authority certificate. Acceptance is not the controller's to grant (§1); it is delegated to the guarded evaluator and remains governed by channel-level diagnostics, closure-ratio bounds, the declared evaluator policy, and typed terminal semantics.

Constructed toy cases exhibit separable channel activation and show that operator routing can reduce composite obstruction in selected cases. These are **worked examples, not experiments**: this paper cites no implementation, and no Lean, Rust, or replay artifact exists for it (§0, §4). No claim is made beyond the specified mechanism.

**Why a researcher should care.** A controller that repairs by minimizing one aggregate score is easy to game: it can drive the headline number down while quietly tearing a protected channel — for example lowering quotient residual by breaking topological closure, or fixing a logical conflict while damaging geometric structure. This paper separates *routing* from *authority*. It monitors channels that activate separately in constructed cases, routes operators by a normalized composite utility, and **refuses** any transition it can see to breach the closure-ratio guardrail or to worsen a declared protected channel beyond its declared tolerance.

The transferable lesson is narrower than "multi-channel diagnostics plus a boundary guardrail keep repair honest," and the narrowing is the point. Adding channels does **not** compound into a guarantee: a combination of diagnostics is not stronger evidence than its parts, and this corpus licenses no inference from "several channels agree" to "the repair is sound." Two failure modes survive every guard stated here. First, **the joint blind spot**: §2 discloses each channel's blind spot individually, but a defect outside the declared manifest is invisible to all five at once, and lowers every monitored number. Second, **detector collapse**: on an acyclic carrier the geometric channel is identically zero for every input, which drives $R_{cl}\equiv 0$ and makes the guardrail structurally unable to fire (§5.2). What multi-channel monitoring buys is that the *specific* defect-hiding move of trading one declared channel against another is refused. That is a real property and it is not adequacy. A regulator built on a collapsing detector regulates nothing.

1. Definition

### 1.0 Genesis-Bias Fence — No Prior World Behind the Controller

This paper does not begin with a world, a substrate, a law of motion, a possibility domain, or an authority source that the controller then discovers. It begins with **declared manifest data**: a finite carrier, a declared cochain complex, a declared observer, a declared operator set, and a declared policy.

$S_t$ below is **not** a state of the world and $\mathfrak O$ is **not** a set of physically available actions. $S_t$ is the manifest-declared record the controller is permitted to read at step $t$; $\mathfrak O$ is the operator set the deployment declares. The index $t$ is a **step counter over an exhibited operator sequence** — not a duration, not physical time, and not a temporal order the controller is embedded in.

Price of the common stronger readings: reading $S_t$ as a world-state imports a container the declaration does not supply; reading $t$ as time imports a temporal order; reading $\mathbb E[\cdot]$ as chance imports a possibility domain and a measure over it (declared as an import in §0, with a deterministic fallback in §1). None of the three is constructed here.

> **GENESIS-BIAS AUDIT.** Does this section explain control by presupposing a prior container, substrate, state-space, law, observer, temporal order, possibility domain, sample space, Hilbert space, physical system, or authority source? If so, declare and price that antecedent, replace it with packet-local data, or stop with an OBSTRUCTION.

Let:

* $S_t$ be the declared controller-visible record at step $t$, including graph, constraints, section, solver state, and metadata;
* $H_t=(S_0,o_0,S_1,o_1,\ldots,S_t)$ be the history;
* $\mathfrak O=\{\Pi_{21},\Pi_{12},\text{SAT repair},\text{rollback},\text{branch},\text{wait}\}$ be the available operator set;
* $\Phi_{\mathrm{total}}$ be a normalized composite obstruction score over multiple observer-relative channels.

**Definition 1.1.** A second-order repair controller is a policy:

$$
\operatorname{Ctrl}:\operatorname{State}\times\operatorname{History}\to\mathfrak O
$$

that selects an operator $o_t\in\mathfrak O$ to reduce expected future obstruction subject to cost and validity constraints. Here \(\Pi_{12}\) denotes a representation-expanding lift from the base graph into an enriched structure, while \(\Pi_{21}\) denotes projection or repair back into the existing numeric carrier.

Equivalently:

$$
o_t
\in
\arg\min_{o\in\mathfrak O}
\mathbb E\left[
\Phi_{\mathrm{total}}(S_{t+1})
+\operatorname{Cost}(o)
\mid S_t,H_t,o
\right]
$$
subject to the invariant guardrail $R_{cl} \le R_{\max}$, whose bound $R_{\max}$ is a declared deployment parameter this paper does not fix (§3).

The expectation $\mathbb E[\cdot]$ is taken with respect to a declared transition model, rollout estimator, or empirical operator-performance prior. **This is an `IMPORTED` probability semantics** (§0 register): the declared degree supplies a finite carrier and finite diagnostics, not a possibility domain or a measure over it. If no such model is declared, the expectation is unavailable, and Ctrl is treated as a deterministic scoring policy over observed candidate successors rather than a stochastic optimizer. If multiple operators minimize the declared routing objective, the controller must use a deterministic tie-break rule or emit a typed nondeterminism terminal. Silent arbitrary operator selection is not certificate-grade.

This is a definition of a controller, not a philosophical definition of mind. The controller **proposes and routes**; it does not accept, certify, or promote. Acceptance is delegated to the guarded evaluator of Paper 011 and its terminal semantics.

**Ownership split (binding for the rest of this paper).** The deployment manifest declares. The controller proposes. The guarded evaluator judges. The audit manifest records. Promotion to any authority-bearing use is outside this paper entirely and requires a CODA-FDA / GTLA descent packet. Wherever the text below says a transition is "accepted" or "rejected", read: *the declared policy routed it, or the declared guard refused it* — never *the controller was entitled to decide it*. A refusal this controller emits is a valid terminal and needs no further license; an acceptance is not the controller's to grant.

2. Obstruction Channels

All obstruction channels are defined relative to a chosen observer $\mathcal{O}$.

This specification declares five primary obstruction channels. (Read "the implementation" throughout as *a conforming implementation*: no artifact is cited by this paper, and conformance to the §1 interface is an audit obligation, not a reported fact — §0, §4.)

| Channel | Definition | Measures | Detects | Blind to |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $\Phi_{\mathrm{geom}}^{(\mathcal O)}$ | $\inf_f \mathrm{Obs}_{\mathcal O}(\omega - d_0 f)$ | quotient geometric residual | cycle-supported geometric obstruction | logical contradictions not encoded geometrically |
| $\Phi_{\mathrm{curv}}$ | $\Gamma_2 = \|d_1 h\|_1$ | topological closure failure | broken structural boundary limits | valid repairable cycle obstruction |
| $\Xi$ | representative distortion (imported from Paper 009 — **Hilbert-host-bearing**, see below) | selected primal-dual alignment | mismatch between selected residual and selected dual witness | absolute mass and closure status |
| $\Phi_{\mathrm{sat}}$ | $\sum_{c\in\mathcal K} w_c\,\mathrm{viol}_c(S)$ | logical inconsistency | incompatible symbolic clauses | geometric cycle structure |
| $\Phi_{\mathrm{rel}}$ | $\sum_{e\in E_{\mathrm{ref}}} w_e \|r_e^{(t)}-r_e^{(0)}\|$ | representational drift | deviation from saved relational baseline | topology and logical satisfiability |

Here $\mathrm{viol}_c(S)\ge 0$ is a declared clause-violation functional, equal to zero exactly when clause $c$ is satisfied under the chosen symbolic encoding. $r_e^{(t)}$ denotes the relational value, restriction map, or encoded edge relation at time $t$, depending on the declared manifest.

$\Xi$ serves as the third-order tiebreaker in the guarded descent **policy** declared in "Operator Proposal Layer for Finite ℓ¹ Obstruction Systems" (Paper 011 types it as a `[POLICY RULE]`, not an established law): when $\Phi_1$ and $\Gamma_2$ are both unchanged, $\Xi$ descent routes the tie. $R_{cl}$ is a hard gate in that policy, not a descent level; the descent priority is the ordered triple $(\Phi_1,\Gamma_2,\Xi)$.

Paper 009's own import register marks **every one of these `IMPORTED`** and concludes that $\Xi$ is not constructed at *its* declared degree either — the $\ell^1$ cochain data supplies no inner product. It is therefore `IMPORTED` / **Q-gated** here, and carries no authority. Two consequences this paper must own rather than paper over:

But $\Xi$ re-imports an $\ell^2$ inner product through Paper 009. The refusal is real for the geometric channel and does **not** extend to $\Xi$. A deployment that puts $\Xi$ in $P_{\mathrm{prot}}$ (§5.1) or relies on it to route ties is running an imported Euclidean structure inside an $\ell^1$ paper, and should say so in its manifest.

In this implementation, the observer for the geometric channel is the replica-extensive $\ell^1$ observer. The geometric channel operates as an observer-relative quotient residual, distinguishing from $\ell^2$ Hodge-style harmonic settings which employ a different observer geometry.

3. Normalized Composite Score and The Diagnostic State Vector

The raw quantities may live on different numerical scales. Operator selection should not depend on arbitrary units. Define normalized channel values:

$$
## \widehat{\Phi}_{i}(S_t)
\frac{\Phi_i(S_t)}{\sigma_i+\epsilon_i},
$$

where $\sigma_i$ is a chosen scale factor.

The composite obstruction is:
$$
## \Phi_{\mathrm{total}}(S_t)
a\widehat{\Phi}_{\mathrm{geom}}
+b\widehat{\Phi}_{\mathrm{sat}}
+c\widehat{\Phi}_{\mathrm{rel}}
+d\widehat{\Phi}_{\mathrm{curv}},
$$
with $a,b,c,d\ge0$ specified by deployment policy. The scale factors \(\sigma_i\), stabilizers \(\epsilon_i\), and weights \(a,b,c,d\) are policy parameters. They must be recorded in the audit manifest; otherwise \(\Phi_{\mathrm{total}}\) is not replayable. Although \(\Xi\) is tracked, it is not included in the default composite score; it remains a guarded tiebreaker used only when the primary diagnostics are unchanged.

**The same obligation binds the guard, and the earlier text did not say so.** $R_{\max}$, the $R_{cl}$ stabilizer $\varepsilon$ below, the tolerances $\tau_i$, the protected set $P_{\mathrm{prot}}$ (§5.1), and — if $\Xi$ is used — Paper 009's six declared selections (§2) are policy parameters on exactly the footing of $\sigma_i$ and $a,b,c,d$, and **must be recorded in the audit manifest**. Otherwise the guardrail is not replayable and the falsifiability criterion of this paper cannot be run: two deployments may choose different $R_{\max}$, both claim compliance, and disagree on every refusal. This paper **does not supply a value for $R_{\max}$**; it imports the bound from Paper 000's $R_{cl}$-guarded-descent law as a free parameter. A deployment that leaves $R_{\max}$ unrecorded has no guardrail, whatever its logs say.

However, the controller does not operate *solely* on minimizing this single scalar $\Phi_{\mathrm{total}}$. As established in the General Theory (Paper 000 v004), a control policy acting on a single scalar $\Phi$ without boundary awareness can easily fake repair (lowering quotient residual while tearing the graph, thus artificially increasing $\Gamma_2$).

The composite score is a routing utility, not an authority certificate. It may rank candidate actions, but it cannot override channel-level guardrails, terminal evaluator law, or typed refusal conditions.

The controller **must** distinguish between valid cycle obstruction ($\Phi_{\mathrm{geom}}$ high, $\Phi_{\mathrm{curv}}$ zero) and broken structure ($\Phi_{\mathrm{geom}}$ high, $\Phi_{\mathrm{curv}}$ high).

Therefore, the controller explicitly monitors the boundary ratio $R_{cl}$, importing the strict guardrail law from "Primitive Axiomatics of Finite Obstruction Calculus". In exact mathematics, \(R_{cl}=\Gamma_2/\Phi_{\mathrm{geom}}\) is meaningful only when \(\Phi_{\mathrm{geom}}>0\). The \(\varepsilon\)-piecewise version is an implementation guardrail convention for finite-precision evaluation:

$$
R_{cl} =
\begin{cases}
\Phi_{\mathrm{curv}}/\Phi_{\mathrm{geom}} & \Phi_{\mathrm{geom}}>\varepsilon,\\
0 & \Phi_{\mathrm{geom}}\le\varepsilon \text{ and } \Phi_{\mathrm{curv}}\le\varepsilon,\\
+\infty & \Phi_{\mathrm{geom}}\le\varepsilon \text{ and } \Phi_{\mathrm{curv}}>\varepsilon.
\end{cases}
$$

where $R_{cl}'$ abbreviates $R_{cl}(S_{t+1})$, the post-transition value.

**Rule (refusal):** Refuse any transition where $R_{cl}' > R_{\max}$.

This is a **refusal rule, not a licensing rule**. Its passing says only that the declared guard did not object; it is never a reason to accept, and acceptance is not this controller's to grant (§1). Refusing is a valid terminal and requires no further authority.

> **OBSTRUCTION — the guardrail is inert exactly where the geometric channel is flat.** The $\varepsilon$-piecewise convention above extends $R_{cl}$ into the region $\Phi_{\mathrm{geom}}\le\varepsilon$, where the exact-mathematics ratio is *not defined* — and the guard reads that extension as "safe." The consequence is structural, not a rounding artifact. On an **acyclic** carrier, $\operatorname{im}(d_0)=C^1$ by dimension count ($\dim C^0=|V|$, $\ker d_0$ = constants, so $\dim\operatorname{im}d_0=|V|-1=|E|=\dim C^1$), hence
>
> $$\Phi_{\mathrm{geom}}\equiv 0 \quad\text{for \emph{every} input }\omega,$$
>
> and at the minimizer $h=\omega-d_0f^*=0$, so $\Phi_{\mathrm{curv}}=\|d_1h\|_1=0$ as well. Both land under $\varepsilon$, the second branch fires, $R_{cl}\equiv 0$, and the refusal rule **can never trigger**. The guardrail admits every transition on every acyclic carrier, regardless of what the transition does. This paper's own tables sit in that regime: the "Exact removal | path/tree" row of §7.2, and the "tree + conflict" row of §7.3 — where SAT repair runs with the closure guard structurally inert.
>
> What would have to be admitted to close this: a `DetectorAdequacy` certificate naming the carriers on which the geometric channel is non-degenerate, and a declared refusal to run the $R_{cl}$ guard as a safety argument outside them. What is lost if it is admitted: the guardrail stops being a general property of the controller and becomes a carrier-relative one — which is what it actually is. See §5.2.

4. Channel-Separation Cases

**Channel-Separation Cases** (named the *Empirical Channel-Separation Observation* through v2.1.1; "empirical" is withdrawn below and the name corrected to match). **[WORKED CASES — explicitly NOT statistical or causal independence, and NOT an observation of a verified system]** There are constructed cases in which each obstruction channel can activate while selected other channels remain below tolerance. This supports treating the channels as separate diagnostics for operator routing. It does not establish statistical independence or causal independence across arbitrary deployments.

5. Operator Selection

The controller maps obstruction patterns to candidate operators. A candidate operator is **proposed** only if it passes a cost-sensitive validation test; passing that test is a necessary condition for the proposal to be forwarded, never a sufficient condition for acceptance (§1).

The rows below are **triggers, not licenses.** A pattern in the left column entitles the controller to *consider and propose* the operator in the middle column. It does not entitle anything to apply it. In the classification of §0: a `ClosedSurvivor` and an `ActiveClosureFailure` may both trigger lift consideration; neither licenses a lift.

| Condition | Candidate operator (proposed, not licensed) | Intended effect |
|---|---|---|
| $\widehat{\Phi}_{\mathrm{geom}}$ dominant and cycles present | $\Pi_{12}$ structural lift | add structure that may absorb a cycle obstruction |
| $\widehat{\Phi}_{\mathrm{geom}}$ dominant and no valid lift found | $\Pi_{21}$ numeric repair | adjust section/gauge without topology change |
| $\widehat{\Phi}_{\mathrm{sat}}$ dominant | SAT repair | reinterpret, remove, or branch conflicting clauses |
| $\widehat{\Phi}_{\mathrm{rel}}$ increasing | rollback or branch | restore baseline or preserve alternatives |

### 5.1 Structural Lift Proposal Gate

A structural lift $\Lambda$ must not be chosen merely because cycles are present. It may be **proposed** only if it improves the validated composite score after cost penalty, and satisfies the structural guardrail:

$$
## \Delta_\Lambda
## \Phi_{\mathrm{total}}(S_t)
## \Phi_{\mathrm{total}}(S_t+\Lambda)
\lambda_\Lambda|\Lambda|.
$$

Propose the lift **only if** — these are necessary conditions, jointly insufficient:

1. $\Delta_\Lambda>0$
2. $R_{cl}' \le R_{\max}$
3. $\Phi_i(S_{t+1})\le \Phi_i(S_t)+\tau_i$ for all protected channels $i$.

Satisfying 1–3 does **not** license the lift. Under the GTOC lift rule, a lift requires five ingredients: an obstruction witness, a preservation witness, a non-destruction check, a budget/authority boundary, and a terminalizer route. Conditions 2–3 are the **non-destruction check**; $\lambda_\Lambda|\Lambda|$ in $\Delta_\Lambda$ is the **budget**. The remaining three — obstruction witness, preservation witness, terminalizer route — are **not supplied by this paper** and are recorded as open (§0). A deployment that treats 1–3 as a lift license is running a three-fifths gate and calling it a guard.

The protected-channel set \(P_{\mathrm{prot}}\subseteq\{\Phi_{\mathrm{geom}},\Phi_{\mathrm{curv}},\Phi_{\mathrm{sat}},\Phi_{\mathrm{rel}},\Xi\}\) must be declared in the deployment manifest and **must be non-empty**. The non-emptiness requirement is not decorative: $\subseteq$ admits $\varnothing$, and $P_{\mathrm{prot}}=\varnothing$ makes condition 3 vacuously true while satisfying the letter of "must be declared." Combined with the acyclic collapse of §3 — where condition 2 is also vacuous — an empty protected set reduces this gate to $\Delta_\Lambda>0$ alone: acceptance by a single weighted scalar, which is precisely the failure mode this section exists to prevent and which §3 opens by describing. Any increase beyond tolerance in a protected channel requires refusal or an explicit typed tradeoff terminal. The set may include \(\Xi\), even though \(\Xi\) is not part of the default composite score — a deployment that does so is protecting an imported, Hilbert-host-bearing quantity (§2) and must record Paper 009's six declared selections in its manifest.

Within the declared protected set, and at the resolution of the declared detector, condition 3 blocks "fake repair" in which a weighted total score decreases while one protected channel (e.g. logical consistency) worsens significantly. That scoping is the honest statement of the property; §5.2 prices what it leaves open. The condition keeps the policy aligned with the corrected quotient-projection theory: structural expansion is an operator proposal evaluated against a declared observer metric, not a repair the controller is entitled to make.

> **DELEGATE GAP — two of the five channels are invisible to the evaluator this paper delegates to.** §1 delegates acceptance to Paper 011's guarded evaluator. That evaluator is defined there as a function **only** of the tuple $(\Phi_1,\Gamma_2,R_{cl},\Xi)$ — Paper 011 is explicit that a proposal invisible to that tuple "is accepted or rejected on that tuple alone." $\Phi_{\mathrm{sat}}$ and $\Phi_{\mathrm{rel}}$ are **not in it**, and neither are $P_{\mathrm{prot}}$ or the tolerances $\tau_i$. So condition 3, for a protected set containing $\Phi_{\mathrm{sat}}$ or $\Phi_{\mathrm{rel}}$, has **no delegate that can enforce it**: either this controller enforces it — contradicting §1's ownership split and making the controller the decider after all — or nobody does, and a transition arbitrarily worsening logical consistency is accepted whenever the four-tuple descends.
>
> This paper does not resolve the gap; it declares it. What would have to be admitted to close it: an evaluator whose tuple spans the declared channel set, with $P_{\mathrm{prot}}$ and $\tau_i$ as evaluator-side declarations rather than controller-side intentions. What is lost if it is not: condition 3 is an intention this specification cannot cash, and no deployment should cite it as an enforced guard until an evaluator that reads $\Phi_{\mathrm{sat}}$ and $\Phi_{\mathrm{rel}}$ is declared and audited. Recorded as an open obligation (§0).

### 5.2 Detector Adequacy

**Detector adequacy warning.** A replayable detector/profile is not automatically adequate for a claim. A collapsing detector can make every profile exact. Any authority-bearing GTLA use requires a `DetectorAdequacy` certificate naming the protected distinctions, adversarial cases, and known blind spots for the declared claim scope.

No such certificate is supplied by this paper. It is an open obligation owned here (§0). Three specific gaps are already known and must be named in any future certificate:

1. **Detector collapse on acyclic carriers.** $\Phi_{\mathrm{geom}}\equiv0$ for every input, forcing $R_{cl}\equiv0$ and making the §3 refusal rule structurally unable to fire (§3 OBSTRUCTION). The guardrail is a carrier-relative property, not a general one.
2. **The joint blind spot.** §2 discloses each channel's blind spot individually. It does not compute their intersection, and no channel covers a defect outside the declared manifest at all. Five channels agreeing is not evidence that a repair is sound: the channels are a five-number projection of the whole state, and two materially different transitions with equal profiles are routed identically. A combination of diagnostics is **not** stronger evidence than its parts, and nothing in this corpus licenses that inference.
3. **Empty or narrow $P_{\mathrm{prot}}$.** The protected set is a deployment choice; the anti-fake-repair property is exactly as wide as that choice and no wider (§5.1).

An "accept" reached downstream of this controller therefore means only: *the declared detector did not object, under the declared policy, gauge, tolerances, and solver convention, on a carrier where that detector is not degenerate.* It does not mean the transition is correct, adequate, safe, or true.

6. Measurable Quantities

### 6.1 Structural Churn

Let $S_t$ denote the full system state. Let

$$
D_t=\operatorname{supp}(p_t^*)
$$

be the support of the selected dual witness at time $t$, where $p_t^*$ is an LP dual solution or documented witness estimate.

Dual-support churn is

$$
## \mathcal C_t
\frac{|D_t\Delta D_{t-1}|}{|D_t\cup D_{t-1}|},
$$

with $\mathcal C_t=0$ when both supports are empty. This measures volatility of the obstruction support, not full-state change.

If full-state churn is needed, use a separate metric such as

$$
## \mathcal S_t
\frac{|\operatorname{Feat}(S_t)\Delta\operatorname{Feat}(S_{t-1})|}
{|\operatorname{Feat}(S_t)\cup\operatorname{Feat}(S_{t-1})|}.
$$

### 6.2 Action Dispersion

For a sliding window $W_t$ of recent actions, let $P_{W_t}(o)$ be the **exhibited tally** of operator $o$ in $W_t$ divided by $|W_t|$ — a finite unit-sum weight profile over the declared operator set, obtained by counting. Define

$$
## H_t
-
\sum_{o\in\mathfrak O}P_{W_t}(o)\log P_{W_t}(o).
$$

Stable routing tends to lower $H_t$; oscillatory policies tend to raise it. This is diagnostic only and should not be treated as a proof of convergence.

> **IMPORT — the entropic reading is borrowed, and the corpus refuses it upstream.** $P_{W_t}$ is a finite class-count over an exhibited window. It is **not** a probability: no chance semantics, sample space, source model, or measure is declared anywhere in this paper, and none is available at the declared degree. The scalar $H_t$ is a **dispersion score of a finite tally**; naming it "entropy," and the $\log$ that computes it, are an `IMPORTED` convention (§0 register), not a construction. Paper 007 — this paper's upstream — lists under *does not produce*: "any probability, chance, or entropy semantics over the $\ell^1$ weights (none is asserted anywhere)." This paper asserts none either, and the section title has been corrected accordingly. A later information-theoretic adapter may read this unit-sum profile as a distribution and $H_t$ as its Shannon entropy; **this paper does not supply that adapter**, and no Shannon-theoretic consequence — capacity, coding bound, mutual information — may be drawn from $H_t$ as defined here.

### 6.3 Convergence Step Count

For tolerance $\epsilon$ and maximum budget $T_{\max}$,

$$
## T_{\Phi<\epsilon}
\min\{t:\Phi_{\mathrm{total}}(S_t)<\epsilon\}.
$$

\(T_{\Phi<\epsilon}\) counts **steps** to a low routing utility, not convergence to a certified optimum. It is an index over an exhibited operator sequence: not a duration, not physical time, and not convertible into one. Since \(\Phi_{\mathrm{total}}\) is a controller scalarization, reaching a threshold does not by itself imply evaluator acceptance, channel safety, or global repair. If no such $t$ occurs before $T_{\max}$, report nonconvergence within budget.

> **Dependency caveat.** That the routed descent loop reaches $\Phi_{\mathrm{total}} < \epsilon$ at all depends on the open descent-convergence conjecture (**Conjecture 4.3 of Paper 007, tracked as PL-007-1**; backlog 899), whose canonical home is Paper 007 and which is **OPEN**. This paper routes the dependency there and does not own, restate, or discharge it. Paper 007's live code is a 1-step subgradient method, not the three-step iterator the conjecture concerns; its `PaperFOC004` Lean artifact is a conditional finite reduction that takes the strict-descent property as a hypothesis and therefore does not discharge the conjecture either. No result in this paper may be read as evidence for it.

9. Conclusion

This paper defines a second-order control layer over constraint-system evolution. The controller monitors observer-relative obstruction channels, normalizes them for policy routing, and proposes repair operators under explicit guardrails. The composite score is a routing utility, not an authority certificate. Acceptance is not the controller's to grant: it is delegated to the guarded evaluator, and remains subject to channel-level diagnostics, closure-ratio bounds, declared operator contracts, and typed terminal semantics.

What the guardrails buy is narrow and worth stating plainly. Within a declared, non-empty protected set, on a carrier where the geometric channel is non-degenerate, and at the resolution of the declared detector, the controller cannot trade one protected channel against the composite score. Outside those three conditions the guards are inert: the $R_{cl}$ refusal cannot fire on an acyclic carrier, condition 3 is vacuous on an empty protected set, and no channel sees a defect outside the declared manifest. Multi-channel monitoring does not compound into adequacy, and a `DetectorAdequacy` certificate — which this paper does not supply — is required before any authority-bearing use. This is a routing instrument. It mints nothing.

Falsifiability

**Precondition — the criteria below are unrunnable without a manifest.** Both guardrail tests quantify over $R_{\max}$, $\varepsilon$, $\tau_i$, and $P_{\mathrm{prot}}$, none of which this paper fixes (§3, §5.1). A deployment that has not recorded them has not made a falsifiable claim, and cannot be caught violating one. Recording them is therefore not audit hygiene but the condition of this section's existence.

Given a manifest, the guardrail discipline is falsified by an accepted transition with $R_{cl}' > R_{\max}$, or by an accepted transition that lowers the composite score while a declared protected channel rises beyond its declared tolerance $\tau_i$ without emitting a typed tradeoff terminal (§5.1, condition 3). **Both tests are void on an acyclic carrier**, where $R_{cl}\equiv0$ makes the first unfalsifiable-because-inert rather than confirmed (§3): a run that never trips the guardrail there is not evidence the guardrail works. A fair test of the closure guard requires a carrier on which $\Phi_{\mathrm{geom}}$ is not identically zero.

The channel-separation cases (§4) are falsified by showing that the listed constructed cases — e.g. $\Phi_{\mathrm{geom}}>0$ with $\Phi_{\mathrm{sat}}=0$, or $\Phi_{\mathrm{rel}}>0$ with the other channels below tolerance — do not reproduce under the stated manifest in a conforming implementation. The paper asserts no statistical or causal independence, no detector adequacy, and no general repair completeness, so none of the three is offered as a falsifiable claim.

Abstract

**Main result (informal).** Repair control should not bake in a single privileged obstruction metric. Given a declared class of observers — each inducing its own obstruction geometry and diagnostics $(\Phi_1,\Gamma_2,R_{cl},\Xi,\Phi_{\mathrm{sat}},\Phi_{\mathrm{rel}})$ — this paper specifies an observer-robust controller that routes repair operators while keeping normalized failure indicators (guardrail violation, descent failure, cost divergence) bounded across the *whole* admissible observer class. This paper defines an observer-robust control architecture for constraint-system evolution. It is not a foundation for obstruction theory; observer-relative foundations are supplied by the primitive finite-obstruction framework established in "Primitive Axiomatics of Finite Obstruction Calculus." The present paper studies a repair-policy layer that operates over declared observers, induced obstruction geometries, diagnostic channels, and typed repair operators.

Given an admissible observer class, a constraint state, and observer-induced diagnostics such as $\Phi_1, \Gamma_2, R_{cl}, \Xi, \Phi_{\mathrm{sat}},$ and $\Phi_{\mathrm{rel}}$ (the dual-witness trace metric $\Xi$ is defined in Paper 009, "Conditional Density Lift and Dual-Witness Xi Functional"), the controller routes candidate repair operators under cost, guardrail, and terminal-semantics constraints. The composite policy score is a routing utility, not an authority certificate, and scalarization is always an observer or deployment choice.

This is not a theory of intelligence, learning, cognition, consciousness, subjective awareness, or truth. It is a finite control architecture for state-dependent repair routing over observer-induced obstruction diagnostics.

**Why a researcher should care.** Most repair controllers fix one error metric and one scalar score, which silently privileges a single observer geometry. When the deployment's actual observer differs from that built-in one, the controller can look healthy under its own metric while failing under another. This paper makes the observer choice explicit and demands robustness across a declared observer class: the same policy must keep guardrail violations and descent failures bounded for *every* admissible observer, and any scalarization is flagged as injected rather than discovered.

**And the payoff is asymmetric — read §5.0 before relying on this criterion.** Quantifying over an observer class buys much less than it appears to. The criterion is a conjunction of *clean* readings, and a clean reading is the direction that fails silently: a gauge that is blind reports zero, and zero is indistinguishable from repaired. So the honest statement is not that the criterion survives a change of observer, but that **its failure reports survive one and its clean reports do not**. A violation seen by any admissible observer is a real defect and cannot be explained away by re-choosing the observer; agreement among all of them certifies only what the declared class was able to see. This paper therefore makes faithfulness of every declared observer an explicit hypothesis (§5.0(a)) rather than an assumption, and states the red/green asymmetry (§5.0(b)) rather than trading on it.

2. Observer-Selected Obstruction Geometry

The theory replaces the older single-metric view with an observer-relative structure.

An observer is specified by data such as

$$
\mathcal O=(\mathcal A,\mathcal M,\mathcal J),
$$

where:

* $\mathcal A$ is the aggregation law,
* $\mathcal M$ is the symmetry or compatibility monoid,
* $\mathcal J$ is the chosen gauge or channel family.

Different observers induce different obstruction geometries. Examples include $\ell^1$ edge aggregation, $\ell^2$ edge aggregation, trace-norm observers, Orlicz-style gauges, or other $\mathcal M$-compatible geometries.

The core quotient object for an $\ell^1$ replica-extensive observer is:

$$
## \Phi_1([\delta])
\inf_f |\delta-d_0f|_{\mathcal J}.
$$

This is not the only possible obstruction metric. It is the geometry induced by the observer class satisfying replica extensivity, decomposability, and the selected symmetry assumptions.

3. The $\Phi$–$\Gamma$ Split

The observer-relative obstruction framework adds a necessary distinction:

$$
(\Phi_1,\Gamma_2).
$$

### 1. Irreducible Mass

$$
## \Phi_1([\delta])
\inf_f |\delta-d_0f|_{\mathcal J}
$$

measures quotient-minimal residual mass under the selected observer.

### 2. Closure Failure

$$
\Gamma_2
$$

measures failure of the relevant second-order closure condition. Closure is defined explicitly relative to the chosen cochain model and its higher-order compatibility operators (e.g., $d_1 d_0 = 0$).

$\Gamma_2$ is defined as the observer-induced norm of the closure defect, typically of the form $\Gamma_2 = \|d_1 h\|_{\mathcal J}$ for a representative $h$, with the precise form determined by the selected observer and cochain model. The structural identity $d_1 d_0 = 0$ (derived in foundational theory) ensures this is well-defined on quotient classes. $\Gamma_2$ detects broken local closure or boundary failure that is not captured by irreducible mass alone.

### 3. Closure Ratio

When $\Phi_1>0$, define

$$
## R_{\mathrm{cl}}
\frac{\Gamma_2}{\Phi_1}.
$$

with the zero-$\Phi_1$ cases handled by the evaluator convention. This ratio separates cases that the old scalar controller could conflate:

| Case | $\Phi_1$ | $\Gamma_2$ | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| clean cycle obstruction | $>0$ | near $0$ | stable quotient obstruction |
| broken local closure | $>0$ | $>0$ | obstruction plus closure failure |
| closure-only diagnostic | near $0$ | $>0$ | local compatibility failure without large quotient mass |
| quiet state | near $0$ | near $0$ | no detected obstruction in this observer |

$R_{\mathrm{cl}}$ is the first stability diagnostic in this layer. The controller should use it before collapsing diagnostics to a single scalar.

\to

5. Definition (Observer-Robust Control, Admissible Form)

### 5.0 Admissibility of $\mathfrak O$, and What a Green Verdict Is Worth

Definition 5.1 quantifies over a declared observer class. Two facts about that quantification are load-bearing, and both cut against the criterion rather than for it. They are stated here, before the definition, because the definition is unsound without the first and is routinely misread without the second.

**(a) Faithfulness is a hypothesis, not a background assumption.** The gauge family $\mathcal J$ of an observer triple is a **seminorm** family. The classifications this paper imports (§7) carry faithfulness on their hypothesis lists precisely because it does not come for free: a non-faithful gauge ($\mathcal J\equiv0$ on the defect subspace; equivalently $w_e\equiv0$ or a vanishing scale) satisfies the aggregation, symmetry, and decomposability axioms and makes

$$\Phi_{\mathcal O}\equiv 0,\qquad \Gamma_2\equiv 0$$

identically — **every cochain reads exact**. Such an observer passes all four invariants of Definition 5.1 vacuously: descent (invariant 1) holds as $0\le0$; the $R_{cl}$ guardrail (invariant 2) passes through the $\Phi_1\le\varepsilon,\Gamma_2\le\varepsilon$ branch of its own piecewise definition; no channel dominates, so responsiveness (invariant 3) is vacuous; and, reporting no guardrail violations and no descent failures, it contributes the *minimum* to $\operatorname{Robust}_{\mathfrak O}$ (invariant 4). A controller doing arbitrary damage is certified observer-robust relative to a class of such observers.

This is **not** the case §4 already handles. §4 refuses the silent-zero reading of a *missing* channel and terminalizes it as `DiagnosticNotDeclared`. A collapsing observer declares its channels and genuinely reports zero. It is a different failure and needs a different fence.

> **Admissibility condition (hypothesis of Definition 5.1).** Every $\mathcal O\in\mathfrak O$ must be declared **faithful on the declared defect space**: $\mathcal J_{\mathcal O}(x)=0\iff x=0$ there (for the weighted $\ell^1$ family, $w_e>0$ for every carrier edge). A declared $\mathfrak O$ containing a non-faithful member does not satisfy Definition 5.1's hypotheses, and any robustness verdict computed over it is void. This paper does **not** supply a procedure for checking faithfulness of a proposed deployment gauge, and supplies no `DetectorAdequacy` certificate; it declares the requirement and stops.

**(b) The verdict is asymmetric, and the criterion is built from the unsound side.** For any gauge, faithful or not, $\mathcal J(0)=0$; so from $\Phi_{\mathcal O}([\delta])=\inf_f\lVert\delta-d_0f\rVert_{\mathcal J}$:

| Verdict | Reading | Status |
|---|---|---|
| $\Phi_{\mathcal O}>0$ (**RED**) | $\forall f,\ \delta-d_0f\neq0$, hence $\delta\notin\operatorname{im}(d_0)$ | **Sound for any declared gauge.** A positive reading certifies a real defect. An observer cannot manufacture obstruction that the carrier does not carry. |
| $\Phi_{\mathcal O}=0$ (**GREEN**) | $\delta\in\operatorname{im}(d_0)$ | **Sound only if $\mathcal J$ is faithful.** Otherwise it yields only $\delta-d_0f\in\ker\mathcal J$: the observer's zero, not the carrier's. |

Definition 5.1 is a **conjunction of green verdicts** — descent holds, the guardrail holds, instability stays low, for *each* $\mathcal O\in\mathfrak O$. It is therefore assembled entirely from the direction that can fail silently. The honest reading of the criterion:

* **A failure report is trustworthy.** If any declared observer reports a guardrail violation or a descent failure, that is a real defect in the controller, and no change of observer within $\mathfrak O$ can explain it away.
* **A clean report is not.** "Observer-robust relative to $\mathfrak O$" is a statement *about $\mathfrak O$*. Its strength is bounded by the finest, most faithful member of the declared class, and this paper does not require $\mathfrak O$ to contain a finest member. All-green across $\mathfrak O$ does not lift to a claim about the core.

The same asymmetry arrives independently at the foundational layer, where an observer is a repair-compatible **quotient map** $\mathcal O:Q^1\to Q^1_{\mathcal O}$ rather than a gauge triple: Lemma O1 (observer passivity / no-creation) [1] gives $\Phi_{\mathcal O}(\mathcal O(q))\le\Phi(q)$, so observers *can* erase or identify obstruction and *cannot* create it — again, red sound, green not. That lemma is stated for the quotient-map notion of observer and is **not** imported here as a premise about the triples $(\mathcal A,\mathcal M,\mathcal J)$ this paper declares; it is cited only as corroboration that the asymmetry is a property of observation in this framework, not an artifact of one construction. Deployments that want O1's guarantee must declare their observers as repair-compatible quotients and check its hypotheses.

Consequently the claim at the end of §5 — that the criterion is "strictly stronger than a single-observer scalar view" — is **withdrawn as stated** and corrected there.

**Definition 5.1 — Observer-Robust Control Criterion.** Let $\mathfrak O$ be a declared class of observers, **each admissible in the sense of §5.0(a)**. A controller is defined as *observer-robust relative to $\mathfrak O$* if and only if it implements a robust policy:

$$
\operatorname{Ctrl}(S_t, \mathcal O, H_t) \to \mathsf{Ops}
$$

such that, for each $\mathcal O\in\mathfrak O$, the induced diagnostic dynamics satisfy the following four core topological and functional invariants:

### 1. Descent (Quotient Reduction)

The geometric quotient residual does not increase under accepted transitions. For each observer \(\mathcal O\in\mathfrak O\), let
\[
\Phi_{\mathcal O}
\]
denote the primary quotient residual induced by that observer’s declared gauge, aggregation, and repair model. The descent invariant is:
\[
\Phi_{\mathcal O}(S_{t+1})\le \Phi_{\mathcal O}(S_t)
\]
up to declared tolerance. Accepted transitions must satisfy the guarded evaluator: the $R_{cl}$ gate passes, $\Phi_{\mathcal O}$ does not increase, and tie-breaking proceeds through $\Gamma_2$ and then $\Xi$. The controller may propose exploratory operators, but acceptance is determined exclusively by the evaluator — no transition that violates descent is accepted into the committed state trajectory. This criterion applies to controllers using the guarded descent evaluator; other policies must declare their own admissible ordering. Here $S_{t+1}$ denotes the committed successor state, not an exploratory proposal.

### 2. Stability (Boundary Guardrail)

The structural boundary ratio $R_{cl}$ remains strictly bounded under accepted transitions:
$$
R_{cl}(S_{t+1}) \le R_{\max}
$$
which is intended to catch the *fake repair* failure mode of §10 — quotient mass falling while closure damage rises. The guardrail catches that mode in its **partial** form: if $\Phi_1$ collapses while $\Gamma_2$ does not, the piecewise rule below returns $+\infty$ and the transition is refused. It does **not** catch **total** collapse: an observer for which $\Phi_1$ and $\Gamma_2$ both vanish passes through the $R_{cl}=0$ branch. The bound is therefore a guardrail conditional on §5.0(a) admissibility, not a guarantee that repair cannot be faked; it is a declared acceptance condition, and it proves nothing on its own. For each observer $\mathcal O$, the policy must state its diagnostics, acceptance guardrails, failure terminals, and normalization rules. In exact mathematics, \(R_{cl}=\Gamma_2/\Phi_{\mathcal O}\) is defined only when \(\Phi_{\mathcal O}>0\). The \(\varepsilon\)-piecewise form is an implementation convention for finite-precision guardrails:

$$
R_{cl} =
\begin{cases}
\Gamma_2/\Phi_1 & \Phi_1>\varepsilon,\\
0 & \Phi_1\le\varepsilon \text{ and } \Gamma_2\le\varepsilon,\\
+\infty & \Phi_1\le\varepsilon \text{ and } \Gamma_2>\varepsilon.
\end{cases}
$$

### 3. Diagnostic Responsiveness

The routing policy is diagnostic-responsive if its selected operator class depends on the dominant declared diagnostic channel. A policy that applies the same repair regardless of whether $\Phi_1$, $\Gamma_2$, $\Phi_{\mathrm{sat}}$, or $\Phi_{\mathrm{rel}}$ dominates is not responsive in this sense.

### 4. Observer Robustness

Performance degradation remains bounded under changes of observer within $\mathfrak O$. One possible robustness score is

$$
## \operatorname{Robust}_{\mathfrak O}
\sup_{\mathcal O\in\mathfrak O}
\operatorname{Instability}_{\mathcal O}(\operatorname{Ctrl}),
$$

The observer class \(\mathfrak O\) must be declared before evaluation. If \(\mathfrak O\) is finite, robustness is checked over that finite list. If \(\mathfrak O\) is infinite, the paper assumes a declared test envelope, compactness/regularity condition, or sampling protocol; otherwise the supremum is only a formal specification, not a computable certificate.

where $\operatorname{Instability}_{\mathcal O}$ is a declared measure of policy failure. \(\operatorname{Instability}_{\mathcal O}\) is not canonical. Each deployment must define it, including units, normalization, thresholds, and failure indicators. Raw diagnostic magnitudes across observers must not be compared without normalization. Only declared normalized failure indicators, such as guardrail violation, descent failure, or normalized cost divergence, are compared. Smaller values indicate stronger observer-robust control.

This criterion is **not** one observer, one scalar, or one notion of error. It is policy over a declared bundle of geometries.

> **Correction (claim withdrawn).** Earlier versions read "This criterion is *strictly stronger* than a single-observer scalar view." That is withdrawn: it is false as stated. Definition 5.1 is a conjunction of green verdicts over $\mathfrak O$ (§5.0(b)), so its strength is bounded by the finest, most faithful member of the declared class — and nothing here requires $\mathfrak O$ to contain one. Against a $\mathfrak O$ whose members are all coarser than the single observer it replaces, the criterion is strictly *weaker*. What survives is a scoped and useful, but smaller, claim: **the criterion is stronger than a single-observer view exactly to the extent that $\mathfrak O$ is declared to contain observers that view is missing, and it is sound in the failure direction regardless** — a violation reported by any admissible $\mathcal O\in\mathfrak O$ is a real defect. Adding observers to $\mathfrak O$ adds conjuncts, which can only catch more failures; it never certifies more.

6. Scalarization Is an Observer Choice

The controller may scalarize diagnostics for routing, but scalarization is injected by the observer or deployment policy. It is not canonical.

Given diagnostics $\Phi_i$, define normalized quantities

$$
## \widehat{\Phi}_i(S_t)
\frac{\Phi_i(S_t)}{\sigma_i+\epsilon_i},
$$

where $\sigma_i$ is a declared scale such as a threshold, baseline, maximum admissible value, or robust historical scale.

A policy scalar may be:

$$
## \Phi_{\mathrm{policy}}(S_t)
\sum_i a_i\widehat{\Phi}_i(S_t),
$$

but this is a control score, not "the" obstruction of the system. A different observer or deployment may choose a vector-valued policy, lexicographic ordering, Pareto frontier, risk-sensitive utility, or nonadditive aggregation.

The scalar expression

$$
\Phi_{\mathrm{geom}}
+\lambda_{\mathrm{sat}}\Phi_{\mathrm{sat}}
+\lambda_{\mathrm{rel}}\Phi_{\mathrm{rel}}
$$

is valid only as one chosen scalarization after scales and observer assumptions are declared. All scalarization choices — \(\sigma_i,\epsilon_i,a_i\), ordering rules, Pareto thresholds, and risk-sensitive utilities — are deployment-policy parameters and must be recorded in the audit manifest. Without them, \(\Phi_{\mathrm{policy}}\) is not replayable.

9. Operator Policy

The controller acts through operators over state, section, graph, observer data, and constraint generators.

| Operator | Symbol | Input -> Output | Candidate condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| numeric repair | $\Pi_{21}$ | adjust section/gauge and re-project | quotient residual is dominant and local repair is available |
| structural lift | $\Pi_{12}$ | add node/edges or lift topology | a reducing lift is available under validation |
| closure repair | -- | adjust local compatibility data | $\Gamma_2$ or $R_{\mathrm{cl}}$ is dominant |
| SAT repair | -- | reinterpret, remove, or branch conflicting clauses | SAT residual is dominant |
| rollback | -- | restore previous state | relational drift is increasing |
| branch | -- | preserve multiple alternatives | repair choice is ambiguous |
| wait | -- | no modification | diagnostics below thresholds |

### Cost-Sensitive Acceptance

A structural lift $\Lambda$ is accepted only if it improves the selected policy score after cost:

$$
## \Delta_\Lambda
## \Phi_{\mathrm{policy}}(S_t)
## \Phi_{\mathrm{policy}}(S_t+\Lambda)
\lambda_\Lambda|\Lambda|.
$$

Accept the lift only if \(\Delta_\Lambda>0\). \(\Delta_\Lambda>0\) is a routing precondition, not acceptance authority. A structural lift enters the committed trajectory only if the guarded evaluator for the relevant observer also accepts it: primary residual non-worsening/descent, \(R_{cl}\) guardrail, protected-channel tolerances, rollback/cost conditions, and typed terminal semantics.

For vector-valued policies, replace this scalar test with the declared ordering rule, such as Pareto improvement or lexicographic priority.

10. Structural Failure Modes

Observer-relative control makes failure modes more precise than a single "error" scalar.

| Failure | Meaning |
|---|---|
| $\Phi_1\downarrow$ but $\Gamma_2\uparrow$ | fake repair: quotient mass falls while closure damage rises |
| $\Phi_{\mathrm{sat}}\downarrow$ but $\Phi_1\uparrow$ | logical fix breaks geometric structure under the selected observer |
| observer instability | policy works for one observer but fails under another $\mathcal O\in\mathfrak O$ |
| channel oscillation | dominant diagnostic alternates without durable repair |
| scalarization failure | weighted sum hides a channel that violates its bound |
| gratuitous lift | structure expands without positive cost-adjusted improvement |

These failures are structural and diagnosable only after the observer and channel split have been declared.

The core contribution is:

\boxed{
\text{observer-robust repair control over declared diagnostics}
}
$$

The valid pieces are:

1. a policy map from state, observer, and history to repair operators;
2. diagnostic channels selected by the observer and modeling split;

Foundational theory supplies the observer-relative foundations; this paper supplies the repair-policy layer for controlling and stabilizing observer-induced obstruction geometries.

> **What the contribution is not.** None of the six mints authority (see the firewall at the head of this paper), and the sixth is the one most likely to be over-read: a controller certified observer-robust relative to a declared $\mathfrak O$ has passed a diagnostic predicate over a class someone chose. The certificate is only as good as $\mathfrak O$ is faithful and complete, and this paper supplies no procedure for establishing either.

13. One-Line Result

$$
\boxed{
\text{Result} = \text{observer-robust repair control over declared diagnostics}
}
$$

Observer-robust control is the property of a declared Ctrl policy that routes repairs over observer-induced diagnostics and keeps normalized failure indicators bounded across the admissible observer class $\mathfrak O$.

Falsifiability

The observer-robustness criterion (Definition 5.1) is falsified by a controller in the claimed class that, for some admissible observer $\mathcal O\in\mathfrak O$ (admissible in the sense of §5.0(a) — a non-faithful $\mathcal O$ is outside the hypotheses and falsifies nothing), violates one of its four invariants on a committed transition: $\Phi_1(S_{t+1})>\Phi_1(S_t)$ beyond tolerance, $R_{cl}(S_{t+1})>R_{\max}$, a routing decision independent of the dominant diagnostic channel, or a normalized failure indicator that diverges under a change of observer within $\mathfrak O$. The example claim "replica extensivity induces $\ell^1$" (§7) is falsified by a **faithful** replica-extensive, decomposable, maximally symmetric observer whose induced edge gauge is not weighted $\ell^1$ — faithfulness is required of the falsifier because the degenerate gauge $w_e\equiv0$ is *inside* the weighted-$\ell^1$ family, not outside it, and so refutes nothing. No single privileged obstruction metric is claimed, so none is offered as falsifiable.

Two further falsifiable commitments are added by the 2026-07-17 pass:

* **§5.0(b)'s asymmetry** is falsified by exhibiting a declared observer and a state with $\Phi_{\mathcal O}>0$ while the core class is exact — i.e. an observer that *creates* obstruction. (It cannot be done for the gauge family declared here: $\mathcal J(0)=0$ forces the red direction. A construction would mean the observer is not of the declared form.)
* **§9's `OBSTRUCTION`** is falsified by exhibiting a finite graph-only lift (with $\hat\omega$ extending $\omega$ by zero, repair class $\operatorname{Adm}=\operatorname{im}(d_0)$, $\ell^1$ observer) that strictly reduces $\Phi_1$ on $C_3$ with $\tau=[1,1,1]$ — which would simultaneously refute Paper 008's Theorem 4′ and restore this paper's withdrawn $\Phi_1=0.0$ observation.
