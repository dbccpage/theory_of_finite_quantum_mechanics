§2 — Matching

**2.1 Correspondence without functions.** A **matching** between exhibited families $A, B$ of loci (or cuts) is an exhibited configuration of *pairings* — cuts of a special role, each linking a member of $A$ to a member of $B$ — governed by a purely **local-incidence** discipline, stated without any appeal to counting: (i) *no double incidence* — no exhibited endpoint (in $A$ or in $B$) participates in two pairing cuts; (ii) *full incidence* — every exhibited endpoint carries a pairing cut; and (iii) *difference-preservation* — the pairings preserve the differences declared relevant. For transitivity of congruence, this requires a **frozen comparison scope**: one frozen comparison signature, identical endpoint typing, and a composition rule for preservation receipts. (Without a frozen scope, transitivity fails). Conditions (i)–(ii) are each a per-exhibition, locally checkable incidence property of the exhibited configuration — one reads off, *at each exhibited endpoint*, whether a pairing cut is present and whether a second is present — and they replace the circular "exactly one pairing per member," which would presuppose the very counting (§3) that matching is meant to ground. Whole-exhibition coverage is still required (condition (ii): every endpoint, both families), but no numerical-uniqueness language enters. "Declared relevant" invokes convention K6 (declared-comparison families — declared at Paper 1 §3.1, flagged): which comparisons a matching must preserve is a declared selection, a proto-observer act at δ2, priced not derived; and per K6's standing exclusion, the exhibition's token-apartness (Paper 0, K3) is never among the declared comparisons — otherwise nothing distinct could ever match. No set-theoretic functions enter; a matching is a configuration (Paper 0 §6.1) with a role-typing.

**2.2 Congruence.** Families $A, B$ are **congruent** when a matching between them is exhibited. Congruence is symmetric (pairings are unoriented at the family level) and — *within a single exhibition* — composable: pairings paste through shared members [DERIVED, finite exhibition; pasting is relational composition, available at δ0/δ1 without sequence]. §4 — Witness Realization, Finite (L1) Atomicity, and Automorphism

**4.1 Witness Realization and Weight.** Residuals are carried by witnesses (Paper 0 §5.2). But a residual may have multiple minimal certificates, overlapping certificates, or one composite certificate. Thus, a residual must be assessed against a **declared witness realizer** $\mathcal{W}(r)$ (carrier, atomicity, coverage, overlap rules, detector adequacy).

Unit witness mass is the multiplicity of a declared atomic witness realization:
$$w_{\mathcal{W}}(r) = |\,\mathcal{W}(r)\,| \quad (\text{a derived scalar, } \ge 0).$$

A general finite weight is an additive valuation on that realization: $w_{\mathcal{W},\mu}(r) = \sum_{x \in \mathcal{W}(r)} \mu(x)$.
Weight descends to be residual-invariant (presentation-independent) only after demonstrating that admissible alternative realizations give the same result, governed by a mass conservation law under witness refinement (split/merge transports).

**4.2 The no-privileged-witness theorem.** The brief demands "weight over residual **with no privileged witness**" (charter, Controlling brief item 3). At δ2 the honest form of this is an invariance theorem about a *declared class* of weightings, together with a priced dichotomy — not an unconditional prohibition:

**Convention K10 (automorphism orbit equivariance — flagged).** A weighting of members of a family $W$ is **admissible** when it is constant on automorphism orbits of the entire declared witness structure. Pairwise failure of selected comparisons is not enough when the packet contains contextual or higher-arity structure (exchanging $x$ and $y$ may alter their relations to a third witness). For every full-structure automorphism $\mu$ of $W$ with itself that preserves all declared comparisons (K6), the weighting assigns matched values to $\mu$-matched members. K10 is a **declared naturality/equivariance condition**, convention-style, deferring the metalinguistic *definability* construction to **PL-GTOC-13**.

**Theorem (No Privileged Witness on Orbits).** Let $W$ be a witness family. If $x$ and $y$ lie in the same internal automorphism orbit of the declared witness structure, then any **admissible (K10)** weighting assigns them the same value: $w(x) = w(y)$. §5 — $L^1$ forced: The Five-Layer Finite Probability Architecture

**5.1 Layer 1: Witness multiplicity & Layer 2: Finite valuation.** A residual's weight is assessed against an atomic decomposition. Under the Finite (L1) Atomicity Theorem (§4.3), total weight over an exhibited decomposition into disjoint witness families is the sum of the atomic parts' weights — aggregation is $\Sigma$:

$$\mathcal{A}(\{w_i\}) = \sum_i w_i \quad \text{[PROVEN at δ2 for multiplicity-weights]}.$$

For patterns of signed weights on cuts (δ1 orientation × δ2 counts), the induced size of a pattern is the sum of member-magnitudes — the $\ell^1$ form. What the additivity theorem forces is precisely *summation of positive witness masses* — a first-order total mass ($\ell^1$; after signs are attached, total variation); it does **not** by itself force that *every* legitimate magnitude on the weight data is an $\ell^1$ norm (an $\ell^2$ magnitude is additive too — but in *square*, over orthogonal components; the exact hypothesis package under which $\ell^1$ is the *unique* aggregation magnitude is stated at §6.3, conditions (i)–(iii)). The corpus's conditional-$\ell^1$ results are hereby *stratified*: Paper 004 Thm 1 forces $\nu_e(x) = w_e \lVert x \rVert_1$ from H1 (edge locality), H2 (permutation symmetry), H3 (disjoint additivity), H5 (homogeneity) for unknown $\mathbb{R}$-valued functionals; at δ2 the functional is not unknown — weights are *constructed* as counts, H3 is the §3.2 theorem, H2 is the §4.2 theorem for K10-admissible weightings on indistinguishables, and H1 is the §4.1 construction (weight attaches to the witness locus it counts). What was an axiom package becomes: one theorem (additivity), one scoped theorem (invariance, K6), one construction (locality), and one flagged convention inherited from scalars (homogeneity, K7, §3.3).

**5.2 The honest fine structure: orbit-uniform, not global-uniform.** Theorem 4.2 forces equal weighting **within** each indistinguishability class (orbit) and forces nothing **across** classes. The derived aggregation is therefore *orbit-weighted* $\ell^1$:

$$\nu(\delta) = \sum_{O} w_O \sum_{e \in O} |\delta_e|,$$

with per-orbit constants $w_O$ genuinely free. This reproduces, from the ground, exactly the fine structure the corpus found from above: Paper 004 Thm 2 (orbit-weighted $\ell^1$ under graph invariance), Thm 3 (unweighted global $\ell^1$ **only** under edge-transitivity or a declared uniformity axiom), and Paper 004 §3.5's finding that the Thm 2 → Thm 3 gap is a genuine LGGR instance — edge-uniformity is "a genuinely additional axiom." At δ2: global uniformity holds exactly when *all* witness loci are mutually indistinguishable (one orbit); otherwise cross-orbit weights are declarations. Paper 000's A6 (fixed $\ell^1$ on $C^2$) is priced accordingly: derived within orbits of indistinguishable triangles, declared across orbits.

**5.3 Layer 3: Proportion and Layer 4: Finite probability.** With ratios available (§3.3), the **normalized weight** of a residual within an exhibited ensemble of residuals is its proportion — the series' term is **finite weight normalization**:

$$\rho_i := \frac{w_i}{\sum_j w_j} \qquad \left(\textstyle\sum_j w_j \neq 0\right),$$

a unit-bounded derived ratio, per exhibition, summing to unity by construction [PROVEN]. To call the $\rho_i$ proportions of one whole, the associated witness families must form a declared **FinitePartition** (coverage of the total carrier, pairwise non-overlap, one assignment per witness).

To obtain a finite probability valuation on events, define the event family generated by exhibited unions of partition cells:
$$ P\!\left(\bigsqcup_{i\in I}E_i\right) = \sum_{i\in I}\rho_i $$
The required packet is a `FiniteProbabilityPacket` (total carrier, atomic partition cells, coverage witness, disjointness witness, positive valuation, nonzero total mass, generated finite event family, additive extension receipt, normalization receipt). No external sample space is required. The total carrier and event family are internal to the packet.

**Layer 5: Interpretation adapters.** What the probability valuation means—frequency, forecast, propensity, credence, physical chance, or quantum outcome—is a separate **adapter** (e.g., `FrequencyAdapter`, `PhysicalChanceAdapter`) and must be earned. A finite probability packet can remain entirely packet-local (e.g., a finite sequence of exhibited trials).

**6.1 What is constructible, and what is not.** Honesty first: a symmetric, positive, $\mathbb{Q}$-valued bilinear pairing of exhibited weight-patterns *is* δ2-constructible — $\langle x, y \rangle = \sum_i x_i y_i$ assembles from per-locus pair-family products (§3.2), signs (§3.3), and disjoint-union summation (§5.1); nothing bars the arithmetic. **6.2 Each requirement priced.**

**6.3 Structural incompatibility, not mere absence.** Importing $\ell^p$, $p > 1$, as the *aggregation* norm would contradict a δ2 theorem: $\ell^1$ is exactly additive on disjoint supports, and for $p>1$ strictly subadditive there (Paper 010 §1.3 Thm 2, with the boxed consequence "$\ell^1$ is the unique $\ell^p$ bookkeeping norm with exact additive aggregation on disjoint positive sectors" — immediately fenced there against universality claims, a fence this paper inherits). **The exclusion is hypothesis-relative, stated exactly.** What the δ2 additivity theorem forces is *summation of positive witness masses* — a first-order total mass ($\ell^1$; after signs, total variation).
