\hypertarget{abstract}{%
\subsection{Abstract}\label{abstract}}

For finite graph cochains with coordinate-valued edge defects, any
edge-local seminorm that is coordinate-permutation symmetric and
additive over disjoint coordinate supports is forced to be a nonnegative
scalar multiple of coordinatewise \(\ell^1\) edgewise; positive weights
require faithfulness/normhood; across edges the sharp conclusion is
orbit-weighted \(\ell^1\), with unweighted global \(\ell^1\) requiring
edge-transitivity or explicit uniformity. The additivity assumption is
justified only for coordinate-separable, replica-extensive observers.
Relaxing those assumptions yields alternative obstruction geometries.

\textbf{Conventions.} Norms are denoted \(\lVert \cdot \rVert\) with
appropriate subscripts; scalar absolute values are denoted \(|\cdot|\).

\textbf{Why a researcher should care.} Many finite diagnostic systems
decompose observations into coordinate channels: graph total variation,
componentwise residuals, finite constraint violations, sparse defect
fields, and coordinate-separable model-evaluation errors all have this
form. This paper shows in this model that if those coordinate channels
are treated as independent replicas and their disjoint supports must add
exactly, then \(\ell^1\) is not an arbitrary choice --- it is forced
inside that observer class. The result also says what is not forced:
\(\ell^2\), \(\ell^\infty\), cross-edge cancellation,
coordinate-weighted \(\ell^1\), and orbit-weighted edge sums all
reappear when the corresponding assumptions are removed.

\hypertarget{introduction}{%
\subsection{1. Introduction}\label{introduction}}

This paper is the first step in a sequence analyzing projection-induced
defects on presheaf coboundaries through progressively richer structural
settings:

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{This paper} establishes that coordinate-wise additivity and
  symmetry on finite graph cochains select coordinatewise \(\ell^1\)
  geometry within the coordinate-separable observer class. The result is
  purely combinatorial and requires no functional-analytic or dynamical
  structure.
\item
  \textbf{The M-compatible Banach-presheaf paper} {[}7{]} extends the
  classification under a different observer class: maximal
  bounded-linear local symmetry fixes the native stalk norm edgewise,
  and edge-additive aggregation then yields a weighted \(\ell^1\) edge
  sum.
\item
  \textbf{The projection-obstruction paper} {[}6{]} applies this
  rigidity in a setting that imports evolution and a projection map, and
  reports coboundary defects measured by a conditional \(\ell^1\)
  diagnostic. Both the dynamics and the projection are imported
  representation structures relative to the present paper; nothing here
  derives them, and the \(\ell^1\) measurement is forced there only
  under that paper's own declared hypotheses, not as an unconditional
  necessity.
\end{itemize}

The current package is
\texttt{009\_\allowbreak{}coordinate\_\allowbreak{}wise\_\allowbreak{}additivity}
(legacy Paper 003 / Paper 004), and is logically prior to the
Banach-presheaf and projection-obstruction papers. The numbering
reflects repository order, not dependency order.

The purpose of the present paper is to isolate the minimal combinatorial
realization of the replica-extensive, coordinate-separable observer
class behind this rigidity. In the broader M-compatible framework, this
corresponds to the case where coordinate channels are required to
aggregate independently. Other symmetry classes yield different
admissible gauges. Rather than beginning with Banach space assumptions
or dynamical systems, we show that \(\ell^1\) geometry is selected for
finite graph cochains under coordinate-separable, replica-extensive
evaluation.

A central modeling vulnerability is treating the summation of
independent measurement channels as an ad hoc assumption (\(L^2\)
root-sum-squares assume an entirely different aggregation). We isolate
that assumption rather than hiding it. When independent observations are
required to compose additively over disjoint coordinate supports, the
finite seminorm argument below forces linear absolute-value summation.
Without that observer boundary, the conclusion does not follow.

\textbf{Falsifiability.} A proposed use of this classification fails if
it cannot specify the finite graph, coordinate dimension \(k\),
edge-local decomposition, coordinate permutation action,
disjoint-coordinate additivity condition, seminorm homogeneity,
graph-isomorphism action, and any edge-uniformity assumption used to
pass from orbit-weighted to unweighted \(\ell^1\). The theorem would be
falsified by a seminorm satisfying all stated hypotheses but not
representable as
\(\sum_{\mathcal O}w_{\mathcal O}\sum_{e\in\mathcal O}\|\delta_e\|_1\).

\hypertarget{setup}{%

\hypertarget{genesis-bias-fence-no-prior-host}{%
\subsubsection{Genesis-Bias Fence --- No Prior
Host}\label{genesis-bias-fence-no-prior-host}}

This paper does not begin with a substrate that hosts the graph, a
possibility domain that contains the cochains, an ambient geometry that
pre-selects a norm, a physical system the edges represent, or an
observer standing outside the comparison. It begins with
\textbf{declared, packet-local finite data}: an index set of edges
\(E\), an index set of coordinates \(\{1,\dots,k\}\), a declared scalar
specialization \(\mathbb R\), and a declared comparison scope (the
axioms H1--H5 below).

\(G=(V,E)\), \(k\), and the coordinate fiber are \textbf{declared}, not
derived and not antecedent. The graph is not a prior container from
which cochains issue; it is the finite index data the declared
comparison ranges over. \texttt{[DEFINITION]} below records that
declaration --- it does not report a discovery about a pre-existing
space.

The ``observer'' of this paper is a \textbf{declared-comparison scope}
(charter convention K6): the bundle H1--H5 and nothing more. It carries
no privilege, no standpoint outside the differences it compares, and no
authority. Where the prose says a geometry is ``forced'' or
``selected'', the scope of that force is exactly this declared bundle.

\textbf{Pricing the stronger readings.} \(\mathbb R\) is a
\textbf{declared scalar specialization}, not a derived one --- absolute
homogeneity over \(\mathbb R\) is admitted here, not earned.
\(\bigoplus_e \mathbb R^k\) is a declared finite carrier, not a
finite-structural state space. Neither the graph nor the fiber is
claimed to be prior to, or to explain, the differences the diagnostic
measures.

Let \(G=(V,E)\) be a finite directed graph and \(k\ge1\) an integer,
declared as above, and

\textbf{{[}DEFINITION{]}} \[
C^1(G,\mathbb R^k)=\bigoplus_{e\in E}\mathbb R^k.
\]

An element \(\delta\in C^1(G,\mathbb R^k)\) assigns to every edge
\(e\in E\) a vector

\[
\delta_e=(\delta_{e,1},\ldots,\delta_{e,k})\in\mathbb R^k.
\]

Let

\[
\nu_G:C^1(G,\mathbb R^k)\to\mathbb R_{\ge0}
\]

be a seminorm satisfying the assumptions below. In H1, the local terms
\(\nu_e:\mathbb R^k\to\mathbb R_{\ge0}\) are nonnegative maps.
Evaluating H1 on the zero cochain gives \(\sum_e\nu_e(0)=0\), hence
every \(\nu_e(0)=0\). Restricting \(\nu_G\) to cochains supported on one
edge then gives exactly \(\nu_e\), so each local term inherits
seminormhood. Equivalently, one can declare the local seminorms at the
outset.

If \(E=\varnothing\), the global carrier is zero-dimensional and its
unique seminorm is zero; orbit statements are vacuous and one may take
\(\alpha=0\). The basis and positivity arguments below use \(k\ge1\);
edge-transitivity is used on a nonempty edge set. The \(k=0\) case is
excluded because it has no first basis vector and its scalar weight is
not identifiable.

The observer assumptions are:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{{[}AXIOM / DEFINITION OF OBSERVER CLASS{]} (H1) Edge locality}
\end{enumerate}

\[
\nu_G(\delta)=\sum_{e\in E}\nu_e(\delta_e).
\]

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\setcounter{enumi}{1}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{{[}AXIOM / OBSERVER ASSUMPTION{]} (H2) Coordinate permutation
  symmetry}
\end{enumerate}

For every permutation \(\sigma\in S_k\),

\[
\nu_e(x_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,x_{\sigma(k)}) = \nu_e(x_1,\ldots,x_k).
\]

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\setcounter{enumi}{2}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{{[}AXIOM / OBSERVER ASSUMPTION{]} (H3) Disjoint-coordinate
  additivity}
\end{enumerate}

If \(\operatorname{supp}(x)\cap\operatorname{supp}(y)=\varnothing\),
then

\[
\nu_e(x+y)=\nu_e(x)+\nu_e(y).
\]

Here ``replica-extensive'' means exact additivity over declared disjoint
coordinate supports. It is an observer assumption, not a property of all
diagnostics or all physical systems.

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\setcounter{enumi}{3}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{{[}AXIOM / OBSERVER ASSUMPTION{]} (H4) Graph isomorphism
  invariance}
\end{enumerate}

If \(\phi:G\to G'\) is a graph isomorphism, then

\[
\nu_{G'}(\phi_*\delta)=\nu_G(\delta).
\]

Here \(\phi_*\) relabels edges; if a nontrivial action on the coordinate
fiber \(\mathbb R^k\) is declared, it is assumed to act by (possibly
signed) coordinate permutations, hence \(\ell^1\)-isometrically. This
explicit fiber hypothesis is what Theorem 2 uses when comparing edge
weights across an orbit.

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\setcounter{enumi}{4}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{{[}AXIOM / OBSERVER ASSUMPTION{]} (H5) Seminorm absolute
  homogeneity}
\end{enumerate}

Carried by the standing assumption that \(\nu_G\), and hence each
\(\nu_e\), is a seminorm:

\[
\nu_e(cx)=|c|\,\nu_e(x)\qquad(c\in\mathbb R).
\]

Listed explicitly because the status claims in the front matter and the
Lean bridges quantify over it. Absolute homogeneity is admitted over the
\textbf{declared scalar specialization} \(\mathbb R\) (Genesis-Bias
fence above); it is not derived here.

\textbf{Hypothesis register.} H1 edge locality; H2 coordinate
permutation symmetry; H3 disjoint-coordinate additivity; H4
graph-isomorphism invariance with \(\ell^1\)-isometric
(signed-permutation) fiber action; H5 seminorm absolute homogeneity.
Every ``{[}PROVEN, CONDITIONAL{]}'' tag in this paper is conditional on
the subset of H1--H5 named at the theorem, and on nothing else.

\hypertarget{proven-conditional-theorem-1-coordinatewise-additivity-forces-a-nonnegative-scalar-multiple-of-coordinatewise-ell1-edgewise-positive-weights-require-faithfulnessnormhood}{%
\subsubsection{\texorpdfstring{2.2 \textbf{{[}PROVEN, CONDITIONAL{]}
Theorem 1 --- Coordinatewise Additivity Forces a Nonnegative Scalar
Multiple of Coordinatewise \(\ell^1\) Edgewise; Positive Weights Require
Faithfulness/Normhood}}{2.2 {[}PROVEN, CONDITIONAL{]} Theorem 1 --- Coordinatewise Additivity Forces a Nonnegative Scalar Multiple of Coordinatewise \textbackslash ell\^{}1 Edgewise; Positive Weights Require Faithfulness/Normhood}}\label{proven-conditional-theorem-1-coordinatewise-additivity-forces-a-nonnegative-scalar-multiple-of-coordinatewise-ell1-edgewise-positive-weights-require-faithfulnessnormhood}}

\textbf{Statement:}

For each edge \(e\), there exists a scalar \(w_e\ge0\) such that

\[
\nu_e(x)=w_e\|x\|_1 = w_e\sum_{i=1}^k |x_i|
\]

for all \(x\in\mathbb R^k\).

If \(\nu_e\) is a norm, then \(w_e>0\). The scalar is unique because
\(k\ge1\) and \(w_e=\nu_e(e_1)\).

\textbf{Proof:}

Let

\[
x=(x_1,\ldots,x_k)\in\mathbb R^k.
\]

Write \(e_i\) for the \(i\)-th standard coordinate vector. Then

\[
x=\sum_{i=1}^k x_i e_i.
\]

The vectors \(x_i e_i\) have pairwise disjoint coordinate support.
Therefore, by disjoint-coordinate additivity,

\[
\nu_e(x) = \nu_e\left(\sum_{i=1}^k x_i e_i\right) = \sum_{i=1}^k \nu_e(x_i e_i).
\]

Since \(\nu_e\) is a seminorm, it is absolutely homogeneous:

\[
\nu_e(x_i e_i)=|x_i|\nu_e(e_i).
\]

By coordinate permutation symmetry, all coordinate basis vectors have
the same seminorm value:

\[
\nu_e(e_1)=\nu_e(e_2)=\cdots=\nu_e(e_k).
\]

Set

\[
w_e:=\nu_e(e_1).
\]

Then

\[
\nu_e(x) = \sum_{i=1}^k |x_i|w_e = w_e\sum_{i=1}^k |x_i| = w_e\|x\|_1.
\]

The scalar is nonnegative since \(\nu_e(e_1)\ge0\), and positive under
normhood since \(e_1\ne0\). Thus each edge seminorm is coordinatewise
\(\ell^1\), up to its unique edge weight. \(\square\)

\hypertarget{proven-conditional-theorem-2-orbit-weighted-global-aggregation}{%
\subsubsection{\texorpdfstring{2.3 \textbf{{[}PROVEN, CONDITIONAL{]}
Theorem 2 --- Orbit-Weighted Global
Aggregation}}{2.3 {[}PROVEN, CONDITIONAL{]} Theorem 2 --- Orbit-Weighted Global Aggregation}}\label{proven-conditional-theorem-2-orbit-weighted-global-aggregation}}

\textbf{Statement:}

Let the automorphism group of the graph, or the declared admissible
graph-isomorphism action, partition the edge set \(E\) into edge orbits

\[
E=\bigsqcup_{\mathcal O} \mathcal O.
\]

Then there exist weights \(w_{\mathcal O}\ge0\), constant on edge
orbits, such that

\[
\nu_G(\delta) = \sum_{\mathcal O} w_{\mathcal O} \sum_{e\in\mathcal O} \|\delta_e\|_1.
\]

Orbit weights may be zero for seminorms. If the global diagnostic is
required to be faithful on each edge orbit, the corresponding orbit
weights are positive.

\textbf{Proof:}

By Theorem 1, for each edge \(e\),

\[
\nu_e(\delta_e)=w_e\|\delta_e\|_1.
\]

By edge locality,

\[
\nu_G(\delta) = \sum_{e\in E}\nu_e(\delta_e) = \sum_{e\in E}w_e\|\delta_e\|_1.
\]

Now let \(e,e'\in E\) lie in the same edge orbit. Then there exists a
graph automorphism \(\phi:G\to G\) such that

\[
\phi(e)=e'.
\]

Take a cochain \(\delta\) supported only on edge \(e\), with value
\(u\in\mathbb R^k\):

\[
\delta_e=u,\qquad \delta_f=0\quad(f\ne e).
\]

Then

\[
\nu_G(\delta)=w_e\|u\|_1.
\]

The pushed-forward cochain \(\phi_*\delta\) is supported only on \(e'\),
with the same coordinate value \(u\) up to the declared coordinate
action --- which, being a (signed) coordinate permutation by Axiom 4,
preserves \(\|u\|_1\). By graph invariance,

\[
\nu_G(\phi_*\delta)=\nu_G(\delta).
\]

Thus

\[
w_{e'}\|u\|_1=w_e\|u\|_1.
\]

Choose \(u\ne0\). Then \(\|u\|_1>0\), so

\[
w_{e'}=w_e.
\]

Therefore edge weights are constant on edge orbits. Write the common
value on orbit \(\mathcal O\) as \(w_{\mathcal O}\). Hence

\[
\nu_G(\delta) = \sum_{\mathcal O} w_{\mathcal O} \sum_{e\in\mathcal O} \|\delta_e\|_1.
\]

\(\square\)

\hypertarget{proven-conditional-edge-transitivity-or-explicit-uniformity-theorem-3-edge-uniformity-reduces-to-unweighted-global-ell1}{%
\subsubsection{\texorpdfstring{2.4 \textbf{{[}PROVEN, CONDITIONAL +
edge-transitivity or explicit uniformity{]} Theorem 3 ---
Edge-Uniformity Reduces to Unweighted Global
\(\ell^1\)}}{2.4 {[}PROVEN, CONDITIONAL + edge-transitivity or explicit uniformity{]} Theorem 3 --- Edge-Uniformity Reduces to Unweighted Global \textbackslash ell\^{}1}}\label{proven-conditional-edge-transitivity-or-explicit-uniformity-theorem-3-edge-uniformity-reduces-to-unweighted-global-ell1}}

\textbf{Statement:}

If the graph action is edge-transitive, or if one imposes an explicit
edge-uniformity/naturality axiom forcing all edge weights to agree, then
there exists a single scalar \(\alpha\ge0\) such that

\[
\nu_G(\delta) = \alpha\sum_{e\in E}\|\delta_e\|_1.
\]

If \(\alpha=1\), this is exactly the standard unweighted \(\ell^1\) norm
on

\[
E\times\{1,\ldots,k\}.
\]

\textbf{Proof:}

From Theorem 2,

\[
\nu_G(\delta) = \sum_{\mathcal O} w_{\mathcal O} \sum_{e\in\mathcal O} \|\delta_e\|_1.
\]

If the graph action is edge-transitive, then \(E\) consists of a single
orbit:

\[
E=\mathcal O.
\]

Therefore

\[
\nu_G(\delta)=w_{\mathcal O}\sum_{e\in E}\|\delta_e\|_1.
\]

Set

\[
\alpha:=w_{\mathcal O}.
\]

Then

\[
\nu_G(\delta)=\alpha\sum_{e\in E}\|\delta_e\|_1.
\]

Alternatively, if there are several edge orbits but an explicit
edge-uniformity axiom imposes

\[
w_{\mathcal O}=\alpha
\]

for every orbit \(\mathcal O\), then

\[
\nu_G(\delta) = \sum_{\mathcal O} \alpha \sum_{e\in\mathcal O} \|\delta_e\|_1 = \alpha \sum_{e\in E} \|\delta_e\|_1.
\]

Finally,

\[
\sum_{e\in E}\|\delta_e\|_1 = \sum_{e\in E}\sum_{i=1}^k |\delta_{e,i}| = \|\delta\|_{\ell^1(E\times\{1,\ldots,k\})}.
\]

Thus the edge-uniform case is the standard finite \(\ell^1\) norm, up to
scalar normalization. \(\square\)

\hypertarget{counterexamples}{%
\subsubsection{2.5 Counterexamples}\label{counterexamples}}

CE1--CE3 witness the roles of H3, H2, and H1, respectively; CE4
witnesses the extra edge-uniformity assumption, which is not one of
H1--H5. CE1 and CE2 use \(k\ge2\). For CE2 and CE3, take trivial fiber
transport so that H4 does not impose an additional coordinate action.
CE5 must weaken the standing seminorm premise as well as H5: homogeneity
cannot be removed while still calling the map a seminorm. Its full

The Lean CE1 and CE3 declarations are narrower arithmetic models: a

\hypertarget{proven-by-counterexample-counterexample-1-if-coordinate-additivity-is-removed-ell2-is-allowed}{%
\paragraph{\texorpdfstring{\textbf{{[}PROVEN BY COUNTEREXAMPLE{]}
Counterexample 1 --- If Coordinate Additivity Is Removed, \(\ell^2\) Is
Allowed}}{{[}PROVEN BY COUNTEREXAMPLE{]} Counterexample 1 --- If Coordinate Additivity Is Removed, \textbackslash ell\^{}2 Is Allowed}}\label{proven-by-counterexample-counterexample-1-if-coordinate-additivity-is-removed-ell2-is-allowed}}

Define, on each edge,

\[
\nu_e(x)=\|x\|_2 = \left(\sum_{i=1}^k x_i^2\right)^{1/2}.
\]

Then \(\nu_e\) is a norm. It is coordinate permutation symmetric:

\[
\|(x_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,x_{\sigma(k)})\|_2=\|x\|_2.
\]

It is absolutely homogeneous and subadditive.

But for disjointly supported vectors \(x=(1,0)\) and \(y=(0,1)\),

\[
\nu_e(x+y)=\|(1,1)\|_2=\sqrt2,
\]

while

\[
\nu_e(x)+\nu_e(y)=1+1=2.
\]

So

\[
\nu_e(x+y)\ne\nu_e(x)+\nu_e(y).
\]

Thus without coordinate additivity, the conclusion \(\nu_e=w_e\|x\|_1\)
fails.

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Coordinate symmetry alone does not force \(\ell^1\).}
\end{quote}

\hypertarget{proven-by-counterexample-counterexample-2-if-coordinate-symmetry-is-removed-weighted-coordinate-ell1-is-allowed}{%
\paragraph{\texorpdfstring{\textbf{{[}PROVEN BY COUNTEREXAMPLE{]}
Counterexample 2 --- If Coordinate Symmetry Is Removed, Weighted
Coordinate \(\ell^1\) Is
Allowed}}{{[}PROVEN BY COUNTEREXAMPLE{]} Counterexample 2 --- If Coordinate Symmetry Is Removed, Weighted Coordinate \textbackslash ell\^{}1 Is Allowed}}\label{proven-by-counterexample-counterexample-2-if-coordinate-symmetry-is-removed-weighted-coordinate-ell1-is-allowed}}

Define

\[
\nu_e(x)=\sum_{i=1}^k a_i |x_i|
\]

with positive but unequal weights \(a_i>0\). For example, in
\(\mathbb R^2\),

\[
\nu_e(x_1,x_2)=|x_1|+2|x_2|.
\]

This is a norm. It is additive on disjoint supports:

\[
\nu_e(x+y)=\nu_e(x)+\nu_e(y)
\]

whenever
\(\operatorname{supp}(x)\cap\operatorname{supp}(y)=\varnothing\).

But it is not coordinate permutation symmetric. Indeed,

\[
\nu_e(1,0)=1,
\]

while

\[
\nu_e(0,1)=2.
\]

Therefore the conclusion becomes coordinate-weighted \(\ell^1\), not
scalar coordinatewise \(\ell^1\).

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Without coordinate symmetry, additivity gives weighted
\(\ell^1\), not uniform coordinate \(\ell^1\).}
\end{quote}

\hypertarget{proven-by-counterexample-counterexample-3-if-edge-locality-is-removed-cross-edge-coupling-is-allowed}{%
\paragraph{\texorpdfstring{\textbf{{[}PROVEN BY COUNTEREXAMPLE{]}
Counterexample 3 --- If Edge Locality Is Removed, Cross-Edge Coupling Is
Allowed}}{{[}PROVEN BY COUNTEREXAMPLE{]} Counterexample 3 --- If Edge Locality Is Removed, Cross-Edge Coupling Is Allowed}}\label{proven-by-counterexample-counterexample-3-if-edge-locality-is-removed-cross-edge-coupling-is-allowed}}

Let \(G\) have two edges \(e_1,e_2\), and take \(k=1\). A cochain is

\[
\delta=(a,b)\in\mathbb R^2.
\]

Define

\[
\nu_G(\delta)=|a+b|.
\]

This is a seminorm on \(C^1(G,\mathbb R)\).

It is not edge-local because it cannot be written as

\[
\nu_G(\delta)=\nu_{e_1}(a)+\nu_{e_2}(b)
\]

for edgewise seminorms \(\nu_{e_1},\nu_{e_2}\).

To see this, take \((a,b)=(1,0)\) and \((0,-1)\). If edge locality held,
then

\[
\nu_G(1,0)=\nu_{e_1}(1)+\nu_{e_2}(0)=1,
\]

and

\[
\nu_G(0,-1)=\nu_{e_1}(0)+\nu_{e_2}(-1)=1.
\]

So locality would imply

\[
\nu_G(1,-1)=2.
\]

But actually

\[
\nu_G(1,-1)=|1-1|=0.
\]

Contradiction.

Thus without edge locality, cancellation between edges is possible. The
global seminorm need not be an edge-sum of \(\ell^1\) terms.

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Without edge locality, cross-edge cancellation destroys edgewise
\(\ell^1\) aggregation.}
\end{quote}

\hypertarget{proven-by-counterexample-counterexample-4-if-edge-uniformity-is-removed-orbit-weighted-ell1-need-not-reduce-to-unweighted-ell1}{%
\paragraph{\texorpdfstring{\textbf{{[}PROVEN BY COUNTEREXAMPLE{]}
Counterexample 4 --- If Edge-Uniformity Is Removed, Orbit-Weighted
\(\ell^1\) Need Not Reduce to Unweighted
\(\ell^1\)}}{{[}PROVEN BY COUNTEREXAMPLE{]} Counterexample 4 --- If Edge-Uniformity Is Removed, Orbit-Weighted \textbackslash ell\^{}1 Need Not Reduce to Unweighted \textbackslash ell\^{}1}}\label{proven-by-counterexample-counterexample-4-if-edge-uniformity-is-removed-orbit-weighted-ell1-need-not-reduce-to-unweighted-ell1}}

Let \(G\) be a directed path with two edges \(e_1,e_2\), and suppose the
admissible graph automorphism group fixes the two edges separately, so
they lie in distinct edge orbits.

Define, for \(k=1\),

\[
\nu_G(\delta)=|\delta_{e_1}|+2|\delta_{e_2}|.
\]

This satisfies edge locality. It also satisfies coordinate symmetry
vacuously since \(k=1\), and disjoint-coordinate additivity is vacuous
on each scalar edge.

It is graph-invariant under the trivial automorphism group.

But it is not proportional to the unweighted \(\ell^1\) norm

\[
|\delta_{e_1}|+|\delta_{e_2}|.
\]

Indeed,

\[
\nu_G(1,0)=1,
\]

while

\[
\nu_G(0,1)=2.
\]

No single scalar \(\alpha\) satisfies both

\[
\alpha=\nu_G(1,0)=1
\]

and

\[
\alpha=\nu_G(0,1)=2.
\]

Thus the best general theorem is orbit-weighted \(\ell^1\), not globally
unweighted \(\ell^1\).

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Graph invariance alone gives orbit weights; edge-uniformity is
an extra axiom.}
\end{quote}

\hypertarget{open-not_verified-counterexample-5-a-nonlinear-gauge-candidate-after-weakening-the-seminorm-premise}{%
\paragraph{\texorpdfstring{\textbf{{[}OPEN / NOT\_VERIFIED{]}
Counterexample 5 --- A Nonlinear Gauge Candidate after Weakening the
Seminorm
Premise}}{{[}OPEN / NOT\_VERIFIED{]} Counterexample 5 --- A Nonlinear Gauge Candidate after Weakening the Seminorm Premise}}\label{open-not_verified-counterexample-5-a-nonlinear-gauge-candidate-after-weakening-the-seminorm-premise}}

Replace the seminorm premise by a nonnegative, zero-at-zero, subadditive
gauge, and retain coordinate symmetry and disjoint-coordinate
additivity. The candidate on \(\mathbb R^k\) is

\[
\Phi(x)=\sum_{i=1}^k |x_i|^p
\]

for \(0<p<1\). This range makes \(0^p=0\) and permits subadditivity. The
broader earlier range \(p\ne1\) was insufficient: \(p\le0\) causes a
zero-coordinate problem, while \(p>1\) loses subadditivity as well as
homogeneity.

This is coordinate permutation symmetric and additive over disjoint
coordinate support:

\[
\Phi(x+y)=\Phi(x)+\Phi(y)
\]

when supports are disjoint.

But

\[
\Phi(cx)=|c|^p\Phi(x),
\]

not

\[
|c|\Phi(x).
\]

For \(0<p<1\), the scalar inequality \(|a+b|^p\le |a|^p+|b|^p\) gives
subadditivity after summing coordinates. Nevertheless the scaling above
fails H5 for a nonzero vector and, for example, \(c=2\). At \(p=1\) the
family becomes the \(\ell^1\) norm. The shipped Lean helper fixes
\(p=2\) and checks only a natural-number scaling inequality; that helper
also lies outside the subadditive candidate range.

The shipped Python and Lean helpers verify narrower, fixed arithmetic
witnesses of non-homogeneity. They do not establish the full parameter
range and every hypothesis asserted by this prose construction.
Therefore CE5 remains
\texttt{OPEN /\allowbreak{} NOT\_\allowbreak{}VERIFIED}; the equations
above state the intended candidate, not a promoted paper result.

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Disjoint additivity plus symmetry without homogeneity does not
force \(\ell^1\) scaling.}
\end{quote}

\hypertarget{structural-consequences}{%
\subsection{3. Structural Consequences}\label{structural-consequences}}

\hypertarget{coarea-duality-k-1}{%
\subsubsection{\texorpdfstring{3.1 Coarea Duality
(\(k = 1\))}{3.1 Coarea Duality (k = 1)}}\label{coarea-duality-k-1}}

\textbf{{[}STANDARD + elementary finite proof{]} Fact 3.1 (Weighted
Discrete Coarea).} For a scalar vertex signal \(v:V\to\mathbb R\) and
fixed weights \(w_e\ge0\), set

\[
\operatorname{TV}_w(v)=\sum_{e=(m,n)\in E}w_e|v_n-v_m|,
\qquad V_t=\{m:v_m>t\}.
\]

Then

\[
\operatorname{TV}_w(v)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}
\sum_{e=(m,n)\in E}w_e
|\mathbf1_{V_t}(n)-\mathbf1_{V_t}(m)|\,dt.
\]

For each edge the indicator difference has absolute value one on the
interval between its two endpoint values and zero elsewhere; its
integral is therefore \(|v_n-v_m|\). Multiply by \(w_e\) and sum
finitely. At \(w_e=1\), the integrand counts boundary-crossing edges.
This is the finite graph specialization of coarea background {[}2,3{]};
see also the explicit graph proof in {[}10, Appendix B{]}.

\textbf{Orientation convention.} Each declared edge is counted once
regardless of its orientation. Opposite directed edges, if both are
listed, both contribute. This absolute-value TV yields the cut of the
underlying undirected weighted multigraph, not a one-way directed-cut
objective.

\textbf{{[}STANDARD + finite threshold argument{]} Cut specialization.}
For distinct vertices \(a,b\), minimize \(\operatorname{TV}_w(v)\) with
\(v_a=1\), \(v_b=0\). Clipping to \([0,1]\) cannot increase any edge
difference. Each threshold \(0<t<1\) gives a cut containing \(a\) and
excluding \(b\), so coarea bounds the minimum below by the minimum such
cut capacity. Conversely, the indicator of a minimum cut is feasible and
has TV equal to that capacity. Thus a binary minimizer exists; the
boundary values alone do not force every minimizer to be binary.
Max-flow/min-cut {[}5{]} applies to the undirected capacitated network,
or its replacement by two opposite equal-capacity arcs for each edge.

\textbf{{[}PROVEN, CONDITIONAL{]} Remark 3.2.} For \(k>1\), the weighted
diagnostic of a vertex coboundary is
\(\sum_{i=1}^k\operatorname{TV}_w(v_i)\), so coarea applies coordinate
by coordinate. This specialization concerns coboundaries of vertex
signals; an arbitrary edge cochain need not be a coboundary.

\hypertarget{the-geometry-of-the-defect-field}{%
\subsubsection{3.2 The Geometry of the Defect
Field}\label{the-geometry-of-the-defect-field}}

The explicit inclusion of \textbf{Coordinate Additivity} formally
captures the declared independence of distinct observed coordinates.
Permutation symmetry and absolute homogeneity alone permit alternative
norms such as \(\ell^\infty\) or \(\ell^2\). The strict structural
requirement that isolated defect channels accumulate their structural
signatures linearly thereby determines the \(\ell^1\) stalk geometry
under these constraints.

Thus, the seminorm compatible with the stated local
coordinate-additivity and coordinate-symmetry constraints is
coordinatewise \(\ell^1\) on each edge. Uniform \(\ell^1\) over the
product index set requires the additional edge-uniformity assumption
stated in Theorem 3.

Conceptually, our theorem establishes (cf.~{[}4{]}) that:
\textbf{{[}PROVEN, CONDITIONAL + uniform weights + \(\alpha>0\){]}} As a
normed diagnostic space under the selected uniform \(\ell^1\) diagnostic
with \(\alpha>0\), \(C^1(G,\mathbb R^k)\) is isometric to the finite
\(L^1\) space on \(E\times\{1,\dots,k\}\). The strict positivity is
load-bearing: on a nonzero carrier, \(\alpha=0\) gives an identically
zero seminorm, not a norm. Its separated quotient is the zero space. For
\(\alpha>0\), the map \(\delta\mapsto\alpha\delta\) is an isometry to
the standard counting-measure \(L^1\) space; equivalently, the identity
map uses mass \(\alpha\) at each point. This identification is a
\textbf{measure-theoretic import} used for identification only; no
measure, proportion, or probability semantics is derived here (see the
Import / Oracle boundary). More generally the result is a finite
weighted \(L^1\) geometry. \textbf{{[}INTERPRETIVE / STANDARD BACKGROUND
+ citation{]}} This serves as a finite combinatorial analogue of
Kakutani's (1941) concrete characterization of abstract \(L\)-spaces
{[}1{]}. Local additivity across independent structural components
selects \(\ell^1\)-type geometry over the relevant finite measure.
Defect fields in the uniform scalar case behave as discrete 1-forms
whose absolute total variation tracks graph cuts and sparse obstructions
{[}3{]}.

\hypertarget{role-in-the-broader-classification-program}{%
\subsubsection{3.3 Role in the Broader Classification
Program}\label{role-in-the-broader-classification-program}}

The result obtained here isolates the combinatorial origin of
\(\ell^1\)-type geometry as a consequence of coordinate-wise and
edge-wise separability. In subsequent work, this principle is
generalized. In particular, when the cochain spaces are replaced by
Banach presheaves and coordinate additivity is replaced by structural
compatibility in the form of functoriality under bounded linear maps
{[}7{]}, the same weighted \(\ell^1\) edge-sum structure is recovered,
with uniformity requiring additional symmetry or normalization.

The present theorem should not be interpreted as asserting universality
of \(\ell^1\) across all observers. Rather, it identifies the unique
geometry compatible with coordinate-wise independence and
replica-extensive aggregation. In the general M-compatible
classification, relaxing coordinate additivity admits alternative
geometries such as \(\ell^2\) and mixed norms. The present theorem
therefore provides the minimal combinatorial base case from which the
more general Banach {[}7{]} and dynamical {[}6{]} results can be
understood as specific structural extensions.

A compressed form of the result is the following. If a diagnostic
\(\Phi\) is coordinate-separable, refinement-invariant, homogeneous, and
additive across independent coordinates, then it has the form

\[
\Phi(x)=\sum_i \psi(x_i)
\]

with one \textbf{even} scalar branch \(\psi\), and with either (a)
\(\psi\) absolutely homogeneous, or (b) \(\psi\) additive and regular
\textbf{on each sign half-line}. Hence \(\psi(t)=C|t|\) with \(C\ge0\),
and

\[
\Phi(x)=C\sum_i |x_i|.
\]

\textbf{The evenness / sign-reflection hypothesis is load-bearing and
was omitted in earlier revisions of this section.} Without it the
compressed statement is false, in both directions: additivity of a
\emph{nonnegative} \(\psi\) across all of \(\mathbb R\) forces
\(\psi(t)+\psi(-t)=\psi(0)=0\) with both terms \(\ge0\), hence
\(\psi\equiv0\); while regular additivity on each half-line without sign
reflection admits distinct positive and negative slopes, which is not
\(C|t|\). The corrected conditional form above is the one recorded in
the encyclopedia at ENC-0526.

Thus \(\ell^1\) is selected by coordinate separability, additive
aggregation, an even branch, and regularity. Relaxing any of these
assumptions admits alternative geometries. This compressed statement
should be read as a heuristic summary of the finite theorem unless the
terms `refinement-invariant' and `regular' are formally defined here.

\hypertarget{quotient-boundaries-and-the-open-not_verified-lggr-interpretation}{%
\subsubsection{\texorpdfstring{3.5 Quotient Boundaries and the
\textbf{{[}OPEN / NOT\_VERIFIED{]}} LGGR
Interpretation}{3.5 Quotient Boundaries and the {[}OPEN / NOT\_VERIFIED{]} LGGR Interpretation}}\label{quotient-boundaries-and-the-open-not_verified-lggr-interpretation}}

Three distinct comparisons must be kept separate. The elementary
factorization facts below correct the earlier fixed-profile non-descent
assertion; they do not claim the broader cross-graph LGGR specialization
is mechanized.

\textbf{Fixed graph and fixed weights.} Define
\(D_w(\delta)=(w_e\|\delta_e\|_1)_e\) and \(\delta\sim_w\delta'\) iff
\(D_w(\delta)=D_w(\delta')\). Then

\[
\nu_G(\delta)=\sum_e D_w(\delta)_e.
\]

Thus \(\nu_G\) factors through \(D_w\), and equal per-edge diagnostics
force equal global totals for every fixed profile, including nonuniform
and zero weights. The pairing \(\delta\mapsto(D_w(\delta),w)\) also
factors through this relation because \(w\) is constant on the carrier.
The same conclusion holds when the fixed-profile relation records the
unweighted per-edge values instead.

\textbf{Coarser total-only relation.} Let
\(U(\delta)=\sum_e\|\delta_e\|_1\) and identify cochains only when
\(U(\delta)=U(\delta')\). For fixed weights \((1,2)\), edge magnitude
profiles \((7,3)\) and \((3,7)\) both have \(U=10\), but weighted totals
\(13\) and \(17\). This witnesses failure of factorization through
\(U\), not through the per-edge relation. Uniformity makes
\(\nu_G=\alpha U\); if \(E\ne\varnothing\) and \(k\ge1\), testing each
unit-edge cochain shows that a representation \(\nu_G=f\circ U\)
requires all edge weights to agree.

\textbf{Varying-profile record carrier.} On records \((\delta,w)\),
suppose the relation records only the unweighted per-edge values and
forgets \(w\). The same cochain with magnitudes \((7,3)\) and profiles
\((1,2)\) or \((2,2)\) gives totals \(13\) or \(20\). A weight-sensitive
total or pairing therefore need not descend. A fixed declared profile
repairs that loss; only requiring each record's profile to be uniform
does not, unless the scalar is shared across the comparison. The shipped
finite \texttt{Nat} record helpers permit weights to vary in this way.
They are not a theorem about two real cochains under one fixed
diagnostic.

\textbf{Programme boundary.} Identifying a specified cross-graph
comparison with LGGR {[}8,9{]} remains
\texttt{OPEN /\allowbreak{} NOT\_\allowbreak{}VERIFIED}. It requires an
explicit real carrier, relation, graph transport, and
claim-to-formalization map. Nonuniform weights alone do not create a
failure of descent through per-edge diagnostics.

\subsection{Appendix: Zenodo

This preprint shows in this model a finite combinatorial classification
theorem for coordinate-valued graph cochain diagnostics. Under edge
locality, coordinate permutation symmetry, disjoint-coordinate
additivity, and seminorm homogeneity, each edge-local seminorm is a
nonnegative scalar multiple of coordinatewise \(\ell^1\). Graph
isomorphism invariance gives orbit-weighted \(\ell^1\); a single
unweighted global \(\ell^1\) diagnostic requires edge-transitivity or
explicit edge-uniformity. The result is conditional on a
coordinate-separable observer class and does not claim finite-structural
\(\ell^1\) geometry.

This paper supplies the finite combinatorial base case for later
obstruction-calculus papers on Banach presheaf coboundaries,
M-compatible gauge classification, and projection-induced defects in
factorized-state dynamics.

supports; permutation symmetry; edge locality; edge orbits; edge
