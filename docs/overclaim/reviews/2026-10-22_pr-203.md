## Physics framing

The PR finds that four Wilson-slab determinants are gauge invariant (full D, interior C, boundary Schur S, fixed-basis chiral block K) and that a lifted cup-product CS term changes by 1/32. This is **expected and carries no information about inflow**:

1. A background U(1) gauge change acts on any gauge-covariant lattice Dirac matrix by a diagonal similarity, `D' = G^-1 D G`. Every block with fixed index sets that carry the same site charges is again a similarity, so its determinant is unchanged. `chiral_fixed_basis_det` (lean4/CampaignFspLatticeAnomalyInflow.lean:75) has exactly this as its hypothesis `hsite : ∀ i, site (row i) = site (col i)`: the invariance is built into the choice of bases. A fixed-basis truncation of a vector-like Wilson regulator cannot register a chiral gauge anomaly.
2. In the constructions where the lattice anomaly and its inflow appear, either the chiral determinant uses a gauge-field-dependent projector and basis, and the anomaly is the gauge variation of that measure (Lüscher, Nucl. Phys. B 549 (1999) 295, Ginsparg-Wilson/overlap), or the total domain-wall determinant is gauge invariant and inflow is the Chern-Simons current of the heavy bulk modes flowing onto the wall (Kaplan, Phys. Lett. B 288 (1992) 342; Jansen, Phys. Lett. B 288 (1992) 348; Golterman, Jansen and Kaplan, Phys. Lett. B 301 (1993) 219, the 2+1-dimensional case; continuum: Callan and Harvey, Nucl. Phys. B 250 (1985) 427). Zero gauge variation of the total finite determinant is what those papers also have. It is not a failure of inflow.
3. The CS candidate is specified, not induced by the fermions. `cs_action` (checker) and `slabCS` (Lean:173) depend only on the link lifts, not on the mass profile m(z) or on D. So an invariant determinant times a non-invariant phase gives a non-invariant product. That is a tautology, not a test of cancellation. The integer-lift witness (same U(1) links, change 1/8) also shows the candidate is not a function of U(1) links. The PR says this itself.
4. The geometry cannot host separated chiral modes in any case: heights 3 and 4, and the mass jump sits between the two end faces used as "boundary". The PR does not claim isolated zero modes.

Verdict: the honest framing is "the tested regulator and CS candidate are the wrong pair for inflow (a known effect)". It is not "a new exact obstruction". The PR title, note title, node statement, LOG line and table used "obstruction". They are corrected below. The PR's mathematics and Lean statements are correct as stated, and none of them was changed.

Statement as it now stands:

> Target: on a finite slab lattice the chiral variation of the boundary fermion determinant cancels the bulk Chern-Simons variation. Not established in this checkout. One tested candidate does not realize it, as expected: on open Wilson slabs with U(1) links, the gauge change acts on the Wilson matrix by a diagonal similarity, so the full, interior, boundary Schur and fixed site-matched chiral-block determinants are gauge invariant by construction, while a lifted cup-product CS term whose coefficient is not derived from the fermions changes by 1/32. This is a fixed-basis truncation of a vector-like regulator, not an obstruction to lattice inflow.

Bottleneck:

> Construct a regulated chiral boundary determinant whose projector, basis or measure depends on the gauge field (domain-wall or overlap/Ginsparg-Wilson type), derive from the same fermions the bulk Chern-Simons term they induce, and show that the two gauge variations cancel. The fixed-basis Wilson-slab test cannot register an anomaly, because site-matched fixed bases give similarity-invariant determinants; its bare lifted CS candidate is neither induced by the fermions nor well defined on U(1) links (an integer lift of the same links changes it by 1/8). The continuum Callan-Harvey mechanism remains a literature import.
