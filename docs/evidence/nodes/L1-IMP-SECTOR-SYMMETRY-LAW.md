# L1-IMP-SECTOR-SYMMETRY-LAW

Stage: [L1_TO_L2](../stages/L1_TO_L2.md). Status: `IMPORTED_ASSUMPTION`. Evidence grade: **E3**. [Evidence map overview](../README.md). AuthorityEffect: None.

## Statement

> Transitive sector symmetry of the quadratic readout: relabelling sectors by any permutation in a set whose generated group acts transitively does not change the readout. This is the second law under which the inner product is selected up to scale; it is supplied, not derived from primitive difference, and sectors with distinguished labels would break it (then the diagonal is constant only on orbits).

## Bottleneck

No finite-theory primitive forces readout invariance under sector relabelling; H006 supplies the permutation envelope only. Deriving the law, or recording which sector labels are distinguished, is item 1 of the run next steps. The campaign module L1SectorSymmetry does not weaken the law: it takes invariance under a permutation set S as a hypothesis and shows that a symmetric, sector-additive readout has a diagonal that is constant on each orbit of the group generated by S (diag_const_on_orbit), with diag(1,2) on two sectors and diag(1,1,2) on three sectors as countermodels to a scalar form when S is not transitive. Transitivity of S is still the supplied law.

## Source status

Supplied law of the quadratic readout in dag/results/L1-IMP-HILBERT-INNER-PRODUCT/runs/20261021T013500Z_sector_form: the readout is invariant under a set of sector permutations whose generated group acts transitively on sectors (SectorFormUniqueness.Invariant, TransitiveOn, Generated; invariance is closed under products and inverses, invariant_of_generated). The finite theory supplies the permutation isometry envelope (PermutationUnitary.permutation_isometry), not the statement that the readout must be invariant under it.
