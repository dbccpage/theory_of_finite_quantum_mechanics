## Picking it apart (primitive difference)

1. What is witnessed in the paper: one numerical run. A declared input, a declared target, an output whose density in a rectangle changes by 1.3% to 21%. That is a difference between two arrays. Everything else is declared.
2. The capacity number is 4 n d / λ³ times an area. Every factor is supplied. The result is as large as the choices make it. The comparison with 30 TB compares one assumption with another.
3. Her claim borrows the paper's word ("holographic") but not its content. The paper says nothing about rendering or about reality waiting to be seen. The rendering claim is hers alone.
4. The offscreen-rendering claim needs a definition of what counts as on screen, a specified observer, and a test distinguishing the proposed rendering rule from ordinary physical predictions. The post supplies none of these. Appendix B supplies a proposed optical readout, but the post still needs evidence connecting that model to its claim that consciousness renders the universe.
5. Appendix B explicitly supplies a reference wave and a conditional reconstruction mechanism: an assumed interference pattern produces an object-wave term under super-radiant illumination. The remaining questions are whether the mechanism operates in brains and how reconstructed optical information relates to conscious experience.
6. Her "human observer vs measurement device" dismissal is the exact point. Saying "consciousness" does not settle which of these she means: physical interaction, a record, an accessible record, an experience, or an authorized inference. Those are different slots.

## One line

The paper supplies carriers and gauges for the brain and leaves the observer empty. Her post supplies an observer by name and leaves every other slot empty.
