# What is supplied: the post and the paper

The post (a public Threads reply, author not named here): "The simulation isn't a giant computer and holographic projectors, it's our consciousness. People getting hung up on whether a human observer or just a measurement device is required, but they're missing the point. It's just like a video game, the universe does not render what is offscreen."

Paper: Nishiyama, Tanaka, Tuszynski, Tsenkova, "Holographic Brain Theory: Super-Radiance, Memory Capacity and Control Theory", Int. J. Mol. Sci. 25(4):2399, 2024, doi:10.3390/ijms25042399. Read: abstract through part of the Discussion. Not read: Conclusions, Appendices A to C (the derivations), references.

## The six slots

| Slot | The post | The paper |
|---|---|---|
| Carrier | "consciousness". Named; no carrier is given. | Supplied: water conformational states modeled as charged bosons, water dipoles, photons, super-radiant light from water in microtubules. |
| Gauge | None. "Render" has no measure. | Supplied: bits (8e10 bit/mm2, 2.5 PB), wavelength 500 nm, cortex thickness 2 mm, field density on a 128x128 lattice. |
| Reference | "offscreen" presumes a screen and a viewpoint. Both supplied silently. | Supplied: flat spacetime, natural units, lattice with periodic edges, uniform starting density 1.0, "1 bit per synapse" baseline. |
| Comparison | "human observer or just a measurement device": the post names the comparison that matters, then sets it aside. | 2.5 PB vs 30 TB: a ratio of two declared assumptions, not a measurement. Output vs chosen target: "approaches the target", no error measure given. |
| Authority | "It's just like a video game", plus the link. PMC's own notice on the link preview: inclusion "does not imply endorsement". | The authors pick the targets and the parameters. The simulation agrees with the targets they picked. No experiment of theirs. |
| Observer | Consciousness is both the renderer and the viewer: the screen and the eye. Defined nowhere. | Not modeled. Discussion lists what a "quantum mind" would need (register, decipher, decide, encode) and says how it "could perceive the macro-world is yet another mystery altogether." |

## Picking it apart (primitive difference)

1. What is witnessed in the paper: one numerical run. A declared input, a declared target, an output whose density in a rectangle changes by 1.3% to 21%. That is a difference between two arrays. Everything else is declared.
2. The capacity number is 4 n d / λ³ times an area. Every factor is supplied. The result is as large as the choices make it. The comparison with 30 TB compares one assumption with another.
3. The post borrows the paper's word ("holographic") but not its content. The paper says nothing about rendering or about reality waiting to be seen. The rendering claim is the post's own.
4. "Does not render what is offscreen" needs: a screen (reference), a viewer (observer), a rule for what counts as on screen (gauge plus comparison), and someone who says the rule holds (authority). The post supplies none. The paper supplies none of these for the mind either. It says so.
5. A hologram is read only by shining a reference beam through it. The reference is a slot. The paper supplies a coherent source (super-radiance) for writing; the text read never says what supplies the reference for reading, or who looks at the result. (Check Appendix B before saying this in public as a fact about the paper; as a fact about holography it is safe.)
6. The "human observer vs measurement device" question the post sets aside is the exact point. Saying "consciousness" does not settle which of these is meant: physical interaction, a record, an accessible record, an experience, or an authorized inference. Those are different slots.

## Purpose

This is a worked example of checking what a claim supplies before arguing with it. The aim is rigour that a reader can repeat, not a verdict on the author.

## One line

The paper supplies carriers and gauges for the brain and leaves the observer empty. The post supplies an observer by name and leaves every other slot empty.
