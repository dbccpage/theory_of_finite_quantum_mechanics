Board 1 · finite

What did we actually witness?

    The logic

    1. Two records exist. Under a declared way of reading them (lines, lengths, directions), they differ. witnessed
    2. "The same tree fell between them" adds identity across the records, an order in time, and a transition. None of these is in either record. supplied
    3. "It made a sound" adds a model of air, and "someone heard it" adds a receiver and a record of hearing. supplied open
    4. Many different histories end in the second record. So a witnessed difference does not fix one history. derived

    What would change this

    A third record, or a record that carries order (a timestamp, tree rings), adds witnessed structure. Every claim should say which record carries it.

    What does "the universe does not render what is offscreen" claim?

    ReadingWhat it saysTestable?
    (i) stops existingUnobserved things are not there.Only if it predicts something different when observation resumes.
    (ii) stops evolvingUnobserved things are frozen.Yes, in principle: a frozen system would lag behind one that kept running. It depends on the dynamics claimed.
    (iii) is not experiencedNobody perceives what nobody perceives.True of everything, and needs no simulation.
    1. (i) implies (iii): what does not exist is not experienced. derived
    2. (ii) implies neither of the others. A paused, saved game chunk stops evolving and keeps its state. A frozen frame stops evolving and can still be seen. derived
    3. So these are three different claims, not three strengths of one claim. The post has to say which it means.

    Same screen, different worlds

    viewport
    channel

    1. A. This observer cannot tell the two worlds apart with this channel.
    2. B. No possible observation can tell them apart.
    3. C. The two worlds are physically the same.
    4. A does not give B: widen the channel and the difference can show. B does not give C. And failing to show C does not show that they differ. derived

    What would change this

    A named channel on which "rendered" and "not rendered" give different records. Then the claim becomes a physics claim and can be tested.

    Board 3 · finite

    If two worlds give the same report, are the theories equally good?

    1. If states a and b give the same report, any judge that sees only the report gives them the same verdict. (Lean: presentation_blind_spot.) derived
    2. A theory is not one state. It assigns odds to states, and so to reports. Theories are compared by the odds they give the data that was recorded: the Bayes factor.
    3. Equal reports for two states do not make two theories' odds equal. Try it below. derived
    1. The Bayes factor is 1 only when both theories give the same distribution of reports, and only when that distribution is nonzero for what was seen. derived
    2. For a run of reports, multiplying single-report odds needs independence. One fair coin flipped twice and one flip copied twice agree on each report, yet give "TT" odds of ¼ and ½: a Bayes factor of 2. derived

    What would change this

    A rendering theory that states its odds for some record, where ordinary physics gives different odds. Without that, its Bayes factor is undefined, not 1.

    Board 5 · the paper, continuum

    Where does the paper's own mathematics fail?

    Appendix A: the gauge freezes the charge it needs to move

    1. The paper sets $A^0=0$ and $\nabla\cdot\mathbf A=0$. Then $\partial^\nu F_{\nu0}=\partial^i(\partial_iA_0-\partial_0A_i)=0$. derived
    2. Its own (A7), $\partial^\nu F_{\nu 0}=J_0+c(\mathbf x)$, then forces $J_0=-e|\varphi|^2$ to stay fixed in time. derived
    3. The super-radiant transition current has $\nabla\cdot(\phi_0\nabla\phi_1-\phi_1\nabla\phi_0)=-2m(E_1-E_0)\,\phi_0\phi_1\neq0$, so by continuity the density must oscillate. The populations also move during the pulse. derived
    4. So, as written, Appendix A is consistent only for stationary states, and its radiating solution violates its own constraint. derived

    Appendix C: the simulation does not keep Gauss's law

    1. From the paper's (A82)–(A86): $\partial_t(\nabla\cdot\mathbf E-J^0)=-M^2\nabla\cdot\mathbf A-\gamma\,\partial_t\nabla\cdot\mathbf A+v\,\nabla\cdot\mathbf A^{(n\pm1)}$. The Maxwell part cancels exactly; the added terms do not. derived
    2. The targets (10)–(11) are curl-free, so $\nabla\cdot\mathbf A\neq0$. The hologram is written in exactly the part that breaks. derived
    3. A rerun of the paper's simulation at its parameters reproduces its density dips. Along that run, the Gauss residual is as large as the charge change itself. numerical

    Retention: how long would a record last?

    1. For a record held by one energy barrier, keeping it for a time t at temperature T needs $\Delta E\approx k_BT\ln(t/\tau_0)$, where $\tau_0$ is an attempt time someone must supply. supplied
    2. The paper gives no barrier and no $\tau_0$. A collective record could have a much larger barrier. open
    1. Landauer's $k_BT\ln2$ (18.5 meV at 310 K) bounds the cost of irreversibly discarding a bit. It says nothing about how long a record lasts. derived

    What would change this

    A transverse-only version of Appendix C that keeps Gauss's law and still forms the hologram, plus a stated barrier and attempt time.

    Two questions for the authors

    Does $\nabla\cdot\mathbf E=J^0+e$ hold along your Figure 6 run? What energy barrier holds a record, with what attempt time?

    Board 6 · method

    How to take a paper apart

    1. Claim. Quote it exactly, with its equation or page.
    2. Break. Find the weakest step. Try a counterexample, a recomputation, or a rerun of the paper's own equations.
    3. Repair. Keep what survives and restate it narrowly enough to check.
    4. Retest. Run the repaired claim against the counterexample that broke it. A repair that only survives one exchange is not done.
    5. Residual obligation. List every assumption the repair added, as something still owed.
    Our own errors, found this wayRepair
    "Equal reports give a Bayes factor of 1"Only with equal report distributions, nonzero odds and, for sequences, independence (Board 3)
    "Every finite observer has unreported differences"Only when states outnumber reports
    "Games keep simulating offscreen"Drawing, simulating and keeping state are three different jobs
    "The three readings are a chain"A paused, saved chunk stops evolving and keeps its state (Board 2)
    "One year needs 1.2 eV"Only for one barrier with τ₀ = 1 ps; 0.46 eV for τ₀ = 1 s (Board 5)
    "The Ward identity forbids longitudinal self-energy"Wrong: media screen charge. The condition is four-dimensional transversality

    The rule that matters most

    Agreement is not evidence. Several language models reviewed this work, and their agreement counts for nothing. A claim stands only on a quoted record, an exact check, a proof, or a rerun.
