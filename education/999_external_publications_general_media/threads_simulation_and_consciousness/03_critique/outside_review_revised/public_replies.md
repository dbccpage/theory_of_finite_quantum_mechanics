# Public replies

Four posts, each below 500 characters. The text in each blockquote is the complete post.

## 1. Define the observation

> Before we discuss what the universe does when nobody observes it, what counts as observing: a physical interaction, a recorded measurement, or conscious experience? And what changes without it? If your answer changes the physics, what measurement would reveal the difference?

## 2. Use the game analogy precisely

> A game can stop drawing an object while still updating it, or pause it while keeping its saved state. Those are different choices. Which one does your universe make? What happens when observation resumes, and which result differs from ordinary physics?

## 3. State what the paper does

> Your paper models light, water-related fields and a possible mechanism for holographic memory in a brain. It supplies a capacity estimate and a density-control simulation. It does not derive a universe that waits for consciousness to render it. Even if its memory mechanism works, the connection to your claim still needs a prediction and a test.

## 4. Ask for the distinguishing result

> If your simulation hypothesis assigns exactly the same likelihood to the complete experimental record as quantum mechanics, the Bayes factor is 1. Those data leave the prior odds unchanged. Name a different prediction, including correlations between measurements, and we have something to compare.
