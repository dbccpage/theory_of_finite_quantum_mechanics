## One example that distinguishes three claims

Let d0(t)=(t,0), d1(a,b)=b over Q. The chain equation holds, but the raw
repair class retains b. Closed classes all have b=0. The identity is
homotopic to zero through supplied maps H1(a,b)=a and H2(t)=(0,t), yet its
image of (0,1) has a different raw repair class. This separates a chain law,
an explicit homotopy and equality of closed classes. The new lab contains
the calculation, a malformed-filler counterexample and a retraction exercise.

## Balance a declared quantum composite

[Declared] Use one complex coordinate at each site. Each local real view
has two coordinates; the ordinary real tensor has four. The complex
composite has two real coordinates. Its balance map is
`T=[1,0,0,-1;0,1,1,0]`, with `TT^t=2I_2` and `P=T^t T/2`.
[Proved] The [tensor derivation](../../../omega_engine_atlas/coda_atlas/F004_papers/001_finite_obstruction_calculus/000_witnessed_difference_programme/docs/complex_tensor_balancing.md)
identifies its kernel, normalized operators and entangled-state lifts.

For rho_A=rho_B=[1], both real density views are I_2/2, so the raw
real product is I_4/4. Predict the trace before computing each expression:

| Expression | Result | Trace |
| --- | --- | --- |
| T(I_4/4)T^t | I_2/2 | 1 |
| T(I_4/4)T^t/2 | I_2/4 | 1/2 |
| P(I_4/4)P | P/4 | 1/2 |
| 2P(I_4/4)P | P/2 | 1 |

The extra operator factor cannot be reused for products of normalized
states. For operators A=B=[1], however, T I_4 T^t/2=I_2 is correct.
Now lift an arbitrary composite view sigma as T^t sigma T/2, then recover
it with T X T^t/2. Explain why this recovery factor differs from the raw
product-state compression above. [Pinned] The native fixtures test both
normalizations and a Bell density with imaginary coherence.
[Open] These supplied carriers and readout rules still need reconstruction
from the selected source commitments; matrix equality does not admit them.
