## Independent review of PR #506 at `446fa049e3b756fc1c9030406d0e1dbb3db74422`

**Verdict.** The two-sheet packet’s quantum calculation checks out. The classroom draft still gives students a formula that can predict the opposite of its own speaker demonstration. I would fix that formula, the claims about what the demonstration proves, and the picture sheet’s arrow scale before publication.

1. **High — The classroom formula can count the sign twice.** Move 2 introduces signed pushes, then multiplies their product by an alignment factor that is also negative for opposite directions. A student following both instructions gets \(1+1+2(1)(-1)(-1)=4\) for the setup that should give zero. Use \((a+b)^2\) for signed scalar pushes. When introducing an alignment factor, switch explicitly to **nonnegative lengths** \(A,B\) and write \(A^2+B^2+2AB\cos\theta\). In the tagged version, use \(|\gamma|\) for the overlap magnitude and include its phase once in the angle. [Classroom draft, lines 20–36](https://github.com/dbccpage/theory_of_finite_quantum_mechanics/blob/446fa049/atlas/papers/everett_mw/marbles_to_everett.md#L20-L36) and [lines 54–64](https://github.com/dbccpage/theory_of_finite_quantum_mechanics/blob/446fa049/atlas/papers/everett_mw/marbles_to_everett.md#L54-L64)

2. **High — The student takeaway misstates the relation between record quality and fringe visibility.** “The stripes fade exactly as fast as the telling-apart gets good” suggests a linear tradeoff. Even in the ideal balanced, pure-tag example, the relation is \(V^2+D^2=1\). For instance, \(D=0.6\) gives \(V=0.8\). A partial measurement can carry route information before the overlap reaches zero. The speaker paragraphs also give conflicting predictions for slightly different pitches. The instantaneous signal beats, and its interference term averages away over complete beat periods. Use those distinctions in the student text as well as the teacher box. [Classroom draft, lines 48–64](https://github.com/dbccpage/theory_of_finite_quantum_mechanics/blob/446fa049/atlas/papers/everett_mw/marbles_to_everett.md#L48-L64) and [lines 89–101](https://github.com/dbccpage/theory_of_finite_quantum_mechanics/blob/446fa049/atlas/papers/everett_mw/marbles_to_everett.md#L89-L101)

**What checked out.** For the supplied balanced preparation, the dark-exit contributions are \(+1/2\) and \(-1/2\). Orthogonal route marks give probability \(1/4\) for each joint exit-and-mark event and \(1/2\) for the exit alone. Undoing the reader copy and then the route mark restores the coherent input and returns the exit probability to zero. The corresponding recorded classical mixture stays at \(1/2\) after the same operations. This gives the lesson an experimentally meaningful distinction between two states that agree on the recorded counts. The packet separately identifies Everett’s reading of the matching memories.
