# PGW-IDENTITY-CONVENTION-UNDECIDABLE: observational identity and incompatible novelty conventions

For arbitrary observer families, equality of all observations is an equivalence relation. Forgetting observers preserves indistinguishability, an empty observer family identifies every pair, and adjoining an exact-identity observer makes indistinguishability equivalent to equality. The supplied inscription `<a>` after `<>` is new relative to the exact observer and not new relative to the empty observer family. No single proposition agrees with both novelty answers.

AuthorityEffect: None.

## Derivation

An observer family is a function obs:I -> X -> V. Two values are observationally indistinguishable when every indexed observer returns equal values. Equality in V gives reflexivity, symmetry and transitivity of this relation.

Restricting the observer indices cannot separate values that were previously indistinguishable. With no observer indices, the universal condition holds for every pair. Conversely, adjoining an identity observation separates every unequal pair. The extended observation codomain is a disjoint union, so this construction works even when V differs from X.

Use the actual strings `<>` and `<a>` as the old and supplied inscriptions. Their exact equality is false. An identity observer therefore reports novelty, while the empty observer family reports no novelty. Assuming a single proposition equivalent to both answers yields a contradiction.

The independent check enumerates all observer families on a two-element carrier as partitions, checks all restrictions, and checks that adding the identity observer yields the discrete partition. It also checks the literal inscriptions differ. These finite checks illustrate the general Lean statements; no algorithmic undecidability result is asserted.
