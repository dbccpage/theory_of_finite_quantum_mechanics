# TFQ-0008 finite dynamics shadow

AuthorityEffect: None.

The node asks for finite dynamics and forbids reading a numerical tolerance as an exact channel. This note proves one finite case and leaves the general obligation open. Nothing here is a continuum generator, a CPTP theorem, or a discharge of the stochastic-kernel gap.

## Path sum

Take vertices 0, 1, and 2, and two directed edges: weight `a` from 0 to 1, and weight `b` from 1 to 2, with `a` and `b` integers. A length-1 contribution from 0 to 2 is the sum of weights of edges with those endpoints. Neither edge has those endpoints, so that sum is 0. A length-2 contribution is the sum, over ordered pairs of edges, of the product of their weights when the first edge starts at 0, the second ends at 2, and the head of the first is the tail of the second. The only such pair is the two given edges, in that order, so the path sum is `a*b`.

## Sector mask

On a pair of integer weights `(w0, w1)`, the mask sends the pair to `(0, w1)`. The first coordinate is 0, the second coordinate is unchanged, and applying the mask again does nothing. If `w1 = 0`, the image is `(0, 0)`. This is an unnormalized support mask on one declared coordinate. It does not renormalize, it does not choose an outcome, and it is not a Lüders instrument.

## Cayley shadow and Euler step

For an integer `t`, write

```
C(t) = (1 - t*t)^2 + (2*t)^2
E(t) = 1 + t*t
```

`C(t)` is the squared Euclidean length of a column `(1 - t*t, 2*t)`. `E(t)` is the squared Euclidean length of a column `(1, t)`. Expanding gives `C(t) = (1 + t*t)^2` for every integer `t`. The same expansion says `E(t) = 1` if and only if `t = 0`, because `t*t = 0` forces `t = 0` in the integers. At `t = 1`, `C(1) = 4`. At `t = 2`, `E(2) = 5`, so `E(2) - 1 = 4`, while `C(2) = 25 = (1 + 4)^2`. The Cayley column therefore matches the squared scale `(1 + t*t)^2` at the same integer where the Euler column is strictly longer than 1. No exponential, no real parameter, and no one-parameter group is used.

## Distance 1 is not an exact kernel

Let `A` be the integer matrix with rows `(2, 0)` and `(0, 2)`, and let `B` be the matrix with rows `(2, 0)` and `(0, 3)`. The largest absolute entrywise difference is 1. Both row sums of `A` are 2. The row sums of `B` are 2 and 3. So `B` lies at distance 1 from a matrix with equal positive row sums, and `B` itself does not have equal row sums. A check that only asks for distance at most 1 from `A` accepts `B` and still does not authorize an exact scaled stochastic kernel.

## What is assumed

Integer arithmetic, the two-edge graph, the mask as defined, and the two explicit 2 by 2 integer matrices. No quantum state, no trace, and no completely positive map is assumed.

## What is open

A general instrument, a general finite path sum, a unitary or Hamiltonian shadow beyond this integer pair, an exact channel, the step from stochastic kernels to completely positive maps, a Rust witness of those general maps, and any continuum generator.
