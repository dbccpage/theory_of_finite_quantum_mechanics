## 2. Borel Factorization of $B_2(\mathbb{Q})$

### 2.1 The Group $B_2(\mathbb{Q})$
Let $B_2(\mathbb{Q})$ be the Borel subgroup of invertible upper-triangular $2 \times 2$ matrices over the rationals $\mathbb{Q}$:
$$B_2(\mathbb{Q}) = \left\{ \begin{pmatrix} d_1 & u \\ 0 & d_2 \end{pmatrix} \in \mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{Q}) \;\middle|\; d_1 \ne 0, \, d_2 \ne 0 \right\}.$$
Matrix multiplication in $B_2(\mathbb{Q})$ is given by:
$$\begin{pmatrix} d_1 & u \\ 0 & d_2 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} d_1' & u' \\ 0 & d_2' \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} d_1 d_1' & d_1 u' + u d_2' \\ 0 & d_2 d_2' \end{pmatrix}.$$
The group identity is $I = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$, and matrix inversion is given by:
$$\begin{pmatrix} d_1 & u \\ 0 & d_2 \end{pmatrix}^{-1} = \begin{pmatrix} d_1^{-1} & -u (d_1 d_2)^{-1} \\ 0 & d_2^{-1} \end{pmatrix}.$$

### 2.2 Subgroups $T$ and $N$
Define the diagonal maximal torus subgroup $T$:
$$T = \left\{ \begin{pmatrix} d_1 & 0 \\ 0 & d_2 \end{pmatrix} \;\middle|\; d_1, d_2 \in \mathbb{Q}^\times \right\} \cong \mathbb{Q}^\times \times \mathbb{Q}^\times,$$
and the standard unipotent radical $N$:
$$N = \left\{ \begin{pmatrix} 1 & n \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} \;\middle|\; n \in \mathbb{Q} \right\} \cong (\mathbb{Q}, +).$$

### 2.3 Semidirect Product Factorization
**Theorem (Factorization):** Every element $M = \begin{pmatrix} d_1 & u \\ 0 & d_2 \end{pmatrix} \in B_2(\mathbb{Q})$ factors uniquely as $M = t \cdot n$ where $t \in T$ and $n \in N$:
$$t = \begin{pmatrix} d_1 & 0 \\ 0 & d_2 \end{pmatrix} \in T, \quad n = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & u / d_1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} \in N.$$

*Proof:*
1. **Existence:**
   $$t \cdot n = \begin{pmatrix} d_1 & 0 \\ 0 & d_2 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & u / d_1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} d_1 \cdot 1 + 0 & d_1 (u / d_1) + 0 \cdot 1 \\ 0 \cdot 1 + d_2 \cdot 0 & 0 \cdot (u / d_1) + d_2 \cdot 1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} d_1 & u \\ 0 & d_2 \end{pmatrix} = M.$$
2. **Uniqueness:**
   Suppose $M = t' \cdot n'$ with $t' = \mathrm{diag}(d_1', d_2')$ and $n' = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & n'_u \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$.
   Then $t' n' = \begin{pmatrix} d_1' & d_1' n'_u \\ 0 & d_2' \end{pmatrix}$. Equating entries with $M$:
   - $(1,1)$ entry: $d_1' = d_1$.
   - $(2,2)$ entry: $d_2' = d_2$. Hence $t' = t$.
   - $(1,2)$ entry: $d_1' n'_u = u \implies d_1 n'_u = u \implies n'_u = u / d_1$. Hence $n' = n$.

### 2.4 Normality of $N$ and Quotient Projection
**Proposition (Normality):** $N$ is a normal subgroup of $B_2(\mathbb{Q})$.
*Proof:*
Let $M = \begin{pmatrix} d_1 & u \\ 0 & d_2 \end{pmatrix} \in B_2(\mathbb{Q})$ and $n = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & \nu \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} \in N$.
$$M n M^{-1} = \begin{pmatrix} d_1 & d_1 \nu + u \\ 0 & d_2 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} d_1^{-1} & -u (d_1 d_2)^{-1} \\ 0 & d_2^{-1} \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & \frac{d_1}{d_2} \nu \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} \in N.$$
Since the diagonal entries are both 1 and the subdiagonal entry is 0, $M n M^{-1} \in N$.

**Proposition (Quotient Projection):** The projection $\pi_T : B_2(\mathbb{Q}) \to T$ defined by $\pi_T \begin{pmatrix} d_1 & u \\ 0 & d_2 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} d_1 & 0 \\ 0 & d_2 \end{pmatrix}$ is a group homomorphism with $\ker(\pi_T) = N$. For any $n \in N$, $\pi_T(M n) = \pi_T(M)$.

## 3. Two-Stage Poset Dressing on the Diamond Graph

### 3.1 The Diamond Graph Complex
The Lean module (`E5` in `B2SemidirectDressing.lean`) uses the 4-vertex graph $D$ with vertices $V = \{0, 1, 2, 3\}$ and 5 directed edges
$$e_0 = (0,1),\quad e_1 = (1,2),\quad e_2 = (0,2),\quad e_3 = (2,3),\quad e_4 = (1,3).$$
This is the complete graph on four vertices without the edge $\{0,3\}$. It is not the Hasse diagram of the diamond poset $0 < \{1, 2\} < 3$: the edge $(1,2)$ joins incomparable elements, and the Hasse diagram has no edge $(1,2)$.
The spanning tree is
$$E_{tree} = \{e_0, e_2, e_3\} = \{(0,1), (0,2), (2,3)\}.$$
The remaining edges $\{e_1, e_4\} = \{(1,2), (1,3)\}$ are the chords. An earlier draft of this note used the tree $\{e_{01}, e_{02}, e_{13}\}$ and an external reference edge; that is not the graph of the Lean module.

### 3.2 Stage-One Dressing: Unipotent Clearing
Assign an initial gauge field configuration $g_e \in B_2(\mathbb{Q})$ to each edge $e \in E$.
In Stage One, local unipotent gauge transformations $u_v \in N$ are applied at each vertex $v \in V$:
$$g'_{e = (v, w)} = u_v \cdot g_e \cdot u_w^{-1}.$$
Because $N$ acts transitively on the upper-triangular entries via unipotent conjugation:
1. Local parameters $u_v$ are solved sequentially along the spanning tree $E_{tree}$ to set the off-diagonal entries of $g'_e$ to 0 for all $e \in E_{tree}$.
2. Consequently, all tree edges become purely diagonal: $g'_e \in T$ for all $e \in E_{tree}$.
3. Because $\pi_T(u) = I$ for all $u \in N$ and $\pi_T$ is a group homomorphism:
   $$\pi_T(g'_e) = \pi_T(u_v) \pi_T(g_e) \pi_T(u_w^{-1}) = I \cdot \pi_T(g_e) \cdot I = \pi_T(g_e)$$
   for all 5 edges $e \in E$.

### 3.3 Stage-Two Dressing: Torus Gauge and Residual Match
In Stage Two, torus gauge transformations $t_v \in T$ are applied at the vertices:
$$g''_{e = (v, w)} = t_v \cdot g'_e \cdot t_w^{-1}.$$
Since $T$ is abelian, $g''_e \in T$ for tree edges, and the vertex values $t_v$ can be selected to set tree edge holonomies to the identity $I$.
What Lean proves (`stage2_reproduces_residual`) is that for $u_T(v) = \pi_T(T(v))^{-1}$, with $T(v)$ the product of the matrices along the tree path from vertex 0 to $v$, and for every edge $e$ with source $s$ and target $t$,
$$u_T(t)\, U^{(1)}_e\, u_T(s)^{-1} = U_{full}(e) := T(t)^{-1}\, U_e\, T(s).$$
The cycle formula $\mathrm{residual}(C) = \pi_T(g_{01}) \pi_T(g_{13}) \pi_T(g_{23})^{-1} \pi_T(g_{02})^{-1}$ that an earlier draft wrote down, on a different graph, is not a Lean statement. That $U_{full}$ is `tree_gauge_indexed.residual` is not a Lean theorem either, and the independent check does not read `tree_gauge_indexed.residual`: it defines the same formula $T(t)^{-1} U_e T(s)$ itself (`res_full`) in SymPy.

## 4. Discrete Poset Curvature and Bianchi Identity

### 4.1 Poset Simplicial Complex $P_5$
Let $P_5$ be the finite poset with 5 elements $\{0, 1, 2, 3, 4\}$ forming an extended diamond complex.
The faces are ordered chains of elements:
- 0-cells (vertices): 1-element chains $c_0 = (v)$.
- 1-cells (directed edges): 2-element chains $c_1 = (v_0, v_1)$ with $v_0 < v_1$.
- 2-cells (triangles/faces): 3-element chains $c_2 = (v_0, v_1, v_2)$ with $v_0 < v_1 < v_2$.

### 4.2 Coboundary Operator and Nilpotency
On cochains over an arbitrary commutative ring $R$, the discrete incidence coboundary operator $b$ is defined on 0-cochains $f$ and 1-cochains $A$:
$$(b f)(v_0, v_1) = f(v_1) - f(v_0),$$
$$(b A)(v_0, v_1, v_2) = A(v_1, v_2) - A(v_0, v_2) + A(v_0, v_1).$$
**Theorem ($b^2 = 0$):** For any 0-cochain $f$:
$$(b(bf))(v_0, v_1, v_2) = (bf)(v_1, v_2) - (bf)(v_0, v_2) + (bf)(v_0, v_1) = (f(v_2) - f(v_1)) - (f(v_2) - f(v_0)) + (f(v_1) - f(v_0)) = 0.$$

### 4.3 Curvature and Cup Product
The cup product $A \smile B$ of two 1-cochains evaluated on a 2-chain $(v_0, v_1, v_2)$ is:
$$(A \smile B)(v_0, v_1, v_2) = A(v_0, v_1) \cdot B(v_1, v_2).$$
The discrete gauge curvature 2-cochain $F$ is defined by:
$$F = b A + A \smile A,$$
$$(F)(v_0, v_1, v_2) = A(v_1, v_2) - A(v_0, v_2) + A(v_0, v_1) + A(v_0, v_1) A(v_1, v_2).$$

### 4.4 Discrete Bianchi Identity
For 2-cochains, the coboundary $b F$ on a 3-chain $(v_0, v_1, v_2, v_3)$ is:
$$(b F)(v_0, v_1, v_2, v_3) = F(v_1, v_2, v_3) - F(v_0, v_2, v_3) + F(v_0, v_1, v_3) - F(v_0, v_1, v_2).$$
Defining the commutator $[A, F]_\smile = A \smile F - F \smile A$, direct algebraic expansion yields the discrete Bianchi identity:
$$b F + [A \smile F - F \smile A] = 0.$$
In the commutative scalar case, $[A, F]_\smile$ reduces via algebraic cancellation, confirming nilpotency and conservation of discrete flux.

### 4.5 What the Lean module states instead
Sections 4.1 to 4.4 describe the alternating-sum coboundary on chains of a finite poset. That is not the operator of `B2SemidirectDressing.lean`, and nothing in Sections 4.1 to 4.4 is a Lean theorem. Lean uses, for a commutative ring $R$ and 1-cochains $A : R \to R \to R$ on triples $(u, v, w)$ of ring elements, with no order relation on $R$:
$$(bA)(u,v,w) = -A(u,w),\qquad (A \smile A)(u,v,w) = A(u,v)\,A(v,w),\qquad K = bA + A \smile A,$$
so $K(u,v,w) = A(u,v)A(v,w) - A(u,w)$ (`curvature_formula`), and on 2-cochains $(bK)(u,v,w,z) = -K(u,w,z) + K(u,v,z)$. Lean proves $b(bA) = 0$ (`b_squared_zero`), $bK + A \smile K - K \smile A = 0$ (`discrete_bianchi_identity`) and $K(A^g) = g(u)\,K(A)\,g^{-1}(w)$ for $A^g(x,y) = g(x)A(x,y)g^{-1}(y)$ with $g(v)g^{-1}(v) = 1$ (`curvature_gauge_covariance`). The poset $P_5 = \{0 < \{1,2\} < 3 < 4\}$ with 9, 7 and 2 strict chains of length 2, 3 and 4, and matrix coefficients, appear only in `checks/independent/tfq-0016.py`.

## 5. Non-Transport of Color Generators

The target statement asserts that this Borel dressing construction "does not transport color generators".
This claim is validated by representation theory:
1. The Borel group $B_2(\mathbb{Q})$ is a subgroup of $\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{Q})$ and has natural representation dimension 2.
2. The color gauge group of quantum chromodynamics is $SU(3)$ (or its Lie algebra $\mathfrak{su}(3)$), whose fundamental representation has dimension 3, spanned by the 8 Gell-Mann matrices $\{\lambda_1, \dots, \lambda_8\}$.
3. A 2-dimensional representation cannot support the action of $\mathfrak{su}(3)$ color generators without trivializing the action, as there is no non-trivial Lie algebra homomorphism $\mathfrak{su}(3) \to \mathfrak{gl}_2(\mathbb{Q})$ (since $\mathfrak{su}(3)$ is simple and $\dim(\mathfrak{su}(3)) = 8 > 4 = \dim(\mathfrak{gl}_2)$).
4. Therefore, $B_2(\mathbb{Q})$ Borel dressing does not transport color generators.

Lean formalizes only `(2 : ℕ) ≠ 3` (`b2_color_dimension_mismatch`). The homomorphism claim in item 3 is not a Lean theorem.

## 7. Summary of Results

### What is proved (formalized in Lean 4 and checked by independent oracle)
1. **Factorization and Normality:**
   - Formalized `UpperTri2` structure over $\mathbb{Q}$ with exact rational arithmetic.
   - Proved group laws, inverse cancellation, and explicit unique factorization $B_2(\mathbb{Q}) = T \ltimes N$.
   - Proved normality of unipotent subgroup $N$ under $B_2(\mathbb{Q})$ conjugation.
   - Proved $\pi_T(u_1 M u_2) = \pi_T(M)$ for unit upper-triangular $u_1, u_2$ (`torusProj_unipotent_invariance`). That $\pi_T$ is a group homomorphism with kernel $N$ is not a Lean theorem.
2. **Dressing on the 4-vertex, 5-edge graph of Section 3.1:**
   - Proved Stage 1 unipotent dressing clears off-diagonal terms on tree edges, making tree edge holonomies purely diagonal.
   - Proved Stage 1 preserves $\pi_T$ projections across all five edges.
   - Proved Stage 2 torus dressing gives $U_{full}(e) = T(t)^{-1} U_e T(s)$ on all five edges. That this is `tree_gauge_indexed.residual` is not a Lean theorem.
3. **Discrete curvature, as in Section 4.5 (commutative ring, no poset):**
   - Proved $b(bA) = 0$ for $(bA)(u,v,w) = -A(u,w)$ on cochains over an arbitrary commutative ring.
   - Proved $bK + A \smile K - K \smile A = 0$ for $K = bA + A \smile A$ with that $b$.
   - Proved curvature gauge covariance with that $b$.
4. **Dimension comparison:**
   - Lean proves `(2 : ℕ) ≠ 3` only.
5. **Text search:**
   - The Python check reports that `qm213`, `two_stage`, and `borel` are absent from the lowercased text of `FiniteQM.lean`. It is not a Lean proof.
