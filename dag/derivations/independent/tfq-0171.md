# TFQ-0171

Node status stays OPEN. This note records one finite separation between positive natural masses and a unit-sum rational pair. It does not read the Rust valuation files and it does not close the obligation to keep an unnormalized valuation from standing in for a law that sums to one.

## Positive natural masses

Let a and b be natural numbers, each at least 1. Then a is at least 1 and b is at least 1, so a + b is at least 1 + 1. The sum 1 + 1 is 2, and 2 is not 1, so a + b is not 1.

The pair (1, 1) is the smallest case: its sum is 2, which is not 1. The empty mass 0 is not 1 either. A coordinate that is 0 can still make a unit sum, since 0 + 1 = 1. That boundary is outside the positive-pair claim.

## The rational unit pair

In the rationals, 1/2 + 1/2 = 1. The ordered pair (1, 1) is not the ordered pair (1/2, 1/2). The rational sum 1 + 1 is not 1.

## Division by a positive total

If a + b is positive, write s for that sum. Then a/s + b/s = (a + b)/s = s/s = 1. For (1, 1) this division returns (1/2, 1/2), which does sum to 1, while the undivided pair does not. The zero total is excluded: there is no division by 0.

## What is proved

For all natural numbers a and b that are at least 1, a + b is not 1. The pair (1, 1) sums to 2. The empty mass 0 is not 1. In the rationals, 1/2 + 1/2 = 1, 1 + 1 is not 1, and (1, 1) is not (1/2, 1/2). If a + b is positive, a/(a + b) + b/(a + b) = 1.

## What is assumed

The natural numbers and the rationals, including addition, the order, and division by a nonzero rational. AuthorityEffect is None.
