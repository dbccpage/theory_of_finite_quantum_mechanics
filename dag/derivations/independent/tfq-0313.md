# TFQ-0313 narrowed fragment: two endomorphisms of (Z/4Z)^2

AuthorityEffect: None.

## Carrier

The coefficient ring is Z/4Z. Addition and multiplication are the usual operations reduced modulo 4. In particular 1+1+1+1 = 0 and 1 ≠ 0. The module is the set of pairs (x, y) with x, y in Z/4Z. There are 16 such pairs, and they are pairwise distinct.

An endomorphism in this note is a 2×2 matrix with entries in Z/4Z, acting by the usual row-column rule. The matrix is additive and homogeneous when that action preserves addition and scalar multiplication on every one of the 16 vectors.

## The two maps

Let

A = [[0, 1], [0, 0]], B = [[0, 0], [1, 0]].

On a vector (x, y), A sends (x, y) to (y, 0) and B sends (x, y) to (0, x). Both formulas are additive and Z/4Z-homogeneous: the image of a sum is the sum of the images, and the image of s·(x, y) is s times the image.

The matrix products are

AB = [[1, 0], [0, 0]], BA = [[0, 0], [0, 1]].

Their difference is

AB − BA = [[1, 0], [0, 3]],

because the lower-right entry is 0 − 1 = 3 in Z/4Z. This difference is not the zero matrix, so AB ≠ BA.

The same split is visible on the vector (1, 0):

A(B(1, 0)) = A(0, 1) = (1, 0), B(A(1, 0)) = B(0, 0) = (0, 0).

The two composite actions differ, and their vector difference is (1, 0). For every vector, the matrix product acts as the composite of the two maps.

## A commuting diagonal pair

Let D = diag(1, 2) = [[1, 0], [0, 2]] and E = diag(3, 1) = [[3, 0], [0, 1]]. Both products equal diag(3, 2) = [[3, 0], [0, 2]], so D and E commute. A itself is not a scalar matrix: every scalar matrix [[s, 0], [0, s]] has upper-right entry 0, while A has upper-right entry 1.

## What is proved

On (Z/4Z)^2, A and B above are additive and homogeneous, AB and BA are the displayed matrices, AB − BA = [[1, 0], [0, 3]] ≠ 0, the two composites differ on (1, 0), D and E commute with product diag(3, 2), A is not a scalar matrix, the coefficient ring satisfies 1+1+1+1 = 0 with 1 ≠ 0, and the carrier lists 16 distinct vectors.
