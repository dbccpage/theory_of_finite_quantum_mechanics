# Derivation Note: Transitive Sector Symmetry and Orbit-Constant Readouts (L1-IMP-SECTOR-SYMMETRY-LAW)

**AuthorityEffect:** None.
**Target Node:** `L1-IMP-SECTOR-SYMMETRY-LAW`
**Outcome Classification:** `Reduced`

## 1. Mathematical Context & Problem Statement

In the finite quantum mechanics framework, the pass from $L_1$ sector weights to a finite Hilbert carrier requires selecting an inner product on the sector carrier space $V = \mathbb{Q}^n$. A quadratic readout on $V$ associated with a Gram matrix $G \in M_n(\mathbb{Q})$ is defined by:
$$q_G(x) = x^T G x = \sum_{i,j=1}^n G_{ij} x_i x_j$$
with corresponding bilinear pairing $\langle x, y \rangle_G = x^T G y$.

In primitive difference theory, there is no physical postulate forcing the quadratic readout to be invariant under arbitrary relabellings of sectors. Node `L1-IMP-SECTOR-SYMMETRY-LAW` investigates the reduction of inner product uniqueness to two specific mathematical hypotheses on the quadratic readout:
1. **Sector Additivity on Disjoint Supports:** For any vectors $u, v \in \mathbb{Q}^n$ with disjoint sector supports (meaning for every coordinate $k$, $u_k = 0$ or $v_k = 0$),
   $$q_G(u + v) = q_G(u) + q_G(v)$$
2. **Transitive Sector Symmetry:** The quadratic readout is invariant under a set of sector permutations $S \subset \mathrm{Perm}(\{1, \dots, n\})$ whose generated group $\langle S \rangle$ acts transitively on sectors:
   $$q_G(P_\sigma x) = q_G(x) \quad \forall \sigma \in S, \forall x \in \mathbb{Q}^n$$
   where $(P_\sigma x)_i = x_{\sigma^{-1}(i)}$, ensuring $P_\sigma e_j = e_{\sigma(j)}$.

The objective is to establish the exact reduction:
- Under both hypotheses together (with symmetry $G^T = G$), $G$ is uniquely forced to be a scalar multiple of the identity, $G = c I_n$ ($c = G_{11}$), selecting the Euclidean dot product up to scale.
- Neither hypothesis alone suffices: sector additivity without transitive symmetry leaves diagonal weights unconstrained; transitive symmetry without sector additivity allows cross-sector interference.
- When sectors carry distinguished physical labels that break transitivity, invariance under $\langle S \rangle$ constrains the diagonal of $G$ to be constant only on each orbit of $\langle S \rangle$, breaking global scalar uniqueness.

## 2. Derivation of the Reduction

### Step 1: Sector Additivity Forces an Orthogonal Basis
Let $G \in M_n(\mathbb{Q})$ be a symmetric Gram matrix ($G_{ij} = G_{ji}$). For any pair of distinct sectors $i \neq j$, the standard unit basis vectors $e_i$ and $e_j$ have disjoint support. Expanding the quadratic readout of their sum yields:
$$q_G(e_i + e_j) = q_G(e_i) + q_G(e_j) + G_{ij} + G_{ji}$$
Sector additivity implies $q_G(e_i + e_j) = q_G(e_i) + q_G(e_j)$, which forces:
$$G_{ij} + G_{ji} = 0$$
Since $G$ is symmetric ($G_{ij} = G_{ji}$), this yields:
$$2 G_{ij} = 0 \implies G_{ij} = 0 \quad (\forall i \neq j)$$
Thus, sector additivity forces every off-diagonal entry of $G$ to vanish, making the sector basis mutually orthogonal. The quadratic readout reduces to its diagonal form:
$$q_G(x) = \sum_{i=1}^n G_{ii} x_i^2$$

### Step 2: Readout Invariance Under Permutations
For any permutation $\sigma \in \mathrm{Perm}(\{1, \dots, n\})$, the coordinate action on a unit vector $e_j$ satisfies $P_\sigma e_j = e_{\sigma(j)}$.
If the readout is invariant under $\sigma$, then:
$$q_G(e_{\sigma(j)}) = q_G(P_\sigma e_j) = q_G(e_j)$$
Since $q_G(e_k) = G_{kk}$, this immediately implies:
$$G_{\sigma(j), \sigma(j)} = G_{jj}$$
Invariance extends to the group $\langle S \rangle$ generated by $S$ via inductive closure:
- Invariance under $\sigma$ and $\tau$ implies invariance under $\sigma \circ \tau$:
  $$q_G(P_{\sigma \tau} x) = q_G(P_\sigma (P_\tau x)) = q_G(P_\tau x) = q_G(x)$$
- Invariance under $\sigma$ implies invariance under $\sigma^{-1}$:
  $$q_G(P_{\sigma^{-1}} x) = q_G(P_\sigma (P_{\sigma^{-1}} x)) = q_G(x)$$

### Step 3: Diagonal Constancy on Orbits
Define the orbit relation on sectors under $S$: two sectors $i$ and $j$ are in the same orbit ($\sim_S$) if there exists $\sigma \in \langle S \rangle$ such that $\sigma(i) = j$.
Because $\langle S \rangle$ is a group, $\sim_S$ is an equivalence relation:
- Reflexivity: $\mathrm{id} \in \langle S \rangle$ maps $i \mapsto i$.
- Symmetry: if $\sigma(i) = j$, then $\sigma^{-1}(j) = i$ with $\sigma^{-1} \in \langle S \rangle$.
- Transitivity: if $\sigma(i) = j$ and $\tau(j) = k$, then $(\tau \circ \sigma)(i) = k$ with $\tau \circ \sigma \in \langle S \rangle$.

**Theorem (Orbit-Constant Diagonal):**
If $G$ is invariant under every permutation in $S$, then for any sectors $i, j$ in the same orbit of $\langle S \rangle$,
$$G_{ii} = G_{jj}$$
*Proof:* Since $i \sim_S j$, there exists $\sigma \in \langle S \rangle$ with $\sigma(i) = j$. By group closure, $G$ is invariant under $\sigma$. Therefore $G_{jj} = G_{\sigma(i), \sigma(i)} = G_{ii}$. $\blacksquare$

### Step 4: Transitive Symmetry Selects Inner Product Up to Scale
When $\langle S \rangle$ acts transitively on $\{1, \dots, n\}$, there is only a single orbit. By the orbit-constant diagonal theorem, all diagonal entries must be equal:
$$G_{11} = G_{22} = \dots = G_{nn} = c$$
Combining this with off-diagonal vanishing from Step 1:
$$G = c \cdot I_n$$
Consequently:
$$\langle x, y \rangle_G = c (x \cdot y), \quad q_G(x) = c \|x\|^2$$
Any two symmetric, sector-additive, transitively invariant forms $G, G'$ are strictly proportional:
$$G' = \left(\frac{G'_{11}}{G_{11}}\right) G \quad (\text{for } G_{11} \neq 0)$$

## 3. Breaking Transitivity: Distinguished Sector Labels

When sectors carry distinguished physical labels, permutations between different sectors are not symmetries of the readout.

### Countermodel 1: Trivial Symmetry on 2 Sectors ($n = 2$)
Consider $V = \mathbb{Q}^2$ with distinguished sector labels (so $S = \emptyset$, $\langle S \rangle = \{\mathrm{id}\}$).
The orbits are singletons: $\mathcal{O}_1 = \{1\}$, $\mathcal{O}_2 = \{2\}$.
The matrix:
$$G = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$$
satisfies:
- Symmetry: $G^T = G$.
- Sector additivity: $G_{12} = G_{21} = 0$, so $q_G(u + v) = q_G(u) + q_G(v)$ for all disjointly supported $u, v$.
- Orbit constancy: $G_{11} = 1$ on $\mathcal{O}_1$ and $G_{22} = 2$ on $\mathcal{O}_2$.
- Invariance under $S = \emptyset$ holds vacuously.
However, $G_{11} = 1 \neq 2 = G_{22}$, so $G \neq c I_2$ for any $c \in \mathbb{Q}$. Transitive sector symmetry fails: under the transposition $\tau = (1\,2)$, $q_G(P_\tau e_1) = q_G(e_2) = 2 \neq 1 = q_G(e_1)$.

### Countermodel 2: Non-Trivial Orbits on 3 Sectors ($n = 3$)
Consider $V = \mathbb{Q}^3$ where sectors 1 and 2 are indistinguishable, but sector 3 has a distinguished label.
The symmetry generator set is $S = \{(1\,2)\}$.
The orbits of $\langle S \rangle$ are:
$$\mathcal{O}_{1,2} = \{1, 2\}, \quad \mathcal{O}_3 = \{3\}$$
The Gram matrix:
$$G = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$$
satisfies:
- $G$ is symmetric and sector-additive (all off-diagonals vanish).
- $G$ is invariant under $(1\,2)$:
  $$q_G(x_2, x_1, x_3) = x_2^2 + x_1^2 + 2 x_3^2 = x_1^2 + x_2^2 + 2 x_3^2 = q_G(x_1, x_2, x_3)$$
- The diagonal is constant on each orbit: $G_{11} = G_{22} = 1$ on $\mathcal{O}_{1,2}$, and $G_{33} = 2$ on $\mathcal{O}_3$.
- But $G_{11} \neq G_{33}$, so $G$ is not a scalar multiple of $I_3$.

### Countermodel 3: Necessity of Sector Additivity ($n = 2$)
Consider $V = \mathbb{Q}^2$ with full transitive symmetry $S = \{(1\,2)\}$, but drop sector additivity.
The matrix:
$$G = I_2 + J_2 = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$$
is symmetric and invariant under $(1\,2)$:
$$q_G(x_2, x_1) = 2 x_2^2 + 2 x_2 x_1 + 2 x_1^2 = q_G(x_1, x_2)$$
However, $G$ fails sector additivity on disjoint vectors $e_1, e_2$:
$$q_G(e_1 + e_2) = q_G((1, 1)^T) = 6 \neq q_G(e_1) + q_G(e_2) = 2 + 2 = 4$$
Because the off-diagonal entries $G_{12} = G_{21} = 1 \neq 0$ do not vanish, $G \neq c I_2$.

## 4. Summary & Claim Hygiene

### What is proved
1. **Reduction Theorem:** For any rational Gram matrix $G \in M_n(\mathbb{Q})$, the conditions of symmetry ($G^T = G$), sector additivity on disjoint supports, and invariance under a permutation set $S$ whose generated group acts transitively reduce the Gram matrix to a single uniform scale factor: $G = c I_n$ where $c = G_{11}$ (`CampaignL1SectorSymmetry.eq_smul_one_generated`).
2. **Orbit-Constant Diagonal:** For any permutation set $S$, readout invariance under $S$ forces the diagonal entries of $G$ to be constant on each orbit of $\langle S \rangle$ (`CampaignL1SectorSymmetry.diag_const_on_orbit`).
3. **Failure Under Distinguished Labels:** Countermodels on $n = 2$ (`diag12`) and $n = 3$ (`G_dist3 = diag(1, 1, 2)`) prove that when sector labels are distinguished, breaking transitivity, the diagonal is constant only on orbits and $G$ fails to be a scalar multiple of $I_n$.
4. **Independent Necessity of Sector Additivity:** Countermodel `unif12 = 1 + J` proves that transitive symmetry alone does not force off-diagonal vanishing or select the inner product up to scale.
5. **Formal Verification:** All theorems compile cleanly under Lean 4.35.0 with zero `sorry`, zero `admit`, zero custom `axiom` declarations, and depend exclusively on `{propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound}`. All identities are verified by `checks/independent/l1-imp-sector-symmetry-law.py` without repository imports.

### What is assumed
1. The carrier vector space $V = \mathbb{Q}^n$ and the quadratic form structure $q_G(x) = x^T G x$ are supplied as the setting, not derived from primitive difference.
2. Sector additivity on disjoint supports is supplied as a hypothesis (transcribing edgewise $L_1$ additivity).
3. Transitive sector symmetry is supplied as an imported assumption (the second law of inner product selection).
4. Finite dimension $n$ and rational scalars $\mathbb{Q}$ (exact arithmetic; no real limits or continuum approximations).

### What is open
1. Derivation of transitive sector symmetry from more primitive dynamical or relational principles: within the existing primitive difference theory, sector labels remain potentially distinguishable.
2. Physical characterization of which physical regimes justify exact permutation symmetry among sectors versus regimes where distinguished boundary or superselection sectors break transitivity.
