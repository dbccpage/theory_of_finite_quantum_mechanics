## Statement

Let S be a theory in classical first-order logic with a primitive recursive coding of its syntax. Write code(psi) for the code of a sentence psi and num(n) for the numeral of a natural number n. Assume (i) consistency: there is no sentence psi such that S derives both psi and the negation of psi. Assume (ii) a primitive recursive relation Proof such that psi is a theorem of S exactly when Proof(p, code(psi)) holds for some natural number p, and Proof is represented at standard numerals: for all natural numbers p and s, S derives the sentence expressing Proof(num(p), num(s)) when the relation holds and derives its negation when the relation fails. Assume (iii) that S derives every true Delta_0 sentence of this coding; that the substitution function subst, sending the code of a formula with one free variable and a number n to the code of the sentence obtained by substituting num(n) for that variable, is represented in S; that the primitive recursive function neg on codes, with neg(code(psi)) equal to code(the negation of psi), is represented in S; and that for each standard natural number n, S derives both that every w with w < num(n) equals one of num(0), ..., num(n-1), and that every w satisfies w < num(n) or w = num(n) or num(n) < w. Then there is a sentence phi such that S derives neither phi nor the negation of phi. The diagonal lemma from representation of subst supplies, for the negation of the Rosser formula RosserProv(v) given by existence of a u such that Proof(u, v) and such that Proof(w, neg(v)) fails for every w < u, a sentence phi such that S derives the biconditional of phi and the negation of RosserProv(num(code(phi))). If S derives phi, a standard derivation code p, clause (i), numeral-wise representation of Proof, and the schema that every number below num(p) equals one of num(0), ..., num(p-1), yield a derivation of RosserProv(num(code(phi))) and then of the negation of phi. If S derives the negation of phi, a least standard derivation code q of that negation, numeral-wise representation of Proof, the same bounded schema at q, and the schema that every number is below num(q), equal to num(q), or above num(q), yield a derivation of the negation of RosserProv(num(code(phi))) and then of phi. Either case contradicts (i). Separately, drop (i) and (ii) and keep (iii): if a formula Tr(x) is such that S derives the biconditional of Tr(num(code(psi))) and psi for every sentence psi, the same diagonal lemma applied to the negation of Tr supplies a sentence lambda for which those biconditionals yield a derivation of both lambda and its negation, so S is inconsistent. Both conclusions use only the hypotheses just stated and classical first-order logic.

## Argument

Representation of a primitive recursive function f at standard inputs means: for each standard tuple, S derives the equation giving the correct output numeral, and S may replace that output inside a formula.

Diagonal lemma, from representation of subst. Let alpha(v) be a formula with one free variable. Let theta(x) be alpha(subst(x, x)), let m be code(theta), and let phi be the sentence theta(num(m)). Representation gives a derivation of the equation subst(num(m), num(m)) = num(code(phi)), and replacement gives a derivation of the biconditional of phi and alpha(num(code(phi))).

Rosser formula. Let RosserProv(v) be: there exists u such that Proof(u, v) and such that Proof(w, neg(v)) fails for every w < u. Apply the diagonal lemma to the negation of RosserProv, and call the resulting sentence phi. Then S derives the biconditional of phi and the negation of RosserProv(num(code(phi))).

Suppose S derives phi, and let p be a standard code of a derivation of phi. Clause (ii) gives Proof(p, code(phi)), and representation puts that fact into S. Consistency, clause (i), says S derives no negation of phi, so Proof(w, code(the negation of phi)) fails for every natural number w. For each standard w < p, representation puts the corresponding negated atom into S. The first numeral schema in (iii) turns that finite list into a derivation of: Proof(w, neg(code(phi))) fails for every w < num(p). Hence S derives RosserProv(num(code(phi))), hence the negation of phi, contradicting (i).

Suppose S derives the negation of phi, and let q be the least standard code of a derivation of that negation. Consistency says Proof(m, code(phi)) fails for every natural number m. For each standard m < q, representation puts the negated atom into S, and the bounded schema at q turns that finite list into a derivation that Proof(u, code(phi)) fails for every u below num(q). The case u = num(q) is one further negated atom: q codes a derivation of the negation, and Proof records which sentence was derived, so Proof(q, code(phi)) fails. The order schema at q says every element is below num(q), equal to num(q), or above num(q). Above num(q), the witness num(q) refutes the claim that Proof(w, neg(code(phi))) fails for every w below that element, because representation has put Proof(num(q), neg(code(phi))) into S. The three cases together are a derivation of the negation of RosserProv(num(code(phi))), hence of phi, contradicting (i).

The second numeral schema is an unbounded sentence. Deriving every true Delta_0 sentence does not yield it. It is a hypothesis, stated in (iii).

Tarski, using (iii) and not (i) or (ii). Assume a formula Tr(x) such that S derives the biconditional of Tr(num(code(psi))) and psi for every sentence psi. The diagonal lemma applied to the negation of Tr supplies lambda such that S derives the biconditional of lambda and the negation of Tr(num(code(lambda))). The truth biconditional for lambda then yields the biconditional of lambda and the negation of lambda. From that biconditional, classical first-order logic derives both lambda and the negation of lambda, so S is inconsistent.

The priced admission language is not an instance of S in this argument. Whether a later definition of that language meets (i), (ii) and (iii) is a separate question.

## What is proved

In prose, and only under the hypotheses in the statement: there is a sentence phi that S derives neither as itself nor as its negation; and a truth predicate with a derivable biconditional for every sentence makes S inconsistent. The argument is the one written above. Nothing in Lean.

## What is assumed

Classical first-order logic. Hypotheses (i), (ii) and (iii), including representation of subst, of neg and of Proof at standard numerals, Delta_0 sentences that are true, and both numeral schemas. For the second conclusion, a formula Tr(x) with a derivable biconditional for every sentence.
