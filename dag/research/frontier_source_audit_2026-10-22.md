## Key Corrections to Source Statements

1. **`CT-INFORMATION-MEDIA-INTEROPERABILITY` (`external:arXiv:1405.5563`):** In Deutsch and Marletto (2015), Section 3 (p. 9) defines a *computation variable* as a set of at least two possible attributes on which all permutation tasks are possible with side-effects, and Section 4 (eq. (3), p. 10) defines an *information variable* as a **clonable computation variable** (and an *information medium* as a substrate with at least one information variable). Distinguishability is **not** assumed in the definition of an information medium; instead, Section 5 (eq. (4), p. 11) defines distinguishability via mapping into an information variable (avoiding the circularity noted in Section 1 eq. (1), p. 3) and then derives that every information variable is distinguishable. Principle III (Section 6, p. 12) states interoperability on $\mathbf{S}_1 \oplus \mathbf{S}_2$ with information variable $S_1 \times S_2$.
2. **`CT-SUPERINFORMATION-MEDIUM-DEFINITION` (`external:arXiv:1405.5563`):** Section 6 (pp. 14-18, Principle VIII p. 16) defines an *information observable* as an information variable $X$ satisfying double-complement closure $x = \bar{\bar{x}}$ for every $x \in X$. Section 7 (p. 20) defines a *superinformation medium* as an information medium with at least two **information observables** $X, Y$ containing mutually disjoint attributes whose union $X \cup Y$ is not an **information observable** (rather than 'two information attributes whose union is not an information attribute'). Sections 8.1 and 8.3 (pp. 20-22) then derive that $X \cup Y$ is neither distinguishable nor clonable.
3. **`CQFT-CAUSAL-FACTORIZATION-IMPORT` (`external:numdam:AIHPA_1973__19_3_211_0`, `external:arXiv:math-ph/9903028`):** In Epstein and Glaser (1973, Section 1.1 condition `(C.A.)` pp. 214-215, Section 2.1 condition `(Caus. 1)` eq. (9) p. 218, and Section 2.2 eq. (30) p. 223) and Brunetti and Fredenhagen (2000, Section 1 eq. (2) p. 4 and Section 3 Condition P3 p. 15), causal factorization $V(g, h_1 + h_2) = V(g, h_1) V(g, h_2)$ (at $g = 0$, $S(g_1 + g_2) = S(g_1) S(g_2)$) and $T(P \cup Q) = T(P) T(Q)$ holds whenever $\operatorname{supp} h_1 \cap (\operatorname{supp} h_2 + \bar{V}^-) = \varnothing$ (that is, $\operatorname{supp} h_1$ does not intersect the causal past of $\operatorname{supp} h_2$, which includes spacelike separation as well as causal future separation, not only when $\operatorname{supp} h_1$ lies inside the causal future of $\operatorname{supp} h_2$).
4. **`LQG-AREA-VOLUME-SPECTRA-SOURCE` (`external:arXiv:gr-qc/9411005`, `external:arXiv:gr-qc/9602046`):** In both Rovelli and Smolin (1995, Section 1 eq. (2) p. 4, Section 4 eqs. (47)-(48) p. 28) and Ashtekar and Lewandowski (1997, Section 3.3 eq. (3.20) p. 16, Section 4.1 eqs. (4.7)-(4.9) and (4.12) pp. 18-19), the canonical bracket is normalized as $\{A_a^i(x), E_j^b(y)\} = G \delta_a^b \delta_j^i \delta^3(x,y)$ without the $8\pi\gamma$ factor, giving $A = \ell_P^2 \sum_i \sqrt{j_i(j_i+1)}$ for simple transverse intersections (and $(\ell_P^2/2) \sum_v \sqrt{2 j_v^{(d)}(j_v^{(d)}+1) + 2 j_v^{(u)}(j_v^{(u)}+1) - j_v^{(d+u)}(j_v^{(d+u)}+1)}$ for general vertices on the surface in eq. (4.9)). As Ashtekar and Lewandowski explain in Section 3.3 footnote 6 (p. 16) and Engle et al. write in `arXiv:0711.0146` Section V eq. (36) (p. 11), restoring the $8\pi G\gamma$ factor from the $(16\pi G)^{-1}$ action prefactor and Barbero-Immirzi parameter $\gamma$ gives $A = 8\pi\gamma\ell_P^2 \sum_i \sqrt{j_i(j_i+1)}$. Also noted that the nonzero trivalent volume formula in `gr-qc/9411005` eqs. (1) and (37) was corrected in the erratum *Nucl. Phys. B* **456** (1995) 753, where trivalent vertices have zero volume.
5. **`LQG-ROBERTS-1999-SOURCE` and `LQG-PR-ASYMPTOTICS-IMPORT` (`external:arXiv:math-ph/9812013`):** Roberts (1999, Section 1 Theorem 1 eq. (2) p. 22 and Section 6.1 eqs. (12)-(13) p. 61) indexes irreducible representations of $SU(2)$ by integers $a_r = 2 j_r \in \mathbb{N}$ ($\dim V_a = a+1$) and obtains the asymptotic prefactor $\sqrt{2 / (3\pi V(\tau) k^3)} \cos(\sum_{r=1}^6 (k a_r + 1)\theta_r/2 + \pi/4)$ where $V(\tau) > 0$ is the volume of the non-degenerate Euclidean tetrahedron with side lengths $a_r = 2 j_r$ and exterior dihedral angles $\theta_r$; since $V(a_r) = 8 V(j_r)$, $2 / (3\pi V(a_r)) = 1 / (12\pi V(j_r))$, matching Ponzano-Regge's $(12\pi V(j_r))^{-1/2}$.
6. **`LQG-EPRL-VERTEX-IMPORT` (`external:arXiv:0711.0146`, `external:arXiv:0708.1595`):** Recorded the explicit Euclidean $\operatorname{Spin}(4) \cong SU(2)_+ \times SU(2)_-$ embedding $j^\pm = |1 \pm \gamma| k / 2$ (eqs. (18)-(20) pp. 6-7 of `0711.0146`, with $k = j^+ + j^-$ for $0 < \gamma < 1$ and $k = j^+ - j^-$ for $\gamma > 1$) and the Lorentzian $\operatorname{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})$ principal-series embedding $(n, \rho) = (2k, 2\gamma k)$ in EPRL's integer-$n$ convention (Section IV eq. (24) p. 8, corresponding to $(j, \gamma j)$ when labeling principal-series representations by half-integer spin $j = n/2 = k$).

## Exact Python `fractions.Fraction` Recomputations

- **Myrheim-Meyer ordering fraction (`CQFT-MYRHEIM-MEYER-ORDERING-FRACTION-TARGET`):** Using exact rational Gamma values at integers and half-integers in $f(d) = \frac{3}{2} \frac{\Gamma(d+1)\Gamma(d/2+1)}{\Gamma(3d/2+1)} = \frac{\Gamma(d+1)\Gamma(d/2)}{2\Gamma(3d/2)}$ (Sorkin 2003 `gr-qc/0309009` Section 7 p. 16), Python `fractions.Fraction` gives $f(1) = 1$, $f(2) = 1/2$, $f(3) = 8/35$, $f(4) = 1/10$ (and half those values in Reid 2003 `gr-qc/0207103` eq. (5) p. 5, which normalizes by $\langle N \rangle^2$ instead of $\binom{N}{2}$). On the $n \times n$ grid with the componentwise product order ($N = n^2$ points, $R = n^2(n-1)(n+3)/4$ comparable pairs), direct enumeration and `fractions.Fraction` give $r(\text{grid}_n) = \frac{2R}{N(N-1)} = \frac{n+3}{2(n+1)}$, taking values $5/6, 3/4, 7/10, 2/3, 9/14, 5/8$ for $n = 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7$.
- **Five-qubit `[[5,1,3]]` code and 6-qubit Choi perfect tensor (`ADS-FIVE-QUBIT-PERFECT-TENSOR-TARGET`, `ADS-LOGICAL-RECONSTRUCTION-TARGET`):** Exact $\mathbb{F}_2$ symplectic enumeration of the 64-element stabilizer group generated by $XZZXII, IXZZXI, XIXZZI, ZXIXZI, XXXXXX, ZZZZZZ$ gives weight distribution $\{0: 1, 4: 45, 6: 18\}$ ($1 + 45 z^4 + 18 z^6$). Every one of the $\binom{6}{3} = 20$ bipartitions of size $3|3$ has trivial subgroup supported on either side, hence entropy $3$ bits. In the 5-qubit code, the normalizer has $64$ elements ($16$ stabilizers and $48$ nontrivial logical operators of minimum weight $3$); each of the $\binom{5}{3} = 10$ triples supports a unique representative of each nontrivial logical class ($\bar{X}, \bar{Y}, \bar{Z}$), and none of the $\binom{5}{2} = 10$ pairs supports any nontrivial logical operator.
- **Six-tensor HaPPY patch (`ADS-HAPPY-ENTROPY-MINCUT-TARGET`):** Contracting a central 6-leg perfect tensor with 5 outer 6-leg perfect tensors along 5 internal bonds and fixing all 6 bulk legs in $|0\rangle_L$ produces a 20-qubit boundary stabilizer state of full $\mathbb{F}_2$ rank $20$. Across all $20 \times 19 = 380$ contiguous circular boundary intervals $A$, $S(A) = \operatorname{mincut}(A)$ holds on $340$ intervals and $S(A) = 4 < 5 = \operatorname{mincut}(A)$ on $40$ intervals ($5$ of length $8$, $15$ of length $9$, $15$ of length $11$, and $5$ of length $12$), because fixing the bulk leg in $|0\rangle_L$ projects each 6-leg tensor to the 5-qubit state $|0\rangle_L$ whose 32-element stabilizer group has weight distribution $\{0: 1, 3: 10, 4: 15, 5: 6\}$.
- **Qubit no-cloning and entangling phase over $\mathbb{Q}(i)$ (`CT-QUBIT-NO-CLONING-FINITE-TARGET`, `CT-ENTANGLING-PHASE-FINITE-TARGET`):** Over $\mathbb{Q}(i)$ with `fractions.Fraction`, $(|0\rangle\langle 0|^{\otimes 2} + |1\rangle\langle 1|^{\otimes 2}) - (|+\rangle\langle +|^{\otimes 2} + |-\rangle\langle -|^{\otimes 2})$ has entry $-1/2$ on $|00\rangle\langle 11|$ and $|11\rangle\langle 00|$. Among the $64$ subsets of the $6$ Pauli eigenstates, $13$ subsets have pairwise squared overlaps in $\{0, 1\}$ ($1$ empty, $6$ singletons, $3$ antipodal pairs) and $51$ contain a pair with squared overlap $1/2$. For $u = (3+4i)/5$, $|u|^2 = (3/5)^2 + (4/5)^2 = 1$ and $C^2 = |u-1|^2/4 = ((-2/5)^2 + (4/5)^2)/4 = 1/5$.
- **Area spectrum, classical $6j$, Cayley-Menger and level-2 quantum pentagon (`LQG-AREA-SPECTRUM-EXACT-TARGET`, `LQG-6J-EXACT-AND-PENTAGON-TARGET`, `LQG-PR-ALLOWED-REGION-TARGET`, `LQG-TV-LEVEL2-PENTAGON-TARGET`):** Exact `fractions.Fraction` evaluation confirms $j(j+1) = a(a+2)/4 \in \{0, 3/4, 2, 15/4, 6, 35/4, 12\}$ for $a = 2j \in \{0..6\}$ and the radical coincidence $\sqrt{12} = 2\sqrt{3} = 4 \times (\sqrt{3}/2)$; confirms that all $3^6 = 729$ classical $6j$ symbols with $2j \le 2$ have rational squares in $\{0, 1/36, 1/9, 1/6, 1/4, 1/3, 1/2, 1\}$; confirms Cayley-Menger $288 V^2 = 4 > 0$ (Euclidean regular $j=1/2$), $0$ (flat $s=(1,1,1,1,1,3)$), and $-6075/16 < 0$ (Lorentzian admissible $a=(4,4,4,4,4,8)$); and verifies the $SU(2)_2$ Ising pentagon identity over $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})$ on all $3^9 = 19,683$ label 9-tuples.
