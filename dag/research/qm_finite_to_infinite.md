## 1. Executive Scope & Theoretical Framework

The central objective of this research is to characterize the relationship between finite, discrete algebraic carriers and the continuum formulations of standard quantum mechanics:

> **Core Research Question:** Which structures of quantum mechanics are realizable on discrete, algebraic carriers of finite dimension or finite description, and precisely how do continuum and infinite-dimensional structures emerge as asymptotic limits, representations, or topological completions?

Rather than viewing discrete and continuum approaches as mutually exclusive or treating the continuum as an adversarial domain, this roadmap provides a systematic, mathematically rigorous classification of the interfaces, scaling limits, representation-theoretic transitions, and embedding theorems connecting them.

## 2. Foundational Mathematical Interfaces

The transition from discrete algebraic structures to continuous quantum mechanics spans five primary mathematical domains:

### 2.1 Operator Algebras and Commutation Relations

- **Wintner-Wielandt Theorem & Finite-Dimensional Obstruction:**  
  In any finite-dimensional normed associative algebra (such as $M_d(\mathbb{C})$ or $M_d(K)$), the trace of a commutator vanishes:
  $$\operatorname{Tr}([A, B]) = \operatorname{Tr}(AB - BA) = 0$$
  Consequently, the canonical commutation relation (CCR) $[X, P] = i\hbar I_d$ cannot be represented by finite-dimensional matrices, since $\operatorname{Tr}(i\hbar I_d) = i\hbar d \ne 0$. Continuous position and momentum operators necessarily require infinite-dimensional Hilbert spaces and unbounded operators with dense domains.

- **Discrete Weyl-Heisenberg Groups as Algebraic Replacements:**  
  On finite-dimensional carriers of dimension $d$, the CCR is replaced by the discrete Weyl-Heisenberg group generated by clock ($X$) and shift ($Z$) operators over cyclotomic fields $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_d)$:
  $$X |j\rangle = \zeta_d^j |j\rangle, \quad Z |j\rangle = |j+1 \pmod d\rangle, \quad XZ = \zeta_d ZX$$
  In the asymptotic limit $d \to \infty$ with appropriate phase-space grid scaling ($\Delta x \sim 1/\sqrt{d}$), discrete Weyl operators converge weakly to the continuous Weyl operators $e^{i\xi X} e^{i\eta P} = e^{-i\xi\eta\hbar} e^{i\eta P} e^{i\xi X}$, demonstrating how continuous phase space arises as a well-defined scaling limit.

### 2.2 Time Evolution, Dynamics, and Generator Theory

- **Stone's Theorem on One-Parameter Unitary Groups:**  
  In continuum quantum mechanics, time evolution is governed by a strongly continuous one-parameter unitary group $U(t)$ acting on a Hilbert space $\mathcal{H}$. By Stone's theorem, there exists a unique self-adjoint (possibly unbounded) operator $H$ such that:
  $$U(t) = \exp(-iHt), \quad H = i \lim_{t \to 0} \frac{U(t) - I}{t}$$
  This formulation requires continuous time $t \in \mathbb{R}$ and topological completeness of $\mathcal{H}$.

- **Discrete Dynamics vs Continuous Generators:**  
  On finite algebraic carriers, time evolution is natively formulated as discrete iterations of a unitary automorphism $U^n$ ($n \in \mathbb{Z}$). Recovering a continuous generator $H = i \log U$ requires choosing a branch cut on the complex unit circle $S^1$, introducing an external continuum interpolation.
  
- **Lattice Dynamics and the Thermodynamic Limit:**  
  For discrete lattice models, continuous-time evolution generated by local Hamiltonians $H = \sum_x h_x$ satisfies Lieb-Robinson bounds:
  $$\|[A(t), B]\| \le c \|A\| \|B\| e^{-\mu (d(A, B) - v t)}$$
  Trotter-Suzuki product formulas provide constructive, certified error bounds for approximating continuous flows via finite discrete steps, establishing the precise asymptotic convergence of lattice dynamics to continuous field theories.

### 2.3 Measure Theory, Gleason's Theorem, and State Space Topologies

- **Dimension Dichotomy in Gleason's Theorem:**  
  Gleason's theorem establishes that for Hilbert spaces of dimension $d \ge 3$, any non-negative frame function $f: \mathcal{S}(\mathcal{H}) \to [0, 1]$ satisfying $\sum_{i=1}^d f(e_i) = 1$ on all orthonormal bases is quadratic: $f(v) = v^\dagger \rho v$ for a unique density operator $\rho$.
  - In dimension $d = 2$, this uniqueness fails: there exist discontinuous binary frame functions $f(v) \in \{0, 1\}$ on $S^1$ summing to 1 on orthogonal pairs that admit no density matrix representation.
  - In dimension $d \ge 3$, interlocking orthogonal triads enforce global quadratic structure on the continuous unit sphere $\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{C}^d)$.

- **Frame Functions on Dense Cyclotomic Sub-Spheres:**  
  A major research frontier is whether Gleason's uniqueness holds when restricted to dense algebraic subsets, such as the cyclotomic sub-sphere $\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)^d)$ for $d \ge 3$. While Meyer's theorem (1999) demonstrated that the purely rational sphere $\mathbb{Q}^3$ is colorable with $\{0, 1\}$ (nullifying Kochen-Specker under finite precision), dense cyclotomic extensions provide rich algebraic structure whose noncontextual frame behavior remains an active open question.

- **Busch's Theorem for POVM Effect Frames:**  
  For generalized measurements represented by positive operator-valued measures (POVMs), Busch's theorem recovers density operators for all dimensions $d \ge 2$ from effect-frame functions, providing an algebraic alternative to projection-only Gleason theorems.

### 2.4 Information Theory, Entropy, and Transcendental Enclosures

- **Baker's Theorem on Linear Forms in Logarithms:**  
  For mixed density matrices $\rho$ with algebraic spectrum, the von Neumann entropy $S(\rho) = -\operatorname{Tr}(\rho \ln \rho)$ exits the base number field $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)$ and is transcendental whenever $\operatorname{rank}(\rho) > 1$.

- **Dual-Track Information Architecture:**  
  To handle this boundary cleanly:
  1. *Internal Algebraic Invariants:* State purity and mixedness are monitored natively within $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)$ via integer Rényi power sums $P_m = \operatorname{Tr}(\rho^m)$ for $m \ge 2$, where $P_2 = 1 \iff \rho$ is pure.
  2. *Certified Rational Interval Enclosures:* When thermodynamic entropy is required, verified rational interval arithmetic $[a, b] \subset \mathbb{Q}$ with width $\le 2^{-k}$ encloses $S(\rho)$ to arbitrary declared precision using uniform Taylor-series remainder bounds.

### 2.5 Field Embeddings and Quantum Nonlocality

- **Spectral Positivity and Real Embeddings:**  
  For pure states, Gram matrices are totally positive across all field embeddings. For mixed states, however, positive semi-definiteness of a matrix with entries in $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)$ depends on the chosen real embedding $\iota: \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)^+ \hookrightarrow \mathbb{R}$. Mixed-state and CPTP formulations explicitly declare this embedding.

- **Asymptotic Correlation Sets ($C_{qa}$ vs $C_{qc}$) and MIP\*:**  
  In relativistic quantum information, correlation sets generated by spatial tensor products ($C_q$) and their topological closure ($C_{qa}$) differ from commuting-operator models ($C_{qc}$) in infinite dimensions, as established by the MIP\* = RE theorem. Discrete algebraic games probe exact rational points within these correlation convex sets.
