The sources are general: Paper.lean and Paper001.lean prove, over List A and List alpha with hand-rolled structures, that an isomorphism induces an alphabet bijection (Classical.choice is used for it, per Paper.lean header) and hence equal sizes. Paper001.lean DiscreteEquiv is, by definition, a pair of mutually inverse maps on objects, so no_discrete_equivalence is the pigeonhole fact; the Python checks free_monoids_nonisomorphic and discrete_categories_inequivalent compare counts (the latter returns left != right). The DAG-local Lean theorem pg_to_sn_and_wdp_route_chain_composition is fixed arithmetic and vacuous for this node. This node re-proves the statement with Mathlib FreeMonoid / MulEquiv and Discrete categories (genuine categorical equivalence), bridges the source WordIso to MulEquiv, adds the converse iff, and records the sharp boundary: a set bijection exists, so the obstruction is multiplicativity; native: exact word counts, brute-force atoms, exhaustive homomorphism search with bounded image length, discrete equivalences, shortlex bijection.

Supported: For arbitrary alphabets (kernel-checked, lean4/FreeMonoidNoniso.lean, Mathlib FreeMonoid and CategoryTheory.Discrete): a monoid isomorphism FreeMonoid a ~ FreeMonoid b induces a bijection of the alphabets (the atoms are the one-letter words and an isomorphism permutes atoms), so unequal finite alphabets give non-isomorphic free monoids, and FreeMonoid a ~ FreeMonoid b iff a ~ b; alphabetEquiv, no_free_monoid_iso, freeMonoid_iso_iff_equiv, set_bijection_exists and the discrete-category theorems use Classical.choice (kernel axiom reports: propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound), as does the source for the same step; an equivalence of discrete categories Discrete a ~ Discrete b (a genuine CategoryTheory.Equivalence) gives a bijection a ~ b, so Fin n and Fin m give equivalent discrete categories iff n = m; every source WordIso yields a MulEquiv (WordIso.toMulEquiv) and the source theorem is recovered through it; the converse MulEquiv -> WordIso is not stated; the boundary is sharp: for n, m >= 1 the underlying SETS of FreeMonoid (Fin n) and FreeMonoid (Fin m) are in bijection (both countably infinite), so the content is multiplicativity, not cardinality; the free monoid on the empty alphabet is one point; words of length k over Fin n number n^k and atoms number n. Exhaustive native checks on 15 alphabet pairs (sizes 0 to 4): all homomorphisms whose generator images have length <= 1 for the pairs (3,4), (4,3), (4,4) and length <= 2 for all other pairs; the candidates injective on words of length <= 2 whose images contain every letter (a necessary-condition filter, not a proof of bijectivity) are exactly the n! letter permutations when n = m and none otherwise; the equivalences of discrete categories are exactly the bijections; and the explicit shortlex bijection of the underlying sets is multiplicative on the first 30 words exactly when n = m (a prefix statement).

Remaining: Any structure on numerals or alphabets other than the free-monoid multiplication or the discrete category (a numeral or alphabet re-presentation may still be a non-isomorphic map, a set bijection, or an isomorphism of some other structure; no other structure, such as free commutative monoids, is examined); language restriction, partial recovery and total erasure (separate ledger row WDP-P001-009); infinite alphabets beyond the general Lean statement; the native homomorphism search covers short generator images only and is an instance, not the general theorem.
