Verdict first, then the lemmas.

**Verdict.** $\Gamma_2=d_1\omega$ is a *map*, so every mass built from it is canonical. $\Phi_1=\mathrm{dist}_1(\omega,B^1)$ is an *infimum*, so it fixes a number and a polytope of minimal repairs, not a point. Sector masses are determined by the residual exactly when that polytope's per-sector mass is constant (an exact LP test); otherwise the residual determines a convex credal set of mass vectors, and a selector is needed. The selector that keeps the whole structure coherent is, provably, a probability measure $\nu$ on the minimal-repair polytope — the same kind of object as the state $\rho$ in Lemma 24 — and no residual-intrinsic or naturality principle picks $\nu$: three natural rules give three different answers on a four-edge example. So "canonical sector probabilities" is either (a) the credal polytope itself, or (b) point masses *relative to a declared repair witness or declared secondary cost*. That is the characterization.

---

**Setup (31–35).** Finite oriented complex: vertices $V$, edges $E$, faces $F$; weights $w_e,w_f\in\mathbb Q_{>0}$. $C^0=\mathbb Q^V$, $C^1=\mathbb Q^E$, $C^2=\mathbb Q^F$; $(d_0\alpha)_e=\alpha(\mathrm{head}\,e)-\alpha(\mathrm{tail}\,e)$; $(d_1\omega)_f=\sum_{e\in\partial f}\pm\omega_e$; $d_1d_0=0$. $\|\omega\|_1=\sum_ew_e|\omega_e|$. For $\omega\in C^1$:
$$\Phi_1(\omega)=\min_{\alpha}\|\omega-d_0\alpha\|_1,\qquad M(\omega)=\{\omega-d_0\alpha:\ \alpha\text{ attains the min}\},\qquad\Gamma_2(\omega)=d_1\omega .$$
A 1-sector structure is a partition $\mathcal S=\{S_1,\dots,S_m\}$ of $E$; a 2-sector structure a partition of $F$. For $r\in M(\omega)$ with $\Phi_1>0$, the sector mass vector is $m_{\mathcal S}(r)_i=\Phi_1^{-1}\sum_{e\in S_i}w_e|r_e|\in\Delta^{m-1}$. Admissible set: $P_{\mathcal S}(\omega)=\{m_{\mathcal S}(r):r\in M(\omega)\}$.

## 31 — $\Gamma_2$-masses are canonical; $\Phi_1$-masses are class-level

*Claim.* For any 2-sector partition $\{T_k\}$ of $F$ with $\|d_1\omega\|_1>0$, $g_k=\|d_1\omega\|_1^{-1}\sum_{f\in T_k}w_f|(d_1\omega)_f|$ is a function of $\omega$ alone, invariant under every repair: $d_1(\omega-d_0\alpha)=d_1\omega$. The regime trichotomy (exact: $\Phi_1=0$; survivor: $\Phi_1>0,\Gamma_2=0$; open: $\Gamma_2\ne0$) is therefore residual-intrinsic. For $\Phi_1$: $M(\omega+d_0\beta)=M(\omega)$ and $M(c\omega)=cM(\omega)$ ($c>0$), so $P_{\mathcal S}$ depends only on the class $[\omega]\in Q^1=C^1/B^1$ and is scale-free. In the open regime, passing to a closed representative $\omega_c\in\arg\min_{d_1\eta=0}\|\omega-\eta\|_1$ is a second infimum with its own polytope; everything below then applies to $\omega_c$, with the closure selector composed in front. $\square$

**Records.** *Assumptions:* $d_1d_0=0$. *Zero cases:* $\Gamma_2=0$ (no 2-masses); $\Phi_1=0$ (no 1-masses, $M=\{0\}$). *Operations:* $\omega\mapsto d_1\omega$; $\omega\mapsto[\omega]$. *Identities:* $d_1(\omega-d_0\alpha)=d_1\omega$; $P_{\mathcal S}(\omega+d_0\beta)=P_{\mathcal S}(\omega)$. *Admissible compositions:* 2-masses compose with any repair; 1-masses only with class-preserving moves.

## 32 — The residual fixes a rational polytope and exact mass intervals

*Claim.* $M(\omega)$ is a nonempty rational polytope; for each sector $S_i$ the admissible masses form an interval $[m_i^-,m_i^+]$ computable exactly over $\mathbb Q$; $P_{\mathcal S}(\omega)$ is a point iff $m_i^-=m_i^+$ for all $i$.

*Proof.* $\Phi_1$ is the LP $\min\sum_ew_es_e$ s.t. $s_e\ge\pm(\omega-d_0\alpha)_e$, rational data, so the optimal set is a rational polyhedron and its image $r=\omega-d_0\alpha$ is bounded by $\|r\|_1=\Phi_1$: a rational polytope. $m_i^-$: LP $\min\sum_{e\in S_i}w_es_e$ s.t. $s\ge\pm r$, $r\in\omega+B^1$, $\sum_Ew_es_e\le\Phi_1$ (the last constraint forces $s=|r|$ and $r\in M(\omega)$). $m_i^+$: maximum of a convex piecewise-linear function over a polytope, attained at a vertex — exact by vertex enumeration or by one LP per sign pattern on $S_i$. The point criterion is immediate. $\square$

*Sufficient conditions for a point.* (i) $M(\omega)$ is a singleton (LP uniqueness, exactly checkable). (ii) Each $S_i$ is a union of connected components of $(V,E)$: then $\alpha$ and the minimization decouple, $\Phi_1=\sum_i\Phi_1^{(i)}$, and every minimizer has sector mass exactly $\Phi_1^{(i)}$ — the only structural case where the residual *is* the sector mass.

*Countermodel (minimal).* $n$-cycle, $w\equiv1$, $\omega=e_1$. $B^1$ = zero-circulation fields, so $r\in\omega+B^1$ iff $\sum_er_e=1$; $\|r\|_1\ge|\sum r_e|=1$ with equality iff $r\ge0$. Hence $M(\omega)=\{r\ge0:\sum r_e=1\}$ and $P_{\text{edges}}=\Delta^{n-1}$ (every mass vector), $P_{\mathcal S}=[0,1]$ for any two-sector split. The residual ($\Phi_1=1$, $\Gamma_2=0$) carries no sector information. With a single edge of strictly smaller weight, the minimizer is unique (all mass there): nonuniqueness is weight-tie / LP-degeneracy, which is exactly where sector information is absent.

**Records.** *Assumptions:* rational weights. *Zero cases:* $\Phi_1=0$ excluded; empty sectors have mass $0$ identically. *Operations:* three LPs ($\Phi_1$, $m_i^-$), vertex max ($m_i^+$). *Identities:* $\sum_im_i^\pm$ need not equal $1$ (intervals are marginals of a polytope); $\sum_e w_e|r_e|=\Phi_1$ on all of $M$. *Admissible compositions:* intervals coarse-grain by $[m_i^-+m_j^-,\ m_i^++m_j^+]\supseteq[m_{i\cup j}^-,m_{i\cup j}^+]$, generally strict.

## 33 — Consistency across partitions forces one selector; selectors are states on $M(\omega)$

*Definitions.* A sector-mass assignment is a family $m_{\mathcal S}\in\Delta$ over all partitions $\mathcal S$ of $E$ with: (N) $\sum_im_{\mathcal S,i}=1$; (CG) merging sectors adds masses; (RF) residual-faithfulness: $m_{\mathcal S}\in\mathrm{conv}\,P_{\mathcal S}(\omega)$.

*Claim.* (N)+(CG) $\iff$ there is one $\mu\in\Delta^{E}$ with $m_{\mathcal S,i}=\sum_{e\in S_i}\mu_e$ for every $\mathcal S$. (RF) at the finest partition $\iff$ $\mu=\sum_{k}\nu_k\,m_{\text{edges}}(r_k)$ for finitely many $r_k\in M(\omega)$ and a probability vector $\nu$ with at most $|E|$ atoms (Carathéodory), rational if $\mu$ is. Thus a coherent selector is exactly a probability measure $\nu$ on $M(\omega)$; the deterministic ones are points $r^*\in M(\omega)$.

*Proof.* (CG) by induction from singletons gives $m_{\mathcal S,i}=\sum_{e\in S_i}m_{\{e\}}$; set $\mu_e=m_{\{e\}}$. Conversely pushforwards of a fixed $\mu$ satisfy (N),(CG). (RF) at the finest level is membership in $\mathrm{conv}\{(w_e|r_e|/\Phi_1)_e:r\in M(\omega)\}$, a convex hull of a finite union of rational polytopes in a space of dimension $\le|E|-1$. $\square$

*What fails without it.* On the 4-cycle of 32, choose uniform masses for the edge partition but $(1,0)$ for the split $\{e_1,e_2\}\,|\,\{e_3,e_4\}$: each is admissible, (CG) fails. This is the exact analogue of Lemma 17 vs Lemma 24: separate choices per partition vs one $\nu$ across all partitions.

**Records.** *Assumptions:* finest partition available. *Zero cases:* $M(\omega)$ a point $\Rightarrow\nu$ forced. *Operations:* pushforward of $\mu$; mixing of repairs. *Identities:* $m_{\mathcal S}=\mathcal S_*\mu$; $\mu=\int m_{\text{edges}}\,d\nu$. *Admissible compositions:* (CG) is preserved under refinement and coarse-graining; mixtures of coherent selectors are coherent.

## 34 — No residual-intrinsic principle selects $\nu$

*Claim.* Naturality (equivariance under isomorphisms of weighted complexes) does not determine $\nu$: three natural selectors differ on one example.

*Example.* Two vertices $a,b$; four parallel edges $a\to b$, $w\equiv1$; $\omega=(0,0,1,2)$; sectors $A=\{e_1,e_2\}$, $B=\{e_3,e_4\}$. Here $d_0\alpha=t(1,1,1,1)$ with $t=\alpha(b)-\alpha(a)$, and $\sum_e|\omega_e-t|=3$ for all $t\in[0,1]$ (median interval), so $\Phi_1=3$ and $M(\omega)=\{(-t,-t,1-t,2-t):t\in[0,1]\}$, masses $\tfrac13(t,t,1-t,2-t)$, sector masses $(m_A,m_B)=(\tfrac{2t}3,1-\tfrac{2t}3)$.
- *Vertex barycenter of $M$:* $t=\tfrac12$ $\Rightarrow(\tfrac13,\tfrac23)$.
- *Secondary cost* $\min_{M}\|r\|_2^2$ (weights $w$, natural): $8t-6=0$, $t=\tfrac34$ $\Rightarrow(\tfrac12,\tfrac12)$.
- *Maximum entropy of the edge masses:* $t^2=(1-t)(2-t)$, $t=\tfrac23$ $\Rightarrow(\tfrac49,\tfrac59)$.
All three are equivariant under the automorphism swapping $e_1,e_2$ (which fixes $\omega$), so naturality is satisfied and still three answers. A secondary cost with weights $v$ moves the answer continuously in $v$; e.g. on the 2-edge cycle with $\omega=(1,0)$, $v=(1,3)$ gives $t=\tfrac14$. So "secondary cost" is a one-parameter-per-edge family, not a principle. $\square$

*Characterization of the three principles.*
- **Declared secondary cost** (lexicographic repair: $\ell^1$, then a strictly convex $v$-weighted $\ell^2$): unique, rational, natural iff $v$ is natural; it is a *repair preference*, extra data.
- **Indifference** (barycenter / centroid): unique, rational, natural, depends only on $M(\omega)$; it is a declared stance (which centroid, of which face structure), not a consequence of residual data.
- **Least commitment** (max-entropy over $\mathrm{conv}P_{\text{edges}}$): unique, natural, but generally irrational — the example is rational by accident — so it is incompatible with an exact-replay program unless the result is certified rational case by case.
- **Witness** ($\nu=\delta_{r^*}$, $r^*$ the repair actually recorded): rational, replayable, and the only option that mints nothing beyond what is declared; masses are then masses *relative to the witness*, with $M(\omega)$ as the admissibility check.

**Records.** *Assumptions:* 32, 33. *Zero cases:* symmetric $M$ (transitive automorphism action on vertices): all natural selectors coincide at the fixed point. *Operations:* the four selectors. *Identities:* on the example, $\Phi_1=3$, masses $\tfrac13(t,t,1-t,2-t)$. *Admissible compositions:* selectors compose with coarse-graining (33) but not across different declared costs.

## 35 — Bridge to the Born chain: residual masses are the commutative case

*Claim.* Given a coherent selector $\mu\in\Delta^E$ (33), set $\rho_\mu=\mathrm{diag}(\mu)\in\mathrm{Herm}_{|E|}(K)$ ($K$-rational if $\mu$ is) and $Q_S=\sum_{e\in S}E_{ee}$. Then $Q_S$ are commuting rational projections with $\sum_{S\in\mathcal S}Q_S=I$, and $m_{\mathcal S,i}=\mathrm{Tr}(\rho_\mu Q_{S_i})$: sector masses are Born probabilities of a classical (diagonal) state. Conversely any density $\rho$ reproducing all $m_{\mathcal S}$ has $\mathrm{diag}\rho=\mu$ and arbitrary off-diagonal part. $\square$

This is the exact content of "residual alone does not determine": the $\Phi_1/\Gamma_2$ data, even after a selector, supply the *diagonal* of a state. The off-diagonal data — the coherence transport of item 3 (C24), the state of Lemma 24 — are not residual data and cannot be manufactured from $\ell^1$ masses by any principle, because every quantity in 31–34 is a function of $|r_e|$.

**Records.** *Assumptions:* 33; Steps 1–10. *Zero cases:* single sector: $Q=I$, mass $1$. *Operations:* $\mu\mapsto\mathrm{diag}(\mu)$; $S\mapsto Q_S$. *Identities:* $\mathrm{Tr}(\mathrm{diag}(\mu)Q_S)=\sum_{e\in S}\mu_e$; $[Q_S,Q_{S'}]=0$. *Admissible compositions:* coarse-graining of sectors $=$ sums of commuting projections (Lemma 13 with $W=I$).

**What to write in the gap row.** The selector is a state $\nu$ on the minimal-repair polytope $M(\omega)$ (33); the residual fixes $M(\omega)$ and the exact credal intervals $[m_i^-,m_i^+]$ (32) and fixes $\Gamma_2$-masses outright (31); $\nu$ is forced only when $M(\omega)$ has constant per-sector mass, structurally when sectors are component-separated (32). Any point value beyond that is relative to a declared repair witness or a declared secondary cost (34), and even then only the diagonal of a state is obtained (35). The canonical object is the polytope, not the point.
