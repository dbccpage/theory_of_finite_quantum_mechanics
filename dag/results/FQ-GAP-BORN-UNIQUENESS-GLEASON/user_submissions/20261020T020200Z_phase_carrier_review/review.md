## 1. Divisibility does not force all roots of unity

The implication 'an n-th root of every existing phase for every n => all roots of unity' is false. Let P=Z[1/2]/Z, embedded into the circle, equivalently the union of mu_(2^r). It contains i, is dense in the circle's inherited topology, and is divisible by EVERY positive integer, but it contains no element of order 3.

Proof of divisibility: for x=k/2^r mod Z and n=2^s m with m odd, choose a with m a congruent k modulo 2^r. This is possible because m is invertible modulo 2^r. Then y=a/2^(r+s) lies in P and n y=x. Every element of P has 2-power order, so 3x=0 implies x=0. This is a general written countermodel proof, not an enumerated experimental result.

To force mu_infinity within an embedded phase group G, explicitly assume G contains an element of exact order n for EVERY n. A primitive n-th root generates all mu_n; hence their union is contained in G. Equality needs the further minimality condition G is generated by those roots. This is an infinite operational/algebraic assumption, not established by finitely many examples. Closure under ALL complex solutions to z^n=1 would also supply the roots, but is stronger than existence of some n-th root.

The colimit identity itself is correct: use Z/nZ -> Z/mZ, [k] -> [(m/n)k] for n|m, and map [k] to [k/n] in Q/Z. Fraction classes cover Q/Z and the maps are compatible and injective. Relating Q/Z to complex roots of unity additionally uses the chosen standard embedding.

## 2. Coefficient field and topology are separate inputs

If a subfield F of C is required and contains every root of unity, then Q(mu_infinity) is contained in F by field generation. The field is a countable algebraic subfield of C. The premise that amplitude carriers must be fields does not follow from unspecified normalization or division: gate-generated rings can support restricted normalized operations without closure under arbitrary inverses. Q(i) has only four roots of unity but has infinitely many other unit-modulus elements; do not identify these two populations.

Q(mu_infinity) is not the full algebraic closure Qbar. It contains Q(i), which is already dense in C in the ordinary complex metric. Thus no algebraic-closure step is needed to obtain C as a chosen metric completion. Under that specified metric, completion of Q(mu_infinity), Qbar, or Q(i) is C. The metric/embedding and completeness are declared inputs, not finite operational consequences. A dense group is not necessarily discrete in the inherited topology; Q/Z in U(1) is not discrete. The abstract colimit of finite discrete groups carries a different topology if equipped with the discrete final topology.

The blanket claim that nothing finite distinguishes a dense subtheory from its closure is too broad. Density supports finite-precision approximation for specified finite collections of continuous observables/protocols. It does not prove equivalence of exact identities, exact membership, zero-probability assertions or all physical preparations.

## 3. Clifford+T is not dense in the full unitary group as written

For standard single-qubit H,T, determinants are -1 and exp(i*pi/4), so every generated determinant is an eighth root of unity. Continuity of determinant prevents this set from being dense in U(2). State the appropriate projective/determinant convention, qubit dimension and ancilla resources. Giles-Selinger's exact synthesis theorem explicitly distinguishes local-ancilla and ancilla-free settings; merely naming the entry ring is not a density theorem for the stated gate group.

## 4. Correct source attribution for continuity and completion

Hardy 2001 Axiom 5 supplies continuous reversible connectivity between pure states within the rest of his reconstruction assumptions. It is not, on its own, a metric completion theorem for a chosen scalar field.

Masanes-Muller 2011 is incorrectly cited as imposing the same continuity axiom. Requirement 4 is reversible transitivity. The paper, arXiv:1004.1483v4, Sec. III, Requirement 4, explicitly says its analysis does not impose continuity. Its local-tomography requirement is also separate. See references.json for the version-specific source.

Stone's theorem already operates with a real time parameter and a Hilbert/unitary setting. The trivial scalar character t -> 1 is strongly continuous and not onto U(1). For a NONTRIVIAL continuous scalar homomorphism from R into the standard U(1), the usual character theorem gives exp(i omega t), omega!=0, which is onto. This is a conditional statement, not a derivation of C from finite dynamics; general one-parameter unitary groups need not have the full circle as their image.

Soler's infinite-orthonormal-sequence hypothesis is correctly identified as an infinitary import. Modal finite-field QM is not an orthomodular Hilbert-space counterexample with the same assumptions: Schumacher-Westmoreland explicitly use possibility/necessity rather than numerical probabilities and lack a natural inner product/orthogonality. Frobenius requires a finite-dimensional associative real division algebra; metric completeness alone does not imply those hypotheses or leave only R,C,H.

## 5. Local tomography has a stated model class

Let K_F(n) count real dimensions of UNNORMALIZED self-adjoint matrix spaces. For standard candidates K_R(n)=n(n+1)/2, K_C(n)=n^2, K_H(n)=n(2n-1). If the full composite is assumed to be the same matrix model at size ab, then

K_R(ab)-K_R(a)K_R(b)=ab(a-1)(b-1)/4;
K_C(ab)-K_C(a)K_C(b)=0;
K_H(ab)-K_H(a)K_H(b)=-2ab(a-1)(b-1).

For a,b>=2 only the complex candidate meets multiplicativity among these three assumed models. This does not select complex QM among all GPTs or define a canonical quaternionic tensor product. Use cone dimension K, not normalized affine dimension K-1. Barnum-Wilce provide a stronger conditional reconstruction with Jordan/composite/qubit hypotheses, not local tomography alone. Calling this a Kunneth theorem would require a specified chain complex and an actual Kunneth statement; it is presently an analogy.

Stueckelberg's J statement should retain his specific real-Hilbert/uncertainty framework. It is not a premise-free exclusion of all real formulations.
