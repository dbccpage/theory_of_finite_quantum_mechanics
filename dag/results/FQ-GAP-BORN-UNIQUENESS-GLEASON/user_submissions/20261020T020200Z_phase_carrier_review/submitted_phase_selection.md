The reviewer is right, and the gap splits into three separable selections. Finite witnesses settle only the first; the other two have to be declared or imported.

**1. What interference and finite examples actually force: a dense skeleton, not a completion**

- Interference with arbitrary-order recombination requires an n-th root of every phase for every n. Closure under that gives all roots of unity: μ∞ = colim ℤ_n (under divisibility) ≅ ℚ/ℤ. That is a theorem, and it is as far as finite phase groups go.
- Normalization and Born-rule division need inverses, so the coefficient structure must be a field containing μ∞: ℚ(μ∞) ⊂ ℚ̄. This kills ℤ[i] (not a field) and ℚ(i) (only μ₄; no cube roots of unity).
- Both ℚ/ℤ ⊂ U(1) and ℚ(μ∞) ⊂ ℂ are dense. So are Clifford+T unitaries over ℤ[1/√2, i] in U(n). Density is the ceiling for algebraic/finite data; nothing finite distinguishes a dense subgroup from its closure.

**2. Completion (ℚ/ℤ → U(1), ℚ̄ → ℂ): no finite witness exists; pick a named axiom**

- Hardy 2001, Axiom 5 / Masanes–Müller 2011: reversible transformations between pure states form a connected (continuous) group. This is the one axiom that separates QM from classical probability in those derivations, and it rules out every discrete phase group.
- Equivalent relocation: continuous time plus strongly continuous evolution (Stone's theorem) — the image of ℝ → U(1) is the full circle. This moves the assumption onto time rather than onto scalars.
- Solèr 1995 derives completeness (Hilbert space over ℝ, ℂ, or ℍ) from orthomodularity plus an infinite orthonormal sequence — but it imports ℵ₀ and says nothing in finite dimension, where orthomodular spaces exist over ℚ(i) and even finite fields (Schumacher–Westmoreland modal QM). For a finite-first programme Solèr is not available without conceding infinity.
- Consistent with your own doctrine that coefficients are among the declared choices: this step belongs in the registry as a frozen axiom, not as a derivation.

**3. ℂ against ℝ and ℍ (once completeness is granted, Frobenius leaves three candidates)**

- Local tomography: the composite state-space dimension satisfies K_AB = K_A·K_B only over ℂ (real gives N(N+1)/2, quaternionic N(2N−1); neither multiplies). In your setting this is a Künneth-type condition on the tensor product of carriers — the most native formulation available.
- Stueckelberg 1960: real QM needs a central operator J with J² = −1 to recover uncertainty relations; J is i by another name.
- Renou et al., Nature 600 (2021): real-scalar QM is falsifiable in network Bell scenarios, later tested experimentally.
