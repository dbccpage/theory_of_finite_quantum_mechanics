**Standing conventions (11–20).** $K=\mathbb Q(i)$, $d\ge1$, $^*$ = conjugate transpose, PSD = positive semidefinite over $\mathbb C$, $\mathrm{Herm}_d(K)$ ordered by PSD with $u=I$. Steps 1–10 may be cited. "Rational" means entries in $K$.

## 11 — Exact rational PSD elimination

**Lemma Z (zero pivot).** $A\in\mathrm{Herm}_d(K)$, $A\succeq0$, $a_{jj}=0$ $\Rightarrow$ $a_{jk}=a_{kj}=0$ for all $k$.

*Proof.* For $k\ne j$, $s\in\mathbb Q_{>0}$, put $t=-s\,\overline{a_{kj}}\in K$ and $v=te_j+e_k\in K^d$. Then
$v^*Av=|t|^2a_{jj}+2\,\mathrm{Re}(t\,a_{kj})+a_{kk}=-2s|a_{kj}|^2+a_{kk}\ge0$ for every $s>0$, forcing $a_{kj}=0$; $a_{jk}=\overline{a_{kj}}=0$. $\square$

**Claim.** There exist a permutation $P$, unit lower-triangular $L$ over $K$, and $D=\mathrm{diag}(\delta_1,\dots,\delta_d)$ with $\delta_k\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}$, such that $P^*AP=LDL^*$.

*Proof.* Induction on $d$. Diagonal entries $a_{jj}=e_j^*Ae_j\in K\cap\mathbb R\cap[0,\infty)=\mathbb Q_{\ge0}$.

*Case all $a_{jj}=0$:* Lemma Z gives $A=0$; take $P=L=I$, $D=0$.

*Case some $a_{jj}=\alpha>0$:* let $P_1$ swap $1\leftrightarrow j$, $A'=P_1^*AP_1=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha&b^*\\ b&C\end{pmatrix}$, $b\in K^{d-1}$, $C\in\mathrm{Herm}_{d-1}(K)$. Set $L_1=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\ b/\alpha&I\end{pmatrix}$, $S=C-bb^*/\alpha$. Direct multiplication:
$$A'=L_1\begin{pmatrix}\alpha&0\\0&S\end{pmatrix}L_1^*.$$
$S\in\mathrm{Herm}_{d-1}(K)$ (no square roots: only division by $\alpha\in\mathbb Q$). $S\succeq0$: $\mathrm{diag}(\alpha,S)=L_1^{-1}A'L_1^{-*}$ with $L_1^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\-b/\alpha&I\end{pmatrix}$ rational; congruence by an invertible matrix preserves PSD ($w^*(M^*A'M)w=(Mw)^*A'(Mw)\ge0$), and $S$ is a principal block of a PSD matrix.

Induction: $P_2^*SP_2=L_2D_2L_2^*$. Put $\Pi=\mathrm{diag}(1,P_2)$, $P=P_1\Pi$, $L=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\ P_2^*b/\alpha&L_2\end{pmatrix}$, $D=\mathrm{diag}(\alpha,D_2)$. Then
$P^*AP=\Pi^*A'\Pi=(\Pi^*L_1\Pi)\,\mathrm{diag}(\alpha,P_2^*SP_2)\,(\Pi^*L_1\Pi)^*=LDL^*$, since $\Pi^*L_1\Pi=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\P_2^*b/\alpha&I\end{pmatrix}$ and $\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\P_2^*b/\alpha&I\end{pmatrix}\mathrm{diag}(1,L_2)=L$. $L$ is unit lower-triangular over $K$; $D$ is nonnegative rational. $\square$

Singular matrices are covered: a zero pivot in $S$ means (Lemma Z) a zero row/column of $S$, so the recursion never divides by zero and $D$ acquires zeros; $\#\{\delta_k>0\}=\mathrm{rank}\,A$ (as $P,L$ are invertible).

**Records.**
- *Assumptions:* $A$ Hermitian over $K$; PSD tested only on vectors in $K^d$ (consistent with Step 10).
- *Zero cases:* $A=0$ (any $d$); $d=1$: $L=1$, $D=(a_{11})$; zero pivots mid-recursion.
- *Operations:* pivot-to-front permutation; rational Schur complement $S=C-bb^*/\alpha$; recursive block assembly $L=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\P_2^*b/\alpha&L_2\end{pmatrix}$.
- *Identities:* $A'=L_1\mathrm{diag}(\alpha,S)L_1^*$; $\mathrm{rank}A=\#$ positive pivots; congruence preserves PSD; Lemma Z.
- *Composition:* recursion valid whenever the active pivot is $>0$; Lemma Z guarantees the alternative is termination.

## 12 — Rational Gram factorization

**Claim.** $A\succeq0$ rational $\Rightarrow$ $A=B^*B$ with $B\in K^{r\times d}$, $r\le4d$.

*Proof.* From 11, $A=PLDL^*P^*=M^*DM$ with $M=L^*P^*\in K^{d\times d}$.

*Four squares.* For $\delta_k>0$ write $\delta_k=a/b$, $a,b\in\mathbb Z_{\ge1}$. Lagrange: $ab=n_1^2+n_2^2+n_3^2+n_4^2$ with $n_i\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}$, so $\delta_k=\frac{ab}{b^2}=\sum_{i=1}^4\big(\tfrac{n_i}{b}\big)^2$. For $\delta_k=0$ use $0=0^2+0^2+0^2+0^2$. Thus $\delta_k=\sum_{i=1}^4c_{k,i}^2$, $c_{k,i}\in\mathbb Q$.

Let $C\in\mathbb Q^{4d\times d}$ have rows indexed by $(k,i)$, row $(k,i)=c_{k,i}e_k^{\mathsf T}$. Then $C^*C=\sum_{k,i}c_{k,i}^2e_ke_k^{\mathsf T}=D$. Hence $A=M^*C^*CM=(CM)^*(CM)$; $B=CM\in K^{4d\times d}$. Deleting zero rows gives $r\le4\,\mathrm{rank}A\le4d$. $\square$

Converse (trivial): $B^*B\succeq0$ since $v^*B^*Bv=\|Bv\|^2$. So rational PSD $\iff$ rational Gram.

**Records.**
- *Assumptions:* Step 11; Lagrange four-square.
- *Zero cases:* $A=0\Rightarrow D=0\Rightarrow C=0$, $B=0$ (or $r=0$); $\delta_k=0$ rows.
- *Operations:* $\delta\mapsto(c_1,\dots,c_4)$ via $ab=\sum n_i^2$; row-stacking $C$; $B=CM$.
- *Identities:* $C^*C=D$; $A=(CM)^*(CM)$; $\mathrm{Herm}_d(K)_{\succeq0}=\{B^*B\}$.
- *Composition:* $B$'s row count is additive under block-diagonal sums; $B$ may be padded with zero rows freely (needed in 13).

## 13 — Rational dilation of a measurement

**Claim.** $A_1,\dots,A_m$ rational PSD, $\sum A_i=I_d$ $\Rightarrow$ rational isometry $W:K^d\to K^N$ and rational orthogonal projections $Q_i$, $\sum Q_i=I_N$, $Q_iQ_j=0$ ($i\ne j$), with $W^*Q_iW=A_i$ and $N\le4md$.

*Proof.* By 12, $A_i=B_i^*B_i$, $B_i\in K^{r_i\times d}$, $r_i\le4d$. Let $N=\sum r_i\le4md$ and
$$W=\begin{pmatrix}B_1\\ \vdots\\ B_m\end{pmatrix}\in K^{N\times d},\qquad Q_i=\mathrm{diag}(0,\dots,I_{r_i},\dots,0).$$
$W^*W=\sum B_i^*B_i=\sum A_i=I_d$, so $W$ is an isometry (rational, no unitary completion used). $Q_i$ are $0/1$ coordinate projections: $Q_i^*=Q_i=Q_i^2$, $Q_iQ_j=0$, $\sum Q_i=I_N$. $W^*Q_iW=B_i^*B_i=A_i$. $\square$

**Records.**
- *Assumptions:* Step 12; $\sum A_i=I$ (used only for $W^*W=I$).
- *Zero cases:* $A_i=0\Rightarrow B_i=0$ (drop it: $r_i=0$, $Q_i=0$); $m=1$: $A_1=I$, any Gram factor $B_1$ with $B_1^*B_1=I$.
- *Operations:* vertical stacking of Gram factors; block coordinate projections.
- *Identities:* $W^*W=\sum B_i^*B_i$; $W^*Q_iW=B_i^*B_i$; $W^*(\sum_{i\in S}Q_i)W=\sum_{i\in S}A_i$.
- *Composition:* coarse-graining outcomes $\leftrightarrow$ summing disjoint $Q_i$; the same $W$ serves every coarse-graining of the given measurement.

## 14 — Effect additivity from measurement consistency

**Setup.** For each rational isometry $W:K^d\to K^N$ (any $N$), $\mu_W$ is defined on rational orthogonal projections of $K^N$, with $\mu_W\ge0$, $\mu_W(I_N)=1$, and $QQ'=0\Rightarrow\mu_W(Q+Q')=\mu_W(Q)+\mu_W(Q')$. **Consistency (C):** $W^*QW=W'^*Q'W'\Rightarrow\mu_W(Q)=\mu_{W'}(Q')$. Values in $\mathbb R_{\ge0}$ (covers $\mathbb Q_{\ge0}$).

**Claim.** $p(A):=\mu_W(Q)$ for any realization $W^*QW=A$ is a well-defined normalized effect-additive $p$ on $E=[0,I]\cap\mathrm{Herm}_d(K)$.

*Proof.* *Existence of a realization:* $A\in E\Rightarrow A,\,I-A$ rational PSD summing to $I$; 13 gives $W,Q_1,Q_2$ with $W^*Q_1W=A$. *Well-defined:* two realizations compress to the same $A$, so **(C)** equates their values. [Use 1 of (C).]

*Normalization:* if $W^*QW=I_d$ then $W^*QW=W^*I_NW$, so **(C)** gives $\mu_W(Q)=\mu_W(I_N)=1$. [Use 2.] *$p\ge0$:* codomain. *$p(0)=0$:* $0\cdot0=0$ orthogonal, so $\mu_W(0)=2\mu_W(0)$.

*Additivity:* $A,B,A+B\in E$. Then $A,\,B,\,I-A-B$ are rational PSD summing to $I$; 13 gives one $W$ and mutually orthogonal $Q_1,Q_2,Q_3$ with $W^*Q_1W=A$, $W^*Q_2W=B$, hence $W^*(Q_1+Q_2)W=A+B$, and $Q_1+Q_2$ is a projection since $Q_1Q_2=0$. By **(C)**, $p(A)=\mu_W(Q_1)$, $p(B)=\mu_W(Q_2)$, $p(A+B)=\mu_W(Q_1+Q_2)$ — the three values now sit in a *single* valuation [Use 3]. Orthogonal additivity of $\mu_W$ gives $p(A+B)=p(A)+p(B)$. $\square$

**Where (C) enters:** (1) independence of $p(A)$ from the dilation; (2) $p(I)=1$; (3) transporting $p(A),p(B),p(A+B)$ into one $\mu_W$ so that orthogonal additivity applies. Without (C), orthogonal additivity constrains each $\mu_W$ separately and nothing relates values across dilations (17 makes this explicit).

**Records.**
- *Assumptions:* 13; per-$W$ normalization and orthogonal additivity; (C).
- *Zero cases:* $A=0$ ($Q=0$), $A=I$; $B=0$ in additivity (trivial).
- *Operations:* realization map $A\mapsto(W,Q)$; joint three-outcome dilation.
- *Identities:* $p(A)=\mu_W(Q)$ for every realization; $p(I-A)=1-p(A)$.
- *Composition:* effect additivity is obtained only when all summands are realized under one $W$ with orthogonal $Q$'s; 13 guarantees this for any finite family of effects summing to $\le I$ (pad with $I-\sum$).

## 15 — Real-valued probabilities

**Claim.** $p:E\to\mathbb R_{\ge0}$, $p(I)=1$, effect-additive, $E=[0,I]\cap\mathrm{Herm}_d(K)$ $\Rightarrow$ unique $\rho\in\mathrm{Herm}_d(\mathbb C)$, $\rho\succeq0$, $\mathrm{Tr}\rho=1$, with $p(A)=\mathrm{Tr}(\rho A)$ on $E$.

*Proof.* Steps 1–7 use only: additivity on $E$, $p\ge0$, rational scaling in the domain, and division by positive integers in the codomain. All hold with codomain $\mathbb R$; $I$ is an order unit (Step 8). So $p$ extends uniquely to a positive unital $\mathbb Q$-linear $F:\mathrm{Herm}_d(K)\to\mathbb R$ — linearity is *derived*, not assumed.

Define $\rho$ from the rational Hermitian basis of Step 9: $\rho_{jj}=F(E_{jj})\in\mathbb R$, $\rho_{jk}=\tfrac12\big(F(S_{jk})+iF(T_{jk})\big)\in\mathbb C$, $\rho_{kj}=\overline{\rho_{jk}}$. Then $\rho$ is complex Hermitian, and $\mathrm{Tr}(\rho\,\cdot)$ agrees with $F$ on the basis; both are $\mathbb Q$-linear $\mathrm{Herm}_d(K)\to\mathbb R$, so $F=\mathrm{Tr}(\rho\,\cdot)$.

$\mathrm{Tr}\rho=F(I)=1$. For $v\in K^d$: $v^*\rho v=F(vv^*)\ge0$; $v\mapsto v^*\rho v$ is a continuous (polynomial) function on $\mathbb C^d$ and $K^d$ is dense, so $\rho\succeq0$. (Continuity of $p$ is never used; only continuity of a fixed quadratic form.)

Uniqueness: if $\mathrm{Tr}(\sigma X)=0$ for all $X\in\mathrm{Herm}_d(K)$ with $\sigma$ complex Hermitian, then $X=E_{jj},S_{jk},T_{jk}$ give $\sigma_{jj}=0$, $\mathrm{Re}\,\sigma_{jk}=0$, $\mathrm{Im}\,\sigma_{jk}=0$. $\square$

**Records.**
- *Assumptions:* Steps 1–9 with codomain $\mathbb R$; density of $K^d$ in $\mathbb C^d$.
- *Zero cases:* $d=1$: $\rho=(1)$; $p(0)=0$ from Step 1.
- *Operations:* basis-coefficient extraction $F\mapsto\rho$ (now with real/complex outputs).
- *Identities:* $F=\mathrm{Tr}(\rho\,\cdot)$ on $\mathrm{Herm}_d(K)$; $\mathrm{Tr}\rho=1$; $v^*\rho v=F(vv^*)$.
- *Composition:* representation is $\mathbb Q$-linear in $F$; uniqueness needs only the $d^2$ basis effects (after scaling into $E$).

## 16 — Extension to all complex effects

**Density with bounds.** Let $A\in[0,I]\subset\mathrm{Herm}_d(\mathbb C)$, $\delta\in\mathbb Q$, $0<\delta\le1/d$. Choose $X\in\mathrm{Herm}_d(K)$ with $|X_{jk}-A_{jk}|\le\delta$ (approximate the upper triangle in $K$, diagonal in $\mathbb Q$, mirror). Then $\|X-A\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le\|X-A\|_F\le d\delta$, so $-d\delta I\preceq X-A\preceq d\delta I$. Define
$$A_\delta=\frac{X+d\delta I}{1+2d\delta}\in\mathrm{Herm}_d(K).$$
$X+d\delta I\succeq A\succeq0$ and $X+d\delta I\preceq A+2d\delta I\preceq(1+2d\delta)I$, so $0\preceq A_\delta\preceq I$: both bounds preserved. Error: $\|A_\delta-X\|=\frac{d\delta\|I-2X\|}{1+2d\delta}\le d\delta(3+2d\delta)$, hence $\|A_\delta-A\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le d\delta(4+2d\delta)\le6d\delta\to0$.

**Continuity derived.** Let $\tilde p:[0,I]\to\mathbb R_{\ge0}$ be any normalized effect-additive function on the complex interval. $\mathrm{Herm}_d(\mathbb C)$ is an ordered $\mathbb Q$-vector space (restrict scalars), so Steps 1–3 apply verbatim: for rational $\varepsilon\in[0,1]$, $-\varepsilon I\preceq A-B\preceq\varepsilon I\Rightarrow|\tilde p(A)-\tilde p(B)|\le\varepsilon$. For Hermitian $X$, $-\varepsilon I\preceq X\preceq\varepsilon I\iff\|X\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le\varepsilon$, and $\|A-B\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le1$ on $[0,I]$. Taking rational $\varepsilon\downarrow\|A-B\|_{\mathrm{op}}$:
$$|\tilde p(A)-\tilde p(B)|\le\|A-B\|_{\mathrm{op}}.$$
So $\tilde p$ is 1-Lipschitz — continuity is a theorem, not a hypothesis.

**Extension and uniqueness.** *Existence:* $\tilde p(A)=\mathrm{Tr}(\rho A)$ with $\rho$ from 15 is linear, normalized, and nonnegative on $[0,I]$ (write $A=B^*B$ over $\mathbb C$: $\mathrm{Tr}(\rho B^*B)=\mathrm{Tr}(B\rho B^*)\ge0$). *Uniqueness:* if $\tilde p$ extends $p$, pick rational effects $A_n\to A$ (above); $\tilde p(A_n)=p(A_n)=\mathrm{Tr}(\rho A_n)\to\mathrm{Tr}(\rho A)$ by continuity of $\mathrm{Tr}$, and $\tilde p(A_n)\to\tilde p(A)$ by the Lipschitz bound. Hence $\tilde p=\mathrm{Tr}(\rho\,\cdot)$ on all of $[0,I]$. $\square$

**Records.**
- *Assumptions:* 15; Steps 1–3 over $\mathrm{Herm}_d(\mathbb C)$ as $\mathbb Q$-space; operator-norm $\leftrightarrow$ order-interval identity.
- *Zero cases:* rational $A$: $\delta\to0$ recovers $A$; $A=0$ or $I$ are already rational.
- *Operations:* shrink-and-shift $X\mapsto(X+d\delta I)/(1+2d\delta)$; rational entrywise approximation.
- *Identities:* $\|X\|_{\mathrm{op}}=\inf\{\varepsilon:-\varepsilon I\preceq X\preceq\varepsilon I\}$; $|\tilde p(A)-\tilde p(B)|\le\|A-B\|_{\mathrm{op}}$.
- *Composition:* any effect-additive normalized function on a dense sub-interval extends uniquely, provided the extension is also effect-additive (which forces Lipschitz).

## 17 — Consistency is independent

**Construction.** $d=2$. $W=I_2:K^2\to K^2$ and $W'=\begin{pmatrix}I_2\\0\end{pmatrix}:K^2\to K^4$, both rational isometries. Take $Q=E_{11}\in\mathrm{Herm}_2$, $Q'=E_{11}\in\mathrm{Herm}_4$. Compressions: $W^*QW=E_{11}=W'^*Q'W'$.

Valuations: $\mu_W(R)=\mathrm{Tr}(E_{11}R)$ on projections of $K^2$; $\mu_{W'}(R)=\mathrm{Tr}(E_{33}R)$ on projections of $K^4$. Each is nonnegative, normalized ($\mathrm{Tr}E_{11}=\mathrm{Tr}E_{33}=1$), and orthogonally additive (linear). But $\mu_W(Q)=1\ne0=\mu_{W'}(Q')$.

**Why.** Normalization and orthogonal additivity are stated per target space; the family $\{\mu_W\}$ may choose an unrelated state $\sigma_W$ on each $K^{N_W}$. (C) is exactly the requirement that all $\sigma_W$ be pullbacks of one object on $K^d$: indeed if $\mu_W=\mathrm{Tr}(W\rho W^*\,\cdot)$ for a single $\rho$, then $\mu_W(Q)=\mathrm{Tr}(\rho\,W^*QW)$ depends only on the compression, so (C) holds. The countermodel has $\sigma_{W'}=E_{33}$ supported outside $\mathrm{ran}\,W'$, which no pullback can produce.

**Records.**
- *Assumptions:* none beyond definitions.
- *Zero cases:* $d=1$ version: $W=e_1,W'=e_2:K\to K^2$, $Q=E_{11},Q'=E_{22}$, $\mu_W=\mu_{W'}=\mathrm{Tr}(E_{11}\cdot)$: compressions both $I_1$, values $1\ne0$.
- *Operations:* per-dilation state assignment $W\mapsto\sigma_W$.
- *Identities:* $\mathrm{Tr}(W\rho W^*Q)=\mathrm{Tr}(\rho W^*QW)$ (pullback form satisfies (C)).
- *Composition:* (C) $\iff$ $\mu_W$ factors through compression $Q\mapsto W^*QW$.

## 18 — Qubit projector countermodel

**Parametrization.** Rational rank-one projections on $K^2$ are exactly $P_{\mathbf n}=\tfrac12(I+x\sigma_x+y\sigma_y+z\sigma_z)$ with $\mathbf n=(x,y,z)\in\mathbb Q^3$, $|\mathbf n|=1$: from $P=\begin{pmatrix}p_{11}&p_{12}\\\overline{p_{12}}&p_{22}\end{pmatrix}$, $z=2p_{11}-1$, $x=2\mathrm{Re}\,p_{12}$, $y=-2\mathrm{Im}\,p_{12}$ are rational, and $P^2=P$, $\mathrm{rank}1\iff|\mathbf n|=1$. Also $I-P_{\mathbf n}=P_{-\mathbf n}$.

**Formula.** $f(P_{\mathbf n})=1$ iff the first nonzero coordinate of $(z,x,y)$ is positive; else $0$. Extend $f(0)=0$, $f(I)=1$.

Deterministic, explicit. $f(P_{\mathbf n})+f(P_{-\mathbf n})=1$ since negation flips the first nonzero sign. Orthogonal additivity: the only orthogonal pairs of rational projections on $K^2$ are $\{0,Q\}$, $\{P,I-P\}$, $\{0,I\}$; $f(0+Q)=f(Q)$ and $f(P+(I-P))=f(I)=1=f(P)+f(I-P)$.

**No representing matrix.** Suppose $\rho$ is any complex Hermitian matrix with $f(P)=\mathrm{Tr}(\rho P)$ on rational rank-one $P$ (positivity not even needed). Write $\mathbf r=(\mathrm{Tr}\rho\sigma_x,\mathrm{Tr}\rho\sigma_y,\mathrm{Tr}\rho\sigma_z)\in\mathbb R^3$, $\tau=\mathrm{Tr}\rho$; then $\mathrm{Tr}(\rho P_{\mathbf n})=\tfrac12(\tau+\mathbf r\cdot\mathbf n)$. Evaluate:
$\mathbf n=(0,0,1)$: $f=1\Rightarrow\tau+r_z=2$; $\mathbf n=(0,0,-1)$: $f=0\Rightarrow\tau-r_z=0$; so $\tau=1,r_z=1$.
$\mathbf n=(1,0,0)$: $f=1\Rightarrow r_x=1$.
$\mathbf n=(\tfrac35,0,\tfrac45)$: $f=1$ (as $z>0$) $\Rightarrow\mathbf r\cdot\mathbf n=\tfrac35+\tfrac45=\tfrac75\ne1$. Contradiction. $\square$

**Records.**
- *Assumptions:* $d=2$; rational Bloch parametrization.
- *Zero cases:* $f(0)=0$, $f(I)=1$; poles $z=\pm1$; equator ties $z=0$ resolved by $x$, then $y$.
- *Operations:* lexicographic hemisphere selector; antipode map $\mathbf n\mapsto-\mathbf n$.
- *Identities:* $I-P_{\mathbf n}=P_{-\mathbf n}$; $\mathrm{Tr}(\rho P_{\mathbf n})=\tfrac12(\mathrm{Tr}\rho+\mathbf r\cdot\mathbf n)$.
- *Composition:* orthogonal additivity at $d=2$ constrains only antipodal pairs, so any antipode-consistent $\{0,1\}$ assignment is a frame function; three non-collinear rational axes already defeat affine representability.

## 19 — Exact obstruction via Step 3

**Projections.** $A=\mathrm{diag}(1,0)=E_{11}$. $B=\tfrac15\begin{pmatrix}4&-2\\-2&1\end{pmatrix}=ww^*/(w^*w)$ with $w=(2,-1)^{\mathsf T}$; directly $B^*=B$, $B^2=\tfrac1{25}\begin{pmatrix}20&-10\\-10&5\end{pmatrix}=B$. Bloch vectors: $\mathbf n_A=(0,0,1)$, $\mathbf n_B=(-\tfrac45,0,\tfrac35)$.

**Bounds.** $A-B=\tfrac15\begin{pmatrix}1&2\\2&-1\end{pmatrix}$.
$\tfrac12I-(A-B)=\tfrac1{10}\begin{pmatrix}3&-4\\-4&7\end{pmatrix}$: trace $1>0$, $\det=\tfrac{21-16}{100}>0$ $\Rightarrow\succeq0$.
$\tfrac12I+(A-B)=\tfrac1{10}\begin{pmatrix}7&4\\4&3\end{pmatrix}$: trace $1$, $\det=\tfrac5{100}>0$ $\Rightarrow\succeq0$.
(A $2\times2$ Hermitian matrix is PSD iff trace $\ge0$ and $\det\ge0$.) So $-\tfrac12I\preceq A-B\preceq\tfrac12I$ with $\varepsilon=\tfrac12$ rational.

**Valuation.** $f(P_{\mathbf n})=1$ iff the first nonzero coordinate of $(2x+z,\ x,\ y)$ is positive; $f(0)=0,f(I)=1$. As in 18 this is deterministic, antipode-consistent, orthogonally additive. $f(A)$: $(1,0,0)\Rightarrow1$. $f(B)$: $2(-\tfrac45)+\tfrac35=-1<0\Rightarrow0$.

**Obstruction.** Suppose $p:[0,I]\cap\mathrm{Herm}_2(K)\to\mathbb R_{\ge0}$ is normalized, effect-additive, and $p|_{\text{proj}}=f$. Step 3 (with $V=\mathrm{Herm}_2(K)$, $u=I$, $\varepsilon=\tfrac12$) gives $|p(A)-p(B)|\le\tfrac12$. But $p(A)-p(B)=1-0=1$. Contradiction. $\square$

*Remark.* The argument uses only $f(A)\ne f(B)$ for one pair with $\|A-B\|_{\mathrm{op}}<1$. Rational rank-one projections form a chain-connected set under $\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{op}}<1$ steps (dense in $S^2$), so every $\{0,1\}$-valued antipode-consistent $f$ — including 18's — is obstructed: it would have to be constant on rank-one projections, contradicting $f(P)+f(I-P)=1$.

**Records.**
- *Assumptions:* Step 3; $2\times2$ Sylvester test.
- *Zero cases:* ties $2x+z=0$ handled lexicographically; $\mathbf n_A,\mathbf n_B$ are not ties.
- *Operations:* rational PSD certificate via trace/det; weight-vector hemisphere selector $\mathbf n\mapsto\mathrm{sign}(\mathbf w\cdot\mathbf n)$, $\mathbf w=(2,0,1)$.
- *Identities:* $\|A-B\|_{\mathrm{op}}^2=\tfrac15$; $\tfrac12I\pm(A-B)\succeq0$.
- *Composition:* Step 3 bounds every pair of effects by their operator distance; a projector valuation extends only if it is $1$-Lipschitz on projections.

## 20 — Composition laws for rational instruments

**Definitions.** An instrument $\mathcal K=\{K_{i,a}:K^d\to K^{e_i}\}_{i\in I,\ a\in A_i}$ (finite index sets) is *normalized* if $\sum_{i,a}K_{i,a}^*K_{i,a}=I_d$. Outcome effect $A_i=\sum_aK_{i,a}^*K_{i,a}\succeq0$, $\sum_iA_i=I_d$. Heisenberg map of outcome $i$: $\Phi_i^*(X)=\sum_aK_{i,a}^*XK_{i,a}$ for $X\in\mathrm{Herm}_{e_i}(K)$. Two instruments are *equal* if they agree up to bijections of outcome and branch indices.

**Composite.** For each $i$, let $\mathcal L_i=\{L_{i,j,b}:K^{e_i}\to K^{g_{i,j}}\}_{j\in J_i,\ b\in B_{i,j}}$ be normalized. Define
$$(\mathcal L\circ\mathcal K)_{(i,j),(a,b)}=L_{i,j,b}K_{i,a}:K^d\to K^{g_{i,j}},\qquad(i,j)\in\textstyle\bigsqcup_i\{i\}\times J_i,\ (a,b)\in A_i\times B_{i,j}.$$
Dimensions: $(g_{i,j}\times e_i)(e_i\times d)=g_{i,j}\times d$. Entries stay in $K$.

**Normalization.**
$$\sum_{i,j,a,b}K_{i,a}^*L_{i,j,b}^*L_{i,j,b}K_{i,a}=\sum_{i,a}K_{i,a}^*\Big(\sum_{j,b}L_{i,j,b}^*L_{i,j,b}\Big)K_{i,a}=\sum_{i,a}K_{i,a}^*I_{e_i}K_{i,a}=I_d.\ \square$$
Composite effects: $A_{(i,j)}=\Phi_i^*(B_{i,j})$ with $B_{i,j}=\sum_bL_{i,j,b}^*L_{i,j,b}$; marginal law $\sum_jA_{(i,j)}=\Phi_i^*(I_{e_i})=A_i$.

**Associativity.** Given a third level $\mathcal N_{i,j}=\{N_{i,j,k,c}:K^{g_{i,j}}\to K^{h_{i,j,k}}\}$, both $(\mathcal N\circ\mathcal L)\circ\mathcal K$ and $\mathcal N\circ(\mathcal L\circ\mathcal K)$ have operators $N_{i,j,k,c}L_{i,j,b}K_{i,a}$ (matrix multiplication is associative), with outcome indices $((i,j),k)\leftrightarrow(i,(j,k))$ and branch indices $((a,b),c)\leftrightarrow(a,(b,c))$ under the canonical bijections of iterated disjoint unions/products. Heisenberg form: $\Phi_{(i,j,k)}^*=\Phi_i^*\circ\Psi_{i,j}^*\circ\Theta_{i,j,k}^*$.

**Identity.** $\mathrm{id}_d=\{I_d\}$ with one outcome $\ast$, one branch $\ast$: normalized. $\mathrm{id}\circ\mathcal K$ has operators $I\cdot K_{i,a}=K_{i,a}$, indices $(i,\ast)\leftrightarrow i$, $(a,\ast)\leftrightarrow a$; $\mathcal K\circ\mathrm{id}$ likewise with $(\ast,i)\leftrightarrow i$. (Here the branch-$\ast$ instrument has $e_\ast=d$.)

**Outcome relabeling.** For a bijection $\varphi:I\to I'$ define $(\varphi_*\mathcal K)_{\varphi(i),a}=K_{i,a}$, $e'_{\varphi(i)}=e_i$. Normalized (same sum). Relabeling commutes with composition: $\varphi_*(\mathcal L\circ\mathcal K)=(\mathcal L\circ\varphi^{-1})\circ\varphi_*\mathcal K$ where the second-level family is reindexed by $\varphi(i)$. Branch relabeling $\psi_i:A_i\to A_i'$ is the same with $A_i$ unchanged. *Coarse-graining* (merging outcomes $i_1,i_2$ with $e_{i_1}=e_{i_2}$): branch set $A_{i_1}\sqcup A_{i_2}$, effect $A_{i_1}+A_{i_2}$; normalization preserved.

**Records.**
- *Assumptions:* finite index sets; normalization of each level; rational entries.
- *Zero cases:* an outcome with all $K_{i,a}=0$ ($A_i=0$, may be deleted); a single-outcome single-branch instrument is a rational isometry $W$ (ties to 13); empty $B_{i,j}$ means outcome $(i,j)$ is absent.
- *Operations:* sequential composition $\circ$; identity; outcome/branch relabeling; coarse-graining; Heisenberg dual $\Phi^*$.
- *Identities:* $\sum K^*L^*LK=\sum K^*K=I$; $A_{(i,j)}=\Phi_i^*(B_{i,j})$; $\sum_jA_{(i,j)}=A_i$; $\Phi_{(i,j)}^*=\Phi_i^*\circ\Psi_{i,j}^*$; $\Phi_i^*$ is positive, $\mathbb Q$-linear, $K$-preserving, $\Phi_i^*(I)=A_i\preceq I$.
- *Composition:* $\mathcal L_i$ must have input dimension $e_i$ exactly; associativity holds up to the canonical index bijections; probabilities under a state $\rho$ compose as $\mathrm{Tr}(\rho A_{(i,j)})=\mathrm{Tr}\big(\Phi_i(\rho)B_{i,j}\big)$ with $\Phi_i(\rho)=\sum_aK_{i,a}\rho K_{i,a}^*$.

**Scope.** 11–13 are purely algebraic over $K$. 14 shows the Born-rule chain (Steps 1–10, 15–16) rests on one non-algebraic input, (C); 17 shows (C) is independent of the per-dilation axioms; 18–19 show that without effect additivity (only orthogonal additivity on projections, $d=2$) the conclusion fails, and Step 3 pinpoints the failure quantitatively. 20 is the bookkeeping that lets instruments be composed without leaving $K$.
