**Target equivalence.** FdQM_ℂ = the monoidal GPT with systems n ∈ ℕ, states D(ℂⁿ), effects {E : 0 ≤ E ≤ I}, reversible maps ρ ↦ UρU†, composition n⊗m = nm with ρ⊗σ. A theory T is *reconstructed* when there are affine bijections φ_A : Ω_A → D(ℂ^{n_A}) that carry effects into POVM elements, reversible transformations into (anti)unitary conjugations, and satisfy φ_{AB}(ω⊗σ) = φ_A(ω)⊗φ_B(σ). That is: a monoidal isomorphism of GPTs. Nothing weaker counts as "complex QM."

**Sufficient axioms.**

- **A0 Framework.** Finitely many distinguishable outcomes per system; compact convex finite-dim Ω_A; composites with bilinear product states and well-defined marginals. (Marginals *are* no-signalling. It is part of what "separate subsystems" means, not a discriminating axiom.)
- **A1 Spectrality.** Every state is a mixture of perfectly distinguishable pure states.
- **A2 Strong symmetry.** Any two frames of equal size are related by a reversible transformation.
- **A3 Local tomography.** Composite states are fixed by joint local statistics: dim V_{AB} = dim V_A · dim V_B.
- **A4 Qubit.** Some capacity-2 system has a 3-parameter state space (pure states form a 2-sphere).

**Proof.**

1. *Single systems are Jordan.* A1 + A2 ⇒ Ω_A is the state space of a Euclidean Jordan algebra J_A (Barnum–Hilgert 2019, closing the gap in Barnum–Müller–Ududec 2014; engine: Koecher–Vinberg, homogeneous self-dual cones = EJA cones of squares). Transitivity by T's reversible group implies transitivity by Aff(Ω), so this step needs no no-restriction hypothesis.
2. *Simple or classical.* Jordan–von Neumann–Wigner: simple EJAs are Herm_n(ℝ), Herm_n(ℂ), Herm_n(ℍ), spin factors V_d, Herm_3(𝕆). A Jordan automorphism permutes central summands, so a direct sum with a rank-≥2 summand has frames it cannot connect ({e,f} inside one summand vs. one idempotent per summand). A2 ⇒ J_A simple, or classical (all summands rank 1).
3. *Complex.* A3 + A4 ⇒ every system is Herm_n(ℂ) (Barnum–Wilce 2014 via Hanche-Olsen 1983). Necessary condition you can check by hand: with K = dim, N = rank, A3 forces K(N_A N_B) = K(N_A)K(N_B); only N² is multiplicative (ℝ: N(N+1)/2, ℍ: N(2N−1), V_d and 𝕆 have no rank ≥ 4 composites at all). Classical systems of rank m ≥ 2 are excluded by the same counting: C⊗Q has dim 4m and rank ≥ 2m, and no simple EJA fits (ℂ needs 2√m ≥ 2m; the others are worse), while a classical composite would make the 3-ball an affine image of a simplex face, i.e. a polytope. So classical systems exist only as decohered restrictions.
4. *Dynamics and measurement.* The reversible group of D(ℂⁿ) is PU(n)⋊ℤ₂ (Wigner); transitivity on n-frames needs an orbit of dim n²−n, and no proper closed subgroup of PU(n) has that dimension, so G_A ⊇ PU(n). One-parameter subgroups are ρ ↦ e^{−iHt}ρe^{iHt}: the von Neumann equation up to the generator. Effects lie in [0,u] of the dual cone, hence 0 ≤ E ≤ I; the Born rule e(ω) = Tr(Eρ) is the duality pairing itself (no Gleason needed; operational states already are probability assignments). Positive maps that stay positive on A⊗B for every B = M_m(ℂ) are completely positive.
5. *Composition.* Step 3 identifies the composite EJA as Herm_{n_A n_B}(ℂ) with the product map the Jordan tensor product; this is the unique locally tomographic composite (Barnum–Wilce). Hence φ_{AB}(ω⊗σ) = ρ⊗σ, and the monoidal isomorphism is assembled.

Contextuality is then a theorem (Kochen–Specker for n ≥ 3; Tsirelson 2√2 for two qubits), not an input.

**Why contextuality + no-signalling fail, by witness.**

| Competing theory | no-sig | contextual | killed by |
|---|---|---|---|
| Classical | ✓ | ✗ | A4 |
| Boxworld (PR boxes) | ✓ | ✓ (maximally) | A1: the gbit square is not spectral, not self-dual |
| Real QM | ✓ | ✓ | A3 (Hardy–Wootters 2012); A4 (rebit is a disk) |
| Quaternionic QM | ✓ | ✓ | A3; A4 (5-ball) |
| V_d, d ≠ 3; octonionic qutrit | ✓ | ✓ | A3: no composites |
| Complex qubits with enlarged composites (e.g. Herm_4(ℍ) pairs) | ✓ | ✓ | A3 alone |

Boxworld and real QM bracket QM from above and below while satisfying both device-independent constraints. The gap is closed by two things device-independence cannot see: single-system convex geometry (A1, A2) and the composition rule (A3).

**Independence audit.**

- A0: operational definition of a subsystem. Clean.
- A1: states are lotteries over ideal alphabets. A classical-reducibility principle; no Hilbert space in sight.
- A3: a global state is a finite record of local tests. Testable: real QM violates it and the Renou et al. 2021 network test disfavors it experimentally.
- A4: Stokes 1852. A two-level system with exactly three real parameters and a spherical pure-state set was measured seventy years before QM existed.
- A2: "equal-size alphabets are physically interchangeable" — a relabeling-invariance principle. This is the load-bearing axiom (it delivers homogeneity and self-duality together) and the one with the thinnest independent justification. Alternatives that trade it away (energy observability, Wilce's conjugate-system assumption) are at least as quantum-flavored.

**Residual.** Reconstruction is proved for finite dimension; infinite dimension is not covered and no known route covers it cleanly. Effects are onto POVM elements only under no-restriction; without it, states and dynamics are still forced and effects embed. The open item on row 8 is now exactly one axiom: derive A2 from something more primitive. For your packet program the certificate to demand of any sub-level theory is that it *implies* A1–A4 at the operational boundary; everything above that is forced.

If useful, next step is the Lean skeleton: A0–A4 as structures, the five theorems as axioms-with-citations, and the counting lemma in step 3 proved outright.
