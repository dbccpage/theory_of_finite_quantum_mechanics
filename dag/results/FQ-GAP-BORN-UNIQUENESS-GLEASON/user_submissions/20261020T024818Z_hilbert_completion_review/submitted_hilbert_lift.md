The lift is three constructions plus three convergence statements, and there is a finite-level test that decides up front whether the completion can be a Hilbert space at all.

**1. Spectral closure at finite stages: K₀ → ℚ̄, no continuum needed**

- The complaint about inverses and eigenvalues is answered by algebraic closure, not by ℂ. Given the ordering on K₀⁺ (see the declared datum below), the real closure of K₀⁺ is ℝ_alg, unique up to unique order-isomorphism (Artin–Schreier), and ℚ̄ = ℝ_alg(i) with * = conjugation.
- The spectral theorem for Hermitian matrices holds over R(i) for any real closed field R: the characteristic polynomial splits, x*x > 0 for x ≠ 0 in an ordered field, so eigenvalues are real and the diagonalization is unitary. Every finite stage — inverses, √, eigenvalues, unitary evolution — lives in ℚ̄. The continuum enters only through the limit over stages.
- Declared datum: an archimedean place of K₀, equivalently a coherent choice of primitive roots (ζ_n) with ζ_{mn}^m = ζ_n, equivalently an orientation. It fixes which of two Born weights is larger: (1+ζ₇)(1+ζ₇⁻¹) vs (1+ζ₇²)(1+ζ₇⁻²) is 3.247 vs 1.555 under ζ₇ ↦ e^{2πi/7} and the reverse under ζ₇ ↦ e^{4πi/7}. The place space is Ẑ^×/±1, profinite, so no finite set of weight comparisons pins it; it is declared, exactly like the completion axiom in row 4. Then ℚ̄ → ℂ is the metric completion at that place (ℚ(i) ⊂ ℚ̄ is dense in ℂ), unique up to isomorphism.

**2. Inner product: fixed before completing, with a decidable test**

- A completion inherits only the norm it is given. Jordan–von Neumann: the completion is a Hilbert space iff the finite-stage norm satisfies the parallelogram law, and ℓ¹ fails it on two cells. If the state norm the derivation carries is ℓ¹, the lift lands in ℓ¹/L¹ — a Banach space — and Hilbert structure is a separate declaration: the polarization of the Born quadratic form.
- The form that respects the cell structure is diagonal, ⟨x, y⟩ = Σ_c w_c x_c* y_c with positive cell weights (or Σ |x_c|²/w_c for cochains read as densities; same construction).

**3. The completion, explicitly**

- Growth (stages are subcomplexes of a union X∞): H = ℓ²(cells of X∞, w), embeddings = extension by zero, isometric. Done.
- Subdivision (stages refine cells): the transition maps are isometries iff the weights are additive under refinement, w_c = Σ w_{c′}. Additive weights are a finitely additive measure on the Boolean algebra B generated by all cells; its Stone space S = lim of the finite stage sets is compact, cells are clopen, and compactness makes any finitely additive measure on the clopen algebra σ-additive on that algebra (a clopen set covered by countably many disjoint clopens uses finitely many). Carathéodory then gives a unique Borel measure μ on S, and H = L²(S, μ). Finite cochains are the locally constant functions, dense. Separable because the index set is countable.
- Corrective to the row's title: all separable infinite-dimensional Hilbert spaces are unitarily isomorphic, so H carries no information about "which continuum". The geometry lives in δ and ∆, not in H; and S is profinite, not a manifold — a spatial continuum is a different row.

**4. Convergence guarantees**

- States: Pα x → x in H, and the Born distributions converge in total variation with the explicit bound Σ_c w_c ||x_c|² − |y_c|²| ≤ ||x − y||·||x + y|| (Cauchy–Schwarz).
- Operators: if the stage coboundaries commute with the transition maps and are uniformly bounded (bounded weighted degree, ||δ||² ≤ 2 sup_v Σ_{e∋v} w_e / w_v), BLT extends them uniquely to H. Otherwise ∆ = δ*δ is a positive symmetric operator on the dense colimit; its Friedrichs extension is self-adjoint, giving the spectral theorem and Stone's e^{−it∆}.
- Dynamics: the compressions ∆α converge to ∆ in strong resolvent sense (monotone form convergence, Simon 1978) provided ⋃ Vα is a form core, and Trotter–Kato upgrades this to e^{−it∆α}Pα → e^{−it∆} strongly, uniformly on compact time intervals.
