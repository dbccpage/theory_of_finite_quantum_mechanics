# Reviewed replacement for Lemma 4.1 and its residual

Status: written theorem with named imports; no Lean certificate. This supplements the original submission and does not edit a frozen registry.

Let K be a commutative field with involution. Assume, for every positive integer n, the norm-one subgroup has a subgroup of exact order n. Then:

1. K has characteristic zero. All roots of unity in K are norm-one elements. Their union is isomorphic, noncanonically, to the abstract directed colimit of Z/n with divisibility transition k -> (n/m)k, hence to Q/Z.
2. The subfield K0 generated by these roots and Q is an involutive Q-field isomorphic to the standard cyclotomic union with complex conjugation. After an embedding, this union equals Q^ab by Kronecker-Weber (the finite-extension theorem followed by a union).
3. For every rational r>=0 there is s in K0 with s*=s and s^2=r, hence ss*=r. The separate rational-weight-witness assumption is redundant.
4. For every embedding in C, the image of K0 is countable, dense and proper for the standard metric, and is neither complete in that metric nor algebraically closed. Its torsion phase subgroup is dense and proper in U(1); the full phase group contains additional nontorsion elements.
5. Independently, fix Qbar within C with standard conjugation. For every finite system of polynomial equations/inequations in amplitudes and their conjugates, and real polynomial weight inequalities, with algebraic parameters, existence of a solution in C is equivalent to existence in Qbar. The proof translates to real and imaginary coordinates and imports real-closed-field quantifier elimination. This claim is about the stated formula language, not arbitrary notions of finite experiments.

D2 is an infinite scheme of finite witnesses. K0 is a minimal required subfield, not a maximal carrier or a sufficient carrier for every finite polynomial system: X^3=2 separates it from Qbar. Within the chosen standard complex metric, completion gives C, and completion of the torsion circle subgroup gives U(1). That metric/completeness choice is an explicit additional input. Continuity of reversible transformations or an unspecified closure postulate alone is not that completion theorem. Any subsequent local-tomography selection retains its declared model/composite class.
