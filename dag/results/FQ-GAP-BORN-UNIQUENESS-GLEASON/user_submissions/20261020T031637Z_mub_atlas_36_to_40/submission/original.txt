What exists as of today is (a) a large body of regional exclusions, (b) a Lean-verified global bound $\mu(6)\le5$, and (c) a three-week-old computer-assisted preprint claiming $\mu(6)=3$ whose atlas and scripts are not public. ## Status (checked today)

- Four MUBs in $\mathbb C^6$ is equivalent to three mutually unbiased complex Hadamard matrices, and a complete classification of complex Hadamard matrices is only available up to dimension 5; the classification in dimension 6 is still out of reach. That is the structural reason chart arguments are conditional: there is no parametrization of the region to be covered.
- Regional exclusions: assuming the first Hadamard comes from the two-parameter Fourier family, a MUB quartet was excluded by discretization and exhaustive computer search; the Fourier matrix $F_6$ (or any element of the Heisenberg–Weyl orbit) cannot be supplemented by two MU Hadamard matrices; among MU product bases, continuous families of pairs and two triples exist but no quadruple, and a complete set cannot contain a triple of MU product bases. Numerically, the maximum average distance of four bases is strictly below unity.
- Global, verified: an exact certificate excludes seven MUBs in $\mathbb C^6$; by Weiner's completion theorem this yields an upper bound of five.
- Claimed: OpenAI's September 2026 preprint claims $\mu(6)=3$ by a computer-assisted argument; the release's Lean proof only gives $\mu(6)\le5$, and its scope document says the bound 3 and the computer-assisted exclusion of four bases are not formalized. A public query notes that the scripts the solution relies on (seed, covout, ub, patchx, atlas, stageB, stageC, proof_run) are not provided.
- Obstruction to the cheapest global route: degree-four vector-coordinate sum-of-squares cannot refute the existence of $m$ mutually unbiased bases in $\mathbb C^d$ for any $m$; projector variables do recover the general bound $m\le d+1$.

So: every quartet is excluded if and only if $\mu(6)=3$, and the only route anyone has proposed for *every* quartet is a certified atlas. Lemmas 36–40 make precise what such an atlas must contain and prove the pieces that do not depend on the atlas.

**Conventions.** $U(6)$ unitary group; $X_0=\{U\in\mathbb C^{6\times6}:|U_{ab}|^2=\tfrac16\ \forall a,b\}$ (every column is a unit vector); $X=X_0\cap U(6)$ = unitary Hadamards. $J$ = all-ones matrix; $|M|^2$ entrywise. Bases $\leftrightarrow$ unitaries by columns. $B_0$ = standard basis. $\|\cdot\|_\infty$ = max entry, $\|\cdot\|_{\rm op}$ = operator norm.

## 36 — Exact reduction and normal form

*Claim.* (i) Bases $U,V$ are mutually unbiased iff $U^*V\in X_0$, i.e. iff $\sqrt d\,A^\dagger B$ has only entries of magnitude one. (ii) A quartet $(B_0,B_1,B_2,B_3)$ exists iff there are $U_1,U_2,U_3\in X$ with $U_i^*U_j\in X_0$ for $i\ne j$. (iii) One may assume $U_1$ dephased ($U_1$'s first row and column equal $\tfrac1{\sqrt6}$), leaving 25 free phases $\theta\in[0,2\pi)^{25}$; the residual symmetry group is generated by monomial unitaries on the left (preserving $B_0$), independent monomial unitaries on the right of each $U_i$, entrywise conjugation, transposition, and permutations of $\{1,2,3\}$; all preserve (ii). (iv) Entries of a quartet are in general not in $\mathbb Q(i)$ (sixth roots of unity already appear in $F_6$), so exact work uses phase coordinates with rational interval enclosures, not $K$-arithmetic.

*Proof.* (i) $|\langle u_a,v_b\rangle|^2=\tfrac16$ for all $a,b$ is $|(U^*V)_{ab}|^2=\tfrac16$. (ii) Unbiasedness to $B_0$ is $U_i\in X_0$, and $U_i$ unitary since $B_i$ is a basis. (iii) Left multiplication by a monomial unitary fixes $B_0$ as a basis; right multiplication reorders/rephases a basis; these let one dephase $U_1$; conjugation and transposition map $X_0,X$ to themselves and commute with $(U,V)\mapsto U^*V$ up to these moves. $\square$

**Records.** *Assumptions:* none. *Zero cases:* $I\notin X_0$; $F_6\in X$ with $(F_6)_{ab}=\tfrac1{\sqrt6}\zeta_6^{ab}$. *Operations:* dephasing; symmetry quotient. *Identities:* MU$(U,V)\iff U^*V\in X_0$; dephased parameter count $36-11=25$. *Admissible compositions:* a Hadamard triple $(U_1,U_2,U_3)$ yields Hadamard pairs $(U_i^*U_j)$; the converse direction is the whole problem.

## 37 — Defect functionals and the two bridges (proved)

For $U\in X_0$ define
$$D(U)=\min_{(V,W)\in X\times X}\ \max\Big\{\big\||U^*V|^2-\tfrac16J\big\|_\infty,\ \big\||U^*W|^2-\tfrac16J\big\|_\infty,\ \big\||V^*W|^2-\tfrac16J\big\|_\infty\Big\},\qquad E(U)=\|U^*U-I\|_\infty .$$

*Claim.* (a) The minimum exists and $D,E$ are continuous on the compact set $X_0$. (b) A quartet with first Hadamard $U$ exists iff $E(U)=0$ and $D(U)=0$. (c) **Quartet bridge:** for $U,U'\in X_0$, $|D(U)-D(U')|\le2\|U-U'\|_{\rm op}$. (d) **Unitarity bridge:** $|E(U)-E(U')|\le2\sqrt6\,\|U-U'\|_{\rm op}$. (e) In phase coordinates with $k$ varying entries, $\|U-U'\|_{\rm op}\le\|U-U'\|_F\le\sqrt{k/6}\,\|\theta-\theta'\|_\infty$; for dephased $U$, $k=25$.

*Proof.* (a) $X\times X$ is compact and the objective continuous. (b) is 36(ii). (c) Columns $u_a,u_a'$ are unit vectors with $\|u_a-u_a'\|\le\|U-U'\|_{\rm op}$; for any unit $y$,
$$\big||\langle u_a,y\rangle|^2-|\langle u_a',y\rangle|^2\big|=\big(|\langle u_a,y\rangle|+|\langle u_a',y\rangle|\big)\big||\langle u_a,y\rangle|-|\langle u_a',y\rangle|\big|\le2\,|\langle u_a-u_a',y\rangle|\le2\|U-U'\|_{\rm op}.$$
Evaluate $U$'s objective at a minimizer $(V,W)$ for $U'$: the $V^*W$ term is unchanged and the other two move by at most $2\|U-U'\|_{\rm op}$; so $D(U)\le D(U')+2\|U-U'\|_{\rm op}$, and symmetrically. (d) $\|U\|_{\rm op}\le\|U\|_F=\sqrt6$ on $X_0$, and $U^*U-U'^*U'=U^*(U-U')+(U-U')^*U'$. (e) $|e^{i\theta}-e^{i\theta'}|\le|\theta-\theta'|$ and each entry has modulus $\tfrac1{\sqrt6}$. $\square$

*Consequences (the bridges).* A certificate $D(U_0)\ge\delta$ at a single point $U_0$ excludes every quartet whose dephased first Hadamard lies in the phase box of half-width $\rho<\delta\sqrt6/10$ around $U_0$. A certificate $E(U_0)\ge\eta$ excludes every Hadamard in the box of half-width $\rho<\eta\sqrt6/(2\sqrt6\cdot\sqrt{25/6})=\eta\sqrt6/10$ — i.e. such a box contains no element of $X$ at all. Note $U_0$ need not be unitary for $D(U_0)$ to be defined: $D$ lives on $X_0$, so box centers can be rational-phase points.

**Records.** *Assumptions:* 36. *Zero cases:* $D(U)=0$ for any $U$ in a quartet; $E=0$ exactly on $X$. *Operations:* $U\mapsto D(U)$, $U\mapsto E(U)$; phase-box inflation. *Identities:* Lipschitz constants $2$ (quartet) and $2\sqrt6$ (unitarity); $\sqrt{25/6}$ phase-to-Frobenius. *Admissible compositions:* box certificates are invariant under the symmetry group of 36, so one certified box excludes its whole orbit.

## 38 — Global coverage: when a finite atlas exists

*Claim.* Let $\delta^*=\min_{U\in X}D(U)$. (a) No quartet exists iff $\delta^*>0$. (b) If $\delta^*>0$, there are $\eta>0$ and a finite set of phase boxes covering $[0,2\pi)^{25}$ such that on every box either $E\ge\eta$ or $D\ge\delta^*/2$; equivalently, a finite atlas of 37-type certificates proving "no quartet" exists. (c) Conversely any finite atlas whose boxes cover the dephased torus and each carry a verified 37-certificate proves $\mu(6)=3$ (with 36 and the $\le5$ bound, $\mu\in\{3\}$).

*Proof.* (a) $D$ is continuous on compact $X\ne\emptyset$, so the min is attained; it is $0$ iff some $U\in X$ has $D(U)=0$ iff a quartet exists (37b). (b) By 37(c), $D\ge\delta^*/2$ on the closed $r$-neighborhood $N$ of $X$ in $X_0$ with $r=\delta^*/4$. On the compact set $X_0\setminus\mathrm{int}\,N$, $E>0$ (as $E=0$ only on $X\subset\mathrm{int}N$), hence $E\ge\eta$ for some $\eta>0$. Uniform continuity of $D,E$ on $X_0$ gives a box half-width $\rho_0$ such that every box of half-width $\le\rho_0$ meeting $N$ has $D\ge\delta^*/4$ on it, and every box missing $N$ has $E\ge\eta/2$; a finite grid of such boxes covers the torus. (c) Each box is quartet-free (37); the boxes cover all dephased first Hadamards (36iii). $\square$

*Why "conditional chart arguments leave this unresolved."* The resolution needed is governed by $\delta^*$ and $\eta$, neither known in advance. An atlas computed at a chosen resolution proves exactly the boxes it certifies; a box that fails to certify is either too coarse or contains a quartet, and the argument cannot tell which without refining. Hence a chart proof is complete only when **every** box certifies, and that fact must itself be a replayable object, not a reported outcome.

**Records.** *Assumptions:* 37; compactness of $X_0$. *Zero cases:* $\delta^*=0$ exactly when a quartet exists (then no atlas can close). *Operations:* neighborhood $N$; grid refinement. *Identities:* $r=\delta^*/4$ gives $D\ge\delta^*/2$ on $N$. *Admissible compositions:* atlases compose with the symmetry quotient; a fundamental domain suffices if the group action is made explicit and verified.

## 39 — Inner certificates: from a point to a margin

For fixed $U\in X_0$ let $S_t(U)=\{v:\ |v_a|^2=\tfrac16\ \forall a,\ \max_b\big||(U^*v)_b|^2-\tfrac16\big|\le t\}$ (vectors $t$-unbiased to $B_0$ and to $U$'s basis).

*Claim.* $D(U)\ge\delta$ iff there do not exist $V,W\in X$ with all columns in $S_\delta(U)$ and $\||V^*W|^2-\tfrac16J\|_\infty<\delta$. Hence a quantitative certificate for a box center consists of (i) a finite box enclosure of $S_\delta(U)$ in the 5-dimensional phase torus of $v$, and (ii) a combinatorial proof that no twelve points of that enclosure can be arranged as two orthonormal bases that are mutually $\delta$-unbiased, each orthogonality and unbiasedness condition checked in interval arithmetic. The exact exclusions already in the literature (Fourier family, $F_6$, product-basis results) prove $D(U)>0$ at those $U$ but return no $\delta$; they therefore cover sets of measure zero in $X$ until each is upgraded to a margin. $\square$

*What the SoS route would need.* A global Positivstellensatz certificate for the system of 36(ii) would be a single atlas-free proof, but degree-four vector-coordinate SoS cannot refute $m$ MUBs for any $m$; the open question is whether projector-variable formulations at degree four, or higher degrees with symmetry reduction, can reach the four-basis case. That is the one route that would avoid coverage entirely.

**Records.** *Assumptions:* 37. *Zero cases:* $S_0(F_6)$ is the classical computed set; $S_t(U)=\emptyset$ for small $t$ never happens ($U$'s own columns are in $S_0(U^*\!\cdot)$ structure only if $U\in X$ — a box with $E$ large may still have nonempty $S_t$). *Operations:* enclosure of $S_t(U)$; twelve-point combinatorial search with interval pruning. *Identities:* $D(U)\ge\delta\iff$ no $\delta$-near quartet over $U$. *Admissible compositions:* enclosures are monotone in $t$; a certificate at $\delta$ implies one at every $\delta'<\delta$, which is what lets boxes be inflated via 37.

## 40 — What closes gap 9 (replay checklist)

A global exclusion is admissible in your DAG when the following are finite, public, and replayable:
1. **Symmetry quotient**: the group of 36(iii) stated as explicit maps on phase coordinates, with a verified fundamental domain or a proof that orbits of certified boxes cover the torus.
2. **Atlas**: a finite list of boxes with rational corners, each tagged `unitarity` ($E\ge\eta$, bridge 37d) or `quartet` ($D\ge\delta$, bridge 37c), with the constants $\eta,\delta$ and half-widths satisfying the inequalities in 37.
3. **Coverage**: a verified statement that the boxes (times symmetry) cover the dephased torus — this is a finite union-of-boxes check, independent of any numerics.
4. **Inner certificates**: for each `quartet` box, the enclosure and combinatorial exclusion of 39, replayable in rational interval arithmetic.
5. **Bridge arithmetic**: 37(c)–(e) applied with the atlas's constants; this is the only place where a point certificate becomes a region certificate, so it is where a reviewer looks first.

Until 1–5 are available for the claimed proof, the correct DAG state is: $\mu(6)\le5$ verified; $\mu(6)=3$ claimed, computer-assisted, not formalized, atlas not public; gap 9 open. That matches your own boundary rule — no target-domain authority without finite realization and descent — and it is the right standard here: the entire content of a global exclusion *is* the atlas.
