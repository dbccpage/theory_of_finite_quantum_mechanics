**Standing setup (applies to every step).** $V$ is an ordered $\mathbb Q$-vector space: $A\le B\Rightarrow A+C\le B+C$, and $0\le A,\ r\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}\Rightarrow 0\le rA$. $u\ge0$, $E=[0,u]$, $p:E\to\mathbb Q_{\ge0}$, $p(u)=1$, $p(A+B)=p(A)+p(B)$ whenever $A,B,A+B\in E$. From Step 1: $p(0)=0$; $A\in E,\ r\in[0,1]\cap\mathbb Q\Rightarrow rA\in E$ (call this **L0**); $p(rA)=r\,p(A)$.

---

## Step 2 — Monotonicity and bounds

**Claim.** $A,B\in E$, $A\le B$ $\Rightarrow$ $B-A\in E$ and $p(A)\le p(B)$. Hence $0\le p(A)\le1$ on $E$.

*Proof.* $A\le B$ gives $0\le B-A$ (translate by $-A$). And $u-(B-A)=(u-B)+A$ is a sum of two elements $\ge0$, so $B-A\le u$. Thus $B-A\in E$.

Now $A$, $B-A$, and $A+(B-A)=B$ all lie in $E$, so additivity applies: $p(B)=p(A)+p(B-A)\ge p(A)$ because $p(B-A)\ge0$ (codomain).

Bounds: $0\le A\le u$ with $0,A,u\in E$, so $p(0)\le p(A)\le p(u)$, i.e. $0\le p(A)\le1$ by $p(0)=0$ and $p(u)=1$. $\square$

**Reusable.**
- *Interval difference:* $A\le B$ in $E$ ⇒ $B-A\in E$ (composition condition: both endpoints in $E$).
- *Monotone decomposition:* $p(B)=p(A)+p(B-A)$ for $A\le B$ in $E$.
- *Bounds:* $p(E)\subseteq[0,1]$; $p$ is order-preserving on $E$.

---

## Step 3 — Quantitative comparison

**Claim.** $A,B\in E$, $\varepsilon\in[0,1]\cap\mathbb Q$, $-\varepsilon u\le A-B\le\varepsilon u$ $\Rightarrow$ $|p(A)-p(B)|\le\varepsilon$.

*Proof.* From $A-B\le\varepsilon u$: $A\le B+\varepsilon u$. The right side may leave $E$, so halve. Set
$$C=\tfrac12B+\tfrac\varepsilon2u .$$
Domain checks: $\tfrac12A,\tfrac12B\in E$ (L0); $\tfrac\varepsilon2u\in E$ (L0 on $u$, $\tfrac\varepsilon2\in[0,1]$); $C\ge0$ as a sum of positives; $u-C=\tfrac12(u-B)+\tfrac{1-\varepsilon}2u\ge0$. So $C\in E$.

Order: $0\le B+\varepsilon u-A$, scale by $\tfrac12$, translate by $\tfrac12A$: $\tfrac12A\le C$. Step 2 gives $p(\tfrac12A)\le p(C)$. Additivity on $\tfrac12B+\tfrac\varepsilon2u=C$ (all three in $E$) and Step 1 give $p(C)=\tfrac12p(B)+\tfrac\varepsilon2$, and $p(\tfrac12A)=\tfrac12p(A)$. Hence $p(A)\le p(B)+\varepsilon$.

Swap the roles of $A,B$ using $B-A\le\varepsilon u$ to get $p(B)\le p(A)+\varepsilon$. $\square$

**Reusable.**
- *Halving trick:* to compare $A\le B+\varepsilon u$ inside $E$, pass to $\tfrac12A\le\tfrac12B+\tfrac\varepsilon2u$; the right side is in $E$ iff $\varepsilon\le1$.
- *Lipschitz bound:* $p$ is 1-Lipschitz w.r.t. the $u$-seminorm $\|X\|_u=\inf\{\varepsilon:-\varepsilon u\le X\le\varepsilon u\}$ on differences of effects.
- Composition condition: $\varepsilon\le1$ is what keeps $C\le u$.

---

**Added assumption for Steps 4–7:** $u$ is an order unit: $\forall X\in V\ \exists n\in\mathbb Z_{\ge1}$ with $-nu\le X\le nu$.

## Step 4 — Well-defined $F_+$

**Claim.** For $X\ge0$ and any integer $n\ge1$ with $X\le nu$ ("admissible $n$"), $X/n\in E$ and $F_+(X):=n\,p(X/n)$ is independent of $n$; $F_+=p$ on $E$.

*Proof.* $X/n\ge0$ by scaling; $u-X/n=\tfrac1n(nu-X)\ge0$. So $X/n\in E$ and $F_+(X)$ is defined.

Let $n,m$ both be admissible. Set $N=nm$. Since $m\ge1$, $Nu-nu=n(m-1)u\ge0$, so $X\le nu\le Nu$; $N$ is admissible. Now $X/N=\tfrac1m\cdot(X/n)$ with $X/n\in E$ and $\tfrac1m\in[0,1]$, so Step 1 gives $p(X/N)=\tfrac1m\,p(X/n)$, hence $N\,p(X/N)=n\,p(X/n)$. By symmetry $N\,p(X/N)=m\,p(X/m)$. So $n\,p(X/n)=m\,p(X/m)$.

For $A\in E$, $n=1$ is admissible and $F_+(A)=p(A)$. $\square$

**Reusable.**
- *Admissible scale:* $X\le nu\Rightarrow X/n\in E$.
- *Scale invariance:* $n\,p(X/n)$ is constant over admissible $n$; the comparison goes through a common multiple.
- $F_+\ge0$ (from codomain of $p$); $F_+|_E=p$.

---

## Step 5 — Additivity on the cone

**Claim.** $X,Y\ge0\Rightarrow F_+(X+Y)=F_+(X)+F_+(Y)$.

*Proof.* Pick $n_X,n_Y$ with $X\le n_Xu$, $Y\le n_Yu$, and set $n=n_X+n_Y$. Then $X\le n_Xu\le nu$ (as $n_Yu\ge0$), likewise $Y\le nu$, and $X+Y\le n_Xu+n_Yu=nu$. So $n$ is admissible for $X$, $Y$, and $X+Y$ simultaneously, and $X/n,\ Y/n,\ (X+Y)/n=X/n+Y/n$ all lie in $E$. Additivity: $p((X+Y)/n)=p(X/n)+p(Y/n)$. Multiply by $n$ and use Step 4 with this common $n$:
$F_+(X+Y)=F_+(X)+F_+(Y)$. $\square$

**Reusable.**
- *Common scale:* $n_X+n_Y$ is admissible for $X$, $Y$, $X+Y$ at once.
- $F_+$ is an additive monoid map $V_+\to\mathbb Q_{\ge0}$.

---

## Step 6 — Homogeneity on the cone

**Claim.** $X\ge0$, $r\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}\Rightarrow F_+(rX)=rF_+(X)$.

*Proof.* $r=0$: $0\le u$, so $n=1$ is admissible for $0$ and $F_+(0)=p(0)=0=0\cdot F_+(X)$.

$r>0$: write $r=a/b$ with $a,b\in\mathbb Z_{\ge1}$. Let $n$ be admissible for $X$. Then $rX\ge0$ and $rX\le rnu\le anu$ (since $(a-r)nu\ge0$), so $N=an$ is admissible for $rX$. Compute, using only Step 1 inside $E$:
$$F_+(rX)=an\,p\!\Big(\frac{rX}{an}\Big)=an\,p\!\Big(\frac1b\cdot\frac Xn\Big)=an\cdot\frac1b\,p\!\Big(\frac Xn\Big)=\frac ab\cdot n\,p\!\Big(\frac Xn\Big)=rF_+(X),$$
where $X/n\in E$ and $\tfrac1b\in[0,1]$ justify the third equality. No linearity on $V$ is assumed: the scale comparison is Step 4's independence plus Step 1's rational homogeneity restricted to $E$. $\square$

**Reusable.**
- *Scale transport:* $n$ admissible for $X$ ⇒ $an$ admissible for $\tfrac abX$.
- $F_+$ is $\mathbb Q_{\ge0}$-homogeneous on $V_+$.

---

## Step 7 — Unique positive linear extension

**Claim.** Every $X\in V$ is $A-B$ with $A,B\ge0$; $F(X):=F_+(A)-F_+(B)$ is well defined, $\mathbb Q$-linear, positive, unital, extends $p$, and is the unique $\mathbb Q$-linear extension of $p$.

*Proof.* *Decomposition:* order unit gives $n$ with $-nu\le X$, so $X+nu\ge0$ and $X=(X+nu)-nu$ with $nu\ge0$.

*Well defined:* if $A-B=A'-B'$ then $A+B'=A'+B$ in $V_+$; Step 5 gives $F_+(A)+F_+(B')=F_+(A')+F_+(B)$, so $F_+(A)-F_+(B)=F_+(A')-F_+(B')$.

*Extends $p$:* $A\in E$: $A=A-0$, $F(A)=F_+(A)-F_+(0)=p(A)$.

*Additive:* $(A-B)+(C-D)=(A+C)-(B+D)$, apply Step 5.

*Homogeneous:* $r\ge0$: $r(A-B)=rA-rB$, apply Step 6. $r<0$: $r(A-B)=|r|B-|r|A$, so $F(rX)=|r|F_+(B)-|r|F_+(A)=rF(X)$.

*Positive:* $X\ge0\Rightarrow X=X-0\Rightarrow F(X)=F_+(X)\ge0$. *Unital:* $F(u)=p(u)=1$.

*Unique:* let $G:V\to\mathbb Q$ be $\mathbb Q$-linear with $G|_E=p$. For $X\ge0$ with admissible $n$: $G(X)=nG(X/n)=n\,p(X/n)=F_+(X)$. For $X=A-B$: $G(X)=G(A)-G(B)=F(X)$. (Positivity and unitality are not needed for uniqueness; they are consequences.) $\square$

**Reusable.**
- *Order-unit decomposition:* $X=(X+nu)-nu$.
- *Grothendieck-style extension:* additive + homogeneous on the cone ⇒ unique linear map on $V=V_+-V_+$.
- Rigidity: a linear functional is determined by its values on $E$.

**Specialization for Steps 8–10:** $V=\mathrm{Herm}_d(\mathbb Q(i))$, $X\le Y\iff Y-X\succeq0$ (PSD over $\mathbb C$), $u=I$.

## Step 8 — Matrix assumptions

**Claim.** $V$ is an ordered $\mathbb Q$-vector space and $I$ is an order unit, with explicit $n$.

*Proof.* *Vector space:* sums and real-rational multiples of Hermitian matrices with $\mathbb Q(i)$ entries are again such.

*Partial order:* reflexive ($0\succeq0$); transitive (PSD closed under $+$); antisymmetric: $X\succeq0$ and $-X\succeq0$ ⇒ $v^*Xv=0\ \forall v\in\mathbb C^d$ ⇒ $X=0$ (a Hermitian form vanishing identically is zero, by polarization). *Translation:* $(Y+C)-(X+C)=Y-X$. *Scaling:* $X\succeq0$, $r\ge0$ ⇒ $v^*(rX)v=r\,v^*Xv\ge0$.

*Order unit:* $I\succeq0$. Let $M=\max_{j,k}\big(|\mathrm{Re}X_{jk}|+|\mathrm{Im}X_{jk}|\big)\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}$ and
$$n=\max\big(1,\lceil dM\rceil\big).$$
For $v\in\mathbb C^d$: $|v^*Xv|\le\sum_{j,k}|X_{jk}||v_j||v_k|\le M\big(\sum_j|v_j|\big)^2\le Md\|v\|^2$ (Cauchy–Schwarz), so $-dM\,I\preceq X\preceq dM\,I$, and since $(n-dM)I\succeq0$, $-nI\preceq X\preceq nI$. $\square$

**Reusable.**
- *Entry bound:* $\|X\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le d\max|X_{jk}|$, hence $n=\max(1,\lceil d\,M\rceil)$ is an admissible scale.
- $\dim_{\mathbb Q}V=d^2$ (basis in Step 9).

---

## Step 9 — Trace representation

**Claim.** Every $\mathbb Q$-linear $F:V\to\mathbb Q$ is $F(X)=\mathrm{Tr}(\rho X)$ for a unique $\rho\in V$.

*Proof.* *Basis:* $E_{jj}$ ($1\le j\le d$), $S_{jk}=E_{jk}+E_{kj}$, $T_{jk}=i(E_{jk}-E_{kj})$ ($j<k$). These are Hermitian with $\mathbb Q(i)$ entries; $d+2\binom d2=d^2$ of them. If $X_{jk}=a_{jk}+ib_{jk}$ ($a,b$ rational, $X_{kj}=\overline{X_{jk}}$, $b_{jj}=0$) then
$$X=\sum_j a_{jj}E_{jj}+\sum_{j<k}\big(a_{jk}S_{jk}+b_{jk}T_{jk}\big),$$
and the coefficients are read off the entries, so the representation is unique: a $\mathbb Q$-basis.

*Construct $\rho$:* for Hermitian $\rho$, $\mathrm{Tr}(\rho E_{jj})=\rho_{jj}$, $\mathrm{Tr}(\rho S_{jk})=\rho_{kj}+\rho_{jk}=2\,\mathrm{Re}\rho_{jk}$, $\mathrm{Tr}(\rho T_{jk})=i(\rho_{kj}-\rho_{jk})=2\,\mathrm{Im}\rho_{jk}$. So define
$$\rho_{jj}=F(E_{jj}),\qquad \rho_{jk}=\tfrac12\big(F(S_{jk})+i\,F(T_{jk})\big),\qquad \rho_{kj}=\overline{\rho_{jk}}\quad(j<k).$$
Then $\rho\in\mathrm{Herm}_d(\mathbb Q(i))$ and $\mathrm{Tr}(\rho\,\cdot)$ agrees with $F$ on the basis. Both are $\mathbb Q$-linear (trace of a product of Hermitian matrices is real, and lies in $\mathbb Q(i)\cap\mathbb R=\mathbb Q$), so they agree on $V$.

*Unique:* if $\mathrm{Tr}(\sigma X)=0$ for all $X\in V$, take $X=\sigma$: $\mathrm{Tr}(\sigma^2)=\sum_{j,k}|\sigma_{jk}|^2=0$, so $\sigma=0$. $\square$

**Reusable.**
- *Rational Hermitian basis* $\{E_{jj},S_{jk},T_{jk}\}$ and the coordinate-extraction identities above.
- *Riesz for $\mathrm{Tr}$:* $V\cong V^*$ via $\rho\mapsto\mathrm{Tr}(\rho\,\cdot)$, with $\mathrm{Tr}(\sigma^2)=\|\sigma\|_F^2$ as the nondegeneracy certificate.

---

## Step 10 — Density-matrix representation

**Claim.** If $F$ is positive and $F(I)=1$, its $\rho$ satisfies $\rho\succeq0$, $\mathrm{Tr}\rho=1$; hence $p(A)=\mathrm{Tr}(\rho A)$ on $E$ with unique $\rho$.

*Proof.* $\mathrm{Tr}\rho=\mathrm{Tr}(\rho I)=F(I)=1$.

For $v\in\mathbb Q(i)^d$, $vv^*\in V$ and $vv^*\succeq0$ (as $w^*vv^*w=|v^*w|^2$). Positivity: $0\le F(vv^*)=\mathrm{Tr}(\rho vv^*)=v^*\rho v$.

*Why rational vectors suffice:* $q(v)=v^*\rho v$ is a real polynomial in the $2d$ real coordinates of $v$, hence continuous on $\mathbb C^d$; $\mathbb Q(i)^d$ is dense in $\mathbb C^d$; a continuous function nonnegative on a dense set is nonnegative everywhere. So $\rho\succeq0$ as a complex matrix, which is the sense required by the order on $V$.

*Conclusion:* By Steps 7–8, $p$ has a unique positive unital $\mathbb Q$-linear extension $F$; by Step 9, $F=\mathrm{Tr}(\rho\,\cdot)$ for a unique $\rho\in V$; by the above, $\rho$ is a density matrix. Thus $p(A)=\mathrm{Tr}(\rho A)$ for every rational effect $A\in[0,I]$. Conversely any density matrix $\rho\in V$ defines such a $p$, so the correspondence is a bijection. $\square$

**Reusable.**
- *Rank-one test effects:* $vv^*$ for $v\in\mathbb Q(i)^d$ (scaled into $[0,I]$ by $1/\|v\|^2$ if needed, which is rational).
- *Density transfer:* positivity on $\mathbb Q(i)^d$ ⇒ PSD over $\mathbb C$, via continuity of Hermitian forms.
- Chain: effect-additivity $\xrightarrow{1\text{–}3}$ monotone 1-Lipschitz $p$ $\xrightarrow{4\text{–}7}$ unique positive unital linear $F$ $\xrightarrow{8\text{–}9}$ $\mathrm{Tr}(\rho\,\cdot)$ $\xrightarrow{10}$ $\rho$ a density matrix.

---

**Scope note.** What is proved: additivity on $[0,I]$ plus the $\mathbb Q(i)$-matrix model forces the trace form. Not proved here: that physical effects form $[0,I]\subseteq\mathrm{Herm}_d$, or that probability assignments must be additive on coexisting effects — those are the inputs your finite-QM program has to supply separately.
