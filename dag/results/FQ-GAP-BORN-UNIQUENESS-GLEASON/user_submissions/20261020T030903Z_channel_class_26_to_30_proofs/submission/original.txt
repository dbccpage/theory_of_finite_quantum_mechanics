## Standing conventions

- $K \subseteq \mathbb{C}$ is a subfield closed under complex conjugation; $K=\mathbb{Q}$ or $K=\mathbb{Q}(i)$ throughout. "Rational" means entries in $K$.
- $M_n = M_n(K)$; $E_{jk} = e_j e_k^{*}$ are matrix units; $D_n \subset M_n$ is the diagonal subalgebra, identified with $K^n$ via $\mathrm{diag}$.
- $\Delta_n : M_n \to M_n$, $\Delta_n(X) = \sum_j X_{jj}E_{jj}$. Facts used: $\Delta_n^2=\Delta_n$, $\Delta_n^{\dagger}=\Delta_n$ (Hilbert–Schmidt), $\mathrm{im}\,\Delta_n = D_n$, $\ker \Delta_n = $ off-diagonal matrices.
- Choi matrix of a $K$-linear $\Phi: M_d \to M_e$: $J(\Phi) = \sum_{j,k} E_{jk}\otimes \Phi(E_{jk}) \in M_d\otimes M_e$ (block $(j,k)$ is $\Phi(E_{jk})$).
- Classical kernel of $\Phi$: $S_\Phi \in K^{e\times d}$, $(S_\Phi)_{kj} = \langle k|\Phi(E_{jj})|k\rangle$. Equivalently $S_\Phi$ is the matrix of $\Delta_e \Phi \Delta_d$ restricted to $D_d \to D_e$ in the bases $\{E_{jj}\}$, $\{E_{kk}\}$.
- A Kraus family $\{K_i\}_{i=1}^r \subset K^{e\times d}$ is *normalized* if $\sum_i K_i^{*}K_i = I_d$.

Results from CC-21–CC-25 used as black boxes: (Choi) $\Phi$ is CP iff $J(\Phi)\succeq 0$; (Kraus) $J(\Phi)\succeq 0$ iff $\Phi(X)=\sum_i K_iXK_i^{*}$ for some finite family; $\Phi$ is TP iff $\sum_i K_i^{*}K_i = I_d$.

## CC-26 — Finite normalization test

**Assumptions.** $\Phi: M_d \to M_e$ is $K$-linear, $d \ge 1$.

**Test family.** $\mathcal{F}_d$ consists of the $d^2$ matrices

$$E_{jj}\ (1\le j\le d),\qquad \rho^{+}_{jk} = \tfrac12 (e_j+e_k)(e_j+e_k)^{*},\qquad \rho^{-}_{jk} = \tfrac12 (e_j - i e_k)(e_j - i e_k)^{*}\quad (j<k).$$

Count: $d + 2\binom{d}{2} = d^2$. Each is a rank-one projector with trace $1$ (the $\tfrac12$ makes the projector itself rational; no $\sqrt2$ enters). Entries lie in $\mathbb{Q}$ resp. $\mathbb{Q}(i)$.

**Statement.** $\Phi$ is CPTP $\iff$ $J(\Phi)\succeq 0$ and $\operatorname{Tr}\Phi(\rho) = 1$ for every $\rho\in\mathcal{F}_d$.

**Proof.**
*CP part.* $J(\Phi)\succeq 0 \iff \Phi$ CP is Choi's theorem (CC-21).

*TP part.* $\Phi$ is TP iff the linear functional $\lambda = \operatorname{Tr}\circ\Phi - \operatorname{Tr}$ vanishes on $M_d$, iff it vanishes on a spanning set. $\mathcal{F}_d$ spans $M_d$ by the explicit inversion identities (exact, verified):

$$E_{jk} = \rho^{+}_{jk} - i\rho^{-}_{jk} - \tfrac{1-i}{2}(E_{jj}+E_{kk}),\qquad
E_{kj} = \rho^{+}_{jk} + i\rho^{-}_{jk} - \tfrac{1+i}{2}(E_{jj}+E_{kk}).$$

Since $|\mathcal{F}_d| = d^2 = \dim M_d$, $\mathcal{F}_d$ is a basis. Hence $\lambda=0 \iff \lambda(\rho)=0\ \forall\rho\in\mathcal{F}_d \iff \operatorname{Tr}\Phi(\rho) = \operatorname{Tr}\rho = 1$.

Explicitly, the $\Leftarrow$ direction on off-diagonal units: $\operatorname{Tr}\Phi(E_{jk}) = 1 - i - \tfrac{1-i}{2}\cdot 2 = 0$ and $\operatorname{Tr}\Phi(E_{kj}) = 1 + i - (1+i) = 0$. $\square$

**Why CP alone is insufficient.** $\{\Phi : J(\Phi)\succeq 0\}$ is a convex *cone*: closed under $\Phi\mapsto t\Phi$, $t\ge 0$. Trace preservation is an *affine* constraint ($\operatorname{Tr}_e J(\Phi) = I_d$), not a cone condition, so it cannot be implied by positivity. Minimal witness: $\Phi(X) = AXA^{*}$, $A = \mathrm{diag}(1,\tfrac12)$. One Kraus operator, so $J\succeq0$; but $\operatorname{Tr}\Phi(E_{11}) = \tfrac14 \ne 1$ (verified). CP with $\sum K_i^{*}K_i \le I$ is a *quantum operation* (post-selected, sub-normalized); the equality $\sum K_i^{*}K_i = I$ is exactly the deterministic (no post-selection) assumption and has to be stated.

**Zero cases.** $d=1$: $\mathcal{F}_1=\{1\}$, test reduces to $\operatorname{Tr}\Phi(1)=1$. $e$ arbitrary (including $e=1$: $\Phi$ a positive functional, TP iff $\Phi(\rho)=1$ on $\mathcal{F}_d$). $\Phi=0$: CP, fails TP at every $\rho$.

**Operations.** CPTP maps are closed under convex combination (both conditions preserved), composition, and tensor product (below). The TP test is linear in $\Phi$; the CP test is a cone condition.

**Identities.** TP $\iff \operatorname{Tr}_e J(\Phi) = I_d \iff \Phi^{\dagger}(I_e) = I_d$ (unital dual). Counting: $\operatorname{Tr}_e J = I_d$ is $d^2$ scalar equations, matching $|\mathcal{F}_d|$; $\mathcal{F}_d$ is a *minimal* test set.

**Composition conditions.** $\Psi: M_d\to M_e$, $\Phi: M_e\to M_g$ CPTP $\Rightarrow$ $\Phi\circ\Psi$ CPTP: $\operatorname{Tr}\Phi(\Psi(X)) = \operatorname{Tr}\Psi(X) = \operatorname{Tr}X$; Kraus family $\{K_iL_j\}$ with $\sum_{ij}(K_iL_j)^{*}K_iL_j = \sum_j L_j^{*}(\sum_i K_i^{*}K_i)L_j = I_d$. Tensor: $\{K_i\otimes L_j\}$, $\sum (K_i\otimes L_j)^{*}(K_i\otimes L_j) = I\otimes I$.

**Exact check.** Rank of $\mathcal{F}_d$ as a $d^2\times d^2$ matrix equals $d^2$ for $d=2,3,4$; both inversion identities hold symbolically; the $A=\mathrm{diag}(1,\tfrac12)$ witness has PSD Choi and trace $\tfrac14$ on $E_{11}$.

## CC-27 — Exact kernel-composition condition

**Assumptions.** $\Psi: M_d\to M_e$, $\Phi: M_e\to M_g$ $K$-linear (no positivity needed). $S_\Psi \in K^{e\times d}$, $S_\Phi\in K^{g\times e}$ as above.

**Statement.**
$$S_{\Phi\circ\Psi} = S_\Phi S_\Psi \iff \Omega := \Delta_g\,\Phi\,(\mathrm{Id}-\Delta_e)\,\Psi\,\Delta_d = 0 .$$

**Proof.** Identify $D_n\cong K^n$. For any linear $\Theta: M_d\to M_g$, the matrix of $\Delta_g\Theta\Delta_d: D_d\to D_g$ in the unit bases is exactly $S_\Theta$ (definition). Two applications:

1. $S_{\Phi\circ\Psi}$ is the matrix of $\Delta_g\,\Phi\Psi\,\Delta_d$.
2. $S_\Phi S_\Psi$ is the matrix of $(\Delta_g\Phi\Delta_e)(\Delta_e\Psi\Delta_d) = \Delta_g\Phi\Delta_e\Psi\Delta_d$ (using $\Delta_e^2=\Delta_e$).

Both maps have domain $D_d$ (they factor through $\Delta_d$) and codomain $D_g$, so they are equal iff their matrices are equal. Their difference is $\Delta_g\Phi\Psi\Delta_d - \Delta_g\Phi\Delta_e\Psi\Delta_d = \Delta_g\Phi(\mathrm{Id}-\Delta_e)\Psi\Delta_d = \Omega$. Hence $S_{\Phi\circ\Psi}=S_\Phi S_\Psi \iff \Omega = 0$. $\square$

Reading: $\Omega$ is the *coherence leak* — the part of $\Psi$'s output off-diagonals (generated from classical inputs) that $\Phi$ folds back into the diagonal.

**Weaker sufficient conditions.** Dephasing covariance of either factor implies $\Omega=0$, but each of the following strictly weaker conditions already suffices:

- (a) *Schrödinger-classical $\Psi$:* $(\mathrm{Id}-\Delta_e)\Psi\Delta_d = 0$, i.e. $\Psi(D_d)\subseteq D_e$. Implied by $\Delta_e\Psi = \Psi\Delta_d$ (then $(\mathrm{Id}-\Delta_e)\Psi\Delta_d = \Psi\Delta_d - \Psi\Delta_d^2 = 0$), but weaker: it constrains $\Psi$ only on diagonal inputs.
- (b) *Heisenberg-classical $\Phi$:* $\Delta_g\Phi(\mathrm{Id}-\Delta_e) = 0$, i.e. $\Delta_g\Phi = \Delta_g\Phi\Delta_e$. Implied by $\Phi\Delta_e=\Delta_g\Phi$ (then $\Delta_g\Phi(\mathrm{Id}-\Delta_e) = \Phi(\Delta_e-\Delta_e^2)=0$). Dual form: (b) $\iff$ $\Phi^{\dagger}(E_{mm})\in D_e$ for all $m$, since $\langle m|\Phi(Y)|m\rangle = \operatorname{Tr}(\Phi^{\dagger}(E_{mm})^{*}Y)$ vanishes for all off-diagonal $Y$ iff $\Phi^{\dagger}(E_{mm})$ is diagonal. (Verified for both a positive and a negative instance.)
- (c) *Disjoint coherence support:* let $P\subseteq\{(a,b):a\ne b\}$ be the set of off-diagonal positions occurring in $\Psi(E_{jj})$, $j=1..d$. If $\Delta_g\Phi(E_{ab}) = 0$ for all $(a,b)\in P$ then $\Omega=0$. This is the exact content of $\Omega=0$ written positionally; (a) is $P=\varnothing$, (b) is $P$ unrestricted.

**Exact rational examples.**

*Failure.* $U = \tfrac15\begin{pmatrix}3&4\\-4&3\end{pmatrix}$, $\Psi=\Phi=\mathrm{Ad}_U$. $S_{\mathrm{Ad}_U} = (|U_{kj}|^2) = \tfrac1{25}\begin{pmatrix}9&16\\16&9\end{pmatrix}$ (unistochastic).
$$S_U^2 = \tfrac1{625}\begin{pmatrix}337&288\\288&337\end{pmatrix},\qquad S_{U^2} = \tfrac1{625}\begin{pmatrix}49&576\\576&49\end{pmatrix},\qquad S_{U^2}-S_U^2 = \tfrac{288}{625}\begin{pmatrix}-1&1\\1&-1\end{pmatrix}.$$
Direct: $\Psi(E_{00}) = uu^{*}$, $u=(\tfrac35,-\tfrac45)$, off-diagonal part $-\tfrac{12}{25}(E_{01}+E_{10})$; conjugating by $U$ and taking the diagonal gives $\Omega(E_{00}) = \tfrac{1}{625}\mathrm{diag}(-288, 288)\neq0$. Note $U^2$ is still rational ($\tfrac1{25}\begin{pmatrix}-7&24\\-24&-7\end{pmatrix}$), so everything stays in $\mathbb{Q}$.

*Success 1 — condition (a), not covariant.* $\Psi$ = measure in the $X$/$Y$ eigenbases and prepare $e_0$ or $e_1$: Kraus $L_m = o_m v_m^{*}/2$ with $v\in\{e_0{+}e_1,\ e_0{-}e_1,\ e_0{+}ie_1,\ e_0{-}ie_1\}$, $o\in\{e_0,e_1,e_0,e_1\}$. $\sum L_m^{*}L_m = I$ (verified). $\Psi(E_{00})=\Psi(E_{11})=\tfrac12 I$, so $\Psi(D_2)\subseteq D_2$ and $S_{\mathrm{Ad}_U\circ\Psi} = S_U S_\Psi$ (verified). $\Psi$ is *not* dephasing-covariant: $\Psi(E_{01}) = \tfrac{1+i}{4}(E_{00}-E_{11})\neq0=\Psi(\Delta E_{01})$.

*Success 2 — condition (b).* $\Phi = \mathrm{Ad}_U\circ\Delta_2$. Then $\Delta\Phi(\mathrm{Id}-\Delta)=0$ trivially and $S_{\Phi\circ\mathrm{Ad}_U} = S_\Phi S_U$ (verified).

*Success 3 — condition (c) only.* $d=3$, $A = \mathrm{diag}(U,1)$, $B=\mathrm{diag}(1,U)$, $\Psi=\mathrm{Ad}_A$, $\Phi=\mathrm{Ad}_B$. $\Psi$ creates coherence only at positions $P=\{(0,1),(1,0)\}$; $B E_{01}B^{*} = e_0 (Be_1)^{*}$ has zero diagonal, likewise $E_{10}$. So $\Omega=0$ and $S_{BA} = S_B S_A$ (verified), although (a) fails ($\mathrm{Ad}_A(E_{00})$ has an off-diagonal entry), (b) fails ($\Delta(BE_{12}B^{*}) = \tfrac{12}{25}E_{11}\ne 0$), and $\mathrm{Ad}_B$ is not covariant.

**Zero cases.** $d=1$: $\Delta_1=\mathrm{Id}$, $\Omega = \Delta_g\Phi(\mathrm{Id}-\Delta_e)\Psi$, still nontrivial if $\Psi(1)$ has off-diagonals. $e=1$: $\Delta_1 = \mathrm{Id}$, $\Omega=0$ always — a classical bottleneck always composes. $\Phi$ or $\Psi$ zero: $\Omega=0$.

**Operations.** $\Omega$ is bilinear in $(\Phi,\Psi)$; conditions (a) and (b) are closed under linear combination and under pre-/post-composition with dephasing-covariant maps.

**Identities.** $S_{\Delta_n} = I_n$; $S_{\mathrm{Ad}_U} = (|U_{kj}|^2)$; $S_{\Phi\circ\Delta_e} = S_\Phi = S_{\Delta_g\circ\Phi}$; $S_{\Phi\otimes\Psi} = S_\Phi\otimes S_\Psi$ (Kronecker, since $\Delta$ is multiplicative on tensor units).

**Composition conditions.** Three-fold: $\Omega(\Phi,\Psi\circ\Theta)=0$ and $\Omega(\Psi,\Theta)=0$ together give $S_{\Phi\Psi\Theta} = S_\Phi S_{\Psi\Theta} = S_\Phi S_\Psi S_\Theta$ (equally: $\Omega(\Phi\circ\Psi,\Theta)=0$ and $\Omega(\Phi,\Psi)=0$). These are sufficient, not necessary — leaks at two junctions can cancel. The condition must be imposed at each junction; kernels do not compose associatively by default.

## CC-28 — Classical lift and identities

**Assumptions.** $S\in\mathbb{Q}^{e\times d}$ column-stochastic: $S_{kj}\ge0$, $\sum_k S_{kj} = 1$ for every $j$. Define $E(S): M_d\to M_e$,
$$E(S)(X) = \mathrm{diag}\big(S\cdot\mathrm{diag}(X)\big),$$
where $\mathrm{diag}(X)\in K^d$ is the diagonal vector of $X$ and $\mathrm{diag}(\cdot)$ on a vector returns the $e\times e$ diagonal matrix. Dimensions: $S$ is $e\times d$, Kraus operators are $e\times d$, $E(S)$ maps $M_d\to M_e$.

**Statement 1 (normalized rational Kraus operators).** $E(S)$ has a Kraus representation with operators in $\mathbb{Q}^{e\times d}$ and $\sum K^{*}K = I_d$.

**Proof.** The obvious family $\sqrt{S_{kj}}\,E_{kj}$ is normalized but irrational when $S_{kj}$ is not a square. Split instead: write $S_{kj} = p/q$ in lowest terms; then $pq\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge0}$ is a sum of four integer squares, $pq = \sum_{m=1}^{4} a_m^2$ (Lagrange), so
$$S_{kj} = \frac{pq}{q^2} = \sum_{m=1}^{4}\Big(\frac{a_m}{q}\Big)^2 .$$
Take $K_{kj,m} = \tfrac{a_m}{q}\,E_{kj}$ (drop $a_m=0$). Then $\sum_m K_{kj,m}^{*}K_{kj,m} = S_{kj}E_{jj}$, and
$$\sum_{k,j,m}K_{kj,m}^{*}K_{kj,m} = \sum_j\Big(\sum_k S_{kj}\Big)E_{jj} = I_d,\qquad
\sum_{k,j,m}K_{kj,m}XK_{kj,m}^{*} = \sum_{k,j}S_{kj}X_{jj}E_{kk} = \mathrm{diag}(S\,\mathrm{diag}X).$$
At most $4\,\#\{S_{kj}\ne0\}$ Kraus operators, all rational. $\square$

(Verified on $S = \begin{pmatrix}\tfrac12&1&0\\ \tfrac12&0&1\end{pmatrix}$ and on $S_7 = \begin{pmatrix}\tfrac17&\tfrac34\\ \tfrac67&\tfrac14\end{pmatrix}$: $\tfrac17 = \tfrac{4+1+1+1}{49}$, $\tfrac67 = \tfrac{25+16+1}{49}$, $\tfrac34 = 3\cdot(\tfrac12)^2$, $\tfrac14 = (\tfrac12)^2$; 11 operators, normalized, equal to $E(S_7)$.)

**Statement 2 (functoriality).** For $T\in\mathbb{Q}^{g\times e}$, $S\in\mathbb{Q}^{e\times d}$ column-stochastic, $E(TS) = E(T)\circ E(S)$.

**Proof.** $\mathrm{diag}(\mathrm{diag}(v)) = v$ for vectors $v$. So $E(T)(E(S)(X)) = \mathrm{diag}(T\,\mathrm{diag}(\mathrm{diag}(S\,\mathrm{diag}X))) = \mathrm{diag}(TS\,\mathrm{diag}X) = E(TS)(X)$. Composition condition: the row count of $S$ must equal the column count of $T$ ($e$). $TS$ is column-stochastic, so $E(TS)$ is again CPTP. $\square$

**Statement 3 ($E(I)=\Delta$ and where it is an identity).** $E(I_d)(X) = \mathrm{diag}(\mathrm{diag}X) = \Delta_d(X)$. $\Delta_d\ne\mathrm{Id}_{M_d}$ for $d\ge2$ ($\Delta_d(E_{01})=0$). $\Delta_d$ *is* the identity on $D_d = \mathrm{im}\,\Delta_d$ (the classical state space $\cong$ probability vectors in $\mathbb{Q}^d$, i.e. diagonal density matrices). Precisely: $E$ is a faithful functor from (column-stochastic matrices, composition) to (CPTP maps, composition); it is not full and does not preserve identities into $\mathrm{Chan}(M_d)$. It *does* preserve identities when the target objects are taken to be $D_d$ (equivalently, in the idempotent-splitting of $\{\Delta_d\}$ inside $\mathrm{Chan}$): $E(I_d)|_{D_d} = \mathrm{id}_{D_d}$.

**Zero cases.** $d=1$ or $e=1$: $S$ is a single column/row, $E(S)(X) = \operatorname{Tr}(X)\,\mathrm{diag}(S)$ (state preparation) resp. $X\mapsto\sum_j S_{1j}X_{jj}$ (a diagonal POVM functional). $S$ with a zero entry: that Kraus operator is omitted. $S$ a permutation matrix $P_\sigma$: $E(P_\sigma) = \mathrm{Ad}_{P_\sigma}\circ\Delta_d$, one Kraus operator per column.

**Operations.** $E$ is linear in $S$ (so convex combinations of stochastic matrices lift to convex combinations of channels); $E(S\otimes T) = E(S)\otimes E(T)$; $E(S)$ absorbs dephasing on both sides.

**Identities.** $E(S)\circ\Delta_d = E(S) = \Delta_e\circ E(S)$; $S_{E(S)} = S$ (the lift is a section of the kernel map $\Phi\mapsto S_\Phi$); $J(E(S)) = \sum_{k,j}S_{kj}E_{jj}\otimes E_{kk}$ is diagonal PSD with rational entries; $E(S)^{\dagger}(Y) = \mathrm{diag}(S^{T}\mathrm{diag}Y)$, unital iff $S$ is column-stochastic.

**Composition conditions.** $S_{E(T)\circ E(S)} = TS = S_{E(T)}S_{E(S)}$ — consistent with CC-27 because $(\mathrm{Id}-\Delta_e)E(S)\Delta_d = 0$ (condition (a) holds for every lift). Mixed composition $\Phi\circ E(S)$ for a general channel $\Phi$ has kernel $S_\Phi S$ (condition (a) again); $E(T)\circ\Psi$ has kernel $T S_\Psi$ (condition (b): $\Delta E(T)(\mathrm{Id}-\Delta) = 0$).

## CC-29 — Channel equality

**Assumptions.** $\{K_i\}_{i=1}^{r}$, $\{L_j\}_{j=1}^{s}\subset K^{e\times d}$, possibly $r\ne s$; $\Phi_K(X)=\sum_i K_iXK_i^{*}$, $\Phi_L(X)=\sum_jL_jXL_j^{*}$.

**Statement 1.** $\Phi_K=\Phi_L$ (as maps $M_d\to M_e$) $\iff$ $J(\Phi_K)=J(\Phi_L)$.

**Proof.** $J:\mathrm{Lin}_K(M_d,M_e)\to M_d\otimes M_e$ is $K$-linear with explicit inverse $\Phi(X) = \sum_{j,k}X_{jk}\,J(\Phi)_{(j,k)\text{-block}}$ (equivalently $\Phi(X)=\operatorname{Tr}_1[(X^{T}\otimes I_e)J(\Phi)]$). A linear map with a two-sided inverse is a bijection, so $\Phi_K=\Phi_L\iff J(\Phi_K)=J(\Phi_L)$. No positivity is used. For Kraus families, $J(\Phi_K) = \sum_i \mathrm{vec}(K_i)\mathrm{vec}(K_i)^{*}$ with $\mathrm{vec}(K) = \sum_j e_j\otimes Ke_j$, so the test is a rational Gram identity. $\square$

**Statement 2 (composition and tensor descend).** If $J(\Phi)=J(\Phi')$ and $J(\Psi)=J(\Psi')$ then $J(\Phi\circ\Psi)=J(\Phi'\circ\Psi')$ and $J(\Phi\otimes\Psi)=J(\Phi'\otimes\Psi')$.

**Proof.** By Statement 1, $\Phi=\Phi'$ and $\Psi=\Psi'$ as maps; hence $\Phi\circ\Psi=\Phi'\circ\Psi'$ and $\Phi\otimes\Psi=\Phi'\otimes\Psi'$ as maps; apply $J$. Kraus level: $\Phi\circ\Psi$ is presented by $\{K_iL_j\}$ and $\Phi\otimes\Psi$ by $\{K_i\otimes L_j\}$ — rational if the factors are. Explicit Choi-level formulas (both verified exactly):
$$J(\Phi\circ\Psi) = \operatorname{Tr}_e\!\big[(J(\Psi)\otimes I_g)(I_d\otimes J(\Phi)^{T_e})\big],\qquad
J(\Phi\otimes\Psi) = P\,(J(\Phi)\otimes J(\Psi))\,P^{T},$$
where $T_e$ is partial transpose on the middle factor and $P$ is the leg permutation $(d,e,d',e')\mapsto(d,d',e,e')$. Composition requires the codomain dimension of $\Psi$ to equal the domain dimension of $\Phi$. $\square$

**Channel equality vs. Kraus-index permutation.** Presentations of $\mathrm{Id}_{M_2}$:
$$\{I\},\qquad \{\tfrac35 I,\ \tfrac45 I\},\qquad \{\tfrac5{13}I,\ \tfrac{12}{13}I\},\qquad \{\tfrac13 I,\ \tfrac23 I,\ \tfrac23 I\},\qquad \{\tfrac12 I\}^{\times4},\qquad \{\tfrac{1+i}{2}I,\ \tfrac{1-i}{2}I\}.$$
Each is normalized and each has $J = |\Omega\rangle\langle\Omega|$, $|\Omega\rangle = e_0\otimes e_0+e_1\otimes e_1$ (verified). Sizes $1,2,2,3,4,2$: no bijection of indices exists between most pairs, so "same multiset of Kraus operators up to permutation" is a strictly finer relation than "same channel". The general relation is $L_j = \sum_i V_{ji}K_i$ with $V$ an isometry after zero-padding (here $V = (\tfrac35,\tfrac45)^{T}$ etc.); index permutations are the special case $V$ a permutation matrix. Same-size, non-permutation example: dephasing $\{E_{00},E_{11}\}$ vs. $\{\tfrac15(3E_{00}+4E_{11}),\ \tfrac15(-4E_{00}+3E_{11})\}$ — same Choi, not a permutation (verified).

**Zero cases.** Empty Kraus family: the zero map, $J=0$. $d=1$ or $e=1$: $J$ is a single PSD matrix in $M_e$ resp. $M_d$; equality is literal matrix equality. One-element families $\{K\}$, $\{L\}$: equal iff $L=\lambda K$, $|\lambda|=1$.

**Operations.** Choi classes are closed under convex combination ($J$ affine), composition, tensor product, and adjoint ($J(\Phi^{\dagger})$ is the swap-conjugate of $J(\Phi)$).

**Identities.** $J(\mathrm{Id}_d) = |\Omega_d\rangle\langle\Omega_d|$; $J(\Delta_d) = \sum_j E_{jj}\otimes E_{jj}$; $J(\mathrm{Ad}_U) = |(I\otimes U)\Omega\rangle\langle\cdot|$; $\operatorname{Tr}_e J(\Phi) = I_d$ iff TP; $J(\Phi_K)=\sum_i\mathrm{vec}(K_i)\mathrm{vec}(K_i)^{*}$.

**Composition conditions.** Well-definedness on classes needs nothing beyond Statement 1; the Kraus-level product $\{K_iL_j\}$ depends on the chosen presentations, but its Choi matrix does not (verified by composing $\mathrm{Ad}_U$ with two different presentations of the same dephasing channel).

## CC-30 — Constructive rational unitary completion

**Assumptions.** $v\in K^{n}$, $v^{*}v=1$, $v\neq e_1$. Set $w = e_1 - v$, $c = 1-\overline{v_1}$, $U = I - \dfrac{ww^{*}}{c}$.

**Claim 1: $c\neq0$.** If $c=0$ then $v_1 = 1$, so $|v_1|^2=1=v^{*}v$ forces $v_k=0$ for $k\ge2$, i.e. $v=e_1$ — excluded. $\square$

**Claim 2: $U^{*}U = I$.** $U^{*} = I - ww^{*}/\bar c$. Compute $w^{*}w = (e_1-v)^{*}(e_1-v) = 1 - v_1 - \overline{v_1} + 1 = c+\bar c$. Then
$$U^{*}U = I - ww^{*}\Big(\frac1c+\frac1{\bar c}\Big) + \frac{w(w^{*}w)w^{*}}{c\bar c}
= I - ww^{*}\frac{c+\bar c}{c\bar c} + ww^{*}\frac{c+\bar c}{c\bar c} = I. \qquad\square$$

**Claim 3: $Ue_1 = v$.** $w^{*}e_1 = 1-\overline{v_1} = c$, so $Ue_1 = e_1 - w\,(w^{*}e_1)/c = e_1 - w = v$. $\square$

All operations are field operations in $K$ (one division by $c\in K\setminus\{0\}$), so $U\in K^{n\times n}$. For $K=\mathbb{Q}$ (real $v$), $U$ is the Householder reflection: symmetric, $U^2=I$, $\det U=-1$. For $v_1\in\mathbb{R}$ with complex other entries, $U$ is still a Hermitian involution (example: $v=(\tfrac35,\tfrac{4i}{5})$ gives $U = \begin{pmatrix}\tfrac35&-\tfrac{4i}5\\ \tfrac{4i}5&-\tfrac35\end{pmatrix}$, $U=U^{*}$, $\det=-1$); for $v_1\notin\mathbb{R}$, $c$ is complex and $U$ is unitary but not Hermitian.

**Separate case $v=e_1$.** Then $c=0$ and the formula is undefined; set $U=I$. Intermediate case $v=\lambda e_1$, $|\lambda|=1$, $\lambda\ne1$: $c = 1-\bar\lambda\ne0$ and the formula gives $U=\mathrm{diag}(\lambda,1,\dots,1)$ (verified for $\lambda=-1,\ i,\ \tfrac{3+4i}5$).

**Inductive completion.** Let $W\in K^{n\times m}$ with $W^{*}W=I_m$, $0\le m\le n$. Construct a unitary $U\in K^{n\times n}$ with $U e_j = We_j$, $j\le m$:

- $m=0$: $U=I_n$.
- $m\ge1$: let $U_1$ be the Claim-1–3 unitary for $v=We_1$ (or $I$ if $We_1=e_1$). Then $R = U_1^{*}W$ has first column $U_1^{*}v = e_1$, and its columns are orthonormal, so columns $2..m$ are orthogonal to $e_1$, hence have zero first entry:
$$R = \begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&W'\end{pmatrix},\qquad W'\in K^{(n-1)\times(m-1)},\quad W'^{*}W'=I_{m-1}.$$
By induction there is a unitary $U'\in K^{(n-1)\times(n-1)}$ completing $W'$. Set $U = U_1\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&U'\end{pmatrix}$: a product of $K$-unitaries, and $U e_j = U_1\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&U'\end{pmatrix}e_j = U_1 R e_j = W e_j$ for $j\le m$. $\square$

Example (verified): $W = \tfrac13\begin{pmatrix}1&2\\2&1\\2&-2\end{pmatrix}$ completes to $U=\tfrac13\begin{pmatrix}1&2&2\\2&1&-2\\2&-2&1\end{pmatrix}$, rational, $U^{*}U=I$, $\det U=-1$.

**Zero cases.** $n=1$: $v=(v_1)$ with $|v_1|=1$; if $v_1\ne1$ then $w=1-v_1$, $ww^{*}/c = (1-v_1)$, $U = v_1$. $m=n$: $W$ is already unitary; the induction returns $W$ up to the trivial last step. $v$ with $v_1=0$: $c=1$, $w = e_1-v$, $U = I - ww^{*}$ (verified for $v=(0,\tfrac35,\tfrac45)$).

**Operations.** Products, inverses ($U^{-1}=U^{*}$), and direct sums of $K$-unitaries are $K$-unitaries, so the construction stays inside $K$ at every step; no square roots are ever taken because $v$ is given with $v^{*}v=1$ exactly.

**Identities.** $U^{*}=U^{-1} = I - ww^{*}/\bar c$; $w^{*}w = c+\bar c$; $w^{*}v = -\bar c$, so $Uv = v + \tfrac{\bar c}{c}\,w$. When $c$ is real (i.e. $v_1\in\mathbb{R}$, which includes all real $v$): $U = U^{*} = U^{-1}$ is a Hermitian involution, $Uv = e_1$ (it swaps $e_1\leftrightarrow v$), $U(e_1+v)=e_1+v$, $U(e_1-v)=-(e_1-v)$; for real $v$ it is moreover real symmetric with $\det U=-1$. When $v_1\notin\mathbb{R}$ (e.g. $v=\tfrac{3+4i}{5}e_1$): $U$ is unitary with $Ue_1=v$ but $U\ne U^{*}$ and $Uv\ne e_1$ in general (verified both ways).

**Composition conditions.** Completion is unique only up to the orthogonal complement: any two completions differ by $U' = U\begin{pmatrix}I_m&0\\0&V\end{pmatrix}$ with $V\in K^{(n-m)\times(n-m)}$ unitary. Relevance to CC-29: a rational partial isometry between two Kraus presentations extends to a rational unitary by this construction, so the "isometric mixing" relation of CC-29 can be stated with a full rational unitary without leaving $K$.
