## Abstract

We present a unified, non-redundant synthesis of the 43 discrete quantum foundation packets (`QM_002` through `QM_212`), formalizing quantum mechanics as an obstruction calculus over finite carrier graphs and algebraic scalar fields. By distinguishing a finite presentation (finite cell complexes, exact algebraic cyclotomic number fields, and rational rays) from a finite set of states, this monograph organizes quantum structures foundation-first from primitive distinction events up to contextuality and asymptotic error thresholds.

The principal results establish exact constructions and discrete obstructions under explicitly declared hypotheses:

1. Bare distinction events construct a directed coboundary operator $d_0$ with an obligation monoid cost floor $\ge 2$, where $\ell^1$ vertex repair $\Phi_1([x]) = \min_\alpha \|x - d_0\alpha\|_1$ admits exact primal/dual certificates with zero duality gap on bipartite carriers, while even cycles ($C_4$) exhibit non-unique minimizers requiring an explicit dihedral selector.
2. Superposition is characterized as a quotient fiber over report equivalence classes; coherent double-slit cross-terms $2ab \ne 0$ are separated from pinched diagonal mixtures; and Sorkin third-order interference vanishes identically ($I_3 = 0$) under quadratic readout, uniquely selecting quadratic forms within symmetric degree-4 polynomial classes.
3. The $U(1)$ phase selector conditionally forces the elliptic division involution $i^2 = -1$ over $\mathbb{Q}$, since split-complex rings $\mathbb{R}[j]/(j^2-1)$ contain zero divisors and dual numbers $\mathbb{R}[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2)$ contain nilpotents.
4. Diagonal observables depend strictly on $\ell^1$ sector weights $w_i \ge 0$, whereas rank-1 Hilbert lifts $\psi_i = \pm \sqrt{w_i} e^{i\theta_i}$ introduce off-diagonal coherence redundancy; Gleason-type frame additivity on rational rays in $\mathbb{Q}^3$ eliminates degree-4 isotropic rivals; and Busch's POVM additivity reconstructs states while highlighting the gap between polyhedral frame cones and the PSD Bloch ball.
5. Continuous Schrödinger evolution is stepped over $\mathbb{Q}(i)$ without transcendental exponentials or norm drift via exact Cayley transforms $U = (I-A)(I+A)^{-1}$, while first-order Euler stepping strictly increases $\ell^2$ norms.
6. Entanglement is characterized as a convex hull separability failure on bipartite correlation tensors, yielding Tsirelson bounds and establishing Mermin–Peres contextuality as an $\mathbb{F}_2$ linear cochain parity obstruction.
7. Completely Positive Trace-Preserving (CPTP) maps are analyzed as stochastic kernel lifts bounded by trace-norm contractions and certified by exact dual witnesses.

In strict adherence to Claim Hygiene rules, these results do not derive the continuum, the infinite-dimensional Hilbert space, the continuous unitary Lie group, or an individual-outcome wavepacket collapse mechanism from primitive difference. Every theorem carries its declared algebraic premises and finite certificates.

## 1. Pre-Genesis, Primitive Difference, and Quotient Repair ($\Phi_1$)

### 1.1 Directed Graphs, Coboundaries, and Difference Events

Let $G = (V, E)$ be a finite directed graph. We define the discrete cochain spaces $C_0(G; K)$ and $C_1(G; K)$ over an algebraic field $K$ (typically $\mathbb{Q}$ or an exact cyclotomic extension $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_n)$). The primitive distinction coboundary map $d_0: C_0 \to C_1$ is defined on directed edges $e = (u, v) \in E$ by:
$$(d_0 f)(u, v) = f(v) - f(u).$$

From bare distinction events without metric or topological data, $(d_0 f)(u, u) = 0$ holds identically. In Lean 4 formalization ([`PrimitiveDifference.lean`](file:///c:/Users/a343o/source/repos/theory_of_finite_quantum_mechanics/lean4/PrimitiveDifference.lean#L1-L958)), distinction events obey an obligation monoid structure where asserting a fresh non-trivial distinction imposes an obligation cost floor $\ge 2$ ([`DerivationDagQMClosure.lean`](file:///c:/Users/a343o/source/repos/theory_of_finite_quantum_mechanics/lean4/DerivationDagQMClosure.lean#L6-L58)).

### 1.2 The $\ell^1$ Quotient Repair Seminorm ($\Phi_1$)

Given an edge cochain $x \in C_1(G; \mathbb{R})$, the vertex-repair functional $\Phi_1([x])$ evaluates the minimum $\ell^1$ distance from $x$ to the coboundary subspace $B_1 = \mathrm{im}(d_0)$:
$$\Phi_1([x]) = \min_{\alpha \in C_0} \|x - d_0 \alpha\|_1 = \min_{\alpha \in C_0} \sum_{(u,v) \in E} |x(u,v) - (\alpha(v) - \alpha(u))|.$$

The dual linear program evaluates the maximum over cycle circulations $\gamma \in \ker(d_0^\dagger) = Z_1$:
$$\Phi_1^*([x]) = \max \left\{ \langle \gamma, x \rangle : \|\gamma\|_\infty \le 1, \, d_0^\dagger \gamma = 0 \right\}.$$

On finite bipartite and simplicial carriers, strong duality holds with exact rational duality gap zero. However, on even cycles ($C_{2k}$ for $k \ge 2$), minimizers are non-unique:

- On $C_4$ with alternating edge residual $x = (1, -1, 1, -1)$, there exist distinct optimal vertex potentials $\alpha^{(1)} \ne \alpha^{(2)}$ attaining the same minimum repair cost $\Phi_1([x]) = 2$.
- Consequently, edge sector weights cannot be uniquely deduced from $\Phi_1$ alone without supplying an explicit dihedral or symmetry selector.

### 1.3 The 8-Rung QM Assumption Ladder and Single-System Countermodels

The transition from bare cochain repair to operational quantum mechanics requires pricing each imported structure:

## 2. Superposition, Sorkin $I_3 = 0$, and the $U(1)$ Phase Selector

### 2.1 Superposition as the Quotient Fiber

A physical state is defined as an equivalence class of edge residuals modulo the repair kernel:
$$[\psi] \in C_1(G; K) / \mathrm{im}(d_0).$$
Basis states correspond to single-edge cocycles, whereas coherent superpositions involve multi-edge distributions with relative algebraic phases. When evaluated under a quadratic readout functional $R(\psi) = |\psi|^2$, superpositions $\psi = a e_1 + b e_2$ yield:
$$R(a e_1 + b e_2) = R(a e_1) + R(b e_2) + 2 \mathrm{Re}(a^* b).$$
The interference term $2 \mathrm{Re}(a^* b)$ is non-vanishing for $a, b \ne 0$, distinguishing coherent superpositions from diagonal classical mixtures $\rho_{\mathrm{mix}} = |a|^2 |e_1\rangle\langle e_1| + |b|^2 |e_2\rangle\langle e_2|$ where the off-diagonal expectation vanishes identically.

### 2.2 Sorkin Third-Order Interference ($I_3 = 0$)

In a three-path interferometer, the Sorkin third-order interference parameter $I_3(a, b, c)$ is defined by:
$$I_3(a, b, c) = P(a+b+c) - [P(a+b) + P(b+c) + P(a+c)] + [P(a) + P(b) + P(c)].$$

Under a pure quadratic readout $P(x) = c_2 x^2$, expanding the sums shows in this model:
$$I_3(c_2 x^2)(a, b, c) = 0 \quad \text{identically for all } a, b, c \in K.$$
If a quartic perturbation $q_4 x^4$ is admitted ($P(x) = c_2 x^2 + q_4 x^4$), evaluating at the unit triple $(1, 1, 1)$ yields:
$$I_3(c_2 x^2 + q_4 x^4)(1, 1, 1) = 36 q_4.$$
Frame normalization on $(1, 0, 0)$ and $(3/5, 4/5, 0)$ requires $c_2 = 1$ and eliminates isotropic higher-degree rivals. Hence, within the symmetric degree-4 polynomial class, $I_3 = 0$ uniquely forces $(c_2, q_4, r_4) = (1, 0, 0)$ ([`FiniteQM.lean`](file:///c:/Users/a343o/source/repos/theory_of_finite_quantum_mechanics/lean4/FiniteQM.lean#L64-L81)).

### 2.3 The $U(1)$ Phase Selector

Why are quantum amplitudes evaluated over $\mathbb{C} \cong \mathbb{R}[i]/(i^2+1)$ rather than alternative two-dimensional commutative algebras over $\mathbb{R}$?
We examine the three real associative unital 2-algebras $\mathbb{R}[x]/(x^2 - \sigma)$:

1. **Elliptic ($\sigma = -1$, Complex $\mathbb{C}$):** $i^2 = -1$. The norm $N(a+bi) = a^2 + b^2$ is strictly positive-definite and multiplicative. $\mathbb{C}$ is a field.
2. **Hyperbolic ($\sigma = +1$, Split-Complex $\mathbb{D}$):** $j^2 = +1$. The algebra contains non-trivial zero divisors: $(1 - j)(1 + j) = 1 - j^2 = 0$. Invertibility fails on light-cone rays $a = \pm b$.
3. **Parabolic ($\sigma = 0$, Dual Numbers $\mathbb{G}$):** $\varepsilon^2 = 0$. The algebra contains non-zero nilpotents $\varepsilon \ne 0$ with $N(\varepsilon) = 0$.

Under the requirement of norm-multiplicative composition and invertibility on all non-zero elements over $\mathbb{Q}$, only the elliptic involution $i^2 = -1$ survives ([`DerivationDagQMClosure.lean`](file:///c:/Users/a343o/source/repos/theory_of_finite_quantum_mechanics/lean4/DerivationDagQMClosure.lean#L685-L704)).

### 2.4 Discrete Plaquette Holonomy and Gauge Invariance

On a 2-dimensional cell complex with directed edges $E$ and faces $F$, a gauge potential is a 1-cochain $A \in C^1(G; \mathrm{U}(1))$. The discrete magnetic flux through face $\sigma \in F$ is the coboundary $\Phi_\sigma = (d_1 A)(\sigma) = \sum_{e \in \partial \sigma} A_e$.
Under vertex gauge transformations $f \in C^0(V; \mathbb{R})$:
$$A_e \mapsto A_e + (d_0 f)(e).$$
Because $d_1 \circ d_0 = 0$, the holonomy $\oint_{\partial \sigma} A = d_1 A$ is strictly gauge-invariant, modulating two-path coherent interference while vanishing on contractible trees.

## 3. Born Normalization, Conditional Hilbert Lifts, and State-Effect Pairings

### 3.1 $\ell^1$ Sector Weights and Rank-1 Hilbert Lifts

Let $w = (w_1, \dots, w_n)$ be non-negative sector weights with $\sum_{i=1}^n w_i = 1$. The diagonal state representation is:
$$\rho_{\mathrm{diag}} = \sum_{i=1}^n w_i |e_i\rangle\langle e_i|.$$
A rank-1 conditional Hilbert lift is defined by choosing phase angles $\theta_i$:
$$|\psi\rangle = \sum_{i=1}^n \sqrt{w_i} e^{i\theta_i} |e_i\rangle, \quad \rho_{\mathrm{lift}} = |\psi\rangle\langle\psi|.$$
For all diagonal projection observables $P_k = |e_k\rangle\langle e_k|$, the expectations coincide identically:
$$\mathrm{Tr}(P_k \rho_{\mathrm{diag}}) = w_k = \mathrm{Tr}(P_k \rho_{\mathrm{lift}}).$$
However, off-diagonal coherence terms in $\rho_{\mathrm{lift}}$ produce a strict purity divergence:
$$\mathrm{Tr}(\rho_{\mathrm{diag}}^2) = \sum_{i=1}^n w_i^2 < 1 = \mathrm{Tr}(\rho_{\mathrm{lift}}^2) \quad (\text{for non-degenerate } w).$$
Thus, the Born rule on diagonal projectors is redundant with $\ell^1$ sector normalization, while off-diagonal phase interference is an extra, supplied Hilbert-space structure.

### 3.2 Rational Gleason Elimination in Dimension Three

Gleason's theorem establishes that any frame-additive measure on projective rays in $\mathbb{R}^d$ ($d \ge 3$) is generated by a density operator $\rho$. On the rational unit sphere $S^2(\mathbb{Q})$, we consider homogeneous polynomial readouts $P(x)$ satisfying frame additivity $\sum_{i=1}^3 P(v_i) = 1$ for all mutually orthogonal triples $\{v_1, v_2, v_3\} \subset \mathbb{Q}^3$.
Decomposing into quadratic and quartic invariants:
$$P_2(x) = x_1^2 + x_2^2 + x_3^2 = 1, \quad P_4(x) = x_1^4 + x_2^4 + x_3^4, \quad P_{22}(x) = x_1^2 x_2^2 + x_2^2 x_3^2 + x_1^2 x_3^2.$$
Using the identity $P_2^2 = P_4 + 2 P_{22} = 1$ on $S^2(\mathbb{Q})$, testing against the rational frames $(1,0,0)$, $(3/5, 4/5, 0)$, and $(1/3, 2/3, 2/3)$ eliminates all degree-4 anisotropic perturbations, forcing the quadratic trace functional ([`FiniteQM.lean`](file:///c:/Users/a343o/source/repos/theory_of_finite_quantum_mechanics/lean4/FiniteQM.lean#L7-L63)).

### 3.3 Busch's POVM Theorem and Polyhedral Frame Cones in $d=2$

While Gleason's theorem fails for projective rays in $d=2$, Busch's theorem restores linearity on Positive Operator-Valued Measures (POVMs). Let $E_i \ge 0$ with $\sum_{i=1}^m E_i = I$. Informationally complete POVMs (such as the symmetric 4-effect tetrahedral POVM in $d=2$) allow linear state reconstruction:
$$\rho = \sum_{i=1}^4 c_i E_i.$$
However, the polyhedral cone generated by finite POVM effects $\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{poly}} = \mathrm{cone}(E_1, \dots, E_m)$ forms a strict interior subset of the continuous Positive Semi-Definite (PSD) Bloch ball cone $\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{PSD}}$, demonstrating the finite polyhedral approximation boundary.

### 3.4 Galois Conjugate Negativity in $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{12})$

Over algebraic number fields, positivity depends on the chosen real embedding. In $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{12})$, the maximal real subfield is $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{3})$. Consider the vector $v = 2 + 2\zeta_{12} - \zeta_{12}^3$.

- In the standard embedding $\sigma_1(\sqrt{3}) \approx +1.732$, the norm square is $N(v) = 7 + 4\sqrt{3} > 0$.
- In the conjugate embedding $\sigma_2(\sqrt{3}) \approx -1.732$, the conjugate norm is $\sigma_2(N(v)) = 7 - 4\sqrt{3} > 0$.
However, linear functional evaluations $f(v) = A N_0 + B N_1$ with $B \ne 0$ break Galois invariance and can flip sign under $\sigma_2$. Total positivity therefore requires explicit Sum-of-Squares (SOS) certificates across all real embeddings.

## 4. Reversible Dynamics, Cayley Stepping, and Feynman Path Sums

### 4.1 The Finite Wintner–Wielandt Trace Obstruction

In infinite dimensions, the canonical commutation relation $[X, P] = i\hbar I$ governs quantum dynamics. In any finite dimension $N < \infty$, taking the trace of both sides yields:
$$\mathrm{Tr}([X, P]) = \mathrm{Tr}(XP - PX) = 0 \ne i\hbar N.$$
Thus, unbounded continuous momentum cannot be represented by finite matrices. The truncated discrete position and momentum operators $X_N, P_N \in M_N(\mathbb{C})$ exhibit a rank-1 boundary defect:
$$[X_N, P_N] = i \hbar \left( I_N - N |e_N\rangle\langle e_N| \right).$$

### 4.2 The Schwinger–Weyl Clock-Shift Algebra

In finite dimension $N$, the canonical algebra is replaced by the Schwinger–Weyl clock and shift operators:
$$Z |k\rangle = \omega^k |k\rangle, \quad X |k\rangle = |k+1 \pmod N\rangle, \quad \omega = e^{2\pi i / N}.$$
They satisfy the commutation braiding relation:
$$Z X = \omega X Z, \quad Z^N = X^N = I.$$
The commutant is strictly scalar ($\{A : [A, Z] = [A, X] = 0\} = \mathbb{C} I$), establishing irreducibility. From any normalized seed eigenvector $v$ of an alternative clock operator $Z'$, the intertwiner matrix:
$$U = \left[ v, \, X' v, \, (X')^2 v, \, \dots, \, (X')^{N-1} v \right]$$

### 4.3 Exact Rational Cayley Stepping vs. Euler Discretization

To simulate Schrödinger evolution $\frac{d\psi}{dt} = -i H \psi$ over an exact rational field $\mathbb{Q}(i)$ without transcendental matrix exponentials $e^{-iHt}$, we employ the Cayley transform. Let $A$ be skew-Hermitian ($A^\dagger = -A$, e.g. $A = -i H \Delta t / 2$).
The Cayley stepping operator is:
$$U = (I - A)(I + A)^{-1}.$$
We verify unitarity directly:
$$U^\dagger U = \left( (I + A)^{-1} \right)^\dagger (I - A)^\dagger (I - A) (I + A)^{-1} = (I - A)^{-1} (I + A) (I - A) (I + A)^{-1} = I.$$
Because $[I-A, I+A] = 0$, $U^\dagger U = I$ holds strictly over $\mathbb{Q}(i)$ without norm drift.
In contrast, first-order Euler discretization $U_{\mathrm{Euler}} = I - A$ violates unitarity:
$$\| (I - A) \psi \|^2 = \langle \psi | (I + A^\dagger)(I - A) | \psi \rangle = \langle \psi | (I - A^2) | \psi \rangle = \|\psi\|^2 + \|A \psi\|^2 > \|\psi\|^2 \quad (\text{for } A\psi \ne 0).$$

## 5. Non-Factorization, Entanglement, and Correlation Separability

### 5.1 Product Non-Factorization as a Bipartite Obstruction

Let $\mathcal{H}_{AB} = \mathcal{H}_A \otimes \mathcal{H}_B$ be a composite quantum state space. A bipartite state $\rho_{AB}$ is separable if it lies in the convex hull of product states:
$$\rho_{\mathrm{sep}} = \sum_k p_k \rho_A^{(k)} \otimes \rho_B^{(k)}, \quad p_k \ge 0, \, \sum p_k = 1.$$

### 5.2 Finite Bell Inequalities and Tsirelson Bounds

In a bipartite Bell test with binary settings $A_0, A_1, B_0, B_1 \in \{\pm 1\}$, the Clauser–Horne–Shimony–Holt (CHSH) parameter is:
$$S = \langle A_0 B_0 \rangle + \langle A_0 B_1 \rangle + \langle A_1 B_0 \rangle - \langle A_1 B_1 \rangle.$$

1. **Classical / Local Hidden Variable Bound:** The existence of a joint probability distribution forces $|S| \le 2$.
2. **Rational Quantum Model:** A supplied rational state and measurement geometry over $\mathbb{Q}$ achieves $S = 14/5 = 2.8$.
3. **Algebraic Tsirelson Bound:** A supplied quantum model over $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2}) \subset \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_8)$ attains the maximal quantum bound $S = 2\sqrt{2} \approx 2.8284$, certified by an exact Sum-of-Squares (SOS) dual certificate:
$$2\sqrt{2} I - S = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \left( (A_0 - \frac{B_0 + B_1}{\sqrt{2}})^2 + (A_1 - \frac{B_0 - B_1}{\sqrt{2}})^2 \right) \ge 0.$$

### 5.3 Mermin–Peres Contextuality as an $\mathbb{F}_2$ Cochain Obstruction

In dimension four (two qubits), consider the nine observables of the Mermin–Peres magic square:
$$\begin{pmatrix} \sigma_x \otimes I & I \otimes \sigma_x & \sigma_x \otimes \sigma_x \\ I \otimes \sigma_y & \sigma_y \otimes I & \sigma_y \otimes \sigma_y \\ \sigma_x \otimes \sigma_y & \sigma_y \otimes \sigma_x & \sigma_z \otimes \sigma_z \end{pmatrix}.$$
Each row and column consists of mutually commuting observables. The product of operators along each row is $+I$, and along the first two columns is $+I$, but along the third column is $-I$:
$$\prod_{j=1}^3 R_{3j} = (\sigma_x \otimes \sigma_y)(\sigma_y \otimes \sigma_x)(\sigma_z \otimes \sigma_z) = -I.$$

Summing all six equations yields $0 = 1 \pmod 2$, establishing a discrete parity obstruction with zero solution space.

## 6. CPTP Channels, Trace Norm Geometry, and Quantum Dual Witnesses

### 6.1 Completely Positive Trace-Preserving (CPTP) Maps

Quantum operations are represented by linear maps $\mathcal{E}: M_m(\mathbb{C}) \to M_n(\mathbb{C})$. A map is CPTP if:

1. **Trace-Preserving:** $\mathrm{Tr}(\mathcal{E}(\rho)) = \mathrm{Tr}(\rho)$ for all $\rho$.
2. **Completely Positive:** The amplified map $\mathcal{E} \otimes \mathrm{id}_k$ is positive for all $k \ge 1$.

In the Choi–Jamiołkowski representation, $\mathcal{E}$ is completely positive if and only if the Choi matrix:
$$J(\mathcal{E}) = \sum_{i,j=1}^m |i\rangle\langle j| \otimes \mathcal{E}(|i\rangle\langle j|) \ge 0.$$

### 6.2 Trace-Norm Contraction and Diamond Norm Bounds

CPTP maps are contractive under the trace norm:
$$\| \mathcal{E}(\rho_1) - \mathcal{E}(\rho_2) \|_1 \le \| \rho_1 - \rho_2 \|_1.$$
The distinguishability of two quantum channels is measured by the diamond norm:
$$\| \mathcal{E}_1 - \mathcal{E}_2 \|_\diamond = \sup_{\rho, \|\rho\|_1 = 1} \| (\mathcal{E}_1 \otimes \mathrm{id}_m)(\rho) - (\mathcal{E}_2 \otimes \mathrm{id}_m)(\rho) \|_1.$$
For any proposed non-CP or trace-violating map, an exact dual witness $W$ with $\mathrm{Tr}(W J(\mathcal{E})) < 0$ certifies the boundary violation.
