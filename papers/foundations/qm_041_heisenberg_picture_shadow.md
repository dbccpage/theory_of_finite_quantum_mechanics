## Preserved Mathematical Content (Dynamics & Heisenberg Evolution)

### 1. Finite-Dimensional Heisenberg Operator Evolution

Let $\mathcal{H}$ be a finite-dimensional inner-product space, $H$ a fixed Hermitian Hamiltonian, and $A$ an observable operator. For continuous parameter $t \in \mathbb{R}$ and unitary map $U(t) = \exp(-i t H)$:

$$
A(t) := U(t)^\dagger A U(t) = \exp(i t H) A \exp(-i t H).
$$

Differentiating with respect to $t$ yields the canonical Heisenberg equation of motion:

$$
\frac{d}{dt} A(t) = i H A(t) - i A(t) H = i [H, A(t)].
$$

*(Sign note: The convention $U(t) = \exp(i t H)$ produces $-i[H, A(t)]$; the generator $\exp(-i t H)$ produces $+i[H, A(t)]$.)*

### 2. Discrete Unitary Step & State-Effect Duality

For a discrete unitary step $U$, expectation values are invariant under the cyclic trace pairing between the Schrödinger state update $\rho \mapsto U \rho U^\dagger$ and the Heisenberg observable pullback $A \mapsto U^\dagger A U$:

$$
\operatorname{Tr}\!\left( (U \rho U^\dagger) A \right) = \operatorname{Tr}\!\left( \rho (U^\dagger A U) \right).
$$

This identity ties the dynamics directly to the dual-picture channel/state–effect pairing established in QM_007 / QM_011.

### 3. Essential Carrier Qualification: Departure from the Diagonal Subspace

Let $G = (V, E)$ be a finite graph. A classical node observable $A \in C^0(G, \mathbb{R})$ is represented as a diagonal matrix in the node basis. Under Heisenberg evolution generated by an off-diagonal Hamiltonian $H$, the evolved operator $A(t)$ generally does **not** remain diagonal.

For example, on a two-dimensional carrier with $H = X$ and $A = Z$:

$$
i [H, A] = i [X, Z] = i (-2i Y) = 2 Y = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -2i \\ 2i & 0 \end{pmatrix} \notin C^0(G, \mathbb{R}).
$$

**Boundary Statement:** The Heisenberg equation of motion lives strictly in the non-commutative matrix observable algebra $\operatorname{End}(\mathcal{H})$. It does **not** constitute a closed dynamical flow within the classical diagonal carrier $C^0(G, \mathbb{R})$.
