1. FOUNDATIONAL GEOMETRY: SIMPLICIAL COMPLEXES AND ℓ¹ COCHAINS


1.1 SIMPLICIAL COMPLEXES AND CAYLEY--MENGER GEOMETRY


Let K be a finite simplicial complex of dimension D. A k-simplex \sigmaᵏ ∈ K⁽ᵏ⁾ is determined by an
ordered set of k+1 vertices (v₀, v₁, \dots, vₖ). The geometry of each simplex is specified by the
matrix of squared edge lengths sᵢⱼ = ℓ(vᵢ, vⱼ)² = sⱼᵢ ≥ 0.

Definition 1.1 (Cayley--Menger Determinant).
For a d-simplex \sigmad, the Cayley--Menger matrix CM(\sigmad) ∈ ℝ⁽d⁺²⁾×⁽d⁺²⁾ is defined by:

CM(\sigmad) = \beginpmatrix
0 & 1 & 1 & \dots & 1
1 & 0 & s₀₁ & \dots & s₀d
1 & s₁₀ & 0 & \dots & s₁d
\vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots
1 & sd₀ & sd₁ & \dots & 0
\endpmatrix

The d-dimensional volume V(\sigmad) satisfies:

V²(\sigmad) = \frac(-1)d⁺¹2d (d!)² \det CM(\sigmad)


A configuration of edge lengths is geometrically realizable in Euclidean space ℝd if and only if
V²(\tau) > 0 for all sub-simplices \tau \subseteq \sigmad.

1.2 BOUNDARY AND COBOUNDARY OPERATORS


Let Cₖ(K, ℝ) denote the vector space of formal real linear combinations of oriented k-simplices, and
let Cᵏ(K, ℝ) = Hom(Cₖ, ℝ) denote the dual space of k-cochains.

The boundary operator ∂ₖ: Cₖ → Cₖ₋₁ is defined on basis simplices by:

∂ₖ (v₀, v₁, \dots, vₖ) = ∑ᵢ₌₀ᵏ (-1)ⁱ (v₀, \dots, \hatvᵢ, \dots, vₖ)

The coboundary operator dₖ₋₁: Cᵏ⁻¹ → Cᵏ is the adjoint of ∂ₖ:

⟨ dₖ₋₁ ω, c ⟩ = ⟨ ω, ∂ₖ c ⟩ \quad ∀ ω ∈ Cᵏ⁻¹, c ∈ Cₖ

In matrix representation with respect to the canonical ordered bases, dₖ₋₁ = ∂ₖᵀ.

Theorem 1.1 (Simplicial Operator Nilpotency).
For every dimension k ≥ 1:

∂ₖ₋₁ \circ ∂ₖ = 0 \quad and \quad dₖ \circ dₖ₋₁ = 0


*Proof.*
For any simplex \sigma = (v₀, \dots, vₖ):

(∂ₖ₋₁ \circ ∂ₖ) \sigma = ∑ᵢ₌₀ᵏ (-1)ⁱ ∂ₖ₋₁ (v₀, \dots, \hatvᵢ, \dots, vₖ)

Expanding the inner boundary splits into indices j < i and j > i:

∑ᵢ₌₀ᵏ (-1)ⁱ ≤ft[ ∑ⱼ<ᵢ (-1)ʲ (v₀, \dots, \hatvⱼ, \dots, \hatvᵢ, \dots, vₖ) + ∑ⱼ>ᵢ (-1)ʲ⁻¹ (v₀, \dots,
\hatvᵢ, \dots, \hatvⱼ, \dots, vₖ) \right]

Relabeling indices shows that each pair of omitted vertices appears exactly twice with opposite
signs (-1)ⁱ⁺ʲ and (-1)ⁱ⁺ʲ⁻¹, canceling identically to zero. Transposition gives dₖ \circ dₖ₋₁ =
∂ₖ₊₁ᵀ \circ ∂ₖᵀ = (∂ₖ \circ ∂ₖ₊₁)ᵀ = 0. \square

2. DISCRETE REGGE ACTION AND CURVATURE DEFICIT COCHAINS


In Regge calculus (Regge 1961), curved Riemannian manifolds are approximated by piece-wise flat
simplicial complexes. Curvature is concentrated entirely on codimension-2 simplices, termed hinges
(h ∈ K⁽ᴰ⁻²⁾).
- In D=2: hinges are vertices v (D-2 = 0).
- In D=3: hinges are edges e (D-2 = 1).
- In D=4: hinges are triangles t (D-2 = 2).

2.1 DIHEDRAL ANGLES AND DEFICIT ANGLES


Definition 2.1 (Simplex Dihedral Angle).
Let \sigmaᴰ be a D-simplex, and let h \subset \sigmaᴰ be a codimension-2 hinge. The hinge h is
shared by exactly two codimension-1 faces (hyperfaces) f₁, f₂ \subset \sigmaᴰ. The dihedral angle
θ(\sigmaᴰ, h) is the angle between the inward unit normals n₁, n₂ of f₁ and f₂:

\cos θ(\sigmaᴰ, h) = - n₁ \cdot n₂

For regular D-simplices where all edge lengths are equal:

θᵣₑg(D) = \arccos≤ft(\frac1D\right)

Specifically:
- D=2 (equilateral triangle at vertex): \arccos(1/2) = \pi/3 ≈ 1.047198 rad.
- D=3 (regular tetrahedron along edge): \arccos(1/3) ≈ 1.230959 rad.
- D=4 (regular 4-simplex along triangle): \arccos(1/4) ≈ 1.318116 rad.

Definition 2.2 (Regge Deficit and Boundary Turning Angles).
For an interior hinge h ∈ int(K), the interior deficit angle εₕ measures local intrinsic curvature:

εₕ = 2\pi - ∑\ₛᵢgₘₐᴰ\ₛᵤₚₛₑₜₕ θ(\sigmaᴰ, h), \quad h ∈ int(K)

For a boundary hinge h ∈ ∂ K, the boundary turning angle \tauₕ measures discrete extrinsic boundary
turning:

\tauₕ = \pi - ∑\ₛᵢgₘₐᴰ\ₛᵤₚₛₑₜₕ θ(\sigmaᴰ, h), \quad h ∈ ∂ K


2.2 THE DISCRETE REGGE ACTION AND ℓ¹ CURVATURE METRIC


The discrete Regge action on a manifold with boundary decomposes into interior and boundary terms,
mirroring the Einstein--Hilbert action with Gibbons--Hawking--York boundary term S = \frac116\pi G
≤ft( ∈tM R \sqrtg \, dᴰ x + 2 ∈t∂M K \sqrth \, dᴰ⁻¹ x \right):

SRₑggₑ = ∑ₕ∈ᵢₙₜ₍K₎ Aₕ \, εₕ + ∑ₕ∈∂K Aₕ \, \tauₕ

where Aₕ is the volume of the hinge (Aᵥ = 1 in 2D, Aₑ = ℓₑ in 3D, and Aₜ = Area(t) in 4D).

Definition 2.3 (Interior Curvature ℓ¹ Norm).
Let ωR ∈ Cᴰ⁻²(int(K), ℝ) be the interior curvature cochain assigning εₕ to each interior hinge h ∈
int(K). The ℓ¹ norm of interior curvature is:

‖ωR‖ᵢₙₜ,₁ = ∑ₕ∈ᵢₙₜ₍K₎ Aₕ |εₕ|

The total boundary turning is tracked separately as \mathcalT∂K = ∑ₕ∈∂K Aₕ |\tauₕ|.

Theorem 2.1 (Local Flatness Invariant).
A triangulated piecewise-linear manifold complex K is locally flat (locally isometric to Euclidean
space ℝᴰ away from the boundary) if and only if the interior curvature norm vanishes:

‖ωR‖ᵢₙₜ,₁ = 0 \quad ⟺ \quad εₕ = 0 \quad ∀ h ∈ int(K)


*Proof & Scope Clarification:*
If K is locally isometric to ℝᴰ, the sum of dihedral angles of simplices meeting around any interior
codimension-2 hinge completes a full rotation of 2\pi radians around the hinge axis in the
transverse 2-plane. Thus ∑ θ = 2\pi \implies εₕ = 0. Conversely, if εₕ = 0 at all interior hinges,
parallel transport around any contractible loop of simplices has trivial holonomy (U = \mathbbI),
ensuring local Euclidean metric recovery.

*Separation of Boundary Turning:* Treating boundary hinges as carrying interior curvature leads to
contradictions. For example, a single flat equilateral triangle in 2D has no interior hinges. At
each of its three boundary vertices, the single triangle provides angle θ = \pi/3, giving boundary
turning \tauᵥ = \pi - \pi/3 = 2\pi/3. Summing over the three vertices yields total boundary turning
∑ \tauᵥ = 3 × (2\pi/3) = 2\pi, which is the topological Gauss-Bonnet turning of a closed planar
curve (∈t \kappag ds = 2\pi), not local interior curvature.

*Local Flatness vs Global Embedding:* Local Euclidean flatness (‖ωR‖ᵢₙₜ,₁ = 0) does not imply global
isometric embedding in ℝᴰ. For instance, a triangulated flat torus Tᴰ is locally Euclidean and
satisfies ‖ωR‖ᵢₙₜ,₁ = 0 everywhere, but cannot be embedded isometrically in ℝᴰ without metric
distortion. \square

3. SIMPLICIAL BIANCHI IDENTITIES IN 3D AND 4D


In continuum differential geometry, the contracted Bianchi identity \nabla_\mu G\mu\ⁿu = 0 ensures
diffeomorphism invariance and the conservation of energy-momentum. In discrete Regge calculus, this
identity manifests through geometric variations on simplicial complexes, though its exact non-
abelian structure requires careful scoping.

3.1 3D CONTRACTED BIANCHI IDENTITY AT VERTICES


In 3D simplicial gravity, hinges are edges e = (v, w), and the discrete action is S = ∑ₑ ℓₑ εₑ.

Theorem 3.1 (3D Contracted Bianchi Identity in Stationary / Flat Backgrounds).
For any interior vertex v ∈ K⁽⁰⁾ of a 3D simplicial complex in a stationary background
configuration, the variation of the discrete Regge action with respect to vertex coordinates xᵥ ∈ ℝ³
satisfies:

\frac∂ SRₑggₑ∂ xᵥ = -∑w\ₛᵢₘᵥ εᵥw \, \hateᵥw = 0

where \hateᵥw = \fracxw - xᵥ‖xw - xᵥ‖ is the unit tangent vector directed along edge (v, w).

*Proof & Physical Scope:*
Under an infinitesimal vertex coordinate shift xᵥ ↦ xᵥ + δ xᵥ, the variation of the Regge action is:

δ S = ∑ₑ ≤ft( δ ℓₑ \, εₑ + ℓₑ \, δ εₑ \right)

By the Schläfli identity for polyhedra (Regge 1961), the second term vanishes identically: ∑ₑ ℓₑ δ
εₑ = 0.
The variation of the edge length ℓᵥw = ‖xw - xᵥ‖ with respect to xᵥ is:

δ ℓᵥw = -\hateᵥw \cdot δ xᵥ

Substituting this yields:

δ S = -≤ft( ∑w\ₛᵢₘᵥ εᵥw \, \hateᵥw \right) \cdot δ xᵥ \implies \frac∂ S∂ xᵥ = -∑w\ₛᵢₘᵥ εᵥw \,
\hateᵥw

When the triangulation is stationary under coordinate relocations (such as on flat backgrounds where
interior vertex displacement is a pure diffeomorphism gauge symmetry), δ S / δ xᵥ = 0, forcing
∑w\ₛᵢₘᵥ εᵥw \hateᵥw = 0.

*Limitation on Generic Curved Complexes:* On generic curved 4D or 3D Regge triangulations without
stationarity, shifting an interior vertex changes the surrounding geometry and deficit angles; exact
vertex gauge invariance is broken at finite lattice spacing and is recovered only in the continuum
or on-shell limit (Bahr & Dittrich 2009). \square

3.2 4D DISCRETE BIANCHI IDENTITY: HOLONOMY OBSTRUCTION AND SCOPED REGIMES


In 4D spacetime, hinges are triangles t ∈ K⁽²⁾. In early versions of this monograph, it was asserted
that the scalar deficit cochain ε ∈ C²(K) satisfies d₂ ε = 0 universally on all closed triangulated
4-manifolds. That unrestricted claim is false and is formally withdrawn.

Withdrawal of Unrestricted Scalar Claim:
In 4D discrete gravity, curvature is fundamentally encoded in parallel transport holonomy matrices
Uₜ ∈ SO(4) associated with triangular hinges. The exact geometric Bianchi identity around any 3-cell
(tetrahedron \tau³) is the non-abelian path-ordered loop product (Hamber & Kagel 2001):

∏ₜ\ₛᵤᵦₛₑₜ∂\ₜₐᵤ³ Uₜ = \mathbbI ∈ SO(4)

Because SO(4) is non-abelian, the logarithm of a product of non-commuting rotation matrices does not
equal the sum of their logarithms (\log ∏ Uᵢ ≠ ∑ \log Uᵢ). Projecting holonomies onto scalar deficit
angles εₜ = θ(Uₜ) discards rotation planes and transport information. Consequently, the linear
scalar coboundary (d₂ ε)(\tau³) = ∑ₜ\ₛᵤᵦₛₑₜ\ₜₐᵤ³ (-1)ⁱ εₜ_ᵢ does not vanish on generic curved
complexes.

Concrete Counterexample on ∂ Δ⁵ ≅ S⁴:
Consider the boundary of a standard 5-simplex ∂ Δ⁵ with 6 vertices 0, \dots, 5, 15 edges, 20
triangles, 15 tetrahedra, and 6 4-simplices.
1. Set 14 edge lengths to ℓᵢⱼ = 1.0, and perturb only a single edge ℓ₀₁ = 1.1.
2. Every 4-simplex remains strictly Euclidean and nondegenerate (smallest Gram matrix eigenvalue ≈
0.4851).
3. Simplicial operator nilpotency holds identically: ∂₂ \circ ∂₃ = 0 and d₂ \circ d₁ = 0.
4. Recomputing the dihedral angles of all six 4-simplices and deficit angles of all twenty
triangular hinges yields:

‖d₂ ε‖₁ ≈ 1.956565, \quad ‖d₂ ε‖_∞ ≈ 0.244571, \quad (d₂ ε)(0,2,3,4) ≈ -0.244571

Thus, even on the paper's own topology, the scalar deficit cochain fails to be closed under d₂.

Theorem 3.2 (Simplicial Bianchi Identity: Scoped Regimes).
The linear scalar coboundary identity d₂ ε = 0 holds strictly under the following declared
conditions:
1. *Uniform and symmetric complexes:* on regular boundary complexes where all deficit angles are
identical (εₜ = c), the alternating face sum across every tetrahedron vanishes identically:

(d₂ ε)(\tau³) = c - c + c - c = 0

*(Incidence count correction:* In ∂ Δ⁵, each triangle belongs to exactly 6 - 3 = 3 (not 5)
4-simplices. Its regular deficit angle is εᵣₑg = 2\pi - 3\arccos(1/4) ≈ 2.328837 rad, rather than
-0.307395 rad).*
2. *Discrete abelian gauge models:* for an abelian connection A ∈ C¹(K, ℝ) with curvature 2-form F =
d₁ A, the operator nilpotency Theorem 1.1 guarantees d₂ F = d₂ d₁ A = 0 identically.
3. *Linearized Regge calculus:* around flat Euclidean space where deficit angles are infinitesimal
(ε = O(δ)), so non-abelian commutator terms [Uᵢ, Uⱼ] = O(ε²) are neglected. \square

4.1 CANONICAL 3+1 CAUCHY SLICES AND PROPOSED CONSTRAINT MODELS


In continuous general relativity, the 3+1 ADM formulation describes gravity as a constrained
Hamiltonian system on a spatial hypersurface Σ, governed by spatial diffeomorphism constraints
\mathcalHₐ and the scalar Wheeler--DeWitt Hamiltonian constraint \mathcalH_\perp.

In a discrete simplicial setting on a 3-complex Σ, one considers:
1. Spatial metric edge lengths: qₑ = ℓₑ > 0 for e ∈ Σ⁽¹⁾.
2. Conjugate momentum cochain: \pi ∈ C¹(Σ, ℝ), representing discrete extrinsic curvature.

A proposed discrete model assigns constraint violations to edges or vertices:
- Spatial Momentum Violation: \mathcalHₐ(v) = ∑ₑ\ₛᵤₚₛₑₜᵥ \piₑ \, \hateₐ.
- Hamiltonian Scalar Violation: \mathcalH_\perp(v) = \frac1\sqrtqᵥ ∑ₑ,ₑ'\ₛᵤₚₛₑₜᵥ Gₑₑ' \piₑ \piₑ' -
\sqrtqᵥ R(v), where Gₑₑ' is a discrete DeWitt supermetric.

*Important Mathematical Scope Note:* The mapping from continuous canonical fields (qₐᵦ, \piab) to a
discrete cochain ω ∈ C¹(Σ), the definition of the discrete supermetric Gₑₑ', and the identification
of spatial coordinate reparametrizations with coboundaries ω ↦ ω - d₀ α constitute a proposed
discrete model rather than an established mathematical derivation from continuous Einstein gravity.
Minimizing an ℓ¹ residual Φ evaluates linear gauge-orthogonality on the discrete complex; it does
not compute Poisson brackets of constraints, verify structure functions, or construct a quantum
Wheeler--DeWitt operator acting on wave functionals Ψ[q].

4.2 LINEAR COCHAIN QUOTIENTS: C¹ / IM(D₀) VS COHOMOLOGY H¹


Let D: C⁰(Σ) → C¹(Σ) be any finite linear coboundary operator (such as the simplicial coboundary d₀
= ∂₁ᵀ). Given an arbitrary constraint cochain ω ∈ C¹(Σ), gauge transformations act as translations
by pure coboundaries:

ω ↦ ω - D α, \quad α ∈ C⁰(Σ)

The gauge-invariant measure of constraint violation is the quotient norm on the quotient vector
space C¹(Σ) / im(D):

Definition 4.1 (ℓ¹ Constraint Quotient Norm).
For any finite linear operator D: ℝ|V| → ℝ|E| and cochain ω ∈ ℝ|E|:

ΦD([ω]) = \minα∈ℝ|V| ‖ω - D α‖₁


*Distinction Between Cochain Quotient and Cohomology:*
For an arbitrary cochain ω, the quotient space is C¹ / im(d₀). It is not necessarily cohomology H¹ =
\ker(d₁) / im(d₀). The quotient class [ω] belongs to cohomology if and only if ω is a cocycle (d₁ ω
= 0).
For example, on a single filled triangle with edges (0,1), (1,2), (2,0) and face (0,1,2), the
circulation cochain ω = (1, 1, 1)ᵀ satisfies (d₁ ω)(0,1,2) = 1 + 1 + 1 = 3 ≠ 0. Thus ω is not a
cocycle and carries no non-trivial class in H¹, yet its quotient distance Φ([ω]) = 3 is strictly
positive.

Theorem 4.1 (Gauge Invariance of ΦD).
For any pure gauge vector γ ∈ ℝ|V|:

ΦD([ω + D γ]) = ΦD([ω])


*Proof.*
By definition:

ΦD([ω + D γ]) = \minα ‖ω + D γ - D α‖₁ = \minα ‖ω - D (α - γ)‖₁

Substituting β = α - γ establishes that the set of vectors over which the minimum is taken is
identical, hence ΦD([ω + D γ]) = ΦD([ω]). \square

Definition 4.2 (Exact Linear Constraint Closure).
A discrete state satisfies exact constraint closure if and only if ΦD([ω]) = 0. Because im(D) is a
closed subspace in finite dimensions, this condition holds if and only if ω ∈ im(D) (i.e. the
